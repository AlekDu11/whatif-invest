You can buy Bitcoin in Ukraine in 15–30 minutes with about $10. The simplest route is P2P on a large exchange. You send hryvnia to another person's monobank or PrivatBank card, and the exchange releases the crypto to you. One Bitcoin costs {{price:btc}} right now, but you don't need a whole coin. You can buy a small fraction of one.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Ukrainian hryvnia cards can't pay an exchange directly for crypto. The National Bank of Ukraine (NBU) banned that in April 2022. The main route is P2P trades in hryvnia.
> - On November 25, 2026, Ukraine's parliament passed law No. 10225-d on virtual assets, with 271 votes in favour. Under it, tax is charged on profit, not on the full sale amount.
> - Buying and holding Bitcoin creates no tax. The tax question comes up only when you sell crypto for money.

## Cards don't work directly, so Ukrainians use P2P

On April 21, 2022, the NBU banned buying crypto from hryvnia bank accounts. So a hryvnia card entered on an exchange will usually be declined. What works:

1. **Exchange P2P.** Pick a highly rated seller and pay them with an ordinary card-to-card transfer in hryvnia. Once the seller confirms the money arrived, the exchange releases the crypto to your account. During the trade the exchange holds the seller's coins in escrow, so the seller can't simply vanish with your money.
2. **A foreign-currency card.** Crypto purchases from your own USD or EUR card are possible within a monthly NBU limit. The limit has changed several times, so check the current figure with your bank.
3. **Exchange offices.** They're fast, but their rates are usually worse and their reliability varies a lot. If you're new, stick to P2P on a large exchange.

The most common approach in Ukraine is to buy the stablecoin USDT first over P2P (a digital dollar that stays close to $1). You then use the USDT to buy Bitcoin on the exchange. That usually costs less in spread than buying Bitcoin for hryvnia directly.

## Step by step: your first Bitcoin through P2P

1. **Sign up on a large exchange with hryvnia P2P**, such as [Binance](/go/binance?from=buy-ua&l=en) or [Bybit](/go/bybit?from=buy-ua&l=en). Fees are compared on our [exchanges page]({lang}/exchanges/). These are partner links, and they never change the price you pay.
2. **Turn on 2FA and complete identity verification (KYC).** You'll need a passport or ID card and a selfie. P2P won't unlock without it. That's normal, because it protects both sides of the trade.
3. **Open P2P → Buy → USDT → UAH** and choose your bank, such as monobank or PrivatBank.
4. **Sort the sellers** by number of trades and completion rate. Pick sellers with hundreds of trades and a completion rate above 98%, and check their "from–to" limits.
5. **Pay only the card shown by the exchange, and only inside the trade.** Leave the transfer comment empty: no "crypto" and no "USDT". After paying, tap "I have paid".
6. **Once the USDT arrives, go to spot** and buy BTC with USDT. Any amount works, even a few dollars.

Never move the conversation to Telegram or Viber, and never pay outside the trade. That is the most common P2P scam. If a seller asks you to message them in another app, cancel the trade.

## Taxes: today's rules and what the new law changes

**Now.** Income from selling crypto counts toward your yearly taxable income. The general rates are 18% personal income tax plus a 5% military levy. Buying and holding create no tax. The tax comes up when you sell crypto for money.

**Law No. 10225-d.** Parliament passed it on November 25, 2026, and it now needs the president's signature. The text prepared for the second reading sets these rules:

- tax is charged on **profit**, meaning the sale price minus the purchase price;
- swapping one crypto for another is not taxed;
- crypto bought before the law takes effect gets a 5% preferential rate until the end of 2028;
- the securities regulator NSSMC becomes the main market regulator, and exchanges must be authorised to operate;
- the law is planned to take effect on July 1, 2027.

Check the final details in the official text of the law. We'll update this guide once it's signed. Keep a record of every purchase (date, amount, rate), because you'll need it to work out your profit.

## Where to keep your Bitcoin after buying

If you top up a small amount each month, a large exchange is a convenient place to keep it. Once the amount grows, many people move their coins to their own wallet, where only they control the keys. Never show anyone your seed phrase (the 12–24 words that restore a wallet), and never photograph it.

## What it means if you buy regularly

You don't have to time the market. Many people just buy a small fixed amount every week or month, an approach called DCA. Someone who put $10 a day into Bitcoin for 5 years invested {{dcainv:5}} in total, and it's worth {{dca:btc:5}} today ({{dcaroi:btc:5}}). But Bitcoin has also dropped more than 70% below its peak in the past. Only put in money you can leave alone for years. You can run your own numbers in the [Bitcoin calculator]({lang}/btc/#calc).

## FAQ

### How much money do I need to buy Bitcoin in Ukraine?

On most large exchanges, about $5–10 is enough. On P2P the seller sets the minimum, often 200–500 UAH.

### Can I buy Bitcoin with monobank or Privat24?

Not inside the bank's app. But you can use those cards to pay a P2P seller with an ordinary card-to-card transfer.

### Is it legal?

Yes. Owning crypto is legal in Ukraine. The new law sets rules for exchanges and taxes. It doesn't ban buying.
