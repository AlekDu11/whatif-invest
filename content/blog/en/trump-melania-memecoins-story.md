**What happened to people who bought the TRUMP and MELANIA memecoins?** Most of them lost money. Both tokens shot up in the first hours after launch in January 2025 and then fell by about 90% or more within months, while most of the supply stayed with companies and wallets linked to the creators.

> **Key takeaways**
> - TRUMP went from about $1.21 to $75.35 in its first two days in January 2025; by April 2025 it had lost about 90% of that value (Fortune).
> - 80% of the 1 billion TRUMP tokens were set aside for two Trump-linked companies, released step by step over three years. Ordinary buyers shared the other 20%.
> - MELANIA peaked at about $13.73 in January 2025 and was down about 98% a few months later; a lawsuit later accused its launch team of a pump-and-dump (claims they deny).

## What are TRUMP and MELANIA?

They are memecoins (tokens with no business behind them — see [what memecoins are]({lang}/blog/what-are-memecoins/)) on the Solana blockchain. "Official Trump" (TRUMP) was announced by Donald Trump three days before his inauguration; MELANIA was launched by Melania Trump two days later. Owning them gives you no share of any company, no profits and no vote — only a token whose price depends on the next buyer.

## The story in dates and numbers

**17–19 January 2025 — the launch.** TRUMP went on sale on 17 January 2025. There were 1 billion tokens; about 200 million (20%) went on the market at launch. The other 80% were for CIC Digital (a Trump Organization affiliate) and Fight Fight Fight LLC, to be unlocked (released for sale) in stages until 2028. Within two days the price went from about $1.21 to $75.35 and the market value reached about $14 billion. One early trader bought about $1.1 million of TRUMP in the first two minutes and reported more than $90 million in proceeds (The Block).

**19 January 2025 — MELANIA.** Just after 21:00 UTC, MELANIA launched. Within two hours its market value passed $6 billion (DEXScreener data reported by Cointelegraph). At the same time TRUMP fell about 38%, from $74.6 to $45.9, in 40 minutes — buyers moved money from one token to the other.

**February–April 2025 — the fall.** By 21 April 2025 TRUMP was at about $8.28, around 90% below its January high. On 18 April 2025 the first big unlock released 40 million more tokens, then worth about $330 million, to the creator companies (Fortune). MELANIA did worse: in April 2025 the analytics firm Bubblemaps said 50 million MELANIA tokens (about $30 million) had been moved out of "community" wallets and partly sold, without an explanation from the team; the price was then about $0.51, down more than 96% (Cointelegraph).

**23 April – 22 May 2025 — the dinner.** The TRUMP website announced a dinner with the president for the 220 biggest holders, counted by their average holdings from 23 April to 12 May 2025. The price jumped more than 50% after the announcement. According to the firm Inca Digital, the top 220 together spent more than $140 million on the token; the dinner took place on 22 May 2025 at Trump's golf club in Virginia (Axios). The contest made the price rise — but the people who bought for it needed others to buy after them.

**October 2025 — the lawsuit.** An amended class-action complaint in New York accused Meteora co-founder Benjamin Chow and Kelsier Ventures' Hayden Davis of running MELANIA, LIBRA and other tokens as "pump-and-dump" schemes. They deny wrongdoing; Chow said earlier that he received no tokens. The claims have not been proven in court (The Block).

**End of June 2026 — the count.** Nansen counted about 989,000 TRUMP buyer wallets in a loss, together about $3.81 billion, against about 492,000 wallets in profit (The Block, July 2026). Today TRUMP trades at {{price:trump}} ({{chg1y:trump}} over 12 months) and MELANIA at {{price:melania}}.

## Why it went this way

1. **Most tokens belonged to insiders.** When 80% of the supply waits to be unlocked, every unlock can add sellers.
2. **The launch was the top.** The first hours, when the whole world heard the news, brought the most buyers and the highest prices. Early wallets and bots (see "sniper" in our [memecoin glossary]({lang}/memes/glossary/)) sold into that rush.
3. **Events give short jumps, not value.** The dinner contest lifted the price for a few weeks, but there was no business to hold it up afterwards.

## What beginners can learn from it

- **Check who holds the supply** before you buy any memecoin: the top-10 holders' share and the unlock schedule. The lesson [How to check a memecoin before buying]({lang}/blog/how-to-check-a-memecoin-before-buying/) shows how; our [memecoins page]({lang}/memes/) has a safety light.
- **A famous name is marketing, not protection.** The same pattern happened with other [celebrity and political memecoins]({lang}/blog/celebrity-political-memecoins-story/).
- **If you still try, use only money you can lose completely**, set the amount in advance and track it in [your portfolio]({lang}/me/).

> **⚠️ Watch out**
> - **Fake copies.** Dozens of tokens use the same name and picture. Only the contract address published on the official site or verified account is the real one.
> - **"Win a prize if you hold" contests.** They push you to buy at the most hyped moment and to hold through unlocks.
> - **Buying in the first hours.** That is when insiders and bots have the cheapest coins and the most to sell.
