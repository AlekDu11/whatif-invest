In India you can buy Bitcoin in about 20 minutes with UPI or an IMPS/NEFT bank transfer, starting from a few hundred rupees. The safe way is an exchange registered with the Financial Intelligence Unit (FIU-IND), because the government has blocked dozens of offshore apps that skipped registration. One Bitcoin costs {{price:btc}} right now, but you can buy a small fraction of a coin.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Crypto is legal to buy and hold in India, but it is not legal tender and there is still no full crypto law. Exchanges must register with FIU-IND under the anti-money-laundering law (PMLA); a Lok Sabha answer listed 54 registered providers as of March 9, 2026.
> - Profit is taxed at a flat 30% plus 4% cess (and surcharge where it applies). Only the purchase cost is deductible, and losses can't be set off or carried forward.
> - When you sell, 1% TDS is deducted once your sales pass ₹50,000 a year (₹10,000 for some payers). The new Income-tax Act 2025, in force since April 1, 2026, kept these rules.

## Who regulates crypto in India

There is no crypto regulator like SEBI for shares. What exists is registration: since 2023 every platform that serves Indians must register with FIU-IND as a reporting entity. In December 2023 FIU-IND issued notices to nine offshore exchanges, including Binance, Kraken, Gate, KuCoin, MEXC and Bitfinex, and asked for their sites to be blocked. Binance, KuCoin and Bybit then registered and paid fines, and are back. Others were not: MEXC stopped serving Indian users on February 28, 2026, BingX was among 25 platforms ordered off app stores in October 2025, and 15 more were targeted in September 2026.

Registration is not a licence or a guarantee. It means the exchange does KYC and reports to Indian authorities. The Reserve Bank of India is still wary: in July 2026 Reuters reported that the RBI wants banks and financial institutions kept away from crypto.

## How people in India pay

1. **UPI.** The quickest way to add rupees on Indian exchanges such as CoinDCX, CoinSwitch, ZebPay, WazirX and Mudrex. Some banks refuse UPI payments to crypto apps (SBI did so in 2022), so try another bank if a payment fails.
2. **IMPS, NEFT or RTGS.** A normal bank transfer to the exchange's account, good for larger amounts.
3. **P2P.** Global exchanges like Binance and Bybit mainly sell crypto for rupees through peer-to-peer trades, where you pay another user directly. It works, but in 2026 users have reported bank accounts frozen after a P2P counterparty's money turned out to be linked to fraud. Trade only with verified, high-volume sellers and keep your records.

## Scams to avoid in India

The most common trap is not an exchange but a fake "trading platform" pushed on WhatsApp or Telegram. In December 2025 the Enforcement Directorate exposed 26 fake crypto investment websites that had run for years with celebrity photos and referral bonuses. Rules of thumb: no real exchange guarantees daily profit, nobody legitimate asks you to pay "tax" or a "fee" before a withdrawal, and an app that is not on the FIU-IND list should not get your money.

The WazirX hack is a second lesson. Hackers stole about $230 million in July 2024, withdrawals were frozen for over a year, and trading resumed only on October 24, 2025, after a Singapore court approved a restructuring. Don't keep more on any exchange than you need.

## Taxes in 2026

- **Rate:** 30% of the gain, plus 4% health and education cess and surcharge if your income is high. It doesn't matter how long you held the coin.
- **Deductions:** only the cost of acquisition. No deduction for fees, internet or anything else.
- **Losses:** a loss on one coin can't reduce the gain on another, or any other income, and it can't be carried forward.
- **What counts as a transfer:** selling for rupees, swapping one crypto for another, and paying with crypto. Crypto received as a gift can be taxed too.
- **TDS:** 1% of the sale value, deducted by Indian exchanges. It is a credit against your final tax, not an extra tax. On foreign platforms and P2P you usually have to declare and pay yourself.
- **Filing:** report every transaction in Schedule VDA of ITR-2 or ITR-3 and match it with the TDS shown in Form 26AS.

Budget 2026 did not change the rates. It added penalties from April 1, 2026 for platforms that fail to file crypto transaction statements, so expect the tax department to see your trades.

## Where to keep your Bitcoin

For small regular buys, a registered exchange is convenient. As the amount grows, many people move coins to their own wallet, where only they hold the keys. Write the recovery phrase (12–24 words) on paper, never photograph it and never type it into a website.

## FAQ

### Is Bitcoin legal in India in 2026?

Yes, buying and holding it is legal and taxed. It isn't legal tender, and exchanges must be registered with FIU-IND.

### Can I buy Bitcoin with UPI?

Yes, on Indian exchanges that accept UPI deposits. If your bank declines the payment, use IMPS/NEFT or another bank.

### Is Binance allowed in India?

Binance registered with FIU-IND in 2024, paid a ₹18.82 crore penalty and resumed services. Rupee purchases there go mainly through P2P.

### Do I pay tax if I lose money on crypto?

You pay 30% on each profitable sale, and losses can't be offset. So you can owe tax even in a year when your total result is negative.
