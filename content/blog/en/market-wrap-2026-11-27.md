Friday, November 27 split the markets in two. In a short Black Friday session the S&P 500 rose **1.6%** to 7,297.49 and the Nasdaq **2.3%**, as chip stocks bounced back, while Bitcoin lost about **2.2%** in 24 hours and dipped below $59,000 during the day (live now: **{{price:btc}}**). Gold and silver kept rebounding from their 14-month lows.

> **Key takeaways**
> - S&P 500 +1.60%, Nasdaq +2.31%, Dow +1.18% on Friday; for the week +5.6%, +6.7% and +5.5% (AP).
> - 247 of the 300 largest companies in our data rose; Micron +7.6%, Anthropic +11.0%, SMIC in Shanghai +15.4%.
> - Only 8 of the 47 coins worth $1 billion or more rose; Bitcoin −2.2%, Ethereum −3.6%, World Liberty Financial −17.9%.
> - Crypto Fear & Greed is 10/100 today, 12 a day ago and 12 a week ago — "extreme fear".

## The big picture: stocks rallied, crypto did not follow

Bitcoin failed to climb back above $62,000 on Friday morning and slipped under $59,000 in the afternoon, Coin-Turk reported. About $6.8 billion of Bitcoin options expired on Deribit at 08:00 UTC; the report notes there is no evidence the expiry alone caused the drop. In our data Bitcoin was near $60,090 early on Saturday and Ethereum near $1,668 (−3.6%). Total crypto market value was about **$2.08 trillion** (CoinGecko) and Bitcoin's share **57.8%**.

Wall Street had a very different day. The S&P 500 closed at 7,297.49 (+114.71 points), the Dow at 50,774.94 (+591.99) and the Nasdaq Composite at 24,129.50 (+543.70); the small-cap Russell 2000 gained 1.7% (AP). The VIX "fear index" fell about 11% to 23.8. The S&P 500 ETF rose **1.6%** and the Nasdaq-100 ETF **2.7%** in our data ([our brief]({lang}/news/why-is-the-stock-market-up-today-black-friday-november-2026/)).

Metals rose for a second day: gold **+3.8%** to about $3,669 an ounce and silver **+9.1%** to about $43.70 in our data ([why gold is up]({lang}/news/why-is-gold-up-today-november-27-2026/)). Brent crude was near $91 a barrel and US crude near $83 ahead of Sunday's OPEC+ meeting.

## Biggest winners

Among the 300 largest companies:

- [Anthropic]({lang}/anthropic/) **+11.0%** to about $91.90, above its $83 IPO price — no company-specific news found; it rose with the rest of AI.
- [Micron]({lang}/mu/) **+7.6%**, [Seagate]({lang}/stx/) **+7.9%**, [Western Digital]({lang}/wdc/) **+7.2%** and [SK hynix]({lang}/sk-hynix/) **+7.5%** — memory and storage makers led the chip rebound as worries about AI spending eased.
- [SMIC]({lang}/smic-cn/) **+15.4%** in Shanghai (+16.4% in Hong Kong) and [Alibaba]({lang}/alibaba/) **+7.0%** — after Beijing pledged state buying to steady its stock market ([brief]({lang}/news/why-are-chinese-chip-stocks-up-smic-november-2026/)).

In crypto, [Bitway]({lang}/btw/) jumped **+19.2%** and [Sky]({lang}/sky/) **+9.0%**, with no news found for either; [JUST]({lang}/jst/) added 3.5%. Away from the big coins, [FET]({lang}/fet/) rose about 12% on Friday ([brief]({lang}/news/fet-jumps-12-percent-ai-token-rebound-november-2026/)).

## Biggest losers

- [World Liberty Financial]({lang}/wlfi/) **−17.9%** to about $0.034. Early on Saturday it fell another 28% within one hour, wiping out about $10.9 million of leveraged long bets (TokenPost). No cause has been reported.
- [Mantle]({lang}/mnt/) **−5.2%**, [MemeCore]({lang}/m/) **−3.9%**, [Venice Token]({lang}/vvv/) **−3.8%** and [Polkadot]({lang}/dot/) **−3.6%** — no coin-specific news found; they fell with the market.
- [Ethereum]({lang}/eth/) **−3.6%** was the biggest mover among the 20 largest coins.
- In stocks, [Vertex Pharmaceuticals]({lang}/vrtx/) **−5.2%** led the decliners, and Chinese energy and bank giants such as [China Shenhua]({lang}/china-shenhua-energy/) (−4.0%) and [Sinopec]({lang}/sinopec/) (−3.4%) lagged; we found no specific news.

## The news that moved markets

1. **Chips came back.** The Philadelphia semiconductor index rose nearly 5% as fears about AI profits eased, Yahoo Finance reported; Nvidia, which jumped after Jensen Huang's return as CEO earlier in the week, ended only +0.8%.
2. **Strong US growth, higher yields.** Third-quarter GDP growth was revised up to 4.3% a year from 3.4%; the 10-year Treasury yield rose to about 4.9%, keeping a December Fed rate hike in play.
3. **Beijing steps in.** A State Council pledge to use the state-backed "national team" to steady prices sent China's chip index up a record 17.6%.
4. **Crypto funds keep bleeding.** US spot Bitcoin ETFs lost $453 million on Wednesday, part of a six-session outflow streak of about $2.6 billion (Coin-Turk).
5. **Holiday shopping.** The NRF expects US holiday sales above $1 trillion, up 4.2%.
