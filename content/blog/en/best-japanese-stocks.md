## Japanese stocks by sector: chips, banks, trading houses

Grouping the winners shows how different the sectors were over the same five years ($10 a day, split equally):

- **Chips** (Advantest, Tokyo Electron, Disco): **+163.6%** — but **−16.9%** over the last year.
- **Big three banks** (MUFG, SMFG, Mizuho): **+140.9%**, and **+57.7%** over three years.
- **Trading houses** (Mitsubishi Corp, Itochu, Mitsui & Co.): **+83.8%**.
- **All 40 stocks:** **+59.0%** over five years, **+23.9%** over three, **+1.7%** over one.

The sector that led for five years can be the one that falls hardest in a single year. That is the main argument for owning many of them rather than guessing one.

## How to buy Japanese stocks from abroad

- **A broker with Tokyo Stock Exchange access.** Shares normally trade in lots of **100 shares**, so one lot of a pricey stock can cost thousands of dollars; some brokers offer fractions or single shares.
- **A Japan ETF.** An MSCI Japan, TOPIX or Nikkei 225 fund buys hundreds of companies at once. Europeans need UCITS ETFs; some versions hedge the yen, which removes the currency swing in both directions.
- **US-listed ADRs.** Toyota, Sony, MUFG and others also trade in New York.

The Tokyo market is open **9:00–11:30 and 12:30–15:30 Japan time** (UTC+9); the afternoon session was extended by 30 minutes in November 2024. See every Japanese company we track by size on our [Japan stocks page]({lang}/stocks-japan/).

## Risks to know before you buy

- **The yen:** a stronger yen helps dollar-based holders, a weaker one hurts — over five years it has mostly weakened.
- **Export dependence:** carmakers and machinery depend on US and Chinese demand and on tariffs.
- **Cyclical winners:** chip equipment and banks led this cycle; leadership rarely lasts forever.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Regular buying worked well in Japan over five years: 36 of the 40 stocks ended above the money put in, and the whole basket beat the S&P 500. But the spread is huge — from {{dcaroi:advantest:5}} to {{dcaroi:daiichi-sankyo:5}} — and someone who started a year ago is roughly at break-even. A plan on a broad basket or an index fund relies far less on picking the right sector. Past results, not a forecast.
