**AI crypto tokens** are cryptocurrencies linked to projects that try to combine blockchains with artificial intelligence. Some pay people for training AI models, some rent out graphics cards, and some want to be the base network that AI "agents" use to pay each other.

The idea attracts a lot of attention every time AI is in the news. But these tokens are among the most volatile assets in crypto. This guide explains what the four best-known ones actually do, how far they have fallen from their peaks, and what to check before you buy any.

## The short version

- **Bittensor (TAO)** — a network where AI models compete and earn tokens for useful work.
- **Render (RENDER)** — a marketplace for renting GPU power for 3D rendering and AI tasks.
- **FET (Artificial Superintelligence Alliance)** — the token of a merger of AI crypto projects led by Fetch.ai, focused on AI agents.
- **NEAR** — a general-purpose blockchain that markets itself as infrastructure for AI apps.

None of them is an AI company in the way Nvidia or Microsoft is. You do not own a share of any business. You own a token whose price depends on demand for the network and on market sentiment.

## Bittensor (TAO)

Bittensor is a decentralised network made of many **subnets**. Each subnet focuses on one task — for example text generation, data scraping or price prediction. Participants called **miners** provide AI models or computing work, and **validators** score how useful that work is. The network pays out new TAO according to those scores.

Key facts:

- Maximum supply is **21 million TAO**, the same cap as Bitcoin, with issuance cut in half at set points ("halvings").
- Holders can stake TAO to validators and subnets to earn a share of rewards.
- Current price: **{{price:tao}}**.

The risk: it is hard for an outsider to judge whether the subnets produce work anyone would pay for outside the network. Much of the demand comes from the rewards themselves.

## Render (RENDER)

Render Network was started by the team behind OTOY, a company that makes 3D rendering software. People with spare graphics cards (GPUs) offer them on the network, and artists, studios and AI developers pay in RENDER to use that computing power. In 2023 the project moved from Ethereum to the Solana blockchain and changed its ticker from RNDR to RENDER.

- Use case: rendering images, animation and some AI workloads on rented GPUs.
- Current price: **{{price:render}}**.

The risk: it competes with large cloud providers and other GPU marketplaces, and token demand does not grow automatically when GPU demand grows.

## FET (Artificial Superintelligence Alliance)

FET started as the token of **Fetch.ai**, a project building software "agents" that can search, negotiate and pay on behalf of users. In 2024 Fetch.ai, SingularityNET and Ocean Protocol merged their tokens into one, under the name **Artificial Superintelligence Alliance**, keeping FET as the ticker. Ocean Protocol later left the alliance in 2025.

- Use case: AI agents, decentralised AI services and data.
- Current price: **{{price:fet}}**.

The risk: mergers, governance disputes and changing plans show how much these projects still depend on a small group of teams and foundations.

## NEAR

NEAR Protocol is a layer-1 blockchain like Ethereum or Solana. It aims to be fast and cheap and has recently focused its message on "AI agents" that can hold assets and act on-chain. One of its co-founders, Illia Polosukhin, is a co-author of *Attention Is All You Need*, the 2017 research paper that introduced the transformer architecture behind today's chatbots.

- Use case: a general blockchain for apps, with an AI-focused roadmap.
- Current price: **{{price:near}}**.

The risk: NEAR competes with many other blockchains, and the AI story is mostly a plan, not current revenue.

## Risks to understand before buying

1. **Extreme volatility.** Falls of 80–95% have already happened. A token can lose most of its value and never come back.
2. **Narrative-driven prices.** Prices often follow AI news (for example, chip earnings) rather than real usage of the network.
3. **Token supply.** New tokens are issued as rewards, and early investors and teams may hold large amounts. More supply can push the price down.
4. **Liquidity and listings.** Smaller tokens can be removed from exchanges or trade with big price gaps.
5. **Scams and fake tokens.** Many copycat "AI tokens" exist. Check the contract address and the official project website.

## FAQ

### Are AI crypto tokens the same as AI stocks?

No. A stock is a share in a company with revenue and profits. A token gives you no ownership of a business; its value depends on network demand and market sentiment.

### Which AI token is the biggest?

By market value, Bittensor (TAO), NEAR, Render and FET have been among the largest in recent years, but the ranking changes often. Check the live list on our AI hub.

### Why did AI tokens fall so much?

They rose very fast in early 2024 on AI excitement. When enthusiasm cooled and the wider crypto market weakened, buyers left and new token issuance kept adding supply.

### Can I buy AI tokens with DCA?

Yes, most large exchanges allow recurring buys. DCA smooths the entry price, but it does not protect you if a token keeps falling for years.
