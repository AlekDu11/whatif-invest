## The crashes you would have had to sit through

Big long-term gains came with deep falls. Based on Nvidia's monthly closing prices:

- **2000–2002 (dot-com crash):** around −87% from peak to bottom. The stock did not return to that high until 2006.
- **2007–2008 (financial crisis):** around −79%. It took until 2016 to recover.
- **Late 2018 (crypto-mining hangover):** around −52% in a few months, as demand for gaming and mining cards collapsed.
- **2021–2022 (rate hikes):** around −63% from November 2021 to autumn 2022, just months before the AI boom began.

Daily falls were even sharper than these monthly numbers. Imagine watching three-quarters of your money disappear — many people sell at exactly that moment. Someone buying $10 a day kept buying at low prices instead, and that is a big part of why DCA results look so strong after recoveries.

This is the key lesson: **a great company can still be a painful investment to hold.** Nvidia's history includes several periods when it looked "finished".

## Why Nvidia's price depends so much on earnings

Today Nvidia earns most of its money from one business: data-centre chips used to train and run AI models. In its fiscal year 2025 (ending January 2025), Nvidia reported revenue of about $130.5 billion, and the data-centre segment made up roughly $115 billion of it — close to 90%.

That creates a few specific risks:

1. **Earnings days move the stock a lot.** Every quarter, investors compare results and guidance with very high expectations. Even a strong report can send the stock down if it is less strong than hoped.
2. **Few, very large customers.** A big part of sales goes to a small number of cloud giants. If they cut their AI spending, Nvidia feels it fast.
3. **Competition.** AMD, and in-house chips from Google, Amazon and Microsoft, all target the same market.
4. **Politics.** US export rules on advanced chips to China have already changed what Nvidia can sell there.
5. **Leadership.** Company-specific news, like a change of CEO, can move the stock sharply — as the November 2026 leadership change showed (see our [news analysis]({lang}/blog/nvidia-after-jensen-huang-earnings/)).

None of this says whether the price will go up or down. It explains why a single-stock position in Nvidia is much more volatile than an index fund.

## What DCA can and cannot do

**DCA helps you:**

- avoid putting everything in at a peak;
- keep buying calmly through falls, when the same $10 gets more shares;
- build a habit that does not depend on reading the news.

**DCA does not:**

- guarantee a profit — if a stock falls for years, regular buying still loses money;
- remove concentration risk — one company is still one company;
- beat a lump sum in a market that only goes up.

## FAQ

### How much would $10 a day in Nvidia be worth after 10 years?

Investing {{dcainv:10}} over 10 years would be worth about {{dca:nvda:10}} today based on monthly data. The number updates every day with the market.

### Is it too late to invest in Nvidia?

Nobody knows. Past growth does not tell you future returns, and the stock has already had several falls of more than 50%. What you can control is how much of your money depends on one company.

### Is DCA better than buying Nvidia all at once?

In a strongly rising market, a lump sum at the start usually ends higher. DCA lowers the risk of buying everything at a bad moment and is easier to stick to emotionally.

### Can I buy less than one Nvidia share?

Yes. Many brokers offer fractional shares, so $10 can buy a small part of a share.
