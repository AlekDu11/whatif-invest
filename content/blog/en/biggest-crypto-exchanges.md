**The biggest crypto exchange by trading volume is Binance: in 2025 it handled about $7.3 trillion of spot trades, 39.2% of everything the ten largest exchanges traded, according to CoinGecko.** Next come Bybit, MEXC, Gate, Crypto.com, Bitget, OKX and Coinbase, each with 6–8% — so the "top crypto exchanges by volume" list is one giant and a pack of similar-sized followers.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Largest crypto exchanges by 2025 spot volume (CoinGecko): **Binance 39.2%**, Bybit 8.1%, MEXC 7.8%, Gate 7.5%, Crypto.com 7.2%, Bitget 6.4%, OKX 6.3%, Coinbase 6.1%, HTX 6.0%, Upbit 5.5%.
> - On 23 November 2026 Binance also held the most customer assets: about **$110 billion** of reserves, four times OKX (about $27 billion), per DefiLlama.
> - Biggest does not mean safest: in October 2026 Binance suffered a hack of about **$6.3 billion**, the largest in crypto history, and paused withdrawals for several days.

## The biggest crypto exchanges by trading volume

Trading volume is the dollar value of all trades on an exchange in a period. It is the usual way to rank exchanges because it shows where buyers and sellers actually meet — more volume usually means tighter prices and easier selling.

CoinGecko's full-year count of spot trading (buying and selling coins for cash or stablecoins, no leverage) for 2025:

1. **[Binance]({lang}/crypto-exchanges/binance/)** — $7.3 trillion, 39.2% of the top-10 total
2. **[Bybit]({lang}/crypto-exchanges/bybit_spot/)** — about $1.5 trillion, 8.1%
3. **[MEXC]({lang}/crypto-exchanges/mxc/)** — 7.8% (almost doubled from 2024)
4. **[Gate]({lang}/crypto-exchanges/gate/)** — 7.5%
5. **[Crypto.com]({lang}/crypto-exchanges/crypto_com/)** — 7.2%
6. **[Bitget]({lang}/crypto-exchanges/bitget/)** — 6.4%
7. **[OKX]({lang}/crypto-exchanges/okex/)** — 6.3%
8. **[Coinbase]({lang}/crypto-exchanges/gdax/)** — 6.1%
9. **HTX** — 6.0%
10. **[Upbit]({lang}/crypto-exchanges/upbit/)** — 5.5% (South Korea's largest)

Together the ten handled about $18.7 trillion in 2025. Binance's lead has been stable for years; the order behind it shifts month to month — Bybit's share fell from 10% to 6% in the month after its own hack in February 2025.

## Why reported volume can mislead

Some exchanges report volumes as big as the leaders' while being far less known. Part of that can be wash trading — the same party buying from itself to look busy — or zero-fee promotions that attract bots. That is why CoinGecko gives each exchange a **trust score from 1 to 10** (liquidity, how real the volume looks, security, reserves, licences) and ranks exchanges by trust, not raw volume. In that ranking Coinbase, OKX, Kraken and Binance hold the top four places.

A second check is the ratio of volume to reserves. CoinGecko found that at Coinbase, Binance and Kraken daily trading was about 0.1 times the assets they hold — a sign that most customers store coins there rather than churn them — while the ratio was several times higher at some smaller exchanges.

## Which crypto exchange holds the most money?

Proof of reserves is the public list of wallets an exchange says belong to its customers; DefiLlama adds up what is in them. On 23 November 2026:

- **Binance** — about $110 billion
- **OKX** — about $27 billion
- **Bybit** — about $13 billion
- **Bitfinex** — about $11 billion
- **Bitget** and **Gate** — about $5 billion each

Reserves show assets, not debts, so they are a useful sign but not an audit. Each exchange page on WhatIfStack shows its reserves by blockchain and a one-year chart.

## The largest crypto derivatives exchanges

Futures and perpetual contracts — bets on the price with leverage — trade many times the spot volume. The usual measure is **open interest**, the value of contracts still open. On 23 November 2026 Binance Futures led with about 417,000 BTC (~$26 billion), followed by OKX (~107,000 BTC), CoinW, Hyperliquid (a decentralised exchange) and Bybit. The full list is on [Derivatives exchanges]({lang}/crypto-exchanges/derivatives/). Leverage can wipe out a deposit in hours; most regular buyers never need it.

## Is the biggest exchange the safest?

Size helps — deep markets, many coins, reserves to cover losses — but it does not remove the risk of keeping coins on someone else's platform:

- **Binance, October 2026:** a hack worth about $6.3 billion, the largest ever; withdrawals restarted on 1 November.
- **Bybit, February 2025:** about $1.5 billion stolen, then the largest hack on record.
- **HTX:** in May 2026 the UK put the company operating HTX on its sanctions list.
- **FTX, 2022:** one of the largest exchanges at the time collapsed; customers waited more than two years for repayments to start.

Before you pick one, check: is it licensed where you live, does it publish proof of reserves, what does a buy cost (trading fee *and* spread), and can you withdraw to your own wallet. ## What it means if you buy regularly

The exchange barely changes the price you pay for bitcoin — the big ones trade within cents of each other. What changes is the fee. Someone who bought $10 of bitcoin every day for five years put in $18,260; today that plan is worth {{dca:btc:5}} ({{dcaroi:btc:5}}). A 0.1% fee on those buys costs about $18 in total; a 1.5% "instant buy" fee costs about $274. For a small regular plan, low fees and a reliable withdrawal matter more than who has the biggest volume.
