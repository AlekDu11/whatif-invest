Bitcoin had its roughest week in months. After slipping under $70,000 on Friday, November 13, it touched about **$62,000** over the weekend — a three-month low — and was trading near **$64,500** on Sunday (live price now: **{{price:btc}}**). That is roughly **49% below the all-time high of $126,080** set on October 6, 2025. The Crypto Fear & Greed Index sits at **10 out of 100** — "extreme fear" — down from 63 a month ago.

Below: what pushed the price down, how this drop compares with earlier Bitcoin crashes, and — the part only our data can show — what people who simply kept buying every day actually have right now.

## Why Bitcoin fell this week

Several pressures hit at the same time:

- **Rate-hike fears.** October inflation came in hotter than expected (CPI +0.4% for the month). Futures markets now price a high chance that the Federal Reserve raises rates again at its December 9 meeting, after a first hike in September. Higher rates make risky assets like crypto less attractive, and the US dollar index climbed to its strongest level since April 2025.
- **ETF money leaving.** US spot Bitcoin ETFs saw about **$1.91 billion** of net outflows over seven straight trading days (November 5–13), according to Farside data cited by crypto.news. When ETF holders sell, funds have to sell real bitcoin.
- **Leverage being flushed out.** Traders who borrowed to bet on higher prices were forced out. CoinGlass data showed close to **$2 billion** of crypto positions liquidated in 24 hours, with around 80% of the Bitcoin liquidations being long (bullish) bets. Each forced sale pushes the price lower and can trigger the next one.
- **Extra worries.** Geopolitical tension in the Gulf lifted oil prices, and index provider MSCI plans to drop Strategy — the company that holds more than 850,000 BTC — from its global indexes on November 30.

Ethereum fell even harder: it dropped below $2,000 for the first time since August and now trades around {{price:eth}}. For live headlines, see our [news page]({lang}/news/).

## How deep is this crash compared with the past?

Our [all-time-high tracker]({lang}/ath/) shows how far every asset is from its peak. For Bitcoin, a 49% drop sounds brutal, but on monthly closing prices it is not unusual:

- **2014–2015:** from the September 2014 peak to January 2015 — **−44%**
- **2017–2019:** from December 2017 to January 2019 — **−76%**
- **2021–2022:** from October 2021 to December 2022 — **−73%**
- **2025–2026:** from July 2025 to June 2026 — **−49%** (and close to that level again now)

Two honest takeaways. First, Bitcoin has fallen 40–75% several times and later recovered — but past recoveries are not a promise; nothing guarantees the pattern repeats. Second, today's drop is still smaller than the two biggest bear markets, which means it *could* go deeper. Analysts quoted this week name levels from $60,000 down to $57,000 as possible floors; nobody knows. ## What steady buyers have right now

Here is the interesting part. Five years ago — November 2021 — Bitcoin was sitting right at its previous peak. It was one of the worst possible moments to buy.

- **$1,000 put in at once** five years ago is worth **{{lump:btc:5}}** today ({{lumproi:btc:5}}).
- **$10 every day** over the same five years means **{{dcainv:5}}** invested, now worth **{{dca:btc:5}}** ({{dcaroi:btc:5}}).

Same coin, same terrible starting point — but the daily buyer kept picking up cheap coins during the 2022 crash, which cushioned the result. Over shorter periods the picture is red: someone who started $10 a day one year ago is at {{dcaroi:btc:1}}, because almost all of their buying happened near the top. That is the real cost of DCA: in a falling market it hurts too, just less sharply than a badly timed lump sum.

## What "extreme fear" means for a regular investor

A Fear & Greed reading of 10 tells you how people *feel*, not where the price goes next. Historically, very low readings have shown up near some bottoms — and also in the middle of drops that kept going. A few calm rules that hold either way:

- **Only invest money you will not need for years.** If a further 30% drop would force you to sell, the amount is too big.
- **Keep the plan boring.** A fixed sum on a fixed day removes the "is this the bottom?" question. Our [habits calculator]({lang}/habits/) shows what a small daily amount adds up to.
- **Avoid leverage.** This week's crash was made worse by borrowed money. Spot buying can lose value; leveraged trades can go to zero in hours.
- **Diversify.** In the table above, the S&P 500 and gold behaved very differently from crypto over five years.
