**Crypto staking providers differ mostly in three things: how much of your rewards they keep, how fast you can get your coins back, and who holds your coins in the meantime.** The fee range is wider than most people expect — Lido keeps 10% of rewards, Kraken 25–30% for small accounts, and Coinbase charges a standard 35% according to Kraken's own comparison — so the same coin can pay you a third less depending on where you stake it.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Staking fees are a **share of your rewards**, not of your deposit: **10%** at Lido, **25%** (bonded) or **30%** (flexible) at Kraken for balances under $1M, about **35%** standard at Coinbase.
> - Getting coins back can take minutes or weeks: Kraken lists **28 days** for Polkadot and **21 days** for Cosmos; Ethereum exits depend on a network queue.
> - Staking pays you in coins, so it does **not** protect you from the price: ether is at {{price:eth}}, {{chg1y:eth}} over 12 months.

## What is crypto staking, in one paragraph?

Staking means locking coins of a proof-of-stake blockchain (Ethereum, Solana, Cardano, Polkadot, Cosmos…) so they help confirm transactions. The network pays new coins as a reward. You can do it yourself, through an exchange, or through a "liquid staking" protocol. For the full basics read [what is crypto staking and is it safe]({lang}/blog/what-is-staking/) — this guide compares **where** to stake.

## Crypto staking providers compared: fees, lock-ups, custody

| Provider | Type | Fee (share of rewards) | Getting coins back | Who holds the coins |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Lido** (stETH) | Liquid staking protocol | **10%** (split between node operators and the Lido DAO) | Swap stETH on an exchange any time, or join a withdrawal queue (daily batches) | Smart contracts; you hold stETH in your own wallet |
| **Kraken** | Exchange | **25%** bonded, **30%** flexible (balances under $1M; lower tiers above $1M) | Flexible: any time. Bonded: unbonding period, "3 or more days" depending on the coin | Kraken |
| **Coinbase** | Exchange | about **35%** standard, roughly 26–32% with Coinbase One (per Kraken's comparison, May 2026) | From minutes to several weeks for ETH | Coinbase |
| **Binance** | Exchange | Set per product — check the commission in the app before you start | ETH staking gives you WBETH, which can be redeemed or traded | Binance |
| **Solo staking** | Your own validator | **0%** to a provider (you pay for hardware and run it 24/7) | Network exit queue | You — but you need **32 ETH** |

Three things the table tells you:

- **The headline APY is before the fee.** Exchanges usually show the rate *after* their cut, protocols often show it before — compare like with like.
- **"Flexible" costs more.** Kraken charges 30% for flexible staking versus 25% for bonded — you pay for the right to leave at any time.
- **Liquid staking trades the lock-up for a new risk.** stETH can be sold immediately, but Lido itself says it is "intentionally not pegged" to ETH, so in a panic it can trade below ether.

## How much does the fee change what you earn?

A simple example: if a network paid **3% a year** before fees (an illustration, not a quote — real rates move daily), 1 ETH would earn you:

- with a 10% fee → **2.70%** a year,
- with a 25% fee → **2.25%**,
- with a 30% fee → **2.10%**,
- with a 35% fee → **1.95%**.

The difference between the cheapest and the most expensive option is about a third of your reward — every year, compounding.

## Which staking provider is safest?

There is no risk-free option, only different risks:

- **Exchange risk:** your coins sit with the company. If it freezes withdrawals or fails, staked coins are stuck with everything else. In the EU, check that the provider holds a MiCA licence (ESMA keeps a public register).
- **Smart-contract risk:** with Lido and similar protocols, a bug could cost money, and there is no customer service to refund you.
- **Slashing:** validators that misbehave or go offline can lose part of the stake. Big providers usually spread this risk, but it is not zero.
- **Lock-up risk:** if the price falls while your coins are unbonding (e.g. 28 days on Polkadot via Kraken), you cannot sell in time.
- **Country rules:** Kraken says on-chain staking is not offered in some locations, and staking rewards are taxable in many countries.

## Is staking on an exchange or with Lido better?

It depends on what you value:

1. **Simplicity and support** → an exchange you already use (higher fee, the company holds your coins).
2. **Lower fee and control over your wallet** → a liquid staking protocol like Lido (you need a self-custody wallet and accept smart-contract risk).
3. **No middleman at all** → solo staking (32 ETH and technical work).

Whatever you choose, start small, read the provider's own staking terms (fee, unbonding time, where you live), and never stake money you may need next month.
