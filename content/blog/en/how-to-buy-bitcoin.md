Buying Bitcoin today takes about as long as opening a bank app. The hard part is not the button — it is choosing a safe place, not overpaying in fees, and not panicking the first time the price drops 20%.

This guide walks you through the whole process in plain language. Bitcoin's price right now is {{price:btc}}, but you do not need to buy a whole coin: almost every exchange lets you buy a small piece, often from $10 or the equivalent in your currency.

## Step 1. Choose a regulated exchange in your country

An exchange is the shop where you swap your money for Bitcoin. This is the most important choice in the whole guide.

What to look for:

- **A licence where you live.** In the EU, look for a MiCA-authorised crypto-asset service provider. In the US, a company registered with FinCEN and licensed by state regulators. In the UK, a firm registered with the FCA. Other countries have their own registers — check the regulator's website, not the exchange's own claims.
- **Deposits in your local currency** by bank transfer or card, so you do not pay double conversion.
- **Clear, low fees.** Compare the trading fee and the spread (the gap between buy and sell price). "Zero fee" often means a wide spread.
- **A long track record** and proof-of-reserves reports.
- **Recurring buy** feature — you will need it in Step 5.

We keep a comparison of large exchanges, their fees and features on our [exchanges page]({lang}/exchanges/). Some links there are partner links; they never change the price you pay.

Avoid anyone who contacts you first on social media, messengers or dating apps offering to "help you invest". That is the most common crypto scam.

## Step 2. Sign up and verify your identity

Registration takes a few minutes:

1. Sign up with your email and a **unique, strong password** (use a password manager).
2. Turn on **two-factor authentication (2FA)** with an authenticator app. Avoid SMS codes where you can — SIM-swap attacks are real.
3. Complete **identity verification (KYC)**: a photo of your ID or passport and a selfie. Regulated exchanges are legally required to do this.

Verification can take from minutes to a couple of days. If a platform lets you trade large amounts with no verification at all, treat that as a red flag, not a feature.

## Step 3. Deposit money

Most exchanges offer several options:

- **Bank transfer** — usually the cheapest, sometimes a few hours or a day.
- **Debit or credit card** — instant, but fees of 1–4% are common.
- **Apple Pay / Google Pay** — convenient, often priced like cards.

Start small. Send an amount you would be fine seeing fall by half — because with Bitcoin, that has happened several times (see the [all-time-high page]({lang}/ath/) for how far it is from its peak right now).

## Step 4. Buy your first Bitcoin

On most exchanges you will see two ways to buy:

- **"Buy" / "Convert"** — the simple screen. Easy, but the spread is often wider.
- **"Spot" / "Trade"** — the order book. A *market order* buys instantly at the current price; a *limit order* buys only at the price you set. Fees here are usually lower.

For a first purchase, the simple screen is fine. Just check the final amount of BTC you receive before you confirm. Bitcoin's ticker is **BTC**, and amounts are shown with up to 8 decimal places — 0.001 BTC is a perfectly normal purchase.

You can track the price and history on our [Bitcoin page]({lang}/btc/).

## Step 5. Set up recurring buys (DCA)

Instead of guessing the "right moment", many people buy the same amount on a schedule — every week or every month. This is called [dollar-cost averaging (DCA)]({lang}/blog/what-is-dca/).

How it looks in practice:

1. Find **Recurring buy** (sometimes "Auto-invest" or "Savings plan") in the exchange app.
2. Choose BTC, the amount and the frequency.
3. Link your bank card or a balance to pay from.

## Step 6. Keep your Bitcoin safe

Security basics that prevent most losses:

- **Never share** your password, 2FA codes or recovery phrase with anyone — including "support staff". Real support never asks.
- **Turn on withdrawal address whitelisting** if your exchange offers it.
- **Bookmark** the exchange website and log in only from the bookmark; phishing sites copy logos perfectly.
- **Consider self-custody** once your amount grows. A hardware wallet keeps your coins offline. You get a 12- or 24-word recovery phrase — write it on paper, store it offline, and never type it into a website or a photo.

"Not your keys, not your coins" is a popular saying: if an exchange fails, coins kept there can be frozen. Self-custody removes that risk but adds a new one — if you lose the recovery phrase, nobody can restore access.

## Taxes and records

In many countries, selling or swapping Bitcoin is a taxable event. Download your transaction history from the exchange once a year and check the rules of your tax authority. Keeping records from day one saves a lot of pain later.

## Common beginner mistakes

- Buying after a huge rally because of fear of missing out.
- Selling everything in the first big crash.
- Using leverage or "futures" before understanding them.
- Keeping all savings in one asset. Many people treat Bitcoin as a small part of a wider portfolio — compare it with stocks and gold in our [calculator]({lang}/compare/).

## FAQ

### How much money do I need to buy Bitcoin?

Usually very little — many exchanges allow purchases from about $10 or the local equivalent. You buy a fraction of one coin.

### Is it safe to keep Bitcoin on an exchange?

It is convenient, and regulated exchanges have strong security, but you rely on the company. For larger amounts many people move coins to a hardware wallet they control.

### Can I buy Bitcoin without verification?

Regulated exchanges require identity checks by law. Platforms that skip them carry higher risk of frozen funds and scams.

### What is the best time to buy Bitcoin?

Nobody can predict short-term prices. That is why many beginners use regular, fixed buys (DCA) instead of trying to time the market.
