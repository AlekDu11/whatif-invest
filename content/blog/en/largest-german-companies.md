The largest German companies by market cap are **SAP, Siemens and Allianz**, followed by Deutsche Telekom, Siemens Energy, Munich Re, Merck KGaA, DHL Group, Infineon and Deutsche Börse. **$10 a day in each of these top 10 companies for five years turned ~$182,600 into ~$332,900 (+82.3%)** in US dollars — ahead of the same plan in the S&P 500 ({{dcaroi:spy:5}}).

> **Key takeaways**
> - The top 10 German companies by market value (Frankfurt listings, November 2026): SAP, Siemens, Allianz, Deutsche Telekom, Siemens Energy, Munich Re, Merck KGaA, DHL, Infineon, Deutsche Börse.
> - $10 a day in each for 5 years: **+82.3%**; across all 62 German stocks we track with 5 years of data: **+41.7%**, and **24 of 62** beat the S&P 500.
> - The famous brands did worst: Mercedes, BMW, VW, Adidas, Puma, Bayer and BASF together made **−6.9%** over 5 years of regular buying.

## The top 10 largest German companies by market cap

This is the ranking of the biggest German companies listed in Frankfurt (Xetra) by market value (share price × shares), as of November 2026. Live prices are on each company's page.

1. **[SAP]({lang}/sap-de/)** — business software (ERP) and cloud, Europe's most valuable tech company. {{price:sap-de}}
2. **[Siemens]({lang}/sie/)** — factory automation, rail, building tech and industrial software. {{price:sie}}
3. **[Allianz]({lang}/alv/)** — one of Europe's largest insurers and asset managers (owns PIMCO). {{price:alv}}
4. **[Deutsche Telekom]({lang}/dte-de/)** — telecoms; the largest shareholder of T-Mobile US.
5. **[Siemens Energy]({lang}/enr/)** — gas turbines, grid equipment and wind power.
6. **[Munich Re]({lang}/muv2/)** — one of the world's biggest reinsurers.
7. **[Merck KGaA]({lang}/mrk-de/)** — pharma, life science and electronics materials (not the US Merck).
8. **[DHL Group]({lang}/dhl/)** — express delivery and logistics (formerly Deutsche Post).
9. **[Infineon]({lang}/ifx/)** — chips for cars and power systems.
10. **[Deutsche Börse]({lang}/db1/)** — the exchange group behind the DAX and Xetra.

Next come Deutsche Bank, Bayer, E.ON, Siemens Healthineers, BASF, RWE, Commerzbank, Porsche, Mercedes-Benz and Hannover Re. Airbus trades in Frankfurt but is headquartered in the Netherlands, so it is not on the list.

- **Market cap** — SAP, Siemens, Allianz (the ranking above).
- **Revenue** — Volkswagen by a wide margin, then Mercedes-Benz, BMW, Deutsche Telekom and Allianz.

## How to buy German stocks

- **Xetra** in Frankfurt trades 9:00–17:30 Central European Time on weekdays; prices are in euros.
- The main index is the **DAX** with Germany's 40 largest companies (expanded from 30 in September 2021). A DAX ETF or fund buys all of them at once.
- Outside Europe, SAP and Deutsche Bank are also listed in New York, and the US-listed **iShares MSCI Germany ETF ([EWG]({lang}/ewg/))** holds the big names. Check what your broker offers and its currency fees.
- More on the market itself, sectors and the DAX: our guide to [German stocks]({lang}/blog/german-stocks/).

## What it means if you buy regularly

Regular buying in the biggest German companies worked over five years — but the spread was huge, from Siemens Energy's {{dcaroi:enr:5}} to Volkswagen's {{dcaroi:vow3:5}}. Picking the famous brands would have lost money, and anyone who started a year ago is roughly flat. A plan on a DAX fund or many stocks depends far less on picking the winning sector.
