**What is diversification?** It means spreading your money across many different investments, so that one coin or one company going wrong cannot sink everything you own. The old saying is: don't put all your eggs in one basket.

> **Key takeaways**
> - In April 2022 the LUNA coin traded at up to $116; by mid-May 2022 it was worth fractions of a cent. Anyone who held only LUNA lost almost everything in days.
> - When Enron went bankrupt in 2001, thousands of its employees lost most of their retirement savings because they held mostly Enron stock.
> - The simplest way to diversify is a fund that holds hundreds or thousands of companies at once (for example an S&P 500 or a world index fund).

## Why one basket is dangerous

A single company can go bankrupt. A single coin can lose 99% and never come back. It does not matter how popular it was the month before.

Two real examples:

- **LUNA (2022).** TIME reported that LUNA was worth as much as $116 in April 2022. After its partner stablecoin UST broke in May 2022, LUNA fell to fractions of a penny within days, and more than $400 billion of value left the whole crypto market.
- **Enron (2001).** Many Enron workers kept their retirement money in Enron shares. When the company collapsed, they lost both their job and their savings. The Economic Policy Institute calculated that a worker who had saved only in Enron stock since 1980 would have lost about 94 cents of every dollar (after inflation).

Diversification does not stop prices from falling. It stops one single disaster from taking everything.

## Step 1 — Count your baskets

Open a list (or [your portfolio on WhatIfStack]({lang}/me/)) and write down everything you own: cash, deposits, stocks, funds, coins, gold.

Ask yourself one question: **if the biggest item went to zero tomorrow, how much would I lose?** If the answer is "most of my money", you are not diversified.

## Step 2 — Spread across types of assets

Different types of assets (asset classes — big groups like stocks, bonds, cash, gold, crypto) often move differently. Compare their last 12 months on our site:

They rarely all go up or all go down by the same amount. That is the point.

## Step 3 — Spread inside each type

Inside stocks, one fund can hold many companies from many sectors (health, technology, food, energy). A world index fund such as [VT]({lang}/vt/) holds thousands of companies from dozens of countries.

Inside crypto, Bitcoin is the oldest and largest coin; most other coins are much riskier. Owning ten small coins is **not** real diversification — small coins often fall together.

## Step 4 — Keep the risky part small

Decide in advance how much of your money can go into very risky things (small coins, memecoins). Many beginners keep it to a small slice they could lose without changing their life. Never borrow for it.

## Step 5 — Check once or twice a year

Prices move, so your mix changes. If one part grew much bigger than you planned, you can bring it back by buying more of the other parts (this is called rebalancing). The US regulator's investor site suggests doing it every 6 or 12 months, not every day.

> **⚠️ Watch out**
> - **"Diversified" across ten coins.** Many coins drop at the same time. Owning lots of the same kind of risk is still one basket.
> - **A fund that is not really spread.** Some funds hold only one sector or a handful of companies. Check the fund's top holdings.
> - **Putting it all in your employer or your favourite company.** You already depend on your employer for your salary — don't let it hold your savings too.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Buying a little every month (DCA) spreads your purchases over **time**. Diversification spreads them over **assets**. Together they protect you from both a bad day to buy and a bad thing to buy.
