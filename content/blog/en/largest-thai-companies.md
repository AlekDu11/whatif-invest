The largest Thai companies by market cap are **Delta Electronics (Thailand), PTT and Advanced Info Service (AIS)**, followed by Gulf Development, Airports of Thailand, Krung Thai Bank, Kasikornbank, PTT Exploration and Production, SCB X and CP ALL. **$10 a day in each of the eight top-10 companies with five years of history turned ~$146,100 into ~$232,900 (+59%)** in US dollars — more than the same plan in the S&P 500 ({{dcaroi:spy:5}}), but almost all of it came from Delta, the banks and AIS.

## How to buy Thai stocks

- The **Stock Exchange of Thailand** trades on weekdays in two sessions, morning and afternoon (Bangkok time, 2 hours behind Japan); prices are in baht. The main large-cap index is the **SET50**.
- Foreign investors often buy **NVDR** shares (non-voting depositary receipts issued by the exchange's subsidiary), which pay the same dividends without voting rights.
- In Japan, SBI Securities offers Thai stocks; elsewhere, brokers with Asian market access (for example Interactive Brokers) do. A US-listed fund such as the iShares MSCI Thailand ETF (THD) holds the big names in one go. Check fees and currency costs first.
- See every Thai stock we track, sorted by market cap: [Thailand stocks]({lang}/stocks-thailand/).

## What it means if you buy regularly

Regular buying in the biggest Thai companies beat the S&P 500 over five years — but only because of a few winners: Delta, the banks and AIS. The famous tourism and consumer names barely broke even, and the whole market (+31% across 18 stocks) lagged the US. A SET50 fund spreads that risk; one stock like Delta can swing tens of percent in a year. Past results, not a forecast.
