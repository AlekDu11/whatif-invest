**What is the difference between trading and investing?** Investing means buying something good and holding it for years, so your money grows with the business or the market. Trading means buying and selling often — days, hours, even minutes — to profit from short price moves. Trading is much harder, and the data shows most people who try it lose money, so a beginner should start with investing.

> **Key takeaways**
> - An investor needs the market to grow over years. A trader needs to be right about the next move **and** faster than other traders — again and again.
> - Every trade costs money (fees, spread, taxes), so a trader starts each trade slightly behind.
> - Regulators and researchers find that most retail traders lose: in the EU, **74–89%** of retail CFD accounts lost money (ESMA, 2018).

## The difference in one table

| | Investing | Trading |
|---|---|---|
| How long you hold | Years | Minutes to weeks |
| What you need | The asset to grow over time | To predict the next move correctly |
| Time per week | Minutes | Hours, often every day |
| Costs | Few trades, low fees | Many trades, fees add up |
| Typical tools | Shares, ETFs, bitcoin bought and held | Charts, limit/stop orders, often leverage |

**Leverage** means trading with borrowed money: with 10× leverage, a 10% move against you can wipe out your whole stake. That is why trading losses can come so fast.

## What the numbers say

- **Day traders in Brazil (2013–2015).** Researchers at FGV followed every person who started day-trading index futures. Of those who kept going for more than 300 days, **97% lost money**, and only about 1% earned more than the minimum wage.
- **CFD traders in the EU (2018).** Before limiting leverage, the EU markets regulator ESMA found that **74–89% of retail accounts** typically lost money on CFDs (contracts that let you bet on a price with leverage). Brokers in the EU must still show this kind of warning today.
- **US households (1991–1996).** In a well-known study of 66,465 brokerage accounts, the households that traded the most earned **11.4% a year**, while the market earned **17.9%** — the extra trading cost them returns.

## Step-by-step: decide which one is for you

1. **Step 1 — Write down your goal.** "A home in 10 years" or "retirement" is an investing goal. If you want "quick money", stop: that is the feeling most scams and losing traders start with.
2. **Step 2 — Check your time.** Trading well needs hours of focused work. If you have a job and a family, regular investing fits real life much better.
3. **Step 3 — Start with investing.** Pick a simple, wide asset — for example a fund that follows the S&P 500 ([SPY]({lang}/spy/)) — and buy a fixed amount every month. See the lesson on [buying a little every month]({lang}/blog/dca-buying-a-little-every-month/).
4. **Step 4 — If you are curious about trading, watch first.** On WhatIfStack you can see how big traders really behave on the [Hyperliquid whale tracker]({lang}/trading/hyperliquid/): their positions, profits — and their liquidations.
5. **Step 5 — Learn before you risk money.** Go through this [Trading]({lang}/learn/) folder: orders, charts, risk per trade, leverage. If you ever trade, use only a small amount you can afford to lose completely, and never leverage at first.

## A worked example: how costs eat a trader

Say a trader has $1,000 and makes 5 trades a day, each with the whole $1,000, paying 0.1% in fees per buy and per sell (0.2% per round trip). That is $2 a trade, **$10 a day**. Over 20 trading days it is **$200 a month — 20% of the account** — before a single winning or losing price move. The investor who buys once and holds pays that fee once.

> **⚠️ Watch out**
> - **"Signal" groups and trading "mentors".** Telegram and Instagram channels selling signals or "guaranteed" strategies earn from your subscription or your losses, not from trading.
> - **Starting with leverage.** It is the fastest way to lose a whole account. Learn what [liquidation]({lang}/trading/) means before ever pressing "10×".
> - **Revenge trading.** After a loss, beginners trade bigger to "win it back". Set a daily loss limit and stop when you hit it.
