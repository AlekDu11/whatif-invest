Usually when stocks fall, something else holds up. Not this month. By Friday's close on 20 November, the S&P 500 fund SPY was **−8.7%** for November, gold **−9.8%**, silver **−16.3%** and Bitcoin **−22.3%**. On our month-end data since 2000, that makes November 2026 the S&P 500's **10th-worst month** and gold's **6th-worst** — and the month is not over yet.

> **Key takeaways**
> - November so far (to the 20 November close): SPY −8.7%, Nasdaq-100 (QQQ) −11.9%, gold −9.8%, Bitcoin −22.3%, Nvidia −38.7%. Long US Treasuries (TLT) were roughly flat, −0.1%.
> - Since Bitcoin data starts (2014), stocks, gold and Bitcoin fell more than 3% in the same month only four times before. This is the deepest of the five.
> - After the S&P 500's nine worst months since 2000, someone who started buying $300 a month right then was up in 8 of 9 cases a year later (+6% to +22%).

## Why the usual hiding places failed

**The AI trade cracked.** The sell-off started in chip stocks and spread. Nvidia is about 45% below its September monthly close on our data, and the whole Nasdaq-100 has given back 2026's gains (we covered Friday's 5.3% drop [here]({lang}/blog/nasdaq-crash-erases-2026-gains/)).

**Rates went the wrong way for gold.** Gold pays no interest, so it suffers when rates are expected to rise. The minutes of the Fed's late-October meeting, released on 18 November, showed that almost all officials saw further hikes as likely, and two raised the idea of a bigger step in December. The dollar sat near a 19-month high, and gold hit a 14-month low on 19 November, according to USAGOLD. A report that Washington was weighing new strikes on Iran pushed the dollar up further instead of helping gold. (Background: [what past Fed hikes did to Bitcoin, stocks and gold]({lang}/blog/fed-rate-hike-december-2026/).)

**Bitcoin traded like a tech stock.** When investors cut risk, they sold crypto together with growth shares. Bitcoin's record is {{ath:btc}} ({{athd:btc}}); it now trades at roughly half of that, and it slipped below the average cost of mining one coin this week ([our piece]({lang}/blog/bitcoin-below-mining-cost/)).

The only big asset on our list that did not lose money was long-dated US government bonds, and even they only went sideways.

## This has happened before, just not this hard

Since September 2014, when our Bitcoin history begins, there were four other months when the S&P 500, gold and Bitcoin all fell by more than 3%: September 2020, September 2021, June 2022 and September 2022. None was as deep for all three as November 2026.

What came next was mixed. A year after September 2020, an equal mix of the three was up sharply, mostly thanks to Bitcoin. A year after September 2021 it was down about a quarter, because 2022 turned into a bear market. After June and September 2022 the mix was up about 24–27% a year later. Four cases are too few to call a pattern; they mostly show that a bad month says little about the next year.

## What it means if you buy regularly

The S&P 500's worst months since 2000 have one thing in common: for a regular buyer, they were usually cheap entry points, not the end of the story. If you had started putting $300 a month (about $10 a day) into SPY at the end of each of its nine worst months, here is what the first 12 purchases were worth a year later (price only, no dividends):

The honest other side: 2001 shows that a bad month can be followed by more bad months, and in 2008 the buyer who started in September first sat through a further −30% before things turned. Starting in a crash feels terrible for months before it feels smart.

## FAQ

**Is November 2026 really one of the worst months ever for stocks?** For the S&P 500 since 2000, on month-end prices of SPY, it ranks 10th of 321 so far. The final rank depends on the last week.

**Why didn't gold protect investors this time?** Gold tends to fall when interest rates and the dollar rise, and both pointed up this month on expectations of a Fed hike.

**Should I stop buying after a month like this?** We don't give advice. History shows both outcomes: most regular buyers who started after the worst months were up a year later, but not all of them.
