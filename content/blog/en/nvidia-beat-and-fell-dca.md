## The numbers were huge again

The quarter ended on 25 October. Nvidia's revenue was $115.8 billion, up 103% from a year earlier and 20% from the previous quarter. Data-centre revenue, the AI chips that cloud companies and AI labs buy, was $107.6 billion, about 93% of all sales. Net income more than doubled to $65.3 billion, and adjusted earnings per share of $2.68 beat the roughly $2.47 analysts expected.

The outlook was also above forecasts: about $130 billion for the current quarter, against around $125 billion expected, with no data-centre sales to China assumed. Nvidia said its new Vera Rubin systems have started shipping, and it returned $28.1 billion to shareholders through buybacks and dividends in the quarter.

This was the first report since Jensen Huang resigned as CEO on 12 November. Interim CEO Colette Kress, previously the finance chief, said the company's momentum is real and the product roadmap is on track.

## "Sell the news" is not new for Nvidia

When a stock is owned by almost every fund, good results are often already in the price. What moves it is the gap between the numbers and what investors *hoped* for, and that bar is very high for Nvidia.

- **February 2026:** after what Morgan Stanley called a historic beat-and-raise, the shares fell more than 4% at the next open.
- **August 2026:** the stock first dipped after hours, then rose about 4% once management said it expected about 70% revenue growth in the next fiscal year.
- **November 2026:** up about 2% after hours, down about 2.5% the next day, even though the growth target for the next fiscal year was lifted to 75%.

A beat is the minimum, not a surprise. One quarter's reaction tells you little about where a stock goes over years.

## Why investors sold this time

There is no single official reason, but several worries came up in the coverage:

- **Margins.** Nvidia guided gross margin to 71–72% for next quarter, down from 74.8%. Still very high, but going the wrong way.
- **Who pays for the chips.** In August Nvidia announced platforms with Apollo, BlackRock, Blackstone, Brookfield, Goldman Sachs and KKR to mobilise over $500 billion of outside money to finance AI computing. On Wednesday the Wall Street Journal reported that no capital has actually been deployed yet, and Kress said Nvidia will be more disciplined about financing customers. When a chip seller helps its buyers borrow, critics see an echo of the dot-com era.
- **Leadership.** The company lost its founder-CEO a week before the report and is still searching for a permanent replacement.
- **Competition for attention.** Anthropic priced its IPO at $83 a share, above its $70–78 range, and starts trading on Nasdaq today. AMD, meanwhile, is up about 140% over the past year on our data while Nvidia is down about 15%.

## Two sides of the debate

**The bull case.** Sales doubled in a year at about 75% gross margin, the next quarter is guided higher again, and the new Rubin chips have started shipping. At around $151 the shares are much cheaper relative to profits than they were in October, and big customers, from cloud giants to Anthropic, keep buying more computing power.

**The bear case.** Growth this fast is hard to keep up, margins are guided lower, China is excluded, and part of the demand may depend on financing that has not appeared yet. A company without its founder at the top may also find it harder to keep the trust that a premium valuation needs.

## FAQ

**Why did Nvidia fall if results beat estimates?** Expectations were higher than the published forecasts. Investors also focused on lower margins, customer financing and the CEO change.

**Is Nvidia cheap now?** It is about 35% below its October peak, but "cheaper than before" is not the same as cheap. Price tells you nothing about the next year.
