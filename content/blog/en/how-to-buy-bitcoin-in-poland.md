In Poland you can buy Bitcoin in about 20 minutes with BLIK or a bank transfer, starting from roughly PLN 10–50. Since July 1, 2026, the legal way to do it is an exchange licensed under the EU's MiCA rules in another EU country and passported into Poland, because Poland itself still has no national crypto law. One Bitcoin costs {{price:btc}} right now, but you can buy a small fraction of a coin.

> **Key takeaways**
> - President Karol Nawrocki vetoed Poland's crypto-assets market act three times (December 2025, February 2026, June 2026), so Poland has no licensing law. Only exchanges authorised in another EU country can legally serve Poles.
> - Binance stopped new sign-ups and spot trading in Poland on July 1, 2026, and is closing Polish accounts on December 31, 2026.
> - Profit from selling crypto for złoty, goods or services is taxed at 19% and declared on PIT-38 by April 30. Swapping one crypto for another is not taxed.

## Why Poland's crypto rules look strange right now

The EU's MiCA regulation applies everywhere in the Union, but each country has to name a supervisor and pass its own act. Poland's parliament passed the act, and the president vetoed it on December 1, 2025, again on February 12, 2026, and a third time on June 11, 2026. Each attempt to override the veto failed; in September 2026 the Sejm was 25 votes short. The president and the government have since put forward new versions, and none had become law at the time of writing.

What this means for you: buying and holding Bitcoin is legal, and nothing penalises ordinary people for doing it. But the Polish register of virtual-currency firms no longer gives the right to operate after the MiCA transition ended on July 1, 2026. So pick an exchange with a MiCA licence from another EU regulator that lists Poland among its passported countries. If something goes wrong, you complain to that foreign regulator, not to the KNF.

## How people in Poland pay

1. **BLIK.** The most familiar option. Kraken accepts BLIK deposits in złoty and converts them to euros, and OKX supports BLIK through the payment firm ZEN.COM.
2. **Bank transfer.** A SEPA transfer in euros, or a złoty transfer where the exchange supports it, from your own account at PKO BP, mBank, ING, Pekao or Revolut.
3. **Bank card.** Visa and Mastercard work on most large exchanges, usually with a higher fee than BLIK or a transfer.

Send money only from an account in your own name. Exchanges reject or freeze payments from other people's accounts.

## Scams to avoid in Poland

The biggest risk is not the exchange you choose but fake "investment platforms". In July 2026 alone, the KNF's security team reported 17,617 dangerous domains and about 1,700 fake ads. Many used famous Poles or pretended to be the KNF or the broker XTB. Rules of thumb: no real exchange calls you to offer guaranteed profit, nobody legitimate asks you to install AnyDesk or a similar remote-access app, and you never pay a "consultant" by BLIK.

The collapse of Zondacrypto (formerly BitBay) in 2026 is a second lesson. It froze withdrawals in April 2026, lost its Estonian licence in June, and its operating company was declared bankrupt in August. Even a well-known local name can fail, so don't keep more on an exchange than you need to.

## Taxes in 2026

- **Rate:** 19% of income, meaning the money you received minus your documented costs (purchase price and fees).
- **What triggers tax:** selling crypto for złoty or another fiat currency, or paying for goods and services with it. Swapping BTC for another crypto is not taxed.
- **Losses:** there is no "loss" on crypto. Costs above your revenue carry forward to the next year. Crypto is settled separately from stocks.
- **Filing:** PIT-38, between February 15 and April 30. Report your purchase costs even in a year with no sales. Exchanges do not send a PIT-8C.
- **Solidarity levy:** a further 4% applies to total income above PLN 1 million.

From 2026, exchanges report their users' transactions to tax offices across the EU under DAC8, with the first reports due in 2027. So keep good records.

## Where to keep your Bitcoin

For small amounts bought every month, an EU-licensed exchange is convenient. As the amount grows, many people move coins to their own wallet, where only they hold the keys. Write the recovery phrase (12–24 words) on paper, never photograph it and never type it into a website.

## FAQ

### Is buying Bitcoin legal in Poland in 2026?

Yes. Owning and buying crypto is legal. Because Poland has no national MiCA act, use an exchange licensed in another EU country that serves Poland.

### Where can I buy Bitcoin with BLIK?

Kraken and OKX accept BLIK deposits, and so does Kanga. Check the current fee and limits in the exchange's deposit screen.

### What is happening with Binance in Poland?

Binance has had no new Polish users and no spot trading in Poland since July 1, 2026, and it is closing Polish accounts on December 31, 2026.

### How do I declare crypto on PIT-38?

Enter your revenue from sales and your costs in the crypto section of PIT-38. Unused costs carry forward to the next year.
