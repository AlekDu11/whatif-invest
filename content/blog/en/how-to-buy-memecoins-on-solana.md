**How do you buy a memecoin on Solana?** In short: you set up a self-custody wallet, put some SOL in it, find the token's contract address, check that it is not a trap, and swap SOL for the token on a decentralised exchange (DEX) or a trading tool such as [GMGN](/go/gmgn?from=blog&l=en). This guide walks through every step in plain language.

Before you start, one honest fact: most new memecoins lose almost all of their value, often within hours. A 2025 report by Solidus Labs classified 98.6% of the tokens launched on the Pump.fun launchpad as rug pulls or pump-and-dump schemes. Only use money you can afford to lose completely.

## What you need

- A smartphone or computer with an up-to-date browser.
- A Solana wallet you control yourself (self-custody), for example Phantom, Solflare or Backpack.
- Some SOL — the Solana network's own coin. You pay for the memecoin and for network fees in SOL.
- 30 minutes and a pen and paper for your seed phrase.

## Step 1. Set up a self-custody wallet

A self-custody wallet means **you** hold the keys, not an exchange. That gives you full control, and full responsibility.

1. Download the wallet only from its official website or your phone's official app store. Fake wallet apps are a common trap.
2. Create a new wallet. The app will show you a **seed phrase** of 12 or 24 words.
3. Write the words on paper, in order, and keep the paper somewhere safe. Do not take a screenshot, do not save it in notes or email, and do not paste it into any website.
4. Set a strong password or PIN for the app.

**Seed phrase rule:** anyone who has these words can take everything in the wallet. No real support team, exchange or "airdrop" will ever ask for it. If someone does, it is a scam.

A good habit: create a separate "trading" wallet for memecoins and keep your long-term savings somewhere else, ideally on a hardware wallet.

## Step 2. Fund the wallet with SOL

The simplest way is to buy SOL on a centralised exchange and withdraw it to your wallet.

1. Pick a licensed exchange from our [exchanges page]({lang}/exchanges/) and buy SOL with your card or bank transfer.
2. Copy your wallet's Solana address (it starts with letters and numbers, around 32–44 characters).
3. On the exchange, choose "Withdraw", select SOL and the **Solana** network, and paste the address.
4. Send a small test amount first. When it arrives, send the rest.

Leave a little SOL (for example 0.01–0.02 SOL) unspent at all times. You need it to pay fees, including the fee to sell later.

## Step 3. Find the token — and the right contract

Memecoins are often copied. Ten tokens can share the same name and logo; only one is the one you mean.

- Get the **contract address** (also called the mint address) from a trusted source: the project's verified account, a well-known data site, or our [memecoins page]({lang}/memes/) for established coins.
- Paste the contract address into GMGN or your DEX instead of searching by name.
- Compare the first and last characters of the address with the source. Scammers create addresses that look similar.

## Step 4. Check the token before you buy

This is the step that saves the most money. Tools like GMGN show most of these checks on the token page.

1. **Liquidity.** How much money sits in the trading pool? With very low liquidity, even a small sale moves the price a lot, and you may not be able to sell at a fair price.
2. **Liquidity lock or burn.** If the creator can withdraw the pool, they can "pull the rug" and leave you holding a token no one can buy.
3. **Mint authority.** If it is not revoked, the creator can print new tokens and dilute you.
4. **Freeze authority.** If it is not revoked, the creator can freeze your tokens so you cannot sell.
5. **Top holders.** If the top 10 wallets (excluding the pool) own a large share, they can crash the price by selling.
6. **Age and history.** A token created a few minutes ago with a vertical chart is a lottery ticket, not an investment.

Our [rug pull checklist]({lang}/blog/how-to-spot-a-rug-pull/) explains all ten warning signs in detail.

## Step 5. Set slippage and make the swap

**Slippage** is the maximum price change you accept between clicking "buy" and the trade being confirmed. Memecoin prices move fast, so the app lets you choose it.

- Low slippage (around 1%) protects you from a bad price but the trade may fail.
- High slippage (10%+) almost always fills, but you may pay far more than you expected, and bots can take advantage of you.
- Start low and only raise it if you understand the trade-off.

Then connect your wallet to [GMGN](/go/gmgn?from=blog&l=en) or a DEX, enter the amount of SOL, check the estimated number of tokens and the price impact, and confirm in your wallet. Your wallet shows what you are signing — read it. If a site asks you to approve something unexpected, cancel.

## Step 6. Understand the fees

On Solana, the base network fee is tiny: 5,000 lamports (0.000005 SOL) per signature. In busy moments you may also pay an optional **priority fee** so your trade is processed faster. On top of that:

- The trading platform or bot may charge its own fee (often around 1% per trade) — check before you trade.
- The pool takes a swap fee that goes to liquidity providers.
- **Price impact** and slippage are hidden costs, often bigger than the fees.

A round trip (buy and sell) can easily cost several percent on a small, illiquid token.

## Step 7. Selling

Selling works the same way in reverse: choose the token, enter the amount, check slippage and confirm. A few practical points:

- Make sure you still have SOL for the fee.
- If the sale fails repeatedly with "insufficient liquidity" or a strange error, the token may be a honeypot — a token designed so that you can buy but not sell.
- Decide in advance how much loss you can accept. Memecoins can fall 50–90% in minutes.
- In many countries, every swap can be a taxable event. Keep a record of your trades.

## The risks, clearly

- **Total loss is common.** CoinGecko counted more than half of all tokens tracked since 2021 as dead by the end of 2025, with about 11.6 million failing in 2025 alone.
- **Insiders move first.** Creators and bots often buy in the first seconds and sell into the first wave of buyers.
- **Scams and phishing.** Fake sites, fake support and malicious "approve" requests can empty your wallet.
- **No protection.** On-chain trades cannot be reversed, and there is usually no regulator or support line to help.
- **It is not DCA.** Regular investing works best on assets with long histories. You can compare how established memecoins did over time on our [memecoins page]({lang}/memes/).

## FAQ

### What is the cheapest way to buy a memecoin on Solana?

Network fees on Solana are very low, so the real cost is usually the platform fee, the pool fee and price impact. Trading small amounts in tokens with decent liquidity keeps hidden costs down.

### Can I buy Solana memecoins on a regular exchange?

Bigger, older memecoins such as Bonk or dogwifhat are listed on many centralised exchanges. Brand-new tokens usually exist only on DEXs.

### Is GMGN a wallet?

GMGN is a trading and analytics tool for on-chain tokens. Always check which wallet you are connecting or creating, and never share your seed phrase with any site.

### How much should I put into memecoins?

Only an amount you are fully prepared to lose. Many people treat it like a lottery ticket, kept separate from their long-term savings.
