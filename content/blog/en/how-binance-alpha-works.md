**What is Binance Alpha, and why does a token jump when it appears there?** Binance Alpha is a list of early-stage tokens — many of them memecoins — that Binance shows inside its app and wallet, describing it as a pre-listing pool. Being picked brings millions of Binance users' attention at once, so the price often jumps; but a spot on Alpha is not a full Binance listing, and many tokens fell back soon after.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Binance launched Alpha on **December 17, 2024** inside Binance Wallet; since **March 2025** (Alpha 2.0) Alpha tokens can also be bought from a normal Binance account.
> - Binance says Alpha tokens **may** later be considered for a Binance listing — with **no guarantee** — and that being featured is not an endorsement.
> - In the first week, **12 of 29** announced tokens were worth less than before their announcement (BeInCrypto, December 23, 2024); a May 2025 review found the month's 23 new Alpha tokens averaged **−5%** by May 14.

## What Alpha is, in plain words

Think of a shop window. Binance picks young tokens "based on market signals and community interest" (its own words) and puts them in a special **Alpha** section. You can buy them there, but they trade on a **DEX** (a decentralized exchange — a trading site that runs on the blockchain, see the [memecoin glossary]({lang}/memes/glossary/)), not on Binance's main order book. Some later get a real Binance spot listing; many never do.

Binance's FAQ says it does not accept token suggestions from users, and that a countdown appears on the Alpha page about 4 hours before new tokens are announced. Tokens are usually highlighted for about a day.

## Step 1 — Find Alpha in the app

Update the Binance app. Then either open **Wallet → Alpha** (the Web3 wallet — see [how to create a Binance Wallet]({lang}/blog/how-to-create-binance-wallet/)), or find the **Alpha** section from your normal exchange account (Alpha 2.0 uses your Spot and Funding accounts, so no separate wallet is needed).

## Step 2 — Read the token page before anything else

Look at four things: the **network** (BNB Chain, Solana, Base…), the **market value**, the **liquidity** (how much money sits in the trading pool — small liquidity means big price swings) and the **age** of the token. Then run the checks from [how to check a memecoin before buying]({lang}/blog/how-to-check-a-memecoin-before-buying/): holders, top-10 share, security audit. Our [memes page]({lang}/memes/) shows a traffic light for each token.

## Step 3 — Understand what "Quick Buy" does

Quick Buy is a swap: it pays with the chain's own coin (BNB, SOL or ETH) or your exchange balance, sets the **slippage** (how far the price may move before your order fails) for you and adds protection against front-running bots. Binance warns orders can fail when the price moves fast. Read the final amount before you confirm.

## Step 4 — Know where the money comes from

Why do prices move? Three reasons, all public:

- **Attention.** Millions of users see the same short list at the same moment, so buying rushes in at once.
- **Small float.** Many Alpha tokens are small (a May 2025 review counted market values from about $1.6 million to $271 million, about $70 million on average), so a little money moves the price a lot.
- **Points and airdrops.** Binance gives **Alpha Points** for holding a balance and for buying Alpha tokens; with enough points users claim free tokens (airdrops). People who farm points buy to collect points, and people who receive airdrops often sell quickly — the same review found that all 7 airdropped tokens of May 2025 were down by May 14, by 31.5% on average. More in our article on [Alpha Points]({lang}/blog/binance-alpha-points/).

## Step 5 — Decide your exit before you enter

The pattern after many listings, on Alpha and on main exchanges, was a spike and then a slide: Cointelegraph counted Binance listings from January–February 2025 that later fell 50–88% from their listing-day high. Decide the amount you can lose completely and stick to it.

> **⚠️ Watch out**
> - **"Alpha = guaranteed Binance listing."** Binance says the opposite: a listing is not guaranteed.
> - **Buying the first green candle.** The jump in the first minutes is often when early holders and airdrop receivers sell.
> - **Fake "Alpha" tokens and links.** Copy the contract address only from the Binance app itself, never from a chat or a DM.

## What beginners can learn from it

A big name showing a token is a reason for attention, not proof of quality. The move after an Alpha announcement comes mostly from timing and attention, and it can go both ways quickly. If you buy anything there, use only a small amount you can afford to lose, check the token first, and remember that chasing points has its own costs in fees and slippage.
