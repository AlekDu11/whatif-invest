Last week split the markets in two. US stocks had one of their best weeks since August, while Bitcoin, Ethereum and gold all fell hard. The common thread was interest rates: hotter inflation made a Federal Reserve rate **hike** in December look very likely, and that hurt assets that pay no income far more than company shares.

Here is what moved between November 9 and 15, and — the part only our data shows — what a simple **$10 a day** habit in Bitcoin, Ethereum, the S&P 500 and gold has returned. Data as of {{date}}.

## The week in numbers

- **S&P 500: about +2.5%** for the week, closing Friday at 7,561. The Dow added about 2% and the tech-heavy Nasdaq-100 about 3.5%. Monday did most of the work: oil prices fell sharply after Washington played down new strikes on Iran.
- **Bitcoin: about −14%** over seven days (CoinGecko). It slipped under $70,000 on Friday and touched roughly $62,000 over the weekend. Live price: **{{price:btc}}**.
- **Ethereum: about −16%**, briefly as low as $1,715 on Saturday, its lowest since July. Live price: **{{price:eth}}**.
- **Gold: about −5%** over five sessions, falling below $4,000 on Wednesday and closing the week near $3,890 an ounce. Silver slid under $50.
- **Nvidia: roughly −11%** for the week after CEO Jensen Huang resigned on November 12; CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO.
- **Crypto Fear & Greed Index: 10 out of 100** — "extreme fear".

## What drove it: inflation and a likely rate hike

October consumer prices rose **0.4%** in a month, with core prices up 0.3% — more than markets wanted. Futures then priced roughly an **85% chance** of a quarter-point Fed hike at the December 8–9 meeting. Producer prices on Friday came in slightly softer (+0.3%), but did not change that picture. The 10-year US Treasury yield ended the week around **5.15%**, and the dollar hit a 19-month high.

Why does that matter for a regular investor?

- **Gold** pays no interest. When a safe government bond yields over 5%, holding gold costs more in missed income, so leveraged traders cut positions.
- **Crypto** reacted the same way, plus its own problems: steady outflows from US Bitcoin ETFs and forced selling of leveraged bets. We covered the details in our [news section]({lang}/news/).
- **Stocks** still rose because cheaper oil eased inflation fears early in the week and company profits are growing. Even so, Friday was red, and Nvidia's turmoil weighed on chip stocks.

## How a DCA investor can read a week like this

- **Weekly swings are noise for a multi-year plan.** A 14% drop in seven days feels huge, but your result depends on years of purchases, not one week.
- **Keep the amount sustainable.** If another bad month would force you to sell, the amount is too large.
- **Skip leverage.** Forced liquidations made the crypto drop sharper.
- **Mind inflation too.** With prices still rising, cash in a zero-interest account loses value — see our [inflation calculator]({lang}/inflation/).
