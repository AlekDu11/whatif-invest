**How do you read a price chart?** First choose the time range, then look at where the price started and where it is now, then check how big the swings were and how much was traded. That is enough to understand 90% of what a chart is telling you — no formulas needed.

> **Key takeaways**
> - A chart only shows the **past**. It tells you how much an asset moved and how bumpy the ride was, not what happens tomorrow.
> - The same asset can look like a crash on a 1-week chart and a steady climb on a 5-year chart. Always check the time range first.
> - Every asset page on WhatIfStack has a live chart. Bitcoin is now at {{price:btc}}, {{chg1y:btc}} over 12 months; its record high was {{ath:btc}} ({{athd:btc}}).

## Step 1 — Choose the time range

Under the chart you see buttons like **1D, 5D, 1M, 3M, 6M, YTD, 1Y, 5Y, All**. They mean 1 day, 5 days, 1 month and so on (YTD = "year to date", since 1 January).

- If you buy a little every month for years, look at **1Y** and **5Y** first.
- Short ranges (1D, 5D) mostly show noise — small ups and downs that do not matter for a long-term buyer.

Tip: tap **1M**, then **5Y**. See how different the same asset looks? That is the most important lesson about charts.

## Step 2 — Compare the start and the end

Look at the left edge (the price at the start of the range) and the right edge (the price now). Is the right side higher or lower? That is the change over the period. On our asset pages you do not have to guess: the cards under the chart show the change in per cent, and the "What would I have" calculator shows the result in money.

## Step 3 — Read the candles

By default our chart shows **candles** (small bars). Each candle is one period — on the daily setting, one day.

- **Green** candle: the price closed **higher** than it opened that day.
- **Red** candle: the price closed **lower** than it opened.
- The thick part (the "body") goes from the opening price to the closing price.
- The thin lines above and below (the "wicks") show the highest and lowest price of that day.

Long wicks mean the price jumped around a lot during the day. You do not need more than this — patterns with fancy names are for traders, and we cover them in the trading folder later.

## Step 4 — Check the volume

The small bars at the bottom of the chart are **volume** — how much of the asset was traded in each period. A big move with high volume means many people took part. A big move with tiny volume is less reliable: a few trades can push the price of a small coin up or down.

## Step 5 — For long ranges, switch to log scale

In the bottom right corner of the chart there is a **log** button. On a normal ("linear") scale, a move from $1 to $2 looks tiny next to a move from $100 to $200, even though both are +100%. On a **log scale** the same percentage move looks the same size. For 5-year or "All" charts of fast-growing assets like Bitcoin, log scale shows the real picture of the past much better.

Finally, find the **record high** — the highest point on the "All" chart. Our pages show it in the cards too. For Bitcoin it is now {{fromath:btc}} away from it. Buying right at a record is not wrong, but it reminds you how far a price can fall afterwards.

> **⚠️ Watch out**
> - **Reading the future into a line.** "It went up for 3 months, so it will keep going up" is the most common beginner mistake. A chart is history, not a forecast.
> - **Judging by a 1-day chart.** A red day says almost nothing about an asset you plan to hold for years. Zoom out first.
> - **Trusting screenshots from strangers.** Charts posted in chats can be cut to any range to make any point. Open the chart yourself on a trusted site.

## What it means if you buy regularly

If you buy a fixed amount every month, the bumps on the chart work in your favour: on the red stretches the same money buys more units. For example, $10 a day in Bitcoin for 5 years ({{dcainv:5}} in total) turned into {{dca:btc:5}} ({{dcaroi:btc:5}}); in the S&P 500 it was {{dca:spy:5}} and in gold {{dca:gold:5}}. Past results, not a forecast. Compare them side by side in the [compare tool]({lang}/compare/) and keep your own buys in [your portfolio]({lang}/me/).
