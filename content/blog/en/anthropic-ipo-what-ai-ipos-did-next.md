Anthropic, the company behind the Claude AI models, is due to price its stock market debut on Wednesday, 18 November, and start trading on Nasdaq under the ticker **ANT** on Thursday. At the top of its $70–78 range it would be valued at about **$1.4 trillion**, more than almost any company has ever been worth on its first day. The question for ordinary investors is simple: what usually happens to a hyped new listing after day one? This year's answer has not been kind: the ten largest US IPOs of 2026 trade on average **about 20.5% below their offer prices**.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Anthropic is selling 825 million shares at $70–78, up to $64.4 billion, plus $26 billion in a private sale to seven chip and cloud companies including Nvidia and Google ($10 billion each).
> - Six of the seven largest US IPOs of 2026 were below their offer price as of 13 November; Cerebras was down about 41%.
> - On our data, CoreWeave, the hottest AI listing of 2025, is now about 69% below its best monthly close.

## What Anthropic is selling and at what price

The offering is huge even by this year's standards. Anthropic plans to sell 825 million Class A shares, raising between $57.8 billion and $64.4 billion, and the banks led by Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley can buy up to 123.75 million more. Separately, AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC agreed to buy $26 billion of non-voting shares at the IPO price.

Demand looks strong. Bloomberg reported that orders reached about six times the shares available, and the company has reportedly guided its price to the top of the range or above. Together with the private sale, the deal could pass SpaceX's June IPO, the largest on record at $85.7 billion. Up to 20% of the shares may go to retail investors.

The business behind it is growing at a pace few companies have ever shown. Anthropic reported $33.2 billion of revenue in the first nine months of 2026, up from about $2.5 billion a year earlier, and an annualised run rate of about $81 billion at the end of October. It also reported a nine-month net loss of $104 billion, mostly from a $94.3 billion non-cash charge, and about $900 billion of commitments to computing and infrastructure.

## Why this matters for AI stocks you may already own

Anthropic is not just a new stock: it is a customer and partner of the companies in most AI portfolios. Nvidia, which reported a strong quarter on Tuesday with revenue of $115.8 billion, up 103% from a year earlier, is one of two $10 billion anchor buyers. Google is the other. Amazon, Microsoft, Broadcom and TSMC sell it cloud capacity or chips.

That link works both ways. A successful debut would support the idea that AI spending turns into real revenue, which helps the chip and cloud stocks. A weak debut would add to the worries behind this month's sell-off, when Nvidia closed at its lowest level since July 2025 the day before its results.

## What big AI listings did after day one

You cannot test DCA on Anthropic yet, because there is no price history. But we can look at earlier AI-related listings in our data:

- **Palantir** (listed September 2020): first month-end close $9.50, best $200.47, now {{price:pltr}}.
- **Arm** (listed September 2023): first month-end close $53.52, best $354.57, now {{price:arm}}.
- **CoreWeave** (listed March 2025): first month-end close $37.08, best $163.06, now {{price:crwv}}.

Three very different paths. Palantir drifted for two years before it took off. Arm rose more than sixfold and then gave back about half. CoreWeave more than quadrupled within months, then fell about 69% from the top and is now only about a third above its first month-end close.

And 2026 has been tougher still. Through 12 November, the ten largest US IPOs of the year had lost about 20.5% on average from their offer prices. Even SpaceX was only about 3% above its offer price in mid-November. New stocks are priced on hope and early demand; the steady flow of sellers, such as insiders after lock-up periods end, comes later.

## The two sides of the debate

**The bull case.** Revenue grew more than tenfold in a year, gross margin was about 85% in the first half, and the company holds nearly $132 billion in cash and short-term investments. Big customers and big partners are buying in at the same price as everyone else.

**The bear case.** At about 17 times its annualised revenue, the price assumes growth continues for years. The company is still losing money, has about $900 billion of computing bills ahead, and new Class A buyers get limited voting power: founders hold a special share class with 50.1% of the votes. And this year's IPO record suggests early prices often run ahead of what later buyers are willing to pay.

## FAQ

**When does Anthropic start trading?** Pricing is expected on Wednesday, 18 November, and trading on Nasdaq on Thursday, 19 November, under ANT.

**Can I buy at the IPO price?** Only if your broker gets an allocation; up to 20% is reserved for retail, and demand is far higher than supply. Most people will buy, if at all, at the market price once trading starts.
