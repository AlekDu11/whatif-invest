A **rug pull** is when the people behind a token pull the money out of its trading pool or dump their own coins, leaving buyers with a token nobody can buy back. It is the most common way people lose money on memecoins: Solidus Labs found that **93%** of 361,000 liquidity pools on Solana's Raydium showed «soft rug pull» traits.

You cannot avoid every scam, but most of them leave the same marks. Here is how the main schemes work and seven checks that take about two minutes.

> **Key takeaways**
> - **98.6%** of Pump.fun tokens launched between January 2024 and March 2025 were classified as rug pulls or pump-and-dumps (Solidus Labs).
> - The median Raydium rug pull took about **$2,832** — scammers repeat small thefts thousands of times.
> - The fastest checks: liquidity, pair age, top holders, whether you can sell, and the exact contract address.

## Three schemes cause most of the losses

**Hard rug pull.** The creator adds liquidity, waits for buyers, then removes all of it in one transaction. The price chart drops 95–100% in a single candle and selling becomes impossible.

**Soft rug pull (slow dump).** Insiders hold a large share of the supply and sell it bit by bit into every bounce. The chart bleeds for days while social posts promise a «second wave».

**Pump-and-dump.** A group buys early, promotes the token in Telegram chats, on X or through paid influencers, and sells to the crowd that arrives. Chainalysis estimated that pump-and-dump tokens made up about 4.5% of tokens launched in 2024.

A related trap is the **honeypot**: a contract that lets you buy but blocks or heavily taxes selling. The chart only goes up — because nobody can sell.

## Seven checks before you buy

1. **Liquidity above $50K.** Smaller pools can be drained or crashed by one seller. Our [DEX memecoins page](/en/memes/) flags «Low liquidity».
2. **Pair older than 3 days.** Most rugs happen in the first hours or days. New pairs get a «New» flag.
3. **Top 10 holders under ~30%.** One wallet with 20%+ of the supply can end the party alone. GMGN shows holder concentration on every token page.
4. **Liquidity locked or burned.** Many trackers show whether the pool's LP tokens are locked. Unlocked liquidity means the creator can take it any time.
5. **Somebody has sold.** Look at the trades: if there are many buys and no successful sells, it may be a honeypot.
6. **The exact contract address.** Copy it from the project's official X or website. Fakes use the same name, ticker and logo.
7. **Volume that makes sense.** Daily volume dozens of times larger than liquidity often means wash trading by bots. We flag it as «Unusual volume».

None of these checks makes a token safe. They only filter out the most obvious traps.

## Promises are the loudest red flag

«Guaranteed 100x», «dev is doxxed, no rug», «last chance before Binance listing» — real projects cannot promise any of that. Be especially careful with:

- Private messages offering early access or «presale» spots.
- Links in replies under popular posts — fake swap sites drain wallets.
- Requests to «verify your wallet» or sign a transaction you do not understand.

Never share your seed phrase. No exchange, wallet or support agent will ever ask for it.
