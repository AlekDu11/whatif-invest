The largest UK companies by market cap are **HSBC, AstraZeneca and Shell**, followed by Unilever, Rolls-Royce, British American Tobacco, Rio Tinto, BP, GSK and National Grid. **$10 a day in each of these top 10 companies for five years turned ~$182,600 into ~$321,100 (+75.9%)** in US dollars — well ahead of the same plan in the S&P 500 ({{dcaroi:spy:5}}).

## Which are the biggest and richest companies in the UK?

"Biggest" can mean three different things, and each gives a different list:

- **By market value** — HSBC, AstraZeneca and Shell (the ranking above).
- **By revenue** — the energy and commodity traders: Shell, Glencore and BP sell the most, followed by Tesco.
- **By profit** — Shell and HSBC earn the most of any UK company, by a wide margin.
- **By employees** — Compass Group, the catering company, employs over half a million people, more than any other UK firm; Tesco is second.

## How to buy shares in big UK companies

- **The London Stock Exchange** trades 08:00–16:30 UK time on weekdays. Prices are quoted in **pence** (GBX): 1,000p = £10.
- **The FTSE 100** holds the 100 largest companies on the exchange; a FTSE 100 index fund or ETF buys all of them in one go.
- **Stamp duty:** buying most UK shares costs **0.5%** Stamp Duty Reserve Tax, added by the broker. ETFs bought on the exchange and AIM shares are usually exempt.
- **From abroad:** HSBC, AstraZeneca, Shell, BP, GSK, Unilever, BAT and Rio Tinto also trade in New York in dollars.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Regular buying in the largest UK companies worked over five years: 23 of the 24 ended above the money put in. But the spread is huge — from Rolls-Royce's {{dcaroi:rr:5}} to Diageo's {{dcaroi:dge:5}} — and a buyer who started a year ago is slightly behind. A plan on many stocks or a FTSE 100 fund depends far less on finding the next Rolls-Royce. Past results, not a forecast.
