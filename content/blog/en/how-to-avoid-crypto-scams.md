Crypto scams are now one of the most expensive types of fraud in the world. In its 2025 report, the FBI's Internet Crime Complaint Center counted about **$11 billion** in crypto-related losses from US victims alone. Blockchain analysts at Chainalysis estimated that scams worldwide took in around **$17 billion** in 2025, with impersonation scams growing fastest.

The good news: almost every scam shows the same few warning signs. Learn them once and you are much harder to fool.

## 12 red flags of a crypto scam

### 1. "Guaranteed" or very high returns

"Earn 2% a day", "double your money in a week", "zero risk". Real investments go up and down. Anyone who promises fixed high profits is either lying or running a scheme that pays old investors with new investors' money.

### 2. Giveaways: "send 1, get 2 back"

Fake videos and posts — often with a famous person's face, sometimes made with AI — promise to double any crypto you send. Nobody gives away free money. Whatever you send is gone.

### 3. Someone you met online steers you into crypto

A new friend or romantic match on a dating app, social network or a "wrong number" message slowly builds trust, then shows you an investing app where they "make a lot". This is often called *pig butchering*. The app shows growing profits, but when you try to withdraw, you are asked to pay "taxes" or "fees" first — and the money never comes out.

### 4. Pressure to act right now

"Only 2 hours left", "last spots", "your account will be closed". Scammers rush you so you do not stop to think or ask anyone.

### 5. "Support" contacts you first

Real exchanges do not call, text or DM you out of the blue to "secure your account". In May 2025 Coinbase disclosed that criminals had bribed overseas support contractors to steal customer data, then used it to phone customers while pretending to be Coinbase and talk them into moving funds. If someone calls you, hang up and contact the company only through its official app or website.

### 6. Anyone asks for your seed phrase

Your seed phrase (recovery phrase) is the master key to your wallet. No exchange, wallet, support agent or government official will ever need it. Anyone who asks is a thief. Learn more in our [guide to crypto wallets]({lang}/blog/crypto-wallets-hot-vs-cold/).

### 7. Lookalike websites and links (phishing)

A link in an ad, email or chat leads to a site that looks exactly like your exchange or wallet, but the address is slightly different. You enter your password or connect your wallet, and it is drained. Type addresses yourself or use bookmarks.

### 8. "Connect your wallet" to claim an airdrop

A surprise token in your wallet or a message about a free airdrop asks you to connect and "approve". The approval actually gives a smart contract permission to take your coins.

### 9. Pump-and-dump groups

Telegram or Discord channels promise "the next 100x coin" and tell everyone to buy at a set time. The organisers already own the coin and sell into the rush, and the price collapses minutes later.

### 10. Celebrity- or politician-backed tokens with no product

In February 2025, a memecoin called LIBRA was promoted on social media by Argentina's president, Javier Milei. Within hours its price soared and then fell by more than 90%, while insiders reportedly cashed out about $107 million. A famous name is not a guarantee — sometimes it is the bait.

### 11. Rug pulls: anonymous team, locked-up liquidity "promises"

A new token or DeFi project raises money and then the developers disappear with it — "pulling the rug". Warning signs: anonymous founders, no audited code, most tokens held by a few wallets, and you can buy but cannot sell.

### 12. Recovery scams after you were scammed

Victims are often targeted a second time by "fund recovery experts", fake lawyers or even fake police who promise to get the money back for an upfront fee. Real authorities do not charge you to investigate.

## Bonus warning signs

- You are asked to pay with crypto, gift cards or at a crypto ATM.
- The platform is not registered with any financial regulator.
- Withdrawals are "temporarily" unavailable.
- Every question is answered with excitement, never with numbers.

## How to protect yourself

1. **Use well-known, regulated platforms.** Compare options on our [exchanges page]({lang}/exchanges/).
2. **Never share your seed phrase, passwords or 2FA codes** — with anyone.
3. **Turn on two-factor authentication** with an authenticator app.
4. **Slow down.** Any offer that cannot wait 24 hours is not worth it.
5. **Check prices and history yourself** — for example on our [Bitcoin page]({lang}/btc/) — instead of trusting screenshots.
6. **Talk to someone you trust** before sending money to a new platform.
7. **Invest only what you can afford to lose**, and prefer plain, boring strategies such as regular buying of large assets. Read [what is DCA]({lang}/blog/what-is-dca/).

## What to do if you have been scammed

- **Stop sending money** immediately, even if you are promised a refund.
- **Contact your exchange or bank** through the official app; they may be able to freeze funds.
- **Save evidence**: wallet addresses, transaction IDs, screenshots, chats.
- **Report it** to the police and your national fraud centre (in the US, ic3.gov and reportfraud.ftc.gov).
- **Beware of recovery scams** that will contact you next.

## FAQ

### Can scammed crypto be recovered?

Sometimes, if it reaches an exchange that can freeze it quickly, or if police seize it. In most cases it cannot, so prevention matters most.

### Is every new token a scam?

No, but most new tokens are very high risk, and many lose almost all their value. Be extra careful with anything that has no real product.

### How do I know if an exchange is legitimate?

Check that it is licensed in your country, has a long track record, and that you reach it only through its official website or app.

### Are AI deepfake videos really used in scams?

Yes. Fake videos and voice clones of celebrities and company bosses are a growing part of giveaway and investment scams.
