The largest companies in the world by market cap in 2026 are **Apple, Nvidia and Microsoft**, followed by Alphabet (Google), Amazon, Saudi Aramco, TSMC, Broadcom, Meta and Tesla. Together these ten are worth about **$26.5 trillion**, and **$10 a day in each of them for five years turned ~$182,600 into ~$340,000 (+86.2%)** — more than twice the same plan in the S&P 500 ({{dcaroi:spy:5}}).

> **Key takeaways**
> - The world's 10 biggest companies by market value (November 2026): Apple (~$5.1T), Nvidia (~$4.1T), Microsoft (~$3.8T), Alphabet, Amazon, Saudi Aramco, TSMC, Broadcom, Meta, Tesla.
> - Eight of the ten are American tech companies; the only non-US names are Saudi Aramco and TSMC (Taiwan).
> - $10 a day in each for 5 years: **+86.2%**; over 3 years **+22.3%**; over the last year **−7.0%**, after the autumn sell-off in AI chips.
> - "Biggest" depends on the yardstick: by revenue Amazon and Walmart lead, by profit Alphabet and Nvidia.

## The 10 largest companies in the world by market cap (2026)

Market cap is the share price times the number of shares — what the whole company is worth on the stock market. This is the ranking as of November 2026; the [full live ranking of the largest companies]({lang}/largest-companies/) is updated every day.

1. **[Apple]({lang}/aapl/)** — iPhone, Mac and services; about $5.1 trillion. {{price:aapl}}
2. **[Nvidia]({lang}/nvda/)** — chips that train and run AI; about $4.1 trillion. {{price:nvda}}
3. **[Microsoft]({lang}/msft/)** — Windows, Office, Azure cloud; about $3.8 trillion. {{price:msft}}
4. **[Alphabet (Google)]({lang}/goog/)** — Search, YouTube, Android, Google Cloud.
5. **[Amazon]({lang}/amzn/)** — online shop and AWS, the largest cloud business.
6. **[Saudi Aramco]({lang}/saudi-aramco/)** — Saudi Arabia's state oil company, the biggest outside the US.
7. **[TSMC]({lang}/tsm/)** — makes the chips designed by Apple, Nvidia and AMD.
8. **[Broadcom]({lang}/avgo/)** — network and custom AI chips, VMware software.
9. **[Meta]({lang}/meta/)** — Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp.
10. **[Tesla]({lang}/tsla/)** — electric cars, batteries and robotaxis.

Next come Berkshire Hathaway, Eli Lilly, Walmart, Samsung Electronics, JPMorgan Chase, Micron, Visa, AMD, ExxonMobil and Johnson & Johnson. The order changes often at the top: in a single month Nvidia and Apple can swap places.

## Which is the biggest company in the world by revenue and by profit?

"Biggest" means different things, and each yardstick gives a different winner (latest 12 months of listed companies we track):

- **By market value** — Apple, Nvidia, Microsoft (the ranking above).
- **By revenue** — [Amazon]({lang}/amzn/) (~$800 billion a year) and [Walmart]({lang}/wmt/) (~$745 billion), then Saudi Aramco, Apple, Alphabet and PetroChina. Fortune's Global 500 also includes unlisted state giants such as China's State Grid. See the [ranking by revenue]({lang}/largest-companies/revenue/).
- **By profit** — Alphabet (~$236 billion) and Nvidia (~$226 billion) earn the most, ahead of Samsung, Microsoft and SK hynix. See the [most profitable companies]({lang}/largest-companies/earnings/).
- **By employees** — Walmart (~2.1 million people) and Amazon (~1.6 million).

Over five years the top 10 basket (+86.2%) beat the S&P 500, gold and bitcoin. Over the last year it made **−7.0%**, a bit worse than the S&P 500 ({{dcaroi:spy:1}}), because Nvidia, Alphabet and TSMC fell from their records. ## How to buy shares of the biggest companies

- Nine of the ten trade in New York in dollars; TSMC also trades there as an ADR. Saudi Aramco trades only on the Saudi exchange (Tadawul), which most retail brokers do not offer.
- Many brokers let you buy **fractions of a share**, so you do not need the price of a whole Apple share to start.
- One **S&P 500 or Nasdaq-100 fund** already holds most of the top 10 — see [how to invest in the S&P 500]({lang}/blog/how-to-invest-in-sp500/).

## What it means if you buy regularly

Regular buying in the world's biggest companies worked over five years, but most of the gain came from a few chip makers, and a buyer who started a year ago is behind. The biggest companies of today are not always the biggest in ten years — ten years ago ExxonMobil and General Electric were in the top 10, and today neither is. Spreading money over many companies or an index fund depends less on picking the next Nvidia. Past results, not a forecast.
