**What is the story of PEPE?** PEPE is a memecoin on Ethereum named after Pepe the Frog, an internet cartoon. It launched in April 2023 by an anonymous team that openly said the coin had no value — and still passed a $1 billion market value within weeks, crashed, survived an insider sell-off and in November 2024 briefly passed $10 billion.

> **Key takeaways**
> - PEPE had **no product, no roadmap and an anonymous team**. Binance even wrote in May 2023, when it listed the coin, that it "has no utility". Its price moved only on attention.
> - It rose fast and fell fast: from about $1.6 billion in early May 2023 to about $800 million a few days later, and in August 2023 former team members sold about **16 trillion** coins (around $15 million).
> - Today PEPE trades at {{price:pepe}}; its record was {{ath:pepe}} ({{athd:pepe}}), and it is {{fromath:pepe}} from that record. A few early buyers made fortunes; many late buyers lost money.

This story continues [the 2023–2024 memecoin wave]({lang}/blog/pepe-bonk-wif-memecoin-wave/). Words like "market value" or "multisig" are explained in the [memecoin glossary]({lang}/memes/glossary/).

## Chapter 1 — 2005: a frog in a comic

Pepe the Frog was drawn by the American artist **Matt Furie** in his web comic *Boy's Club* in 2005. Over the next decade the calm green frog became one of the most copied pictures on the internet. In 2016 the Anti-Defamation League (ADL) listed some versions of the meme as a hate symbol, and Furie worked with the ADL to win his character back. In other words: the frog was famous long before any coin.

The coin has **nothing to do with Matt Furie**. Anyone can create a token with any name — a lesson worth remembering.

## Chapter 2 — April 2023: "for entertainment purposes only"

In mid-April 2023 an anonymous team launched the PEPE token on Ethereum. They created **420.69 trillion** coins (a joke number), and the website said plainly that the coin had no intrinsic value and no expectation of returns. CoinGecko started tracking it on 18 April 2023.

What made it different from many other new coins at the time:

- no tax on buying and selling;
- the team said it had no presale (no cheap coins sold to insiders before launch);
- a simple, very recognisable picture that millions of people already knew.

## Chapter 3 — May 2023: a billion dollars in three weeks

Then the hype exploded. On social media people shared screenshots of tiny bets that had turned into large sums. On 5 May 2023 PEPE passed **$1 billion** in market value for the first time and entered the top 50 coins, The Block reported; its price had risen more than tenfold in a week. The same day **Binance** announced it would list PEPE — while warning in its own announcement that the coin had no utility and was made by an anonymous team.

According to Fortune, PEPE passed **$1.6 billion** in less than three weeks after launch. And then, just as fast, it fell: by 9 May 2023 its market value was about **$800 million** — half gone in a few days. People who bought on the day the news was loudest had the biggest losses.

## Chapter 4 — August 2023: the insiders sell

The project kept part of the supply in a **multisig** wallet (a wallet that needs several people to approve a transfer). On 24 August 2023 three former team members changed the rules of that wallet, moved about **16 trillion** PEPE (about $15 million) to exchanges and sold them. The price fell about 18% that day, The Block reported. The last remaining team member said the three had deleted their accounts and promised to burn (destroy) the remaining coins.

This is the classic memecoin risk: even a famous coin can have insiders who hold huge amounts and sell when they want.

## Chapter 5 — 2024: the comeback

PEPE did not disappear. In 2024 memecoins came back with the rest of the market. On 13 November 2024, after **Robinhood** listed PEPE and **Coinbase** announced it would add it, PEPE's market value briefly passed **$10 billion**, The Block reported. Big exchanges listing a coin brought millions of new possible buyers.

After every rally came deep drops. The record price so far is {{ath:pepe}} ({{athd:pepe}}); right now PEPE is {{fromath:pepe}} from it. Its 12-month change: {{chg1y:pepe}}.

## What beginners can learn from it

1. **"No value" means no value.** PEPE's own team said so. The price depends only on whether more people want to buy tomorrow.
2. **The loudest day is often the worst day to buy.** The big jump in May 2023 was followed by a 50% drop within days.
3. **Check who holds the coins.** Large insider wallets can sell at any time — as in August 2023. Our lesson [How to check a memecoin before buying]({lang}/blog/how-to-check-a-memecoin-before-buying/) shows how.
4. **Famous name ≠ official.** The coin has no link to the frog's creator; copycat "PEPE 2.0" coins are even riskier.

> **⚠️ Watch out**
> - **Fake PEPE tokens.** Scammers create tokens with the same name and logo. Only use the contract address shown on CoinGecko or a big exchange.
> - **"PEPE giveaway" and "airdrop" messages.** Nobody gives away free coins if you "send a little first" or connect your wallet to a strange site.
> - **Buying more than you can lose.** Memecoins can fall 50–90%. Use only money you could lose without problems.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Buying a memecoin a little at a time does not remove the risk — it only spreads the moment you buy. $10 a day in PEPE over the last year would be {{dca:pepe:1}} ({{dcaroi:pepe:1}}) today, versus {{dca:btc:1}} ({{dcaroi:btc:1}}) in Bitcoin.
