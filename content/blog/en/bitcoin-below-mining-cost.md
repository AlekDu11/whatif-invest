Bitcoin traded near **$61,500** early on Friday, November 20 (live price now: **{{price:btc}}**). That is below **$62,862** — CryptoQuant's estimate of what it cost a large public miner to produce one bitcoin in the third quarter. In plain words: for some of the biggest miners, the coin is now worth less than the electricity, machines and overheads it took to make it.

It is also about **50% under the all-time high of $126,080** from October 6, 2025, and the Crypto Fear & Greed Index sits at **12 out of 100** ("extreme fear"). Below: why the price fell, what "below mining cost" really tells you, and the number only our data shows — what people who bought a little every day actually paid per coin.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Bitcoin slipped to about $61,500 on November 20, down 2.7% in a day, about 13% in a week and roughly half its October 2025 peak.
> - That is the third time in 2026 the price has tested miners' costs — after February (about $64,000) and June–July (briefly under a roughly $60,000 cost line).
> - Someone who bought $10 of bitcoin every day since the November 2022 low paid about $49,300 per coin on average — still below today's price and below the miners' cost.

## Why Bitcoin fell again this week

There was no single trigger. Several pressures stacked up:

- **Rate-hike bets.** Minutes of the Fed's last meeting and comments from Governor Christopher Waller kept a December rate hike on the table, after October inflation came in at 0.4% for the month against 0.3% expected. Higher rates make risky assets less attractive.
- **ETF money leaving.** US spot Bitcoin ETFs have seen roughly **$2.6 billion** of net outflows in November, according to figures cited by BeInCrypto. CryptoQuant's Julio Moreno says both spot and speculative demand have dropped sharply since late October.
- **A bad day for stocks.** On Thursday the Dow lost more than 1,000 points and closed below 50,000, while the Nasdaq fell about 2% — on the same day Anthropic made its stock-market debut. Crypto fell alongside.
- **Japan.** With the US 10-year yield above 5% and the Bank of Japan's governor signalling more hikes, analysts warned about an unwinding of the yen "carry trade" — cheap yen loans that fund risky bets around the world.
- **Leverage.** More than $391 million of crypto positions were liquidated in 24 hours, about $288 million of them bullish bets, U.Today reported.

## What "below mining cost" does — and does not — mean

Miners spend real money (mostly electricity and machines) to win new bitcoins. When the price drops under that cost, the weakest operators either sell coins to pay bills or switch machines off. Earlier in 2026 this showed up clearly: six public miners sold a record **32,000 BTC** in the first quarter, and the network's difficulty fell about 10% in early June as machines went offline, 24/7 Wall St. reported.

Two honest caveats. First, there is no single "cost of bitcoin": the $62,862 figure is based on one large miner's filings, while JPMorgan put the all-in cost near $78,000 in mid-2026 and CoinShares estimated an average cash cost of about $75,500. Second, this time miners are not giving up — the 30-day average hashrate is about 1.02 ZH/s, less than 10% under its record, and some miners are earning extra by renting power to AI companies.

A price below cost has sometimes come near a bottom, because selling pressure from miners eventually runs out. It is not a floor. Bitcoin can stay below cost for months, and analysts quoted this week name $60,000 and the 2026 low near $57,700 as the next levels to watch. On our monthly closes, the June test ended at $58,559 — and the price was above $83,000 by September, before falling again. Past moves are history, not a forecast.

## What it means if you buy regularly

A miner's break-even is the price of making a coin. A regular buyer has a break-even too: the average price paid. Here is what $10 a day bought, on our monthly data at a price of about $61,500:

- **Since the November 2022 low:** about $14,900 invested, now worth about **$18,600 (+25%)**. Average price paid: **about $49,300** per coin.
- **Since the October 2025 peak:** about $4,260 invested, now worth about **$3,510 (−18%)**. Average price paid: about $74,700.
- **Since February 2026** (the first cost test this year): about $3,040 invested, now about **$2,680 (−12%)**. Average price paid: about $69,800.

The same habit gives very different results depending on when it started. Those who started near the top are in the red; those who kept buying through 2022 still hold coins cheaper than miners can make them. ## Calm rules for an "extreme fear" week

- **Only invest money you will not need for years.** If another 30% drop would force you to sell, the amount is too big.
- **Keep the plan boring.** A fixed sum on a fixed day removes the "is this the bottom?" question. The [habits calculator]({lang}/habits/) shows what small amounts add up to.
- **Skip leverage.** Liquidations made this drop sharper; borrowed bets can be wiped out in hours.
