**What is risk?** Risk is the chance that your investment is worth less than you paid — for a short time or for good. Every investment has it: even the biggest stock markets have fallen by more than half, and a single coin or company can go to zero.

> **Key takeaways**
> - The S&P 500 (the 500 largest US companies) fell about 57% between October 2007 and March 2009 and needed about five and a half years to get back to its old high.
> - Bitcoin lost about 65% in 2022 alone. A single small coin or company can lose 100%.
> - After a 50% fall you need a +100% rise just to get back to where you started. That is why the first rule is: invest only money you can afford to lose.

## Why prices fall

A price is simply what the next buyer is willing to pay. It falls when more people want to sell than to buy. Common reasons:

- **The whole market gets scared** — a recession (when the economy shrinks), a financial crisis, a war, higher interest rates. Then almost everything falls together. This is called market risk.
- **Something goes wrong with one company or coin** — bad results, a scandal, a hack, a project that is abandoned. This hits only that one asset, but it can hit it to zero.
- **Hype ends** — when a price went up mostly because of excitement, it often falls just as fast once the excitement is gone.

## How much can you lose?

It depends on what you own. History gives a rough picture (these are past numbers, not a forecast):

| What you own | A deep fall in the past |
|---|---|
| A broad stock index (many companies at once) | about −57% in 2007–2009 |
| Bitcoin | about −65% in 2022 |
| One small company or coin | can be −100% (bankruptcy, a scam, a dead project) |

## The maths that surprises everyone

Losses and gains are not symmetrical:

- Fall 20% → you need +25% to get back.
- Fall 50% → you need +100%.
- Fall 80% → you need +400%.

So avoiding a huge loss matters more than catching a huge gain.

## Step by step: how to take risk sensibly

**Step 1 — Build a safety cushion first.** Before investing, keep money for emergencies (many guides suggest 3–6 months of expenses) in a bank account or deposit. Then you will never be forced to sell at a bad moment.

**Step 2 — Decide your "afford to lose" amount.** Ask: "If this money went to zero, would my life change?" If the answer is yes, it is too much. Write the number down.

**Step 3 — Think in years, not days.** Money you need within 1–2 years (rent, a car, school fees) should not be in stocks or crypto. Time lets you wait out a fall.

**Step 4 — Don't put everything in one thing.** One coin or one company can go to zero; a whole index very rarely does. That is the next lesson: [diversification]({lang}/learn/).

**Step 5 — Buy a little at a time.** Buying a fixed small amount every month (DCA) means you do not put everything in at the top. See [buying a little every month]({lang}/blog/dca-buying-a-little-every-month/).

**Step 6 — Test yourself before the fall.** Imagine your $1,000 shows $400 tomorrow. Would you panic-sell? If yes, choose something calmer or a smaller amount.

## What it means if you buy regularly

If you had bought $10 of Bitcoin every day for 3 years, you would have put in {{dcainv:3}} and today it would be worth {{dca:btc:3}} ({{dcaroi:btc:3}}). Along the way, though, your balance would have gone down sharply more than once. Regular buying spreads out *when* you buy — it does not remove the risk of the asset itself. Compare calmer and riskier assets in the [compare tool]({lang}/compare/) and track your own in [your portfolio]({lang}/me/).

> **⚠️ Watch out**
> - **"Guaranteed returns" or "no risk".** Real investments never promise this. The US regulator (SEC) lists guaranteed high returns as a classic sign of fraud.
> - **Borrowing to invest.** Loans, credit cards and leverage (trading with borrowed money) can make you lose more than you put in.
> - **Selling in panic.** Many people lock in a loss by selling at the bottom. Decide your plan before the market falls, not during.
