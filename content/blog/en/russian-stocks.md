## Can you still buy Russian stocks?

From an EU country: **not through a normal broker, and no legal route is offered**. Three walls stand in the way:

1. **Western brokers and custodians stopped.** ABN AMRO, for example, writes that it stopped trading Russia-related shares on 2 March 2022 because intermediaries and custodians had stopped the Russia trade completely. Those products are no longer searchable in its app, and a sell order returns "currently not tradable". Russia-only ETFs and funds were suspended or wound up.
2. **EU sanctions.** The EU put Russia's National Settlement Depository (NSD) — where almost all Russian shares are held — on its sanctions list in June 2022, and EU sanctions also cover the shares and bonds of a number of Russian companies. The European Commission confirmed in July 2024 that EU persons cannot even take part in Russia's asset-swap scheme, because the NSD is sanctioned.
3. **Russian counter-measures.** Since March 2022 Russia has restricted sales by investors from "unfriendly" countries (the Netherlands and the whole EU are on that list), and their dividends go to blocked "type C" accounts in Russia.

On top of that, the US sanctioned the Moscow Exchange group itself in June 2024; since 13 June 2024 the exchange no longer trades in dollars or euros, only rubles. This page does not suggest workarounds: buying through intermediaries that sidestep sanctions can be illegal and leaves your money without investor protection.

## What happened to Russian stocks in 2022?

- **24 February 2022** — the day of the invasion, the Moscow Exchange's main index fell about a third in a single session.
- **28 February – 24 March 2022** — share trading in Moscow was suspended for almost a month.
- **Early March 2022** — MSCI and FTSE Russell called Russian stocks "uninvestable" and removed them from their emerging-market indexes at a price of practically zero. Index funds that tracked them had to write the shares down to almost nothing.
- **March–April 2022** — London suspended Russian GDRs, New York halted ADRs, and a Russian law from April 2022 forced most depositary-receipt programmes to close.

Ever since, roughly €14 billion of securities belonging to Russian private investors has been frozen at Euroclear, and Western investors' Russian shares sit in Russia, out of reach. Neither side shows signs of a quick thaw.

## What it means if you buy regularly

- **Country risk is real.** Within weeks in 2022, an entire market became untradable for foreign investors — regular buying cannot protect against that. Spreading across many countries can.
- **A low price is not a bargain if you cannot sell.** A share you are not allowed to sell or collect dividends from is worth little to you, whatever it costs in Moscow.
- **Check what your fund holds.** Broad emerging-market trackers dropped Russia in 2022; single-country funds were frozen. Diversified funds limited the damage.
- **Count in a hard currency.** Gains in rubles largely mirror inflation and the exchange rate; in dollars, five years of buying the biggest Russian names did not pay.

Past results, not a forecast.
