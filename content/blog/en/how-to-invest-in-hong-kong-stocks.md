## What "Hong Kong stocks" actually means

The Hong Kong Stock Exchange lists two big groups of companies. Local firms such as **HSBC**, **AIA**, **CK Hutchison** and **Sun Hung Kai Properties**, and mainland Chinese companies such as **Tencent**, **Alibaba**, **Xiaomi**, **Meituan** and the big state banks. So when you buy Hong Kong stocks, you are mostly betting on China's economy, priced in Hong Kong dollars.

The Hong Kong dollar is pegged to the US dollar in a band of 7.75–7.85 HKD per USD, run by the Hong Kong Monetary Authority. For a dollar-based investor that removes most of the currency swing that you get in Japan or Europe. The main index is the **Hang Seng Index**; see all the companies we track, sorted by market value, on our [Hong Kong stocks page]({lang}/stocks-hong-kong/).

## How to invest in Hong Kong stocks from abroad: 3 ways

1. **A broker with HKEX access.** Large international brokers let non-residents buy shares directly on HKEX in HKD. Check that the broker is licensed in your country, that it offers the Hong Kong market, and what it charges per trade and for currency conversion.
2. **An ETF.** One fund buys many Hong Kong companies for you. The Tracker Fund of Hong Kong (code 2800) follows the Hang Seng Index; US-listed funds such as KWEB hold Chinese internet companies, many of them listed in Hong Kong. European investors usually need UCITS versions.
3. **ADRs in New York.** Alibaba, JD.com, Baidu and NetEase also trade in the US, so a regular US-stock broker is enough. Tencent's ADR trades over the counter, with lower volume.

The direct route gives the widest choice; ETFs are the simplest for small regular amounts; ADRs are handy if your broker only offers US stocks.

## What it costs to buy Hong Kong shares

HKEX publishes the fees that apply to every trade, on top of your broker's commission:

- **Stamp duty:** 0.1% of the trade value, paid by both the buyer and the seller (rounded up to the nearest dollar).
- **HKEX trading fee:** 0.00565% per side.
- **SFC transaction levy:** 0.0027%; **AFRC levy:** 0.00015%.
- **Board lots:** each stock trades in fixed lots set by the company — often 100 to 1,000 shares or more — so one lot of a pricey stock can cost hundreds or thousands of dollars. Some brokers offer odd lots or fractions.

Dividends from mainland companies listed in Hong Kong usually have tax withheld (often 10% for individuals). Your home-country tax rules apply on top.

## When the Hong Kong market is open

Regular trading runs **9:30–12:00 and 13:00–16:00 Hong Kong time** (UTC+8), Monday to Friday, with a lunch break. Trades settle two business days later (T+2). For Europe that is the early morning; for the Americas it is the evening or night before.

## Risks to know before you buy

- **China policy risk:** regulation of tech, property and education has moved prices sharply in the past.
- **Concentration:** a few companies (Tencent, Alibaba, HSBC, Meituan) dominate the index.
- **Geopolitics:** US–China tensions, sanctions and delisting debates can hit ADRs and Hong Kong lines alike.
- **Liquidity of small names:** outside the top 100, trading can be thin and spreads wide.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Regular buying smoothed the ride: someone who kept buying through the 2021–2022 tech crash bought Tencent and Alibaba cheaply and still ended near break-even, while a lump sum at the 2021 peak lost far more. But DCA did not rescue a falling sector — five years of $10 a day in KWEB still lost money. The lesson from the numbers: a regular plan works better on a broad basket (an index fund or several sectors) than on one theme. Past results, not a forecast.
