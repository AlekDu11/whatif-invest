## How to convert the silver price per ounce to euros per gram

Silver trades in London (LBMA) and New York (COMEX) in **troy ounces** and **US dollars**. Two steps get you to euros per gram:

1. **Ounce → gram:** divide the ounce price by 31.1035. $43.17 ÷ 31.1035 = **$1.388 per gram**.
2. **Dollars → euros:** multiply by the dollar-to-euro rate ($1 = €0.9136). 1.388 × 0.9136 = **€1.268 per gram**.

## How many grams are in an ounce of silver?

Precious metals use the **troy ounce of 31.1035 grams** — not the kitchen ounce of 28.35 g. Any "ounce of silver" quote means a troy ounce. Shortcut: divide the ounce price by 31 and multiply by the exchange rate; you will be within about 0.3%.

## Why a dealer's price per gram is higher than the spot price

The price on screen is the **spot (wholesale) price**. Buying physical silver adds:

- **The spread:** dealers sell higher and buy back lower. For silver the gap is usually wider than for gold.
- **Tax:** in Germany silver bars normally carry **19% VAT**, while some coins are sold under the margin scheme (Differenzbesteuerung) with a lower effective tax. In Germany, gains on physical silver held by a private person **for more than a year** are tax-free. Rules differ in every EU country.
- **Fabrication:** small bars and coins carry a higher premium per gram; a 1 kg bar is the cheapest per gram.

So in a European shop, 1 gram of silver usually costs noticeably more than the ~€1.27 spot price. For German capital-gains rules see our [Germany tax page]({lang}/taxes/de/).

## Why silver swings so hard: the 2026 record and crash

Silver first passed its 1980 Hunt-brothers record ($49.95) on **9 October 2025**. A weaker dollar, expected rate cuts, geopolitical tension and rising industrial demand from solar panels and electronics drove it higher; according to S&P Global, about half of silver demand is industrial and supply has run a deficit for years.

The peak came at **$121.62 on 29 January 2026**. The next day, 30 January, CME margin hikes and forced selling of leveraged positions knocked silver down more than 30% in one session. By 28 November 2026 an ounce was about $43 — roughly a third of the record. The lesson: silver falls and rises much harder than gold.
