## Why most memecoins go to zero

Dogecoin, Pepe and Bonk are the rare survivors. Most memecoins never get that far:

1. **No cash flow, no product.** A memecoin's price depends only on attention and on the next buyer. When attention moves on, buyers disappear.
2. **Endless new supply.** Millions of new tokens are launched every year. CoinGecko counted more than half of all tokens tracked since 2021 as dead by the end of 2025, with about 11.6 million failing in 2025 alone.
3. **Insiders sell into the hype.** Creators and early wallets often hold a large share and sell to later buyers. Solidus Labs classified 98.6% of tokens launched on Pump.fun as rug pulls or pump-and-dumps.
4. **Survivorship bias.** We remember Dogecoin because it survived. The thousands of dog coins that died are not on any chart.

Read our [rug pull checklist]({lang}/blog/how-to-spot-a-rug-pull/) before touching any new token.

## What this means for a regular investor

- DCA works best on assets that have a reason to grow over decades. Memecoins have no such reason built in.
- If you hold memecoins at all, many people keep them to a small share of their portfolio — money they could lose without changing their life.

## FAQ

### Did $10 a day in Dogecoin make money?

The 5-year result above ({{dcaroi:doge:5}}) updates daily. Past results depend heavily on the start date and say nothing about the future.

### Is Pepe or Bonk a good long-term investment?

Nobody can say. Both are very young, very volatile and have already fallen far from their peaks. Treat them as speculation, not savings.

### Why compare memecoins with Bitcoin?

Bitcoin is the benchmark for crypto. If a memecoin cannot beat it over the same period with far higher risk, the extra risk was not rewarded.

### Can a memecoin come back after falling 90%?

Some have, many have not. A fall of 90% needs a 900% rise just to return to the peak.
