Futures markets now treat a Federal Reserve rate hike on **9 December** as all but certain: CME's FedWatch tool showed a 100% chance of a hike on 12 November, up from 67% a week earlier. Since the end of August, the last monthly close before the Fed's September hike, Bitcoin has fallen about **18%**, gold **14%** and the S&P 500 about **4%**. Below: why the Fed is raising rates again, why almost everything fell at once, and what people who kept buying through the last hiking cycle in 2022 actually ended up with.

> **Key takeaways**
> - The Fed raised its rate to 3.75–4% on 16 September, its first hike in three years. Futures now price another 0.25 point in December, with some chance of 0.5.
> - The 10-year US Treasury yield climbed to about 5.15%, up roughly 0.3 points in one week, and US producer prices rose 6.5% year on year in October.
> - Through the 2022 hikes and everything after, $10 a day from March 2022 grew to about $25,700 in Bitcoin and $25,200 in the S&P 500 on $17,300 invested — but Ethereum is still 16% under water.

## Why the Fed is hiking again

Inflation has come back. October consumer prices rose faster than expected, driven by energy costs, and producer prices were up **6.5%** from a year earlier, the most since November 2022. Oil climbed again after hopes for a Middle East peace deal faded, which feeds straight into prices for fuel and transport.

The Fed already moved once: on 16 September it lifted the target range for its key rate by a quarter point to **3.75–4%**, by a 12–0 vote. It held in October. After the hot October data, Fed Governor Christopher Waller said a half-point hike "should be considered" in December. The decision is due on 9 December; minutes of the last meeting come out on 19 November.

## Higher rates hit everything at once

A rate hike makes safe money pay more. When a 10-year US government bond yields above 5%, assets that pay no interest — Bitcoin, gold — and expensive growth stocks look less attractive by comparison. Borrowing for leveraged bets also gets pricier.

That is why the usual "hedges" did not hedge this time. Gold fell 3% in a single day on 12 November to about $3,868 an ounce, its lowest since October 2025, as the dollar hit a near 19-month high. Spot Bitcoin ETFs had about **$1.07 billion** of net outflows in the week to 13 November, and roughly $1.5 billion of leveraged long positions were liquidated on 13 November alone. The Crypto Fear & Greed Index is at **12 out of 100** — extreme fear.

## The last hiking cycle: what $10 a day returned

We have seen this before. In March 2022 the Fed started its fastest hiking cycle in decades, taking its rate from near zero to above 5% by mid-2023. Bitcoin fell below $20,000 that year, the S&P 500 had its worst year since 2008, and long bonds crashed.

So what happened to someone who started buying **$10 a day** in March 2022 — the very month the hikes began — and never stopped? On our data, with about **$17,300** invested by now:

- **Gold:** about $26,750 (+54%)
- **Bitcoin:** about $25,700 (+48%), even after this month's fall
- **S&P 500 (SPY):** about $25,200 (+45%), price only, before dividends
- **Long US bonds (TLT):** about $16,200 (−7%)
- **Ethereum:** about $14,500 (−16%)

Two lessons stand out. First, the worst time to start felt like the worst time to start: the buyers who began in 2022 spent a year looking at losses before the averaging worked in their favour. Second, averaging does not rescue everything: Ethereum is still below what was put in, and long bonds — supposedly the safe choice — lost money as rates rose.

## What it means if you buy regularly

A rate hike is not a forecast for any single asset. Markets usually price the move weeks ahead — that is partly what the November sell-off was. The size of the December hike and what the Fed signals for 2027 will matter more than the hike itself.

For regular buyers, falling prices cut both ways. Each $10 now buys more Bitcoin than it did in October, but the value of what you already hold is lower, and nothing guarantees a recovery on any timetable — in 2022 the bottom came months after the first hike. $10 a day in Bitcoin for 5 years is now worth **{{dca:btc:5}}** ({{dcaroi:btc:5}}) on {{dcainv:5}} invested; in the S&P 500 it is **{{dca:spy:5}}** ({{dcaroi:spy:5}}).
