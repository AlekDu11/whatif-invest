The **Crypto Fear & Greed Index** is a single number from 0 to 100 that tries to sum up the mood of the crypto market. Low numbers mean people are scared; high numbers mean they are greedy.

You can see today's reading on the live gauge on our [home page]({lang}/). This guide explains what goes into the number, how to read it, and what happened after past extreme readings.

## What the score means

The index is published daily by Alternative.me and is mainly based on Bitcoin. The scale is split into zones:

- **0–24: Extreme fear** — panic, heavy selling, bad news everywhere.
- **25–49: Fear** — caution, prices often falling or flat.
- **50: Neutral.**
- **51–74: Greed** — optimism, rising prices.
- **75–100: Extreme greed** — euphoria, fear of missing out (FOMO).

The idea comes from an old investing saying: markets swing between fear and greed, and crowds often feel most confident near tops and most hopeless near bottoms.

## What it measures: the components

According to Alternative.me, the score combines several data sources, each with a weight:

1. **Volatility (25%)** — how much Bitcoin's price is jumping compared with its 30- and 90-day averages. Unusually big swings are read as fear.
2. **Market momentum and volume (25%)** — strong buying volume in a rising market is read as greed.
3. **Social media (15%)** — how many posts and interactions mention Bitcoin, and how fast they grow.
4. **Surveys (15%)** — weekly polls of investors (this part has been paused for long periods).
5. **Bitcoin dominance (10%)** — a rising Bitcoin share of the market can signal fear (people retreat to the "safest" coin); a falling share can signal speculative greed in altcoins.
6. **Search trends (10%)** — Google searches for Bitcoin-related terms. A spike in searches like "bitcoin price crash" points to fear.

Two things follow from this. First, it is a **sentiment** measure built from market data and online behaviour — not a measure of value. Second, the exact method belongs to one publisher and can change. Other sites run their own versions with different formulas, so numbers can differ.

## How to read it

The simplest way to use the index is as a **thermometer**, not a compass:

- It tells you how others feel right now.
- It does not tell you where the price goes next.
- It is most useful at the extremes, and most useful as a check on your own emotions.

If the index shows extreme greed and you feel a strong urge to buy more than your plan, that is a moment to slow down. If it shows extreme fear and you want to sell everything, that is also a moment to pause and re-read your plan.

Also look at the trend: the gauge on our home page shows yesterday, last week and last month, so you can see whether sentiment is getting better or worse.

## History: extreme readings vs later prices

Here are some of the most extreme readings reported over the years, with what Bitcoin did over the following 12 months. Returns are based on Bitcoin's monthly closing prices in our data (the month of the reading vs 12 months later).

- **95 (extreme greed), June 2019** — 12 months later Bitcoin was down about **16%**.
- **95 (extreme greed), November 2020** — 12 months later up about **190%**.
- **6 (extreme fear), June 2022** — 12 months later up about **54%**.
- **10 (extreme fear), February 2025** — 12 months later down about **21%**.
- **5 (extreme fear, record low), February 2026** — less than 12 months of data so far.

What does this show?

- **Extreme greed did not always mean a top.** In June 2019 a reading of 95 came before a fall. In November 2020 the same reading came before Bitcoin nearly tripled.
- **Extreme fear did not always mean a bottom.** June 2022 was close to that cycle's low. But a year after the fearful reading of February 2025, Bitcoin was lower still — and the index then fell to a record low of 5 in February 2026.
- **Extremes can last.** Fear or greed can stay "extreme" for weeks.

A handful of dates is far too small to build a rule on. The honest summary: extreme readings tell you the crowd is emotional, and emotional markets move a lot — in either direction. Compare how far Bitcoin has come from its peaks on our [all-time highs page]({lang}/ath/).

## Why DCA investors watch it

For someone buying a fixed amount every week or month, the index can play a calm, practical role:

- **It explains the mood.** When everyone around you is panicking, the index confirms it is a market-wide feeling, not your personal mistake.
- **It helps you stick to the plan.** Many regular investors keep buying the same amount whatever the score — the point of [DCA]({lang}/blog/what-is-dca/) is not to guess.
- **Some people adjust slightly.** A few investors buy a bit more in extreme fear and a bit less in extreme greed. This is still a form of market timing and can be wrong, as the list above shows.

## Limits to keep in mind

- It is **Bitcoin-centred**: altcoins can behave differently.
- It is **backward-looking**: every input describes the recent past.
- **Social and search data** can be distorted by bots, news cycles and platform changes.
- **One publisher's formula**: there is no official, regulated "fear index" for crypto.

## FAQ

### Is a low Fear & Greed reading a buy signal?

No. It shows that sentiment is very negative. Prices have sometimes risen after extreme fear and sometimes kept falling, so it is not a reliable buy or sell signal.

### How often is the index updated?

Once a day. The value on our home page refreshes with our daily market data.

### What is the lowest reading ever?

The index reached 5 in February 2026, below the earlier low of 6 in June 2022, according to published reports.

### Is it the same as CNN's Fear & Greed Index?

No. CNN's index measures the US stock market using different inputs. The crypto index is a separate measure focused on Bitcoin.
