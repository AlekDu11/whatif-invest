The biggest company story of the year landed on Thursday, 12 November: **Jensen Huang, who co-founded Nvidia in 1993, resigned as CEO, president and board member** with immediate effect. CFO Colette Kress is now interim CEO, and the board named its first-ever chair, former Synopsys boss Harvey C. Jones. Nvidia reports quarterly results on Tuesday, 17 November, and Anthropic is expected to start trading on Nasdaq two days later.

So this week a lot of people are asking the same thing: *is Nvidia still a good long-term holding, and should I change my plan?* We can't answer that for you, but we can show what the numbers say.

## What happened, in short

- Nvidia said Huang stepped down after a board review of his personal conduct, which followed press reports in late October. The company says operations, customer commitments and the product roadmap continue as planned, and the board is looking at internal and external CEO candidates.
- The stock fell more than 11% at Thursday's open, closed down 4.3% at $166.19, then lost another 2% on Friday. Over the week it dropped about 12.75%.
- The sell-off spread on Friday: AMD fell 8.75% to $534.06, Dell 7.3%, CoreWeave 6.2% and Broadcom 4.9%. HSBC cut Nvidia to "hold", arguing the shares may struggle until a clear succession plan exists.
- Nvidia's market value peaked at about $5.8 trillion in October. At Thursday's close it was about $4 trillion, and Apple is now the world's most valuable company.

## What to watch on Tuesday

Nvidia's own guidance for the quarter was **$108 billion in revenue (±2%)** with a gross margin near 74%, and it assumed no data-centre chip sales to China. Last quarter it reported $96.2 billion, more than double a year earlier.

Things the market will focus on:

1. **Did it beat $108 billion, and what is the next-quarter outlook?** Strong numbers alone may not be enough this time. Analysts also want clarity on customer commitments and how big AI buyers are financing their data centres.
2. **Who is running the company.** Expect questions about the CEO search and whether key engineers and partners are staying.
3. **Big Tech spending.** Nvidia's revenue is other companies' AI capex. Any hint that clouds are slowing orders would hit the whole sector.

## The two sides of the debate

**The bear case.** Nvidia was built around one founder's vision, and nobody knows yet who replaces him. HSBC thinks the stock may stay under pressure until there is a plan. On top of that, investors already worried about huge AI infrastructure bills, and after the midterms there is talk of stricter rules for data centres.

**The bull case.** The products, customers and order book did not leave with Huang. The board says the roadmap is unchanged, demand for AI chips was still growing very fast last quarter, and one bad week has not changed the 5-year DCA result above. Some investors see a sharp drop in a still-growing business as a chance to keep buying on schedule.

Both views can be true at once: the business may stay strong while the stock stays volatile for months.

## Anthropic's IPO and AI tokens

Anthropic plans to sell 825 million shares at **$70–78** under the ticker **ANT**, aiming for a value of about $1.2 trillion at the midpoint. It reported $35.3 billion of revenue for the 12 months to September. Pricing is expected on Wednesday and trading on Thursday. Newly listed stocks often swing wildly in their first weeks, and there is no price history to run a DCA test on yet.
