Ether is trading around **$1,820** (live: **{{price:eth}}**) — the same level as its monthly close in March 2023 and about **63% below its all-time high of $4,946** from August 24, 2025. For people who buy a little every day, that means something rare: **five years of $10 a day in ETH is worth {{dca:eth:5}} on {{dcainv:5}} invested ({{dcaroi:eth:5}})**, while the same habit in Bitcoin, the S&P 500 and gold is in profit.

Below: why ether is falling now, how this drop compares with its earlier crashes, and what the numbers say about buying regularly through a long sideways market.

## Why ether is falling this month

- **ETF money is leaving.** US spot ether funds recorded about **$398.9 million** of net withdrawals in the November 9–13 trading week, according to Farside Investors data cited by crypto.news and Coinpaper. The biggest single day was $184.6 million.
- **A sharp weekend drop.** Ether fell more than 8% on November 14 and briefly hit about **$1,712** on Sunday, November 15, before bouncing back above $1,800. It is down roughly 14% in a week.
- **Rate-hike fears.** Traders on Kalshi put the chance of a quarter-point Federal Reserve **hike** in December at about **89%**. Higher rates make assets that pay no fixed income, like most crypto, less attractive.
- **Mood at rock bottom.** The Crypto Fear & Greed Index read **12 out of 100** ("extreme fear") on November 17, down from 53 a month earlier.

Not everyone is leaving: about **36.5%** of circulating ETH is staked — locked up by holders to earn network rewards — according to a validator report cited by Coinpaper. Live headlines are on our [news page]({lang}/news/).

## Ether has crashed harder than this before

On monthly closing prices from our data, ether has had three deep falls:

- **2018–2019:** from the January 2018 peak to January 2019 — **−90%**. It took three years to reach a new monthly high (January 2021).
- **2021–2022:** from November 2021 to December 2022 — **−74%**.
- **2025–2026, so far:** from the August 2025 monthly close — about **−59%**.

So today's fall is not the worst in ether's history, which also means it *could* go deeper. Past recoveries are history, not a promise. Our [all-time-high tracker]({lang}/ath/) shows how far every major coin is from its peak.

## What steady ETH buyers have now

Five years ago, in November 2021, ether was at its highest monthly close ever: about **$4,630**. Buying then was close to the worst possible timing.

- **$1,000 put in at once** five years ago is worth **{{lump:eth:5}}** today ({{lumproi:eth:5}}).
- **$10 every day** for the same five years: **{{dcainv:5}}** invested, now worth **{{dca:eth:5}}** ({{dcaroi:eth:5}}).

Both are in the red, but the daily buyer lost far less, because they kept buying cheap coins in 2022 and 2023. That pulled their average price down to roughly **$2,200** per ETH (as of November 16). The lump-sum buyer, by contrast, paid about $4,630 and needs ether to more than double just to break even.

Here is the sentence worth remembering: **in a five-year sideways market, regular buying did not make money in ether — it only made the loss smaller.**

Time in the market still mattered a lot. Someone who started **$10 a day eight years ago**, in late 2018 near the bottom of the first crash, is at **{{dcaroi:eth:8}}** ({{dca:eth:8}} on {{dcainv:8}} invested). And someone who started just **one year ago** is at {{dcaroi:eth:1}}, because most of their buying happened near the 2025 top.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Ether's last five years show both sides of DCA honestly:

- **DCA spreads timing risk; it does not remove price risk.** If an asset goes nowhere for years, a regular buyer goes nowhere too — or lower.
- **The breakeven is closer than the headline.** A 63% fall from the top sounds hopeless, but a regular buyer's average cost is far below the peak. Whether ether gets back there is unknown.
- **One coin is a concentrated bet.** Over the same period the S&P 500 and gold rose steadily while ether swung wildly. Mixing assets changes the ride; try it in the [comparison tool]({lang}/compare/).
- **Size the habit so you can keep it.** Only money you won't need for years belongs in crypto. Our [habits calculator]({lang}/habits/) shows what a small daily amount adds up to, and the [compound interest calculator]({lang}/compound-interest/) shows the slower, steadier path.
