A **store of value** is something that keeps its purchasing power over time, so you can save today and spend later. For thousands of years that role went to **gold**. Since 2009, **Bitcoin** has been called "digital gold".

Which one has actually done the job? This guide compares them using real price history and lays out the arguments on both sides — without picking a winner for you.

## What makes a good store of value

Most economists point to a few qualities:

- **Scarcity** — supply cannot be increased easily.
- **Durability** — it does not rot, rust or disappear.
- **Portability and divisibility** — easy to move and split.
- **Trust and acceptance** — many people agree it has value.
- **Stability** — its price does not swing wildly in the short term.

Gold and Bitcoin both score well on scarcity and durability. They differ most on **stability** and **track record**.

## Volatility and drawdowns

A **drawdown** is the fall from a peak to the next low. It shows how much pain a holder had to sit through. Using monthly closing prices in our data:

- **Bitcoin** fell about **76%** from its December 2017 peak to January 2019, and about 73% again from late 2021 to late 2022.
- **Gold** fell about **42%** from August 2011 to December 2015 and took years to recover.
- **S&P 500 (SPY)** fell about **51%** during the 2007–2009 financial crisis.

Daily moves tell a similar story: Bitcoin often moves more in a day than gold does in a month. Gold is not risk-free either — after its 1980 peak it took around 28 years to get back to the same price in dollars.

## The inflation hedge debate

"Protects against inflation" is the most common claim for both. History is mixed.

### The case for gold

- Over very long periods — decades and centuries — gold has broadly kept its purchasing power.
- It tends to do well when people lose trust in currencies or governments, and during crises.
- Central banks hold it as a reserve. According to the World Gold Council, they bought more than 1,000 tonnes in each of 2022, 2023 and 2024.

### The case against gold

- Over shorter periods it can lag inflation for years. In the 1980s and 1990s, gold lost a large part of its real value.
- It pays no interest or dividends.

### The case for Bitcoin

- Its supply is capped at 21 million coins by code, and new issuance halves roughly every four years (see the [Bitcoin halving]({lang}/bitcoin-halving/) page).
- It is easy to store and send across borders, which matters in countries with capital controls or a falling currency.
- Over its history so far, it has risen far faster than prices in general.

### The case against Bitcoin

- In 2022, when US inflation hit its highest level in about 40 years, Bitcoin fell about 64% over the year (monthly closes, our data). Gold ended the year roughly flat. In that test, Bitcoin behaved more like a risky tech asset than a hedge.
- Its history is short — about 17 years — compared with gold's thousands.
- Its value depends on continued adoption, technology and regulation.

## Both sides in one view

**Gold**

- ✅ Thousands of years of trust, held by central banks.
- ✅ Lower volatility, often steady in crises.
- ❌ Lower long-term growth, no income.
- ❌ Physical storage and buying/selling spreads can be costly.

**Bitcoin**

- ✅ Fixed supply, easy to move and divide.
- ✅ Much higher historical returns.
- ❌ Very large drawdowns (70%+ more than once).
- ❌ Short history; regulatory and technology risks; risk of losing keys or being scammed.

## How regular investors think about it

Many people do not choose one or the other. Common approaches include:

- **Small allocations.** Some investors hold a small share in gold, Bitcoin or both, alongside a broad stock or bond portfolio, so that a crash in one does not ruin the whole plan.
- **Regular buying.** Buying a fixed amount every month ([DCA]({lang}/blog/what-is-dca/)) spreads the entry price and reduces the stress of volatility.
- **Matching the time horizon.** Money needed in 1–2 years is usually kept away from assets that can fall 50–75%.

## FAQ

### Is Bitcoin really "digital gold"?

It shares gold's scarcity, but not its stability or long history. Whether it becomes a lasting store of value is still debated.

### Which is safer, gold or Bitcoin?

Historically gold has been much less volatile, with smaller drawdowns. Bitcoin has had far higher returns but has lost over 70% of its value more than once.

### Does gold always protect against inflation?

No. Over very long periods it has roughly kept its purchasing power, but it can lag inflation for many years.

### Can I hold both?

Yes, many people do. How much depends on your goals, time horizon and how much volatility you can live with.
