Tyson Foods, America's biggest meat company, had one of its best days in years on Monday. The quarter itself was weak, but investors cared more about one line in the outlook: the beef business, which lost more than half a billion dollars this year, should lose far less — or even turn a profit — in 2027.

## What happened

- **Monday, November 16 — results before the US open.** Tyson reported fiscal Q4 sales of $14.59 billion, up 5.2% from a year earlier and above the roughly $14.1 billion analysts expected. An extra week in the quarter helped; without it and legal accruals, sales fell 4%.
- **Profit was down sharply.** Adjusted earnings were $0.61 per share — slightly above analyst estimates of about $0.58–0.60, but 47% lower than a year ago. On a reported basis Tyson lost $0.22 per share.
- **The stock rallied anyway.** TSN opened about 10% higher at $64.86 versus Friday's $58.84 close and finished the day up about 8.7% near $64, by our data.
- **Dividend up.** The quarterly Class A dividend was raised 2% to $0.52 ($2.08 a year, about a 3.3% yield).

## Why it moved

The reason is the beef outlook. The US cattle herd is near multi-decade lows, so cattle are expensive — Tyson says its cattle costs rose about $2.1 billion this year — and its beef unit lost about $654 million (adjusted) in fiscal 2026. In the last quarter alone the adjusted beef loss grew to $171 million from $52 million a year earlier. For fiscal 2027 Tyson now expects beef to land somewhere between a $300 million loss and $100 million of profit, helped by plant closures, early signs that ranchers are rebuilding herds and the slow restart of cattle imports from Mexico.

That was enough for investors to look past the rest. Piper Sandler raised its price target from $56 to $72, saying beef profitability is "trending in the right direction". The weak spots: Tyson expects 2027 sales to fall 3.5–5.5% (analysts had pencilled in about −1%), chicken profit to drop to $1.3–1.6 billion from $1.9 billion on higher feed costs, and pork to shrink too. Its total adjusted operating income target of $1.7–2.15 billion brackets this year's $1.98 billion.
