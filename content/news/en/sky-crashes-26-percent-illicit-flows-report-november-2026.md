Sky, the DeFi protocol behind the DAI and USDS stablecoins (formerly MakerDAO), saw its governance token lose about a quarter of its value in a day. The trigger was an investigation suggesting that hackers, including North Korea's Lazarus Group, used Sky's stablecoin swap module — and that a large chunk of Sky's reserves could in theory be frozen.

## What happened

- **Thursday, November 19 — the crash.** SKY fell about 25–26% in 24 hours, from roughly $0.050 at the daily open to a low near $0.037. Its market value dropped to about $870 million.
- **A key level broke.** The price fell below $0.040, its November 2025 low. SKY has lost more than 35% in a week and over 60% since its early-October peak of about $0.10.
- **Selling was unusually fast.** The 14-day RSI fell to about 19, a level that shows how one-sided the selling was — though on its own it does not signal a rebound.

## Why it moved

On November 19 CoinDesk published an investigation by blockchain analytics firm Inca Digital. Its main findings:

- About **$518 million** of transactions from 422 wallets linked to hacks, phishing and sanctioned entities passed through Sky's Peg Stability Module (PSM), the contract that swaps USDC for DAI/USDS.
- About **$101.9 million** was linked to North Korea, mostly wallets funded by the February 2025 Bybit hack. Nearly $30 million moved after the FBI had publicly blamed North Korea for that theft.
- About 95% of the flagged flows came through third-party aggregators such as 1inch, ParaSwap and MetaMask Swaps, not Sky's own website.
- The bigger worry is the **reserve**: about $2.7 billion of USDC backing DAI and USDS sits at a single Ethereum address. Circle, the USDC issuer, can technically freeze addresses after a court order or a government request. No freeze has been reported, and the report covers past flows, not illicit money sitting in the reserve now.

The Sky Frontier Foundation disputes the conclusions. It says the flagged amounts are under 0.06% of the module's lifetime volume of more than $930 billion, mostly date from 2021 to mid-2024, and that its official apps screen wallets and block sanctioned regions. A community member has already proposed pausing USDS staking rewards and speeding up SKY buybacks until the price recovers above $0.05.

The fall also came on top of an already weak crypto market, with bitcoin near $63,000.
