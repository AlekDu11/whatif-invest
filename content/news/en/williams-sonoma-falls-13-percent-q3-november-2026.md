Williams-Sonoma, the owner of Pottery Barn, West Elm and the Williams Sonoma kitchen stores, reported a solid quarter on paper — yet its stock had one of the worst days in the S&P 500. Investors had priced in more, and the company gave them no new reason to pay up.

## What happened

- **Thursday, November 19, before the US open.** Williams-Sonoma released results for the quarter ended November 1. The stock, which closed at $260.09 the day before, fell about 8% in early trading and slid to roughly $225 later in the session — a drop of about 13%.
- **Revenue slightly short.** Net revenue rose 5.8% to $1.99 billion, a touch below the roughly $2.0 billion analysts expected. Comparable brand revenue grew 5.1%: e-commerce +6.8%, stores only +1.5%.
- **Profit a hair above.** Adjusted earnings were $2.19 per share versus about $2.18 expected (+12% year on year). GAAP earnings of $2.38 included a one-off boost of about $29 million from refunds of IEEPA tariffs, which added about 1.4 percentage points to gross margin.
- **No upgrade.** The full-year forecast was only reaffirmed: revenue growth of 4.7–7.2%, comparable growth of 4–6.5% and an adjusted operating margin of 17.8–18.2%.

## Why it moved

The stock was trading near its 52-week high of $274.45 going into the report, so "in line" was not enough. On the call, management said the US housing market got worse during the quarter — bad news for a furniture seller — and warned of up to $60 million of extra diesel costs this year, with US diesel prices up more than 60% from a year ago. It also said it does not expect further tariff refunds in the fourth quarter and is deliberately cutting back on holiday discounts while rivals are much more promotional, which protects margins but can cost sales.
