The US Securities and Exchange Commission is moving toward stock markets that look more like crypto: tokenized shares and trading close to 24 hours a day. In an interview published this week, SEC Chairman Paul Atkins and Trading and Markets Director Jamie Selway walked through the plan and the open questions.

## What happened

- **Tokenized stocks.** The SEC's five-year "Innovation Exemption" from September 17 lets qualifying venues trade certain tokenized US stocks. Synthetic products are excluded, the issuer gets 30 days to object, and applicants must be US persons.
- **Lighter rules for pilots.** The proposed relief covers some market-data reporting, Consolidated Audit Trail duties and the order-routing Rule 611.
- **Longer hours.** Extended trading already runs 4 a.m. to 8 p.m. New York time, and some venues offer 24-hour weekday access but handle only about 1% of volume. DTCC can now clear trades for 23 hours on five weekdays.
- **Open questions.** Selway flagged investor protection (SIPC cover) for tokenized holdings and dollar stablecoins, and said the SEC is coordinating with the CFTC.
