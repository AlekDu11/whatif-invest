XTB, the Warsaw-listed broker behind the popular xStation app, had its worst day in years on Tuesday. A single sentence from Poland's financial regulator was enough: more rules are coming for CFDs, the leveraged contracts that still earn XTB most of its money.

## What happened

- **Tuesday, November 17, around 11:00–11:15 UTC.** Speaking at a Parkiet conference in Warsaw, KNF deputy chair Dariusz Adamski said the regulator will soon carry out another product intervention on CFDs (contracts for difference). He gave no details or dates.
- **The stock dropped within minutes.** XTB fell as much as about 19% intraday, to roughly 113 zł, and closed at 115.24 zł, down 17.5% from Monday's 139.72 zł. About 2.5 billion zł of market value was wiped out in one session, on record turnover for the stock.
- **The numbers behind the warning.** Adamski said 267,000 Poles lost almost 2 billion zł on CFDs in 2025 — about seven times more than in 2019, when the KNF first limited leverage for retail clients. He said those 2019 rules had not worked as intended.

## Why it moved

Investors are pricing in the risk to XTB's core business. Although most XTB clients now buy ordinary stocks and ETFs, CFDs still generate more than 90% of its result from trading financial instruments. Nobody knows yet what the KNF will do: the market is guessing anything from lower leverage caps and tighter suitability tests to a Spanish-style ban on mass CFD advertising. A KNF official said any new rules would apply equally to Polish and foreign brokers serving Poland.

This is not the first hit. XTB fell more than 7% in May when the KNF first warned about steering beginners into CFDs, and the regulator fined the company 20 million zł in April (upheld in August) over how it assessed clients in 2022–2023. XTB did not issue an immediate comment; its full Q3 report is due on Friday, November 20. Preliminary figures showed a net profit of 213.7 million zł, more than four times a year earlier.
