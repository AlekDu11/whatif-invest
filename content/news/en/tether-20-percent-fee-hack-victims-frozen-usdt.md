Tether, the company behind the biggest stablecoin, USDT, asked some victims of crypto hacks to pay fees of up to 20% before it would reissue their frozen coins, according to a Bloomberg investigation published after the record Binance hack. Tether says the fees cover the cost of recovery, including legal work.

## What happened

- **The report.** Bloomberg looked at who really controls money in crypto after the October 29 Binance hack, in which stablecoin issuers froze stolen funds within hours. It found that Tether charged some hack victims a reissuance fee of up to a fifth of the frozen amount.
- **An old case.** Singapore exchange Bittok lost about $50 million in a 2020 hack and, more than five years later, still has not recovered its frozen USDT, the report says.
- **Tether's answer.** A spokesperson said Tether has reissued $1.2 billion of USDT so far and that fees pay for recovery and legal costs. CEO Paolo Ardoino said funds are returned in stages in civil disputes because legal guidance is unclear.
- **Published policy.** Tether's public recovery rules cover coins sent to the wrong address and charge up to 10% (at least $1,000); hacks are handled case by case.

## What it means for ordinary holders

A stablecoin issuer that can freeze stolen money can also decide when, and at what price, you get it back. USDT and USDC together hold about 86% of the stablecoin market, per Bloomberg, so these rules touch almost everyone who keeps "digital dollars" on an exchange.
