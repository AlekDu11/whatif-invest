Binance has turned to the courts to chase the money taken in last month's attack. The exchange said a court in Abu Dhabi granted it a worldwide freezing order worth $6.5 billion against the still-unidentified people behind the October 29 hack — one of the largest such orders ever made public.

## What happened

- **Who and where.** The order came from the Abu Dhabi Global Market (ADGM) Courts and targets the unknown attackers. Extra orders cover specific assets already frozen by third parties, which Binance does not accuse of wrongdoing.
- **26,000 wallets.** Binance's lawyers have listed about 26,000 addresses holding stolen funds and will keep updating that list.
- **How much is still out there.** Binance has said about $6.34 billion in assets were affected and roughly $2.85 billion has been frozen or intercepted. Elliptic estimated earlier that about $3.3 billion sits in bitcoin, ether and XRP that no company can freeze.
- **Not the only action.** US prosecutors won court approval last week to seize or restrain about $1.2 billion of the stolen crypto, and the hackers have kept moving ether this week.
