American shoppers kept spending in October, but much more slowly. US retail sales rose 0.2% from September, the Census Bureau said on Tuesday, November 17, after a 0.8% gain the month before. Economists had expected only 0.1%, and spending outside car dealers grew a stronger 0.6%.

## What happened

- **Headline beat, but slower.** Total retail and food services sales reached $748.2 billion, up 0.2% on the month and 6.8% on the year. The margin of error (±0.4%) means the monthly change is not clearly different from zero.
- **Cars and furniture down.** Motor vehicle dealers fell 1.6%, furniture stores 2.3% and clothing stores 0.7%.
- **Online up.** Non-store retailers gained 1.2% on the month and 11.8% on the year; restaurants and bars rose just 0.2%.
- **Core stronger than expected.** Sales excluding autos rose 0.6% against a 0.4% forecast. The figures are not adjusted for inflation, so part of the rise is higher prices.

## Why it matters for markets

A still-solid consumer gives the Fed less reason to back off. Markets already price roughly a 90% chance of a December rate hike, and the 10-year Treasury yield is above 5%.
