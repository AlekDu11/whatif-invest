Zelle, the bank-owned payments app that handled more than $1.2 trillion of US transfers in 2025, will use the Solana blockchain for its ZelleUSD stablecoin and its first international payments. The news came at Solana's Breakpoint conference on November 16, with SOL trading near a four-month low.

## What happened

- **Solana chosen.** Zelle had announced ZelleUSD (ZLUSD) in June without naming a blockchain; it now says the token will run on Solana for person-to-person transfers abroad.
- **Bank-backed.** Zelle is run by Early Warning Services, owned by seven US banks including JPMorgan Chase and Wells Fargo. The stablecoin is issued by Anchorage Digital Bank.
- **India first.** The first corridor is India, which Early Warning has said it expects to go live before the end of 2026. No launch date was given at Breakpoint.
- **Hidden amounts.** Zelle is testing confidential transfers that hide the sum on the public chain while letting member banks see it for compliance checks.
