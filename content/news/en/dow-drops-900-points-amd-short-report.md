Wall Street gave back an early gain on Thursday, November 19, and slid to session lows by early afternoon. Chip stocks led the drop, with AMD hit hardest after short-seller Hunterbrook Capital said it was betting against the company over alleged links to blacklisted Chinese firms.

## What happened

- **A sharp reversal.** The S&P 500 opened about 1% higher on the day of Anthropic's listing, then turned. Around 1:15 p.m. New York time the Dow was down about 900 points (−1.8%), the S&P 500 −1.5% and the Nasdaq −1.6%. The VIX fear gauge rose about 9% to near 27.
- **AMD's China problem.** Hunterbrook says Chinese server maker IEIT Systems kept getting newer AMD firmware after it was put on the US export blacklist in March 2025. China and Hong Kong made up about 22% of AMD's 2025 revenue. AMD fell around 8–9%, to near $484; Bloomberg reported that AMD said the report misrepresents its practices.
- **Other chips gave up gains.** Nvidia, Broadcom and Micron slipped into the red; Intel and Qualcomm held up.
- **Elsewhere.** The 10-year Treasury yield stayed above 5%, gold fell about 1.6% and bitcoin traded near $62,600.
