Salesforce, the world's biggest seller of customer-relationship (CRM) software, had its sharpest one-day fall in two and a half years. The drop came without any news from the company itself.

## What happened

- **Tuesday, November 17:** CRM fell 9.4%, from $214.83 to $194.62 at the close. It was the worst performer in the S&P 500 that day and the stock's biggest one-day drop since May 2024. About $17 billion of market value was wiped out.
- **Lowest close since mid-August.** The shares are down about 19% over the past month.
- **The whole software group fell:** Intuit lost 7.2%, HubSpot about 7%, ServiceNow about 6.5% and Workday about 5%; Atlassian also slid.
- **A weak market overall:** the S&P 500 lost 1.0% to 7,340, its lowest close since late July, and the Dow fell 1.6%.

## Why it moved

There was no Salesforce-specific announcement. The sell-off hit software stocks across the board, for three overlapping reasons:

- **AI agents vs. seat licences.** Salesforce mostly charges per user. Investors worry that if AI agents do the work of sales and support staff, companies will need fewer seats. Salesforce's own answer, Agentforce, is growing fast (over $1.5 billion in annual recurring revenue, up more than 240%), but the market doubts it can make up for the risk.
- **Anthropic's IPO.** Anthropic, an AI company whose tools compete with parts of the software stack, is set to start trading on Thursday, November 19, in an offering reported at around $64 billion. Analysts say money is rotating out of application-software stocks into AI names ahead of it.
- **New AI rivals and spending worries.** An AI-native start-up, Dreamteam, raised $53 million to take on CRM vendors such as Salesforce and SAP, and some investors are uneasy about Salesforce's $5.7 billion Braze deal.

The company's recent results were solid: fiscal Q2 revenue rose 11% to $11.3 billion. The next report is on **December 2**, after the close. Before the drop the stock traded at about 15 times expected earnings, versus a five-year average near 27.
