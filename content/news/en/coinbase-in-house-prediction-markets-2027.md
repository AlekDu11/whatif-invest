Coinbase is preparing to run its own prediction markets in the US from the first half of 2027, Bloomberg reported, citing a person familiar with the plans. Today Coinbase customers can bet on events only through a partnership with Kalshi, which takes part of the fees.

## What happened

- **The report.** On November 19, Bloomberg said Coinbase plans in-house event contracts for retail and institutional traders in the first half of 2027. A Coinbase spokesperson did not confirm the timing and pointed to earlier plans to build its own clearing infrastructure.
- **A fast-growing business.** The Kalshi-powered product, live nationwide since January 2026, passed a $200 million annualized net revenue run rate in the third quarter, with more than half of contracts not related to sports.
- **The building blocks.** Coinbase already runs a CFTC-regulated derivatives exchange, received approval for its clearinghouse this autumn and agreed in December 2025 to buy the startup The Clearing Company.
- **Legal fog.** On November 12 a Louisiana judge issued a temporary restraining order against 31 firms, including Coinbase, Kalshi, Polymarket and Robinhood, over claims that some contracts are illegal gambling. The CFTC says it alone has jurisdiction.

## What it means for Coinbase shareholders

Owning the contracts would give Coinbase control over which markets it lists and keep the fees it now shares, a way to earn money that depends less on crypto trading volumes.
