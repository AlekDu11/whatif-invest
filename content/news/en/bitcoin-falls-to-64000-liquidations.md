Bitcoin had a rough week. After losing the $72,000 level on November 12, it kept sliding and traded near $64,000 by the evening of November 14, then briefly dipped to around $62,000 on November 15 before a short-lived rebound.

## What happened

- **A chain of broken levels.** Bitcoin dropped through $70,000 and then its 200-week moving average (about $67,800–68,000), a level many long-term traders watch as a floor.
- **Leverage made it worse.** Roughly $1.2 billion of leveraged long positions were liquidated across November 13–14, forcing more selling into a falling market.
- **Pressure on big holders.** At around $64,000, Strategy (formerly MicroStrategy), the largest corporate bitcoin holder, was sitting on paper losses of about $9 billion on its bitcoin.
- **Sentiment hit extreme fear.** The Crypto Fear & Greed Index fell to 10 out of 100.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Crashes feel very different for a regular (DCA) buyer: the same $10 buys more bitcoin when the price is low. Someone who bought $10 of bitcoin every day for the last 5 years has put in {{dcainv:5}} and now holds {{dca:btc:5}} ({{dcaroi:btc:5}}) — even after this drop. Past drawdowns of 50–80% were eventually recovered, but each took a long time and nothing guarantees it will happen again. Compare with stocks and gold in the [DCA calculator]({lang}/compare/).
