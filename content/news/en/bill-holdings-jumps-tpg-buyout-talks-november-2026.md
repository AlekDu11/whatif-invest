Bill Holdings, which makes bill-pay and invoicing software for small and mid-sized businesses, jumped to a 52-week high on Tuesday. The reason is a possible buyout: a report that private-equity giant TPG is close to taking the company off the stock market.

## What happened

- **Tuesday, November 17, US session.** The Financial Times reported that TPG is nearing an agreement to take Bill Holdings private, at a premium to its roughly $4.8 billion market value. Sources said a deal could be signed within days, but warned the timing is fluid and talks could still fall apart.
- **The stock spiked.** BILL jumped about 8% within minutes of the report, briefly triggering a volatility trading halt, and kept climbing. It closed at $64.97, up 12.7% from Monday's ~$57.60, by our data.
- **It was already rising.** On Monday, November 16, the shares gained about 5% after an analyst upgrade, so the stock is up roughly 18% in two sessions.
- **No confirmation yet.** TPG declined to comment and Bill Holdings did not respond to requests, according to the reports. No price per share has been made public.

## Why it moved

When a buyer is expected to pay more than the current market price, traders bid the stock up toward that likely offer. The story has been building for a year: activist funds Elliott Management and Starboard Value both became top-10 shareholders in 2025 and pushed the board to explore a sale, and Bill later added four new directors in a settlement with Starboard. Hellman & Friedman also held talks with the company earlier in 2026. TPG knows the space — it bought rival AvidXchange for $2.2 billion last year.

The low valuation is what makes Bill attractive to buyers. Its market value peaked near $34 billion in 2021 and fell more than 80% as growth slowed and investors soured on fintech software.
