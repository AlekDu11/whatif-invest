A bill that would turn the US government's bitcoin into a permanent reserve has been sent to the full House of Representatives. It would ban selling the coins for at least 20 years, but it does not buy any new bitcoin, and it has only about five weeks left to pass.

## What happened

- **The bill.** H.R. 8957, the "American Reserve Modernization Act of 2026", was introduced in May by Rep. Nick Begich (R-Alaska) with Rep. Jared Golden (D-Maine). The House Financial Services Committee approved it 28–21 along party lines in September; on November 24 it was formally reported and placed on the House calendar.
- **The 20-year rule.** Bitcoin in the reserve could not be sold, swapped, auctioned or pledged for at least 20 years. After that, the Treasury could recommend selling at most 10% in any two-year period.
- **No new purchases.** The committee version dropped the funding options from the original text. The reserve would hold what the government already has: about 306,800 BTC in addresses linked to the US government, per Arkham data cited by CoinDesk. It also adds yearly proof-of-reserve reports and an independent audit.
- **A narrow path.** No floor vote is scheduled. The bill still needs the Senate and the president's signature before the current Congress ends on January 3, 2027; otherwise it must start again. Polymarket put the odds of a national bitcoin reserve by year-end at about 4%.

## What it means for bitcoin holders

A law would not add new demand, but it would take a large pile of coins off the table as a possible seller for two decades.
