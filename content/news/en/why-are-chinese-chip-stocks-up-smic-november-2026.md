Chinese chip stocks are up because Beijing promised to defend its stock market: on Friday, November 27, a State Council meeting chaired by Premier Li Qiang called on the state-backed "national team" of investors to steady prices, and chipmakers led the rebound. SMIC closed up 16.4% in Hong Kong and 15.4% in Shanghai.

## What happened

- **The pledge (during Asian trading).** The State Council said Central Huijin should act as a market stabilisation fund, keep liquidity ample in turmoil, steer insurance and pension money into shares and encourage dividends and buybacks (Xinhua via Reuters/CNBC). It was the first time such a readout used the phrase "national team".
- **The close (07:00 UTC Shanghai, 08:00 UTC Hong Kong).** The CSI semiconductor index jumped 17.6%, its biggest one-day gain on record (Wind). The STAR 50 rose 15.1% and the CSI 300 4.2%, its best day since October 2024. The Hang Seng gained 2.1%.
- **Leaders.** Hua Hong rose 20% (the daily limit in Shanghai), Cambricon 18.7%, Hygon 18.1%. Turnover was about half higher than the day before.

## Why are SMIC and Chinese chip stocks rising?

Before the pledge, Chinese chip stocks had been hit by the global AI sell-off and by a new US rule that limits Chinese firms' access to advanced AI chips through overseas data centres. Beijing's promise of state buying, plus a cut in the minimum margin ratio from 100% to 80% from November 30, turned the mood. At midday the CSI 300 was up only 0.2%, so most of the move came after the news. Such policy rallies can fade quickly if the buying does not follow.
