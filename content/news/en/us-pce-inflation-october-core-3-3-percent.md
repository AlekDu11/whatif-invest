The last big US inflation report before the Federal Reserve's December 9 decision came in mostly as expected on Wednesday, November 25. Prices measured by the PCE index rose 3.8% in the year to October, while core PCE, which leaves out food and energy, climbed to 3.3%, a tenth of a point above forecasts.

## What happened

- **Headline.** PCE prices rose 0.3% in October and 3.8% from a year earlier, both in line with expectations, up from 3.6% in September. The data came out at 13:30 UTC.
- **Core.** Core PCE rose 0.3% on the month and 3.3% on the year, against a 3.2% forecast and 3.1% in September.
- **Households.** Personal income rose 0.7%, spending 0.3% before inflation; after inflation spending was roughly flat and the saving rate rose to 4.1%.
- **First reaction.** In the first minutes, the S&P 500 ETF was down about 0.2% more and the Nasdaq-100 ETF about 0.3%, on top of a 2% pre-market slide. Polymarket put the odds of a 25-basis-point Fed hike in December at 94%.

## What it means for your money

Inflation near 4% means cash loses buying power fast, and a central bank that is raising rates usually weighs on risk assets: bitcoin is down about a quarter this month, near $58,000 on Wednesday morning.
