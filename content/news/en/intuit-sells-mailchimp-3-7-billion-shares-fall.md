Intuit, the maker of TurboTax and QuickBooks, reported a 12% jump in quarterly revenue on Thursday, November 19, and agreed to sell its Mailchimp email-marketing business. Investors focused on a softer outlook instead, and the shares fell sharply in after-hours and overnight trading.

## What happened

- **Mailchimp goes for $3.68 billion.** The buyer is Italian tech group Bending Spoons, paying about $3.31 billion in cash and $368 million in its own shares. Intuit bought Mailchimp for about $12 billion in 2021. The deal is expected to close in early 2027.
- **A solid quarter.** Revenue for the quarter to October 31 reached about $4.4 billion; adjusted earnings were $2.91 per share, up 56%. Mailchimp's own revenue slipped 1% to $313 million.
- **The outlook disappointed.** Intuit expects 6–7% revenue growth next quarter and $23.1–23.4 billion for the full fiscal year, which reports said was below what analysts were hoping for.
- **The stock reaction.** Shares were down about 14.5% shortly after the report and about 8% in overnight trading, on top of a year-to-date fall of roughly half.
