Paytaca, a self-custodial Bitcoin Cash wallet and payments app, says attackers broke into its backend twice in two days. The company reports no confirmed losses so far and says users' private keys were not exposed, but it is asking users to treat any unexpected order or payment request with suspicion.

## What happened

- **First attack, November 17.** A flaw in the backend of Paytaca's peer-to-peer "Ramp" service let attackers create fake P2P orders.
- **Second attack, November 18.** A different weakness allowed unauthorised payment requests to be generated.
- **Keys stayed on devices.** Because private keys live on users' phones, not on Paytaca's servers, backend access alone cannot move funds — every transfer still needs the user's signature.
- **Many unknowns.** Paytaca has not said how many fake orders or requests were created, how many users were affected, who is behind it or whether the two incidents are linked.

## What it means for you

This is a good reminder that "self-custody" protects your keys, not your attention: attacks increasingly try to trick you into signing something. Only approve transactions you started yourself, check amounts and addresses before signing, and never share a recovery phrase.
