Roughly $6.5–7 billion of bitcoin options expire on Deribit at 08:00 UTC on Friday, November 27 — the monthly expiry, falling a day after Thanksgiving and an hour before US spot bitcoin ETFs start trading again. Bitcoin held near $61,000 on Thursday in thin holiday trading, with Wall Street closed.

## What happened

- **Size.** About 112,000 BTC of contracts expire, worth $6.5–7 billion in notional value, plus about $600 million of ether options (CryptoTicker, CoinInsider, crypto.news). Notional is the value of the underlying coins, not money that has to change hands.
- **Bets lean defensive.** Deribit's Jean-David Pequignot told CoinDesk that puts cluster between $63,000 and $75,000, with notable size at $60,000, $58,000, $50,000 and $40,000. The put/call ratio for Friday is about 0.89, up from 0.55 in late October.
- **Max pain far above the price.** The "max pain" level — where the most options expire worthless — was about $73,000 on November 21, well above where bitcoin trades, so most call options are set to expire with no value.
- **ETFs reopen into it.** Spot bitcoin ETFs lost about $570 million on November 23 and $232 million on November 24 (SoSoValue), part of what is heading for their worst month on record. US markets close early on Friday, at 18:00 UTC.

## What it means for regular buyers

Big expiries can bring sharp moves in thin trading, but they don't decide the trend, and "max pain" is not a price forecast.
