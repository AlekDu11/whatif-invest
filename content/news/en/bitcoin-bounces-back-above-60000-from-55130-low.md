Bitcoin clawed back to $60,000 on Saturday, November 21, hours after sliding to $55,130, its weakest level since September 2024. The rebound of about 9% came after a wave of forced selling cleared out a large pile of leveraged bets.

## What happened

- **Low and rebound.** Bitcoin bottomed at about $55,130 early on Saturday and traded near $60,065 at 17:50 UTC, per CoinDesk.
- **Leverage flushed out.** About $1.8 billion of leveraged positions were liquidated in 24 hours, $1.3 billion of them bets on higher prices, per CoinGlass. Short sellers then got squeezed: more than $227 million of shorts were closed in four hours.
- **ETF money still leaving.** US spot bitcoin ETFs lost roughly $1.1–1.25 billion over the past week, including $582 million on Thursday.
- **Same macro worries.** The Nasdaq fell more than 4% on Friday and about 10% for the week on AI-spending doubts, a hot October inflation print and talk of further Fed hikes. CryptoQuant described the drop as extreme capitulation; QCP Capital said a lasting recovery needs calmer tech stocks and returning institutional money.
