HP Inc, the PC and printer maker, was one of the few gainers in the S&P 500 on Wednesday, November 25: its shares rose about 11% after results that beat forecasts late on Tuesday. The jump came on a day when chip and AI stocks fell hard.

## What happened

- **Results (after 21:00 UTC on November 24).** Fiscal Q4 revenue rose 9.3% to $16.0 billion against about $15.3 billion expected; adjusted earnings were $0.83 a share versus roughly $0.74 and above HP's own $0.69–0.79 range.
- **Outlook.** For fiscal 2027 HP guided adjusted earnings of $3.15–3.45 a share (midpoint $3.30, close to the $3.28 analysts expected) and $2.9–3.1 billion of free cash flow; the dividend rises to $0.31 a share.
- **Trading (about 17:30 UTC).** Shares were up close to 11%, while the Nasdaq was down more than 2% and Dell lost about 5% despite its own beat.

## Why it moved

PC sales were the driver: Personal Systems revenue rose 14% to $11.8 billion, helped by business customers (+23%), while printing slipped 1%. Part of the beat was one-off — both profit measures include a $0.15 a share tariff-refund benefit, which Bernstein called a "low-quality" beat. UBS called the 2027 outlook prudent but sees no near-term catalyst, and HP still flags rising memory and storage costs as a risk. The company is run by an interim CEO; a new chief is expected in early 2027.
