CrowdStrike agreed on Monday, November 23, to buy rival Zscaler in an all-stock deal valued at about $43.9 billion. Zscaler shareholders get 1.135 CrowdStrike shares for each share, worth about $260.41 at Friday's prices — roughly 34% more than Zscaler's last close.

## What happened

- **The terms.** After the deal, CrowdStrike holders would own about 84.1% of the combined company and Zscaler holders about 15.9%. Zscaler founder and CEO Jay Chaudhry, whose group holds about 34% of the shares, agreed to vote in favour and will join CrowdStrike's board.
- **Why.** CrowdStrike protects laptops and cloud servers; Zscaler secures network traffic. Together they expect more than $10 billion of annual recurring revenue, about $1 billion of extra sales within 30 months and $500 million of yearly cost savings.
- **When.** Closing is planned for the second half of CrowdStrike's fiscal 2028 and needs Zscaler shareholder and regulatory approval. Both companies also reported quarterly results on Monday, with a joint call at 13:00 UTC.
- **Context.** Calcalist called it one of the largest deals in the industry's history. It lands as tech stocks rebound in premarket trading after last week's AI sell-off.
