SoftBank Group, one of the biggest investors in the AI boom and a major backer of OpenAI, fell 16.1% in Tokyo on Wednesday, November 25. Asian chip and AI stocks gave back the jump they made after Nvidia's record Monday, and SoftBank, as the most leveraged AI bet in Japan, fell the hardest.

## What happened

- **Tokyo session (00:00–06:30 UTC).** SoftBank Group closed down 16.1%; memory maker Kioxia lost 15.4% and the Nikkei 225 fell 3.6% to 56,502.97.
- **Korea and Taiwan.** The Kospi dropped 5.6% to 4,725.84, with Samsung Electronics down 9.1% and SK Hynix 8.6%. Taiwan's Taiex fell more than 6% and TSMC 8.1%.
- **Wall Street the night before.** Nvidia fell 5.9% on November 24, a day after a 31.5% jump. The Nasdaq lost 1.5% and the S&P 500 0.5%.

## Why it moved

We found no SoftBank-specific news behind the drop. Reports describe it as the reverse of Tuesday's rally: the boost from Jensen Huang's return as Nvidia CEO lasted about one day, and investors went back to doubting whether AI spending will earn enough to justify the data centers being built. Analysts quoted by the Seoul Economic Daily pointed to overinvestment and "circular financing" between AI companies. High rates (the US 10-year yield was 4.93%) and talk of tighter AI rules after the US midterms added to the pressure. SoftBank's large stakes in AI make it move more than the market in both directions.
