Nvidia's first report without Jensen Huang came in well above expectations. Revenue for the quarter that ended October 25 reached $115.8 billion, more than double a year earlier, and the company forecast about $130 billion for the current quarter. The board also closed the door on Huang's comeback, and the stock rose about 3–4% in after-hours trading.

## What happened

- **A clear beat.** Revenue of $115.8 billion was up 103% year on year and 20% on the previous quarter; Wall Street expected about $109 billion. Data center sales, the AI chip business, were $107.6 billion. Adjusted earnings were $2.68 per share against roughly $2.47 expected.
- **A higher outlook.** Guidance of $130 billion (±3%) is above the roughly $125 billion analysts had pencilled in, and assumes no data center revenue from China.
- **More cash to shareholders.** Nvidia returned $28.1 billion in the quarter and added $150 billion to its buyback programme.
- **Leadership.** Interim CEO Colette Kress led the call. Board chair Harvey Jones said the board does not intend to reinstate Huang, who resigned on November 12, and will take its time finding a permanent CEO.
