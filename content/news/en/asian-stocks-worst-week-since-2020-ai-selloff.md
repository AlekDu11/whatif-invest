The AI sell-off moved to Asia on Friday, November 20. Japan's Nikkei 225 and South Korea's Kospi both dropped about 4.5% in early trading and Taiwan's main index fell close to 6%, putting the region's stock markets on course for their worst week since March 2020.

## What happened

- **Big moves across the region.** The Nikkei fell to about 56,775 and the Kospi to about 4,905; Hong Kong's Hang Seng lost nearly 2% and Shanghai about 1.7%. MSCI's broad Asia-Pacific index was down 2.5%, heading for a weekly fall of about 7%.
- **Chip makers led the drop.** Names tied to AI hardware, such as SK Hynix, Samsung Electronics, TSMC, Advantest and SoftBank Group, were among the hardest hit.
- **Wall Street set the tone.** On Thursday the S&P 500 fell 2% and the Nasdaq 2.3%. Nvidia lost 3.3% after being up 4.7% early, and Anthropic closed its first day at $97.17, well below its $111.50 opening trade.
- **Extra pressure.** The US announced new licence rules aimed at Chinese AI developers renting computing power in other countries, and the 10-year Treasury yield stayed above 5%.
