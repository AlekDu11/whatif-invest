Lighter, a decentralized exchange for perpetual futures, paused all withdrawals on Monday after an attacker used one trader's leaked API key to move about $10.4 million in USDC out of its Ethereum contract. The team says its protocol and smart contracts were not compromised.

## What happened

- **The size.** On-chain investigators traced about $10.4 million in USDC. Some researchers put the loss as high as $15.5 million across two market-maker accounts; Lighter has not confirmed that figure.
- **Where the money went.** The USDC was swapped for ether within minutes. About 1,920 ETH (around $3.5 million) then went through 12 wallets to Hyperliquid, where it was used to buy a Monero-linked token.
- **The response.** Lighter switched on its Guardian mode, flagged a wallet it believes the attacker controls and prepared a contract upgrade. It aimed to reopen withdrawals on Monday; that had not been confirmed when we published. Its LIT token fell more than 10% to under $2.48, then recovered to about $2.62.
- **Not the only incident.** The same day, a single Gnosis Safe multisig wallet lost about $6.45 million through permissions set up in one of its add-on modules, according to PeckShield. Part of that money was also swapped into Monero.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Neither case was a flaw in Ethereum itself: both came from keys or permissions that gave someone too much access. For a long-term buyer the lesson is about storage. Money sitting on a trading venue can be frozen while an incident is sorted out, which is why many regular buyers move what they accumulate to a wallet they control. Ether trades at {{price:eth}}.
