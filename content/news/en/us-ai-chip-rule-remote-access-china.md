The US Commerce Department on Monday closed a loophole that let Chinese companies rent AI computing power in data centers abroad. The interim rule arrived days after Jensen Huang stepped down as Nvidia's CEO, on a day when the Philadelphia semiconductor index was down about 3.7% by midday.

## What happened

- **Remote access now needs a license.** Exporters need US permission to ship AI chips and servers to any data center in the world that may sell computing to companies in China or other restricted countries. It applies to sites above 12 million "total processing performance", roughly the power of 750 Nvidia H100 or H200 chips, and also covers access through brokers.
- **Five more countries.** Advanced chip exports to Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia, Thailand and Brazil now need a license, with a carve-out for chips going to US-headquartered companies.
- **Timeline.** The rule takes effect once it is published in the Federal Register. Data centers already serving such clients get 90 days to obtain a license. It also formally replaces the January 2025 AI Diffusion Rule.
- **The Nvidia link.** Reports tie the move to Megaspeed, a Singapore company suspected of channeling Nvidia chips to China (it denies this), and to the scandal behind Huang's resignation.
