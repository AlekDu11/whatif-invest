Federal Reserve Chair Kevin Warsh used a speech on Monday to lay out the best and worst cases for artificial intelligence. AI could push US growth well above its usual pace, he said, but if the huge spending on data centers fails to pay off, stock prices could fall and unemployment could rise. He said nothing about the next rate move.

## What happened

- **Where.** Warsh opened a one-day Fed conference on AI, productivity and jobs at the San Francisco Fed branch.
- **The upside.** He repeated his long-held view that AI can make the economy more efficient, allowing faster growth without extra inflation, and said it "could supercharge idea generation".
- **The downside.** He named widespread job losses and a sharp fall in stock prices as the risks if the AI investment wave disappoints.
- **What he left out.** He did not mention inflation, although other Fed officials link the data-center boom to higher prices for chips, electricity and copper. Markets still fully price a December rate hike after hotter inflation data.

## Why it matters for AI stocks

The speech landed on a weak day for the AI trade: at midday the Nasdaq was down about 1.7% and Nvidia about 2%, a day before its quarterly results. Nvidia trades at {{price:nvda}} and bitcoin at {{price:btc}}, about 49% below its $126,080 record. Both have been moving with rate expectations.
