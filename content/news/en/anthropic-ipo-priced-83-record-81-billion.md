Anthropic, the company behind the Claude AI models, set the final price of its initial public offering at $83 a share on the evening of November 18 — above the $70–78 range it had marketed. The deal raises about $81 billion, the largest US IPO on record, and shares start trading on Nasdaq under the ticker ANT on Thursday, November 19.

## What happened

- **Bigger and pricier.** The offering grew to about 980 million shares (roughly 956 million new shares plus about 23.6 million sold by existing holders), up from the 825 million planned earlier.
- **A new record.** At about $81 billion, the raise tops SpaceX's roughly $75 billion listing in June. The price values Anthropic at about $1.5 trillion.
- **Still loss-making.** The company reported $16.3 billion of revenue and an $80.7 billion net loss in the first half of 2026, carries $6.2 billion of debt and has committed to roughly $900 billion of computing spending over the coming years.
- **Index funds may follow.** A Nasdaq rule change in May lets some very large new listings join the Nasdaq-100 quickly, which could bring the stock into many index funds.

## What it means for regular investors

The IPO price is what large institutions paid; ordinary buyers will pay whatever the market sets once trading opens, and first days of mega-listings are often volatile. A new stock has no history to test a regular-buying plan on.
