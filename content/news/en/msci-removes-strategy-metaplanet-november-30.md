MSCI removes Strategy (MSTR), the largest corporate holder of bitcoin, and Japan's Metaplanet from its stock indexes at the close of Monday, November 30. Funds that track those indexes must sell their shares by then — Strategy itself estimates about 13 million MSTR shares, roughly 3.1% of its basic shares.

## What happened

- **The decision.** In its November 11 review, MSCI applied a new screen for "non-operating" companies for the first time: a firm whose operating assets are under half of total assets, and which fails most of five financial tests, is no longer eligible. Five companies were cut, including Strategy, Metaplanet, Yellow Cake and Upexi (Bitcoin Magazine, The Block).
- **The scale.** Strategy is more than 90% of the removed market value. The 13 million shares MSCI-linked funds hold equal about 60% of MSTR's 30-day average daily volume (BitcoinTreasuries).
- **Strategy's view.** The company called the screen "pretextual" and aimed at bitcoin treasury firms, but says the exit has no meaningful impact on its business (Motley Fool).
- **The price.** MSTR closed at $122.35 on November 11 and traded near $81.60 on November 27, about 33% lower, while bitcoin also fell hard. Other index providers, including S&P Dow Jones, have not adopted MSCI's rule.

## What it means for holders

Index removals are usually known weeks ahead, so much of the selling may already be priced in, but the last trading day can bring a volume spike. Strategy owns about 850,000 BTC, so MSTR still moves mostly with bitcoin.
