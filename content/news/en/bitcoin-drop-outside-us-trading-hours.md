An hour-by-hour look at bitcoin's slide shows a striking pattern: almost all of the losses happened while US stock exchanges were closed. From October 6 to November 16 bitcoin fell about 24% on Coinbase, yet during regular US trading hours it actually gained 1.6%, according to an analysis by TECHi.

## What happened

- **The split.** Bitcoin went from about $85,600 on October 6 to about $65,000 on November 16 (4 p.m. ET). Between 10 a.m. and 4 p.m. ET on trading days it rose 1.6%; in all other hours combined it fell 25.3%.
- **Early mornings hurt most.** The 4–10 a.m. ET window, just 18% of the hours, produced about 40% of the drop. The low of the slide, $61,946, came on Saturday evening, November 14.
- **The ETF shows the same thing.** BlackRock's IBIT lost 27% in overnight and weekend gaps over those 29 sessions but gained 3.9% during regular sessions — its worst overnight stretch since it launched in January 2024.
- **Tighter link to tech.** The 30-day correlation between bitcoin and the Nasdaq-100 fund QQQ rose to 0.68, from 0.30 two months earlier. Bitcoin held near $64,000 on Tuesday as tech stocks slid before Nvidia's earnings.
