A wallet linked to the October 29 Binance hack, the largest crypto theft on record at about $6.3 billion, came back to life this week. On November 17 it sent about 34,280 ETH, worth roughly $63 million, to three newly created addresses after sitting idle since October 30.

## What happened

- **Fresh wallets, no exit yet.** At the time of reporting the funds had not reached an exchange or a mixing service, and there was no further movement.
- **Who is behind it.** The FBI has attributed the attack to TraderTraitor, a hacking group linked to North Korea.
- **Part of the loot is frozen.** The US Justice Department announced court-approved seizures and restraints of more than $1.2 billion of the stolen crypto last week. Blockchain analytics firm Elliptic counts about $2.85 billion frozen or seized in total, leaving roughly $3.28 billion spread across 950+ wallets.
- **Users were made whole.** Binance has said it replaced the stolen $6.3 billion from its own funds.

## What it means for ordinary holders

Large movements of stolen coins can add selling pressure if they are eventually cashed out, though this transfer was small next to daily trading volume.
