Bitcoin fell below $58,000 on Wednesday, November 25, dropping more than 4% to about $57,300 before recovering to around $59,400, The Block reported. The slide came with hot US inflation data and a fresh sell-off in tech stocks, and it pushed the value of the whole crypto market to its lowest since September 2024.

## What happened

- **The drop.** Bitcoin traded near $58,650 at 11:50 UTC, slid under $58,000 after the US PCE release at 13:30 UTC and touched about $57,300 before bouncing.
- **Liquidations.** About $515 million of leveraged positions were closed in 24 hours (CoinGlass); total crypto market value fell to about $2.08 trillion.
- **Altcoins.** Ether was near $1,650 (−2.6%), Solana near $68 (−3%) and Hyperliquid's HYPE about 8% lower.
- **Macro.** PCE inflation rose to 3.8% a year in October; markets price another 25-basis-point Fed hike, and the US proposed new tariffs on 14 economies.

## What this means for regular buyers

CoinShares research head James Butterfill said he thinks the market is "close to the tail end of the bear market", while expecting more swings and a $60,000–90,000 range. Bitget flags $55,000 as support, and 21Shares warns that a break below the realized price near $53,000 could open the $40,000s. Nobody knows which view is right.
