A wrapped version of Monero traded on Hyperliquid, known as XMR1, lost 99.8% of its value in about half an hour on Friday, November 20, after tokens from its deployer account were dumped on the market. The coin's operator blames a hack at its hosting provider. Native Monero (XMR) was not affected.

## What happened

- **Key compromised.** About 5.2 million XMR1 moved from the deployer account to a fresh wallet in three transfers, blockchain analytics firm Bitquery found. How the private key was obtained has not been established.
- **A fast dump.** The wallet sold 369,623 XMR1 to 420 buyers for about $13.5 million, three-quarters of it in the first 11 minutes. The price fell from roughly $500 to $1.
- **Money on the move.** About $13.3 million in USDC went from Hyperliquid to Arbitrum, was swapped for about 7,467 ETH via CoW Protocol and mostly bridged to Ethereum.
- **Operator response.** Wagyu's pseudonymous operator PerpetualCow linked the breach to an alleged attack on the hosting firm 1984, promised to reimburse affected traders personally and said Wagyu will return later. No timeline has been given.

## What it means for ordinary investors

A wrapped token is only as safe as whoever holds its keys: XMR1 depended on one operator's account, while XMR itself kept trading normally. Bitquery also found about $6.6 million of earlier XMR1 purchases paid for with funds from November hacks, though it saw no link to this attack.
