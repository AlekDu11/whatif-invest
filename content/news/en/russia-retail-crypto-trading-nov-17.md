Russia's new crypto rules take effect on Tuesday, November 17. For the first time, ordinary (non-qualified) investors will be allowed to buy bitcoin, ether and Tether's USDT through regulated Russian intermediaries — but only up to 300,000 rubles, roughly $3,700, per year with each one.

## What happened

- **Three coins, one cap.** The Bank of Russia picked assets with a large market value, high daily trading and at least five years of price history. Qualified investors get a wider list and no annual limit.
- **A test first.** Every buyer, qualified or not, has to pass a knowledge test and confirm they understand the risks before trading.
- **Exchanges step in.** Trading organisers can admit the three coins from November 17. Moscow Exchange plans a gradual pilot from December 1, and Sberbank is aiming for the same date.
- **Limits remain.** Paying for goods and services in crypto stays banned inside Russia, and peer-to-peer deals between individuals are due to end on July 1, 2027.
