The Bank of Korea raised its base rate by 25 basis points to 3.25% on Thursday, November 26, its highest level since November 2024. It is the third hike this year after July and August, following a pause in October, and 32 of 34 economists polled by Reuters had expected it.

## What happened

- **The vote.** The seven-member board decided 5–2; two members wanted to hold at 3.00%.
- **Faster growth.** The bank now sees the economy growing 3.9% in 2026 and 4.1% in 2027 (up from 2.9%), the fastest in six years, driven by booming semiconductor exports.
- **Sticky prices.** It nudged its 2027 inflation forecast up to 2.4%; Governor Shin Hyun-song said inflation may not return to the 2% target until 2028 and called the move preemptive. A heated housing market also weighed.
- **What's next.** The statement now refers only to the "timing" of further hikes; board members' six-month views ranged from 3.25% to 3.75%. Kospi was up about 1.9% in early trading, after a 16% slide so far this month.
