NetEase, China's second-largest video-game company (Where Winds Meet, Marvel Rivals, Eggy Party), had its worst day in Hong Kong in about three years. The quarter was profitable, but its games business — almost 82% of revenue — stopped growing.

## What happened

- **Thursday, November 19, after the Hong Kong close.** NetEase reported results for July–September. Net revenue fell 1.8% year on year to RMB 27.9 billion, about 8% below the roughly RMB 30.2–30.4 billion analysts expected.
- **Games slowed.** Revenue from games and related services fell 1.7% to RMB 22.9 billion and about 8% from the previous quarter. In Q2 online games had still grown about 10%.
- **Profit missed too.** Adjusted earnings came in near RMB 14.2–14.3 per ADS against forecasts of about RMB 16.4. Net income fell 2.5% to RMB 8.4 billion, partly because investment gains shrank.
- **Friday, November 20 — the sell-off.** The Hong Kong shares (9999) dropped as much as 19% to about HK$151 and closed near HK$152, down roughly 18.7% from HK$186.9. It was their worst session since late 2023, on a day the Hang Seng index lost about 2%.

## Why it moved

Investors pay up for NetEase as a growth story, and this quarter broke it: momentum in its two big recent hits, Where Winds Meet and Marvel Rivals, faded, and there is no major new game to fill the gap yet. CEO William Ding said the company is "carefully preparing" new titles across genres and markets, but gave no near-term dates. Upcoming games such as Ananta and an international launch of Sea of Oblivion are seen as the next catalysts. JPMorgan kept its Overweight rating (target HK$280) but warned 2026 forecasts may be cut and the stock may trade sideways until launch dates are clear; Citi kept a Buy.

On the plus side, margins improved, the company holds about RMB 175 billion in net cash and keeps paying dividends and buying back shares.
