Gold edged lower in Asian trading on Monday, with spot prices down 0.3% at $3,867 an ounce by 04:59 GMT. A strong dollar and growing certainty that the Federal Reserve will raise rates next month are weighing on metals ahead of a speech by Fed Chair Kevin Warsh.

## What happened

- **The dollar is near a 19-month high.** It is holding close to its strongest level since April 2025, which makes gold pricier for buyers paying in other currencies.
- **Markets expect a hike.** CME FedWatch shows about a 90% chance of a quarter-point rate rise in December and roughly 10% for a half-point move.
- **Fed officials sound hawkish.** Kansas City Fed President Jeff Schmid said inflation is too hot. Warsh speaks later on Monday, and minutes of the October meeting are out on Wednesday.
- **Silver fell harder.** Spot silver lost 2.8% to $47.38, while platinum and palladium also dropped.

## What it means for regular buyers

Higher rates make assets that pay no interest, like gold, less attractive in the short run, which is why rate expectations move the metal day to day.
