IREN shares jumped about 29% on Monday, November 23, one trading day after closing at a fresh 52-week low. The bitcoin miner turned AI cloud provider rose with the whole group of GPU-rental stocks after Nvidia reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO.

## What happened

- **Friday, November 20:** IREN fell almost 18% to close at $11.82, after touching a 52-week low of $11.75 — about 83% below its 52-week high of $70.71.
- **Monday, around 15:10 UTC:** the stock traded near $15.30, up about 29%, on more than 45 million shares.
- **The peer group:** CoreWeave and Nebius rose as much as 30%; miners MARA and Hut 8 gained more than 15%.

## Why it moved

The move was about AI, not bitcoin: bitcoin itself rose only about 2% on the day. IREN rents out Nvidia GPU capacity to AI customers, so it trades with Nvidia and the other neoclouds. Huang's return eased one of the worries behind last week's slide; Bernstein stayed positive, pointing to IREN's contracted revenue backlog. The other worries have not gone away: neoclouds borrow heavily to build data centres and buy chips, and investors are asking whether that debt load is sustainable.
