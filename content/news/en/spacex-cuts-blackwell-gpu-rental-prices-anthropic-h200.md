The price of renting Nvidia's AI chips is falling. According to research firm SemiAnalysis, SpaceX has cut what it charges for small clusters of Blackwell GPUs by 20–35%, and it is offering about 50,000 older H200 chips that Anthropic chose not to keep at a steep discount.

## What happened

- **Blackwell rents cut.** SpaceX lowered the rate for small Nvidia Blackwell clusters from about $15–15.50 to $10–12 per GPU-hour, close to what giant customers such as Anthropic and Google pay.
- **Anthropic hands back H200s.** Anthropic will not renew a lease on roughly 50,000 H200 GPUs from SpaceX. SpaceX is re-offering that capacity for early-2027 delivery at about $5 per GPU-hour, around 23% below Monday's H200 market price of $6.53 on the Ornn index.
- **The wider slide.** Ornn data show B200 rents down 8.8% from their November 8 peak to $9.07 an hour, H200 down 16% since November 11 and B300 down 24% since mid-October.
- **Credit is tightening.** Goldman Sachs' Sean Johnstone said CoreWeave bonds hit record lows, and HPE's lending arm said it will not finance capital-hungry AI cloud start-ups. SemiAnalysis still sees new Blackwell projects as profitable.

## Why it matters for AI stocks

Falling rents squeeze "neoclouds" such as CoreWeave and Nebius, which borrowed heavily to buy chips and earn money by renting them out. Cheaper rents can also mean more chips than demand, the key worry for Nvidia's next orders. Over the past week CoreWeave and Nebius fell about 11% and 20%, per our data.
