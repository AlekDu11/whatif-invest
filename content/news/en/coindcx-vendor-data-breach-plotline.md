India's CoinDCX told users on Tuesday that some of their personal data was exposed through a hack at Plotline, an outside company that sends the exchange's in-app messages and notifications. The exchange says its trading systems, wallets and customer funds were not affected.

## What happened

- **Two intrusions.** Attackers got into Plotline's platform on October 24–26 and again on October 30–31, 2026. Plotline informed CoinDCX on October 31; the customer notice went out on X on November 17.
- **What leaked.** For a "small number" of customers: names, email addresses and the names of their linked banks. Bank account numbers, passwords, private keys and balances were not listed as exposed.
- **Login alerts still unexplained.** One user posted new-login warnings received on November 16; no link to the Plotline incident has been established.
- **Not the first incident.** In July 2025 CoinDCX lost about $44 million from an internal account in a server breach and covered it from its own reserves.

## What it means for you

A leak of names, emails and bank names is mainly a phishing risk: expect convincing fake messages "from your exchange" or "from your bank". A real exchange will never ask for your password, 2FA code or seed phrase. Use an authenticator app instead of SMS where you can, and turn on withdrawal-address whitelisting. Supply-chain attacks like this one, and the recent [Optimizely address-swap hack]({lang}/news/optimizely-hack-crypto-address-swap/), hit platforms through their vendors, so it also helps to keep on an exchange only what you actively trade.
