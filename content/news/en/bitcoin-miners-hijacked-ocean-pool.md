Attackers took control of roughly 64,000 bitcoin mining machines over the weekend and pointed their computing power at the OCEAN mining pool, with payouts set to an address they control. The attack appears to be over, and the hackers did not manage to mine a single block.

## What happened

- **A big but limited hijack.** About 13 EH/s of hashrate was redirected. OCEAN's own dashboard briefly jumped toward 50 EH/s before settling near 40 EH/s.
- **When it started.** The switch is believed to have begun around 8 p.m. US Central Time on November 14. Hosting provider CoreX was the first to raise the alarm.
- **How it may have happened.** One operator pointed to Luxor's Commander mining-management software as a possible entry point; this has not been confirmed. Some miners say unauthorized pool switches started days earlier.
- **The damage.** CoreX later said the hijacked hashrate fell to about 100 PH/s. No blocks were found, so the attackers earned nothing, but affected miners lost the revenue for that window.

## What it means for bitcoin holders

This was an attack on miners' equipment and management software, not on the bitcoin network or anyone's coins.
