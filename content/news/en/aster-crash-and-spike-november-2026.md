Aster, the token of the perpetual-futures exchange backed by Binance founder CZ, has had two violent days in a row. It first lost almost a quarter of its value, then shot up and fell back again within minutes.

## What happened

- **Saturday, November 14 — the crash.** ASTER fell about 22–24% in 24 hours, down to roughly $0.49–0.50 on Binance. It was the biggest loser among the top ~90 coins: 81 of the 89 largest cryptocurrencies were down that day, per TokenPost. Injective, Uniswap and Bittensor each lost 15–16%.
- **Leverage made it worse.** As the price slid, traders betting on a rise were forced out: CoinGlass counted about $7.8 million of ASTER positions liquidated, almost all of them longs. Across the whole crypto market, liquidations topped $1.9 billion in a day.
- **Sunday, November 15 — the spike.** Around 21:00 UTC ASTER jumped about 18.6% in one hour to $0.65 on Binance spot, briefly touching about $0.71. This time short sellers were squeezed. Within roughly 30 minutes the whole move was gone and the price was back near $0.50.

## Why it moved

We found no Aster-specific news behind either move. The crash followed the wider market: bitcoin's drop, ETF outflows and Fed rate worries. The Sunday spike looks like a squeeze in thin weekend trading, where a few large orders and forced liquidations can move the price fast in both directions.

Two background factors add pressure. A Stage 6 airdrop claim window (November 4 to December 4) is releasing about 63 million new ASTER, roughly 2% of the circulating supply. On the other side, Aster uses platform fees to buy back and burn tokens; it burned about 2.6 million ASTER in early November.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Moves like these are exactly why we don't recommend chasing a spike: someone who bought at $0.71 on Sunday was down about 30% half an hour later.
