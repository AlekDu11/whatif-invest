Telus, one of Canada's three big telecom companies, had its best day in years on Monday. A smaller rival, Québecor, wants to buy it, and Telus made the approach public. The board is not convinced, and the market clearly isn't either.

## What happened

- **Monday, November 16.** Telus confirmed it had received an unsolicited, non-binding takeover proposal from Québecor (the owner of Vidéotron and Freedom Mobile). The first version arrived on June 27, an amended one on November 9.
- **The offer.** C$6.35 in cash plus 0.17 of a Québecor Class B share for each Telus share — about C$19.45 in total when announced, roughly a 60% premium to Friday's Toronto close.
- **The stock reaction.** Telus briefly jumped about 29% early in the session, then gave back more than half of the gain. On the Toronto Stock Exchange it closed up about 13%, near C$13.72; the New York-listed shares (TU) traded about 10–11% higher at around $9.40. Volume was well above average.
- **Québecor fell about 13%**, to roughly C$67. Because part of the offer is paid in Québecor shares, that drop alone cut the offer's value to about C$17.75 per Telus share.

## Why it moved

The jump is a straightforward takeover premium: someone has put a price on Telus far above where it traded. The reason it stopped at +13% rather than +60% is that investors doubt the deal will happen.

Telus' board formed a special committee of independent directors (advised by TD Securities and Stikeman Elliott) and said it is not satisfied the price reflects Telus' value. It also flagged three big problems:

- **Regulation.** Québecor has not explained how a merger of a big-three carrier with the fourth national player would get past Canada's telecom and competition rules, which have pushed for more wireless competition, not less.
- **Control.** Telus shareholders would supply most of the combined company's value but get a minority of the votes; Québecor's controlling shareholder would control the board.
- **Debt.** The combined company would be more leveraged at a time of higher interest rates.

The background matters too. Before Monday, Telus shares had fallen about 40% in a year. In October it cut its quarterly dividend by 55% to pay down debt, and on November 6 it reported a C$660 million quarterly net loss, mostly from an C$800 million write-down at Telus Health, plus a plan to save about C$900 million a year by 2028. A beaten-down price is what made the company a target.

## What it means if you buy regularly

A takeover approach can lift a stock in one day by more than years of patient buying — but this one is non-binding, the board is pushing back, and the gap between the offer and the price shows how uncertain it is. If the talks fail, much of Monday's gain could disappear.
