**Warren Buffett** is the best-known investor of our time. Born in Omaha in 1930, he took control of a struggling textile company, Berkshire Hathaway, in 1965 and turned it into one of the largest companies in the world: it owns insurers, the BNSF railroad, energy companies and big stakes in listed firms. > **Key takeaways**
> - From 1965 to 2024 Berkshire's share price grew about 19.9% a year on average, almost double the S&P 500 with dividends (about 10.4% a year), according to Buffett's 2024 letter.
> - He buys good businesses at sensible prices and holds them for years — often decades.
> - Buffett handed the CEO job to Greg Abel at the start of 2026 and stayed on as chairman.
> - He has said that for most people a low-cost S&P 500 index fund is the best choice.

## How he invests

Buffett looks for companies that are easy to understand, earn money year after year and have a lasting edge over rivals — a strong brand, low costs or a network that is hard to copy. He calls this edge a "moat". He tries to buy when the price is below what the business is really worth, and he does not mind waiting: "our favourite holding period is forever". His long-time partner Charlie Munger, who died in November 2023, pushed him towards paying a fair price for great companies instead of a cheap price for mediocre ones.

## What he owns and why it matters

Berkshire's biggest stock holdings have long included [Apple]({lang}/aapl/), American Express, Bank of America, Coca-Cola and Chevron, but the list changes: in 2024 Berkshire sold a large part of its Apple shares and built up a record cash pile. Because the 13F shows only US-listed stocks, it misses Berkshire's own companies and its stakes in Japanese trading houses. You can follow the portfolio on this page with one click.

## What you can learn from him

- **Think like an owner:** buy a share as if you were buying the whole business.
- **Patience beats activity:** fewer, better decisions and low costs.
- **Keep a safety margin:** do not overpay, and never borrow heavily to invest.
- **Stay within what you understand:** for years he avoided technology stocks he could not value, and he has been a sharp critic of bitcoin.

## Main limits of copying him

- 13F data arrives up to 45 days after the quarter, so you see old moves.
- Berkshire's size means it can only buy very large companies; small investors have other options.
- Past returns, even Buffett's, do not guarantee future results.
