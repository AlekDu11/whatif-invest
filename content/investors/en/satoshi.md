**Satoshi Nakamoto** is the unknown person — or group — who created [Bitcoin]({lang}/btc/). Satoshi published the bitcoin whitepaper on 31 October 2008, mined the first block on 3 January 2009 and worked on the software until 2010, then disappeared. Research into the earliest blocks suggests Satoshi mined about 1.1 million BTC, coins that have never been moved.

> **Key takeaways**
> - About 1.1 million BTC is an estimate from blockchain analysis, not a confirmed figure.
> - That would be more than 5% of the 21 million bitcoins that will ever exist — and makes Satoshi one of the largest holders on paper.
> - Nobody knows who Satoshi is; in March 2024 a UK court ruled that Craig Wright, who long claimed the title, is not Satoshi.
> - The coins have not moved for more than 15 years.

## Where the estimate comes from

In 2013 researcher Sergio Lerner noticed a pattern in many of the earliest blocks — later called the "Patoshi pattern" — which suggested they were mined by one machine. Adding up the rewards of those blocks gives roughly 1.1 million BTC. Because the coins were never spent, no one can prove who owns them, and the number stays an estimate.

## Why it matters

Satoshi's coins are often called a "sleeping giant". If they ever moved, many traders would worry about a sale, and the price could swing sharply. Many people also see Satoshi's silence as important: bitcoin has no founder who can change the rules or cash out.

## What we know about Satoshi

Satoshi wrote in fluent English, communicated only through e-mail and forum posts, and handed the project to other developers in 2010; the last widely accepted messages date from early 2011. Many people have been named as possible Satoshis, but none has been proven.
