**JasmyCoin (JASMY)** is the token of Jasmy, a Tokyo company founded in 2016 by former Sony executives, led by ex-Sony president Kunitake Ando. Its idea is "data democracy": letting people keep and sell the data their phones, cars and smart devices produce instead of handing it to big platforms for free. ## How it works

Jasmy builds tools for the Internet of Things — the world of connected devices. The **Personal Data Locker** is a personal store where your device data stays under your control; **Smart Guardian** manages and secures devices; the **Secure Knowledge Communicator** links devices to data owners. Companies that want such data are meant to ask for it and pay with JASMY, and the person who owns the data gets the payment. The token was first issued as an ERC-20 on [Ethereum]({lang}/eth/). In March 2025 it was bridged to Base, and on 19 January 2026 JasmyChain went live — a layer-2 network built on [Arbitrum]({lang}/arb/) Orbit technology, with JASMY as its gas.

## What the token is used for

JASMY is meant to be the payment unit inside Jasmy's data marketplace and the fuel for transactions on JasmyChain. The project also points to work with Japanese companies — for example a joint platform with Panasonic's technology unit announced in March 2024 — and to its status as a token approved for trading on regulated Japanese exchanges such as bitbank, BITPoint and Coincheck. In practice, most JASMY changes hands on exchanges as a speculative bet on that vision.

## Its story so far

Jasmy Corporation was founded in Tokyo in April 2016. JASMY first traded in Japan and Korea in early 2021, and on 16 February 2021 thin trading pushed it to about $4.79 — a level it never came close to again. Binance listed it in November 2021 and Coinbase in February 2022, but the token lost almost all its value in the 2022 crypto crash, falling below $0.003 by December 2022. A rally in 2024, helped by interest in Japanese crypto and "DePIN" projects that link blockchains to real devices, took it to roughly $0.03–0.05. After that it slid again through 2025 and 2026, even as JasmyChain launched.

## Alternatives

Other projects that connect blockchains with devices and data include [IOTA]({lang}/iota/), [IoTeX]({lang}/iotx/), [VeChain]({lang}/vet/) for supply chains and [Helium]({lang}/hnt/) for wireless networks. For data and AI there are also tokens such as the [Artificial Superintelligence Alliance]({lang}/fet/).

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** JASMY has lost more than 99% from its 2021 peak; drops of 80–90% have happened more than once.
- **Little real usage:** the data marketplace is still small; if adoption stays low, demand for the token rests mostly on speculation.
- **Concentration:** the largest wallets hold a meaningful share of the supply, and their sales can move the price.
- **Company dependence:** the token's future depends on one private company and its partners keeping the project going.
- **Regulation and exchanges:** rules on data and crypto can change, and listings can be reviewed or removed.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your entry price over time, so you are not betting everything on one day. With a small token like JASMY that can still mean steady losses for years if the project does not catch on — regular buying does not remove that risk. Many people keep such coins to a very small part of their crypto, money they could afford to lose. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
