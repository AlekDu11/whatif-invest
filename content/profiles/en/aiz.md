**Assurant** is an American insurance company you have probably used without knowing it: it runs many of the phone protection, trade-in and extended-warranty plans that mobile carriers, retailers and car dealers sell under their own names. It also insures homes for mortgage lenders and renters. Founded in 1892 and based in Atlanta, it has about 14,800 employees and trades on the New York Stock Exchange.

> - Q3 2026 net income was $275.2 million ($5.49 per share), up 4% on the year.

## How it makes money

Assurant has two main businesses. **Global Lifestyle** sells protection for "connected" things: insurance and repair for smartphones, upgrade and trade-in programs for mobile carriers, extended warranties for electronics and appliances, and service contracts for cars sold through dealers. Customers usually buy these from a partner brand, while Assurant handles the claims, repairs and logistics in the background. **Global Housing** sells "lender-placed" homeowners insurance — cover that a mortgage lender buys when a borrower's own policy lapses — plus renters, manufactured-home and flood insurance. Housing earns more but is exposed to hurricanes and storms; Lifestyle is growing faster.

In the third quarter of 2026 Assurant earned $275.2 million, or $5.49 per share, compared with $265.6 million a year earlier. Adjusted earnings per share excluding reportable catastrophes were $6.46, up 12%. Global Lifestyle's adjusted EBITDA (profit before interest, taxes and depreciation) grew 15% to $236.9 million; Global Housing's rose 5% to $268.3 million, after $19.7 million of catastrophe losses. The company now expects 2026 adjusted EBITDA to grow about 10% and adjusted earnings per share to grow by low double digits, both excluding catastrophes. At the end of September its holding company had $954 million of liquidity.

## The stock's story

Assurant was the US arm of the Belgian-Dutch group Fortis until it listed on the New York Stock Exchange in February 2004 and took its current name. It grew by focusing on niche insurance that partners sell for it. In 2018 it bought The Warranty Group, expanding into vehicle and electronics warranties, and in 2021 it sold its pre-need funeral insurance business to focus on Lifestyle and Housing. Keith Demmings has been CEO since 2022.

## Competitors

In phone protection and warranties, Assurant's biggest rival is privately owned Asurion; [Allstate]({lang}/all/) competes through SquareTrade, and carriers and phone makers also offer their own plans. In home insurance it competes with large property insurers such as [Chubb]({lang}/cb/), [Travelers]({lang}/trv/) and [AIG]({lang}/aig/), although lender-placed insurance is a smaller, specialised market.

## Main risks

- **Big partners:** a large share of business comes through a few mobile carriers, retailers and lenders; losing one contract can hurt.
- **Catastrophes:** hurricanes, wildfires and storms can cause large losses in Global Housing.
- **Phone cycles:** fewer upgrades or cheaper repairs reduce trade-in and protection income.
- **Regulation:** lender-placed insurance and consumer warranties are watched closely by regulators.
- **Interest rates:** investment income on the money it holds changes with rates.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Assurant is a mid-sized, profitable insurer that pays dividends and buys back shares, but its price can still fall sharply after a bad storm season or the loss of a big partner. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more shares when the price is low and fewer when it is high, without trying to time the market. Many people hold one company only as part of a wider portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
