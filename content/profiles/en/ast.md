**Astarta** is one of Ukraine's biggest farm companies: it grows wheat, corn, soybeans and sugar beet, turns beet into sugar and soybeans into oil and animal feed, and keeps a dairy herd of about 29,000 cattle. Founded in 1993 and run from Kyiv, it became the first Ukrainian company to hold an IPO on the Warsaw Stock Exchange in August 2006, so its shares trade in Polish zloty.

> **Key takeaways**
> - 2025 revenue: €472 million (−23%), with 62% earned from exports.
> - Profit fell hard: €19.9 million net in 2025, then a €14.1 million net loss in the first half of 2026.
> - No dividend for 2025 — the board chose to keep cash after paying about PLN 2.1–2.3 a share in earlier years.
> - The stock traded above PLN 60 in May 2026 and fell 19% in one day on 20 October 2026, to PLN 30.2.

## How it makes money

In 2025 farming (selling grain and oilseeds) brought €149 million, or 32% of revenue; sugar €147 million (31%); soybean processing €112 million (24%); and dairy €56 million (12%). It works roughly 200,000 hectares of land.

Sugar is the brand people know: in 2025 Astarta made 362,000 tonnes from 2.3 million tonnes of beet, about 21% of all sugar produced in Ukraine. Its soybean plant crushed 229,000 tonnes, and almost 90% of soy products are sold abroad.

## The numbers right now

2025 was a weak year. Revenue fell 23% to €472 million, EBITDA (profit before interest, tax and depreciation) fell 37% to about €100 million with a 21% margin, and net profit dropped to €19.9 million — about four times less than in 2024. Debt grew: net debt including leases reached €226 million, and net financial debt without leases was €94 million against a net cash position a year earlier.

The first half of 2026 was harder still. Revenue held at €226 million, and farming grew 27% to €78 million on bigger crop sales, but sugar fell 10% to €72 million on lower prices, soy processing fell 7% to €51 million and dairy dropped 15% to €25 million on cheaper milk. The company swung to a net loss of €14.1 million, compared with a €42.3 million profit a year earlier.

## The stock's story

Astarta has been public for 20 years. Its founder and CEO Viktor Ivanchyk and his family hold about 44% of the 25 million shares, Canada's Fairfax Financial about 30%, and the rest trades freely. For years the company paid steady dividends — PLN 2.23 a share in 2021 and roughly PLN 2.1–2.3 a share each year in 2023–2025.

In 2026 the mood turned. After trading above PLN 60 in May, the stock fell 19.14% on 20 October 2026, from PLN 37.25 to PLN 30.2 — its lowest since September 2024 — after the weak half-year report. In May the board had already recommended keeping 2025 profit in the company instead of paying a dividend.

## Competitors

On the Warsaw exchange Astarta is usually compared with other Ukrainian agroholdings such as Kernel and IMC. In sugar it competes with other Ukrainian sugar producers at home and with big exporters such as Brazil abroad. Grain prices are set globally; Astarta's edge is scale, its own processing plants and exporting most of its output itself.

## Main risks

- **War and logistics:** fields, plants and export routes are exposed to Russian attacks; Black Sea port disruptions raise transport costs.
- **Commodity prices:** cheaper sugar and milk hit profit in 2026, and Astarta cannot set world prices.
- **Debt and no dividend:** net financial debt went from a net cash position to €94 million in a year, and the dividend was skipped to preserve liquidity.
- **Currency:** results in euros, costs largely in hryvnia, shares in zloty.
- **Concentrated ownership:** two shareholders hold about three quarters of the shares, so the price can jump on low volume.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Astarta's story shows how a profitable, dividend-paying company can still swing sharply when prices, the war and the weather turn against it. Regular buying spreads your purchases across good and bad periods, and the 10-year figure above includes both the 2022 invasion and the 2026 slide. Many people keep a single stock like this as a small part of a broader portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
