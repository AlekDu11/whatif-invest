**Bitcoin SV (BSV)** is a cryptocurrency that split from Bitcoin Cash in November 2018. "SV" stands for "Satoshi Vision": its backers say it restores the original Bitcoin design and lets blocks grow as large as miners want, so the network can handle huge numbers of cheap transactions. > - Like bitcoin, it will never have more than 21 million coins; the April 2024 halving cut the block reward to 3.125 BSV.

## How it works

BSV runs on the same basic rules as bitcoin: miners use proof of work to add a new block about every 10 minutes and receive new coins as a reward, and the reward halves every 210,000 blocks. The big difference is block size. Bitcoin keeps blocks small, Bitcoin Cash raised the limit to 32 MB, and BSV went further: in February 2020 its "Genesis" upgrade removed the fixed cap, leaving it to miners. In October 2025 the BSV Association, the Swiss non-profit that supports the network, publicly released Teranode, new node software built to spread the work across many machines; in a two-week test it reported more than one million transactions per second.

## What BSV is used for

The idea is to use the blockchain not only as money but as a cheap public database: small payments, data records, tokens and app data written straight onto the chain for a tiny fee. Supporters point to the very large blocks the network has processed. Critics answer that real everyday use is still small compared with the capacity, and that most crypto users and big exchanges have stayed with bitcoin and stablecoins.

## Its story so far

In 2018 the Bitcoin Cash community split into two camps. One, around the Bitcoin ABC software, Roger Ver and Bitmain, wanted new features; the other, led by Australian computer scientist Craig Wright, the technology company nChain and online-gaming entrepreneur Calvin Ayre, wanted bigger blocks and fewer changes to the original protocol. After a bitter "hash war" between miners, the chain split on 15 November 2018 and BSV became a separate coin. In April 2019 Binance, Kraken and ShapeShift delisted BSV after Wright threatened legal action against critics who disputed his claim to be bitcoin's creator; the price fell sharply. In 2021 the network was hit by several 51% attacks, in which someone controlled most of the mining power and rewrote recent blocks. In March 2024 the UK High Court, in a case brought by the Crypto Open Patent Alliance, ruled that Wright is not Satoshi Nakamoto; the written judgment of May 2024 found he had forged documents, and in December 2024 he received a suspended prison sentence for contempt of court. The project continued under the BSV Association, which released Teranode in 2025.

## Alternatives

BSV's closest rivals are its own relatives. [Bitcoin]({lang}/btc/) is far bigger and is bought mainly as "digital gold", not for cheap payments. [Bitcoin Cash]({lang}/bch/) chose moderately larger blocks, and [eCash]({lang}/xec/) later split from Bitcoin Cash in turn. [Litecoin]({lang}/ltc/) is another old proof-of-work coin used for low-cost payments. For everyday payments in crypto, most people today use dollar stablecoins.

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** BSV is a small coin and has lost the vast majority of its peak value; sharp drops can happen again.
- **Exchange access:** several major exchanges delisted it in 2019, so fewer places trade it and liquidity is thinner than for big coins.
- **Security:** with far less mining power than bitcoin, the network has already suffered 51% attacks.
- **Reputation:** the project is still linked in many people's minds with Craig Wright and the court findings against him.
- **Adoption:** the value case depends on businesses actually using large-scale on-chain data; if that does not grow, demand may stay weak.

## What it means if you buy regularly

BSV's history since 2021 has been mostly a long decline with short rallies. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your entry price over time, so you buy more coins when the price is low — but it cannot turn a falling asset into a rising one, and it does not remove the risk of losses. With small coins like this, many people keep only a very small part of their savings that they can afford to lose. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
