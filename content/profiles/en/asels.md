**Aselsan** is Turkey's biggest defence electronics company: it builds the radars, electronic warfare kits, military radios, optics and air defence systems that the Turkish armed forces use, and it sells more of them abroad every year. By the end of September 2026 it had a record $30.7 billion of signed orders still to deliver — more than seven years of sales at the 2025 pace of about $4 billion. Its shares on Borsa Istanbul (ticker ASELS, "aselsan hisse") rose 220% in 2025.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Order backlog: a record $30.7 billion at the end of September 2026, after $14 billion of new contracts in nine months.
> - First nine months of 2026: revenue 151.5 billion lira (+28% after inflation), net profit about 26.4 billion lira (+75%).
> - In 2025 exports were 25% of deliveries (19% in 2024); Aselsan has sold to 95 countries in total.
> - The Turkish Armed Forces Foundation (TSKGV) owns 74.2% of the company, so only about a quarter trades freely.

## How it makes money

Aselsan signs long, multi-year contracts, mostly with Turkey's armed forces and defence procurement agency, and is paid as it delivers. Its main products are radars, electro-optics (night-vision and targeting sights), electronic warfare systems that jam or detect enemy signals, military communications, naval systems and, increasingly, air defence. It is one of the main companies behind **Steel Dome** (Çelik Kubbe), Turkey's layered shield against drones, aircraft and missiles; in 2025 it handed 47 Steel Dome components to the armed forces in one ceremony.

Exports are the growth story. In 2025 Aselsan signed 272 export contracts worth over $2 billion — more than double 2024 — across 58 countries, including a $410 million electronic warfare deal with Poland, a NATO member.

## The numbers right now

Turkey uses inflation accounting, so the lira figures below are restated in today's money and growth is "real", after inflation. For the first nine months of 2026, revenue was 151.5 billion lira, up 28%. Adjusted EBITDA (profit before interest, tax and depreciation) rose 38% to 40.6 billion lira, a 26.8% margin. Net profit reached about 26.4 billion lira, up 75%; the third quarter alone earned 11.2 billion lira. Research and development spending rose 36% to $1.3 billion.

For full-year 2025, sales were 180.5 billion lira (+15% real) and net profit 29.9 billion lira, about $684 million (+50%). For 2026 the company guided for revenue growth above 10% and an adjusted EBITDA margin above 24%.

## The stock's story

Aselsan was founded in Ankara in 1975, after the US arms embargo that followed Turkey's 1974 intervention in Cyprus. Fifty years later the shares had their best year ever: up 220% in 2025, ahead of Hanwha Aerospace (+188%) and Rheinmetall (+153%), as Europe rearmed and Turkey expanded its air defence. The market value passed 1 trillion lira in September 2025 and 2 trillion lira (about $44 billion) by May 2026, when it was Turkey's most valuable listed company.

Remember the currency. Turkish annual inflation was still 29.77% in October 2026, so prices and the lira's exchange rate move a lot: a gain measured in lira looks much bigger than the same gain measured in dollars.

## Competitors

At home Aselsan works alongside other Turkish defence makers such as Roketsan (missiles), Turkish Aerospace and Havelsan (software and simulation). Abroad it competes with Thales, Leonardo, Hensoldt, Rheinmetall and L3Harris for radar, electronic warfare and air defence orders.

## Main risks

- **One main customer:** most work still comes from the Turkish state, so a change in the defence budget or priorities hits Aselsan directly.
- **Currency and inflation:** the lira's fall can wipe out much of a lira-priced gain for a foreign investor.
- **Delivery pressure:** a $30.7 billion backlog only becomes profit if factories deliver on time; the company is spending heavily on new capacity.
- **Politics and export rules:** sanctions, export licences and relations with NATO allies can block deals or parts.
- **Small free float and a high price:** with 74.2% held by TSKGV, fewer shares trade, and after the 2025–2026 rally a lot of good news is already priced in.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Aselsan has grown fast, but defence stocks can fall hard when budgets, wars or politics change, and a Turkish stock adds currency risk on top. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your purchases over good and bad months without trying to time the market. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
