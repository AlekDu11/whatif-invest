**Amcor** makes the packaging most people throw away without a second look: the plastic film around cheese and coffee, pouches for pet food, bottles for drinks and shampoo, caps, blister packs for pills and sterile packs for hospitals. It sells to food, drink, healthcare and household brands, not to shoppers. In April 2025 it merged with US rival Berry Global, which made it one of the world's largest packaging companies, with about 75,000 employees and roughly 400 plants.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Sales: $23.5 billion in the year to June 2026, +57% thanks to Berry; adjusted earnings $4.02 a share (+13%).
> - Q3 2026 (July–September): sales $6.13 billion (+7%), but adjusted earnings per share slipped to $0.95 from $0.97.
> - Merger savings: about $50 million in the quarter on the way to a $650 million yearly target by the third year.
> - Net debt $14.0 billion, expected at about 3.6 times yearly EBITDA (profit before interest, tax and depreciation); quarterly dividend 66 cents.

## How it makes money

Amcor has two divisions. Global Flexible Packaging (films, pouches, wraps, about 55% of sales) earned $3.36 billion in the latest quarter, +10%. Global Rigid Packaging (bottles, containers, closures, about 45%) earned $2.77 billion, +3%. About 81% of sales come from developed markets and 19% from emerging ones; no single customer buys more than 10%. Prices follow plastic resin costs: when resin gets more expensive, Amcor passes it on to customers with a delay, so sales can jump without profit moving.

## The numbers right now

In the quarter to 30 September 2026 Amcor reported $6.13 billion in sales (+7%), with volumes up about 1% (1.5% excluding businesses it is selling or has sold). Adjusted operating profit was flat at $689 million, a margin of 11.2%. Net income fell 8% to $242 million, or $0.52 a share. Free cash flow was negative $820 million in the quarter, because rising resin prices tied up $1.13 billion in working capital. Amcor is switching to a calendar financial year, so July–December 2026 is a six-month "transition period"; it cut its outlook for it to $1.70–1.80 of adjusted earnings per share (from $1.80–1.90), blaming energy and transport costs it has not yet passed on. In 2027 it plans a new US headquarters in Miami.

## The stock's story

Amcor is an old Australian company that listed in New York in 2019 when it bought US packager Bemis. The stock peaked in June 2022 and then lost ground as volumes and earnings disappointed; it fell about 15% in 2023 and 8% in 2025. After the Berry deal the share count ballooned, and on 15 January 2026 Amcor did a 1-for-5 reverse split: every five shares became one, so the price went from roughly $8 to roughly $40 without anyone's holding changing in value. ## Competitors

Amcor itself names 3M, AptarGroup, Avery Dennison, Ball, Crown Holdings, Graphic Packaging, Huhtamaki, International Paper, O-I Glass, Silgan, Smurfit WestRock and Sonoco, plus many private firms. Paper and can makers matter most: when brands move away from plastic, they move to them.

## Main risks

- **Debt:** $14.0 billion of net debt after the Berry deal leaves less room if earnings fall.
- **Resin and energy costs:** price swings hit cash flow first (−$820 million in one quarter) and profit when pass-through lags, as the latest guidance cut showed.
- **Weak volumes:** volume growth of about 1% means growth depends on savings and prices, not on selling more.
- **Plastic rules:** bans, recycling quotas and packaging taxes in Europe and elsewhere could force costly changes.
- **Integration:** the $650 million savings target is only part delivered; a big merger can still disappoint.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Amcor is a slow-moving, dividend-heavy stock: a quarterly payout of 66 cents is about 6% a year at a share price near $42, and over the last decade most of the return came from that cash rather than from price gains. Regular buying spreads purchases over years when packaging demand is weak and strong. The flip side is a company with high debt whose growth now depends on a merger working out. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
