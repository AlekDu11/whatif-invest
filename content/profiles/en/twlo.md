**Twilio** is the invisible pipe behind many of the texts, calls and emails apps send you: the login code from your bank, the "your driver is arriving" SMS, a support call. Companies don't build phone networks — they plug into Twilio's software (APIs) and pay for every message or minute. Founded in 2008 in San Francisco, it listed on the New York Stock Exchange in June 2016.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: $1.56 billion in Q3 2026, +20% a year (+16% without carrier fees and acquisitions).
> - Now profitable on a GAAP basis: operating income $148.6 million in the quarter, free cash flow $369.6 million.
> - The stock trades far below its 2021 peak of $443 even after recovering from the 2022–2023 crash.
> - Main question for 2027: can AI voice agents keep growth above 10%?

## How it makes money

Twilio charges per use. Messaging (SMS, WhatsApp, RCS) is the core — about 59% of revenue in Q3 2026. Voice (calls, call centres, AI voice agents) grew more than 20% that quarter. Email comes from SendGrid, bought in 2019, and customer data tools from Segment, bought in 2020. Existing customers spent 14% more than a year earlier (dollar-based net expansion rate of 114%), which shows Twilio grows mostly with the clients it already has.

## The numbers right now

In the quarter to 30 September 2026 Twilio earned $1.56 billion in revenue, up 20% year on year, with a gross margin of about 49%. It ended the quarter with $1.06 billion in cash plus $1.83 billion in short-term securities against $994 million of long-term debt, and it spent $127 million buying back its own shares. For Q4 2026 it guided to $1.595–1.605 billion of revenue, and management expects at least 10% organic growth in 2027.

## The stock's story

Twilio was a pandemic star: the share price hit a record close of $443.49 in February 2021. When growth slowed and losses stayed high, it fell by more than 80% by 2023. A new CEO, Khozema Shipchandler (since January 2024), cut costs and turned the company profitable, and the stock has been recovering since — but it is still well below the peak.

## Competitors

Twilio competes with Sinch, Infobip, Bandwidth, Vonage (owned by Ericsson) and Bird (formerly MessageBird), and with cloud giants such as Amazon Web Services that sell similar building blocks. Its advantages are scale, a huge developer community and the range of channels in one place.

## Main risks

- **Carrier fees and pricing:** mobile operators keep raising fees for business SMS; Twilio passes them on, which inflates reported growth but not profit.
- **Slowing growth:** the Q4 2026 outlook implies organic growth of about 11–12%, slower than this year.
- **AI cuts both ways:** AI agents create new voice and messaging demand, but chat apps and new AI platforms could also bypass classic SMS.
- **Competition** keeps prices under pressure, especially for large customers.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Twilio is a volatile stock: it fell more than 80% from its peak once already. Regular buying spreads purchases across good and bad months, but a single company can stay down for years — many people hold a stock like this only as a small part of a broader portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
