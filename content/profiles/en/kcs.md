**KuCoin Token (KCS)** is the token of KuCoin, a large crypto exchange launched in 2017. Holders get cheaper trading fees and other perks on the exchange, and KuCoin uses part of its profit to buy KCS back and destroy it. The token is worth {{mcap:kcs}} today ({{rank:kcs}} among cryptocurrencies).

## How it works

KCS was first issued in 2017 as an ERC-20 token on Ethereum. In June 2021 KuCoin launched KCC (KuCoin Community Chain), its own Ethereum-compatible blockchain, and KCS became its native coin, used to pay network fees there. Most KCS, however, sits and trades on the KuCoin exchange itself. Each month KuCoin spends a portion of its net profit on buying KCS on the market and sends the tokens to an address no one controls, which removes them for good. For example, the 56th burn, for February 2025, destroyed 45,214 KCS, and the 66th, for December 2025, destroyed 20,240 KCS, leaving a total supply of about 142.2 million.

## What the token is used for

KCS works like a loyalty token for the exchange. Paying trading fees in KCS gives a discount of up to 20%. KuCoin has also paid holders a daily bonus from a share of trading fees, and KCS gives access to token launches and airdrops on the platform (Spotlight, GemPool, GemVote) and can be staked through KuCoin Earn. The burns aim to make each remaining token a slightly larger share of a shrinking supply. None of this is a legal claim on KuCoin's profits: the perks are set by the company and can change.

## Its story so far

KuCoin opened in September 2017 and sold KCS to early users the same year. In September 2020 hackers stole about $280 million from its hot wallets; much of it was later frozen or recovered, and the exchange covered the rest. The 2021 bull market and the KCC launch pushed KCS to a peak in December 2021. In its March 2022 whitepaper the founding team said it would permanently burn 20 million of its own KCS. In January 2025 KuCoin's operator, Peken Global, pleaded guilty in the US, paid about $297 million in fines and forfeiture and agreed to exit the US market for at least two years; in March 2026 a US court approved a CFTC settlement that permanently bars US users unless KuCoin registers.

## Alternatives

KCS is one of several exchange tokens. The biggest is [BNB]({lang}/bnb/) from Binance; others include [OKB]({lang}/okb/) from OKX, [GateToken]({lang}/gt/) from Gate, [Cronos]({lang}/cro/) from Crypto.com and [LEO]({lang}/leo/) from Bitfinex. People who want exposure to crypto in general rather than to one company often choose [Bitcoin]({lang}/btc/) or [Ethereum]({lang}/eth/).

## Main risks

- **One company:** if KuCoin loses customers, is hacked or fails, KCS can lose most of its value fast — as FTX's token did in 2022.
- **Regulation:** KuCoin is banned from serving US users and needs licences country by country; new actions can cut its business.
- **Perks can change:** fee discounts, bonuses and burns are company decisions, not guaranteed rights.
- **Concentration:** the team and the exchange hold or control a large part of the supply.
- **Big swings and thin trading:** most trading happens on KuCoin itself, so prices can move sharply.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Buying KCS regularly is, in effect, a bet on one exchange staying healthy for years. A fixed amount each month buys more when the price is low and less when it is high, but it cannot protect you if the company runs into trouble. Many people keep exchange tokens to a small part of a crypto portfolio and never hold more on an exchange than they can afford to lose. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
