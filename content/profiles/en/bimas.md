**BİM** (Birleşik Mağazalar) is Turkey's biggest discount grocery chain: small, simple shops with a short list of everyday food and household goods, many of them under its own brands, sold at low prices. It also runs the larger FİLE supermarkets and has stores in Morocco and Egypt. Its shares trade on Borsa Istanbul under the ticker BIMAS, and its sales move with Turkish inflation and how much families have left to spend.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: TL 720.5 billion in January–September 2026, +8% on the year after adjusting for inflation; net income TL 26.2 billion, +79%.
> - In the third quarter of 2026 revenue was TL 245.7 billion (+5%) and net income TL 10.4 billion (+52%).
> - At the end of September 2026 it had 14,964 BİM stores in Turkey, 378 FİLE supermarkets, 1,031 stores in Morocco and 466 in Egypt.
> - It held TL 42.7 billion of net cash and made TL 23.1 billion of free cash flow in nine months.

## How it makes money

BİM copies the hard-discount model of Germany's ALDI: it keeps the range small, buys huge volumes of each product, runs plain stores with few staff and owns every shop itself — there are no franchises. Low costs let it charge less than supermarkets and still earn a thin but steady margin: in the first nine months of 2026 its net margin was 3.6%, up from 2.2% a year earlier. Most money comes from the BİM chain in Turkey; FİLE adds bigger stores with a wider range, and the Morocco and Egypt chains are still small — in the third quarter of 2026 Morocco made TL 11 billion of revenue with a profit, while Egypt made TL 1.4 billion and was still loss-making at the EBITDA level.

## The stock's story

BİM was founded in 1995 by a group of investors around Cüneyd Zapsu and grew store by store across Turkey. In 2005 44% of the shares were sold to the public on the Istanbul stock exchange; Mustafa Latif Topbaş is the main shareholder. For investors who count in dollars, the ride has been bumpy: the stock rose about fivefold from its 2005 listing to mid-2008, then roughly halved in the 2008–2009 crisis. It lost almost half again in the 2018 lira crisis and about half once more in 2021, when the lira collapsed. After Turkey returned to tight money in 2023, the shares rose more than fourfold in dollars from the end of 2021 to July 2024, then gave part of that back in 2025.

## Competitors

BİM's closest rivals are other discount chains: A101 and ŞOK Marketler, which have also opened thousands of small stores. Above them sit supermarket chains such as Migros and CarrefourSA, plus local grocers and open-air markets. Online grocery and quick-delivery apps are a smaller but growing threat.

## Main risks

- **The lira and inflation:** profits are in Turkish lira; when the lira falls, a foreign investor's return falls with it, as in 2018 and 2021.
- **Price rules and politics:** food prices are a political issue in Turkey, and the government can step in with new rules or price checks.
- **Thin margins:** a few percent of net margin means wage hikes, rent and energy costs can quickly eat profits.
- **Weaker shoppers:** in 2026 the number of customers per store was already slipping while basket size grew with inflation.
- **Abroad:** Egypt was still losing money in 2026, and both foreign markets carry their own currency risks.

## What it means if you buy regularly

BİM sells things people buy every week, which makes its business steadier than many Turkish companies — but its share price in dollars has still halved several times in the past 20 years.
