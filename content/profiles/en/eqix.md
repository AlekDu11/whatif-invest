**Equinix** rents out space, power and cooling inside data centers — and, more importantly, the cables that connect the companies sitting in them. Banks, cloud providers and AI firms put servers in the same Equinix building to link to each other directly. It runs 280 data centers in 77 markets across 36 countries and is organised as a REIT, a real-estate company that pays out most of its income as dividends.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: $2.57 billion in Q3 2026, +11% a year (+12% in constant currency); 95% of it is recurring.
> - Record bookings: $533 million of new annual business signed in one quarter, +35% a year.
> - More than 10,500 customers and over 500,000 direct connections between them — the most in the industry.
> - Dividend: $5.16 per share a quarter, raised 10% in 2026, against $25.2 billion of debt.

## How it makes money

Most money comes from **colocation**: customers rent cages and cabinets and pay monthly for space and power — $1.83 billion, or about 71% of revenue in Q3 2026. **Interconnection** brought in $465 million (18%): a fee for every cable or virtual link between two customers, for example between a bank and Amazon Web Services.

In 2026 Equinix completed an investment in Nordic operator atNorth together with Canadian pension fund CPP Investments and launched Fabric One, a connection service with AWS and Google Cloud as lead partners.

## The numbers right now

In the quarter to 30 September 2026 Equinix earned $2.571 billion in revenue. Net income was $489 million, helped by a one-off fee linked to atNorth. AFFO — the cash-profit measure REITs use — was $11.64 per share, up 18% (about 12% without that fee).

Management raised its 2026 outlook for the third quarter in a row: revenue of $10.325–10.365 billion (about +12%) and AFFO of $43.71–44.11 per share. Growth is expensive: $5–6 billion of capital spending this year, and at the end of September it held $3.6 billion in cash and short-term investments against $25.2 billion of debt.

## The stock's story

Equinix was a steady climber for years and set a record close of $985 on 6 December 2024. The mood turned at its June 2025 investor day: management guided to only 5–9% yearly AFFO growth through 2029 because of the cost of building AI-ready capacity, and the stock lost almost 16% in a week. It kept sliding to a 52-week closing low of $726.09 on 4 December 2025.

Then demand caught up. Record bookings and a strong 2026 outlook in February lifted the shares about 36% from that low, and by mid-March 2026 they were trading around $986 — at the level of the old record.

## Competitors

The closest rival is Digital Realty, the other giant data-center REIT. Others include NTT Data and fund-backed private operators, and cloud giants also build their own. Equinix's edge is its network effect: the more companies sit in its buildings, the more valuable each connection becomes.

## Main risks

- **Debt and spending:** $25.2 billion of debt and $5–6 billion of yearly capex mean higher interest rates hurt both costs and the share price.
- **Power:** new data centers need huge amounts of electricity; grid delays can slow growth.
- **AI demand cycle:** record bookings depend on AI and cloud spending continuing; a slowdown would hit new deals.
- **Slower per-share growth:** the company's own 5–9% AFFO target is modest for a stock priced like a growth company.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Equinix behaves like a mix of real estate and tech: a growing dividend, but falls of more than 25% when growth expectations change — as between December 2024 and December 2025. Regular buying spreads your entry across such swings and the dividend adds to the return, but one stock can stay flat for years. Many people hold it as one part of a broader portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
