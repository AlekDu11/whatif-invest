**Dell Technologies** is best known for laptops and desktops, but today most of its money comes from the machines inside data centres: servers, storage and networking gear. The AI boom turned it into one of the main suppliers of the powerful servers, packed with Nvidia chips, that cloud companies and big firms use to train and run AI. Founder Michael Dell still runs the company from Round Rock, Texas.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: a record $47.0 billion in the quarter to 31 July 2026, +58% a year.
> - AI server orders of $60.9 billion in one quarter; the backlog of orders not yet delivered reached $95 billion.
> - Dell raised its forecast for the year to February 2027 to $192 billion of revenue, including $74 billion from AI servers.
> - The shares more than tripled in 2026, setting record after record.

## How it makes money

Dell has two main businesses. The **Infrastructure Solutions Group** sells to companies and cloud providers: it brought $31.8 billion in the last quarter (+89%), of which $16.4 billion came from AI-optimised servers, $10.5 billion from traditional servers and networking and $4.9 billion from storage. The **Client Solutions Group** sells PCs: $15.0 billion (+20%), almost all of it ($13.2 billion) to businesses and only $1.8 billion to consumers. Big AI servers carry thinner margins than storage or software, but the sheer volume now drives profit.

## The numbers right now

In the second quarter of fiscal 2027 (to 31 July 2026) Dell earned $5.4 billion in operating income, three times more than a year earlier, and $4.1 billion in net income, or $6.34 per share. It returned a record $4.3 billion to shareholders through buybacks and dividends; the quarterly dividend is $0.63 a share. On the other side of the balance sheet are $11.6 billion in cash against $34.5 billion of debt. For the third quarter Dell guided to about $49 billion of revenue (+81%), and for the full year to $25.50 of adjusted earnings per share, up from $17.90 it expected three months earlier.

## The stock's story

Dell's history on the stock market is unusual. The company went private in 2013, bought data-storage maker EMC in 2016 in a deal valued at $67 billion, and returned to public trading on 28 December 2018 by swapping shares that tracked its stake in VMware; it later spun VMware off. For years after that the stock was a cheap, sleepy PC-and-server name. AI changed that: by early August 2026 the shares were up more than 260% for the year, with a record close of $467.27 on 4 August after Dell was named technology provider for a $10 billion AI-factory project in Norway. After the record second quarter the shares jumped as much as 14% in a day, and in the past 52 weeks they have traded between about $110 and $595.

## Competitors

In AI and traditional servers Dell's rivals are Hewlett Packard Enterprise, Super Micro Computer and Lenovo, plus Asian contract manufacturers that build servers directly for the largest cloud companies. In storage it competes with NetApp and Pure Storage; in PCs with HP Inc., Lenovo and Apple.

## Main risks

- **The AI cycle can turn:** a large part of growth depends on a handful of AI and cloud customers; if they cut spending, orders can shrink as fast as they grew.
- **Component costs:** memory prices swing sharply, and Dell has to pass higher costs on to customers or accept lower margins.
- **Dependence on Nvidia:** AI servers are built around chips Dell does not make, and supply or pricing decisions by Nvidia affect Dell directly.
- **Thin margins and debt:** AI servers earn a small percentage on each dollar of sales, and the company carries about $34.5 billion of debt.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Dell's 2026 shows how fast a mature hardware company can be re-rated when it lands in the middle of a boom. Someone buying every day caught both the quiet years and the surge; the flip side is that a stock which tripled in a year can also fall hard if AI spending pauses. Many people keep a single stock like this as a modest slice of a broader portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
