**Coal Energy** started as a Ukrainian coal miner and listed in Warsaw in 2011. Russia's full-scale invasion in February 2022 shut down all its Ukrainian operations. Today it is a very small company: a Polish subsidiary that repairs and services underground coal mines, plus plans to open mines of its own. Revenue is a few million dollars a year, and its own reports warn that it may not be able to keep going.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue fell 45% to $1.18 million in the six months to 31 December 2025. The net loss was $1.36 million.
> - In the year to June 2025 revenue was $3.76 million. That year's $4.12 million net profit came from selling four subsidiaries, not from operations.
> - Liabilities exceed assets by $3.2 million, and the report says this "may cast significant doubts" on its ability to continue as a going concern.
> - Market value is only about PLN 76 million, and over 52 weeks the share price moved between PLN 1.58 and PLN 3.35.

## How it makes money

Almost all revenue comes from Advanced Industrial Technologies, a 100%-owned company in Poland. It repairs and installs equipment in underground coal mines, and its main client is the Polish coal group JSW. All sales are made in Poland. Coal Energy also owns 50% of Greentech Solutions, a joint venture that processes old mine waste heaps.

The bigger plans are still on paper. The company is seeking a licence for the Bobrek-Miechowice coal deposit in Poland, where a study found more than 9 million tonnes of reserves in the first seams. In October 2025 it signed a memorandum on two deposits in Albania, and it has talked about critical raw materials and mining advisory work.

## The numbers right now

The company's financial year ends on 30 June. In the first half of fiscal 2026 (July–December 2025) revenue fell 45.4% to $1.18 million, because JSW ordered less work. Gross profit was only $80,000, the operating loss was $496,000 and the net loss was $1.36 million. At the end of December it had just $39,000 in cash against $1.33 million of loans and $546,000 of bonds.

To pay its bills, Coal Energy sells convertible bonds to the investor Global Tech Opportunities 31 and has received PLN 4.5 million so far. Some of these bonds have already been turned into 1,079,447 new shares at PLN 2.316 each, so every conversion makes existing shareholders' stakes smaller. The group employed 96 people on average in fiscal 2025.

## The stock's story

Coal Energy sold 25% of its shares on the Warsaw Stock Exchange in July 2011, when it was an active producer in Ukraine. After the 2022 invasion it lost its Ukrainian business, and in 2025 it sold its Ukrainian and Cypriot subsidiaries. Today the stock is a small, thinly traded share whose price depends on news about licences, contracts and financing.

## Competitors

In mining services Coal Energy competes with other contractors that repair and dig tunnels for Polish coal companies. Here it is tiny, and it depends on a single client. If it ever starts producing coal, it would sell into a Polish market dominated by large producers such as JSW and LW Bogdanka.

## Main risks

- **Going concern:** the company has more liabilities than assets and needs outside money to keep operating.
- **One client:** most revenue comes from JSW, and a cut in orders hit revenue by 45% in a single half-year.
- **Dilution:** convertible bonds are paid back in new shares, which shrinks each existing shareholder's slice.
- **Projects not yet approved:** Bobrek-Miechowice still needs a mining licence, and the Albanian deposits are only at the memorandum stage.
- **Coal's future:** the EU is moving away from coal, so long-term demand and financing for new mines are uncertain.
- **Low liquidity:** with a market value of about PLN 76 million, a few trades can move the price sharply.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Regular buying works best with companies that are likely to still exist and grow over many years. Coal Energy is a micro-cap with going-concern warnings and one main client, so this stock carries a high risk of losing most of the money put in. Anyone who follows it should read the company's own reports, not just the price chart. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
