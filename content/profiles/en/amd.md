**AMD** (Advanced Micro Devices) designs the chips inside many laptops, game consoles, servers and, more and more, the AI data centres that run chatbots. It does not own factories: it designs processors and graphics chips and pays manufacturers to make them. In 2015 its shares cost $1.61; in September 2026 the company was worth more than $1 trillion.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Record revenue of $13.4 billion in Q3 2026, up 45% in a year; data centre sales nearly doubled (+97%) to $8.5 billion.
> - Management expects about $17 billion in Q4 2026, which would be roughly 66% more than a year earlier.
> - A 2025 deal with OpenAI covers 6 gigawatts of AMD GPUs and a warrant for up to 160 million AMD shares (about 10% of the company).
> - The stock rose about 185% in 2026 by late September and trades at roughly 105 times last year's profit.

## How it makes money

AMD sells chips in three groups. **Data Center** is now the engine: EPYC server processors and Instinct AI accelerators (the MI450 series launched this year), $8.5 billion in Q3, about 64% of revenue. **Client and Gaming** brought $3.7 billion: Ryzen processors for PCs grew 7%, while gaming fell 40% as sales of chips for consoles and Radeon graphics cards dropped. **Embedded** — chips for cars, factories, telecom networks and defence — added $1.1 billion, up 29%.

Its customers are cloud and AI companies, PC makers and console makers. AMD says Anthropic, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI and Oracle are already running work on its first Helios rack systems, which pack MI450 GPUs and EPYC processors into one ready-made cabinet.

## The numbers right now

In the quarter to 26 September 2026 AMD earned $13.4 billion in revenue with a 54% gross margin. Net profit was $2.4 billion ($1.46 per share), up 95% from a year earlier. It ended the quarter with $15.8 billion in cash and short-term investments against $7.1 billion of debt; debt more than doubled during the quarter after AMD borrowed $4.7 billion. For Q4 it guided to about $17 billion in revenue (plus or minus 3%) and a non-GAAP gross margin of about 55%.

## The stock's story

AMD was close to the edge in the early 2010s: the share price fell to $1.61 in July 2015. Lisa Su, CEO since 2014, bet the company on new chip designs, and the first Ryzen and EPYC processors arrived in 2017 and started taking market share from Intel. The next jump came from AI: when the OpenAI deal was announced in October 2025, the stock rose about 30% in a morning. On 21 September 2026 it hit a record $613.31 and the company passed $1 trillion in market value. Over the past 52 weeks the price ranged from $188.22 to $658.52, so swings of hundreds of dollars are normal here.

## Competitors

In AI accelerators the main rival is Nvidia, the market leader. In PC and server processors AMD competes mainly with Intel, and increasingly with Arm-based chips from Apple, Qualcomm and the cloud companies' own designs. AMD's edge is that it sells both the processor and the AI GPU, plus networking, as one system.

## Main risks

- **Expectations are sky-high:** at about 105 times earnings, the price already assumes years of fast growth; a weaker quarter can hit the stock hard.
- **Few huge customers:** a large share of AI revenue depends on a handful of tech giants and their spending plans.
- **Dilution:** if OpenAI earns the full warrant, up to 160 million new shares go to it.
- **Supply and geopolitics:** AMD relies on outside factories to make its chips, and trade rules can limit where it may sell them.
- **Gaming is shrinking:** console and graphics card sales fell 40% in Q3.

## What it means if you buy regularly

AMD shows both sides of a single tech stock. Someone buying through 2015 paid under $2 a share; someone buying in 2026 paid hundreds. Regular buying spreads the entry price over good and bad months, but a stock priced for perfection can fall 50% or more, and AMD has done so before. Many people keep a stock like this as one part of a wider portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
