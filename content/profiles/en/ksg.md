**KSG Agro** is a small Ukrainian farming group: it raises pigs, grows wheat, barley, sunflower and corn, and makes fuel pellets on the side. Its farms are in Dnipropetrovsk region in central-eastern Ukraine (in 2022 it also farmed in Kherson region), while the holding company is registered in Luxembourg and its shares trade on the Warsaw Stock Exchange. It is a tiny company by stock-market standards — about 225 employees and a market value of roughly 35 million zloty.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: $18.9 million in 2025, down 14% from $22.1 million, as grain sales shrank.
> - Net profit: $4.2 million in 2025, up from $0.8 million in 2024 (unaudited figures).
> - Pigs are now the main business: $11.1 million of sales in 2025, versus $7.4 million from crops.
> - The shares listed at 22 zloty in 2011; over the past year they traded between about 2.4 and 4.4 zloty.

## How it makes money

KSG Agro has two main segments. **Pig breeding** sells live pigs and pork carcasses, mostly in its home region; in early 2022 the company said it supplied about half of the Dnipropetrovsk region's pork market. In 2025 it brought $11.1 million, up from $8.9 million. **Crop farming** grows grain and oilseeds on leased land and sells the harvest; its sales fell from $12.3 million to $7.4 million in 2025. A small **other** segment ($0.4 million) covers pellets, heat and trading. At the end of 2025 the group had 3,749 sows and gilts and about 33,900 pigs and piglets, fewer than a year earlier, because it replaced low-yield sows with 1,300 animals of Canadian genetics.

## The numbers right now

In 2025 KSG Agro earned $3.6 million in gross profit (2024: $1.7 million), $7.5 million in EBITDA (profit before interest, tax and depreciation) and $4.2 million in net profit. The company reports in US dollars, and the 2025 accounts were published unaudited. Its balance sheet is stretched: bank and other loans of $14.4 million against almost no cash ($21,000 at year-end) and equity of $8.9 million. In the first half of 2026 operating profit rose to $2.5 million from $0.8 million and EBITDA to $2.8 million from $1.0 million, driven by the pig business. The company plans to replace about 1,500 of its 4,500 sows during 2026, including Danish-bred animals.

## The stock's story

KSG Agro floated in Warsaw in spring 2011, selling shares at 22 zloty and raising about 108 million zloty, at a time when many Ukrainian agricultural companies were listing there. Since then the share has lost the vast majority of its value: the company reported losses in 2022 and 2023 (a $1.2 million loss in 2023), and Russia's full-scale invasion in 2022 made every Ukrainian asset riskier. Trading is thin, so prices can move sharply on small orders.

## Competitors

In pork KSG competes with much larger Ukrainian pig producers and with imported meat. In grain it is a small player next to big agricultural holdings such as Astarta, which is also listed in Warsaw. Its advantage is a local, vertically integrated chain — its own feed, farms and processing — in one region.

## Main risks

- **War:** the farms and pig complex are in a country under attack; the company itself flags a material uncertainty about the war's effects on its business.
- **Debt and cash:** loans of $14.4 million and almost no cash leave little room for a bad year.
- **Prices and disease:** pork and grain prices swing, and an outbreak of African swine fever can wipe out a herd.
- **Small and illiquid stock:** a controlling shareholder owns close to half the shares, trading volumes are low, and the 2025 report was unaudited.

## What it means if you buy regularly

KSG Agro is a micro-cap: small, thinly traded and exposed to a war. Regular buying spreads purchases over time, but it cannot fix a business that loses value for years, and a stock like this can fall to almost nothing. If someone holds it at all, it is usually a very small part of a broad portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
