**Quant** is a British technology company that builds Overledger, software that lets different blockchains and older bank systems talk to each other. Its token, QNT, is what businesses and developers use to pay for access to that software. With only about 14.6 million tokens, QNT is worth {{mcap:qnt}} today ({{rank:qnt}} among cryptocurrencies).

> - Quant was founded in the UK in 2018 by Gilbert Verdian; its Overledger software launched the same year.
> - The supply is small and fixed: about 14.6 million QNT, almost all already in circulation.
> - Companies that use Overledger must buy and lock QNT for their licences, so demand depends on real business customers.

## How it works

Banks and companies use many different blockchains — public ones such as Ethereum and private ones that only invited members can join. These networks normally cannot share data or move value between each other. Overledger works like a universal translator: an app connects to Overledger once through an API (a standard software interface) and can then work with several blockchains and with ordinary bank databases at the same time. Quant calls apps that run across several chains "mApps". Unlike most crypto projects, Overledger is not open-source: it is a paid product sold by a company.

## What the token is used for

QNT is an ERC-20 token on Ethereum. Businesses pay for Overledger licences and developer access in QNT and lock the tokens for the length of the licence. The tokens Quant collects are not burned; they are passed on to network operators and to the company's treasury. In this way the token is a ticket to use the software: the more companies use Overledger, the more QNT has to be bought and held. Investors also buy QNT as a bet that banks and governments will adopt blockchain technology and choose Quant for it.

## Its story so far

Quant raised money in a token sale that closed in September 2018 and released the first version of Overledger the same year. The team received about 19.6% of the tokens, which were fully unlocked by April 2020. During the 2021 bull market QNT rose above $400 in September 2021, then fell below $50 by June 2022 as the whole crypto market crashed. Since then the company has focused on banks, tokenised money and central bank digital currency projects, and later presented Quant Fusion, a network for linking many ledgers.

## Alternatives

Several projects try to connect blockchains. [Chainlink]({lang}/link/) offers CCIP, a cross-chain messaging system that has also been tested with banks and Swift. [Polkadot]({lang}/dot/) and [Cosmos]({lang}/atom/) connect chains built on their own technology. Banks can also choose private enterprise software, such as tools from big IT companies, without any public token.

## Main risks

- **Business adoption:** QNT's value depends on companies paying for Overledger; slow or small adoption means little real demand.
- **Closed software:** Overledger is a paid, closed product, so outsiders cannot fully check how widely it is used.
- **Company risk:** one private company controls the technology and the treasury; its decisions and finances matter a lot.
- **Competition:** Chainlink and other interoperability projects compete for the same bank and government customers.
- **Big swings:** QNT lost about 90% from its 2021 peak within a year; small-supply tokens can move sharply.

## What it means if you buy regularly

QNT has moved in long waves with the crypto market and with news about bank adoption. Buying a small fixed amount regularly spreads your purchases over good and bad months instead of betting on one moment — but it does not protect you if the business story does not work out. Many people keep such a token to a small share of their savings. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
