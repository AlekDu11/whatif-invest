**Allegro** is the place where Poland shops online: a marketplace where thousands of shops and private sellers list everything from phone cases to car parts. Allegro does not own most of what it sells — it takes a cut of each sale and charges sellers for ads and delivery. The company is registered in Luxembourg and listed on the Warsaw Stock Exchange in October 2020, in the largest IPO in that exchange's history.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Goods sold on the platform (GMV): PLN 19.8 billion in Q3 2026, +17.8% a year; revenue PLN 3.41 billion, +20%.
> - Net profit jumped 45% to PLN 574.7 million in the quarter.
> - 21.5 million active buyers, 15.8 million of them in Poland.
> - The shares roughly doubled in 2026, from under PLN 33 in January to a 52-week high of PLN 68.97, but they first sank below PLN 20 in 2022.

## How it makes money

Most revenue is commission. In Q3 2026 marketplace fees brought in PLN 2.40 billion, about 72% of revenue. Advertising — sellers paying to appear higher in search — added PLN 467.5 million (14%) and grew by more than a third in Poland. The rest comes from logistics, its own retail sales, the Ceneo price-comparison site and other services. Two tools keep shoppers loyal: Allegro Smart!, a paid subscription with free delivery used by more than 7.8 million people in Poland, and Allegro Pay, buy-now-pay-later loans that financed 16.2% of Polish GMV.

## The numbers right now

In the quarter to 30 September 2026 Allegro's GMV rose 17.8% to PLN 19.83 billion: +14.7% in Poland, the fastest since 2022, and +102% in Czechia, Slovakia and Hungary. Revenue and other operating income rose 20.1% to PLN 3.41 billion, adjusted EBITDA (operating profit before depreciation and one-off items) 22.9% to PLN 1.13 billion, and net profit 45.1% to PLN 574.7 million.

Allegro raised its 2026 outlook again: GMV of PLN 79.5–80.5 billion, revenue of PLN 13.6–13.7 billion and adjusted EBITDA of PLN 4.2–4.3 billion. Part of the upgrade comes from a new delivery agreement with InPost.

## The stock's story

The IPO priced shares at PLN 43; on the first day they reached PLN 70, and within about two weeks they came close to PLN 100 — a pandemic-era valuation. Then came slower growth, tougher competition, repeated share sales by the big early owners and the costly 2022 purchase of Czech Mall Group for about EUR 881 million. By autumn 2022 the price was below PLN 20. In January 2026 Allegro agreed to sell Mall's Slovenian and Croatian units to the Mutares fund, as growth and profit sped up, the stock climbed back above the IPO price and in November 2026 traded close to its 52-week high of PLN 68.97.

## Competitors

The main threat is Chinese platforms. In March 2026 Temu overtook Allegro in Poland by monthly users (19.1 million against 18.5 million, Mediapanel data), with AliExpress, Shein and Amazon further behind. Allegro's edge is fast local delivery, a huge choice of Polish sellers and Smart!.

## Main risks

- **Cheap Asian rivals:** Temu and Shein compete on price and are now as widely used as Allegro.
- **Foreign expansion:** the Czech, Slovak and Hungarian business is growing fast but is expected to lose PLN 0.47–0.49 billion (adjusted EBITDA) in 2026.
- **Regulation:** as the leading marketplace in Poland, Allegro is a natural target for competition authorities and new EU platform rules.
- **Polish consumer:** almost all profit comes from Poland, so a slowdown in spending there hits results directly.
- **Lending:** Allegro Pay grew 37% in a year; more loans mean more exposure if borrowers stop paying.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Allegro shows how far a stock can drift from its business: GMV and profit kept growing, yet the shares spent most of five years below the IPO price. Regular buying spreads purchases across low and high prices and removes the need to pick a moment, but it does not make a single company safe — many people keep a stock like this as a small part of a broader portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
