**Arweave (AR)** is a blockchain built to store data permanently. You pay once, in AR, and your file — a document, a photo, a website, an NFT's image — is meant to stay online for at least 200 years, copied by miners around the world and impossible to quietly edit or delete. It launched in 2018 and is often called the "permaweb", and all AR in circulation is worth {{mcap:ar-crypto}} today ({{rank:ar-crypto}} among cryptocurrencies).

> - Most of each upload fee goes into an endowment that pays miners to keep data for the long term.

## How it works

When you upload data, you pay a one-time fee in AR. A small part goes to the miner who adds it to a block; most of it is put into a storage endowment. Arweave's bet is that storage keeps getting cheaper, so the endowment can pay miners for centuries. To win new blocks, miners have to prove they hold randomly chosen pieces of older data, so the network rewards those who keep as much of the history as possible. The blocks are linked in a structure called a "blockweave". Gateways such as those in the AR.IO network then serve the stored files to ordinary web browsers.

## Why people think it has value

Links on the internet break all the time and companies delete content. Arweave sells the opposite: data that stays where you put it and can be checked by anyone. NFT projects use it for images and metadata, publishing tools and archives use it for articles and records, and developers host whole apps on it. AR is needed for every upload, so demand for storage is demand for the coin. AO adds computing on top: programs whose every step is recorded on Arweave, which widens what the network can be used for.

## Its story so far

Sam Williams and William Jones started the project in 2017 under the name Archain and launched the Arweave mainnet in June 2018. In 2020 investors including Andreessen Horowitz and Union Square Ventures backed it. Use by NFT projects in the 2021 boom pushed AR to its record in November 2021; it then fell more than 90% in the 2022 crypto crash. In February 2024 the team presented AO, which reached mainnet in February 2025, and part of the new AO tokens went to AR holders.

## Alternatives

Arweave's closest crypto rival is [Filecoin]({lang}/fil-crypto/), which works more like renting storage for a set period, with deals that can be renewed. Older networks such as [Storj]({lang}/storj/) and [Siacoin]({lang}/sc/) also sell decentralized storage. Outside crypto, the competition is ordinary cloud storage and archives, which are cheaper today but come with no promise of permanence and can be changed by their owners.

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** AR has already fallen more than 90% from its peak and can do so again.
- **The endowment bet:** if storage costs stop falling, the fund may not cover hundreds of years of storage.
- **Content that cannot be deleted:** illegal or harmful uploads create legal pressure on miners and gateways.
- **Small network:** fewer users and developers than the large blockchains, and attention can move to AO or rivals.
- **Regulation and exchanges:** crypto rules differ by country; delistings or new taxes can hit smaller coins hard.

## What it means if you buy regularly

AR's history is a sharp rise in 2021 followed by a long fall. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your entry price and avoids putting everything in at a peak, but it does not remove the risk of a coin that keeps losing value. Many people keep smaller cryptocurrencies to a small part of their savings. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
