**Arch Capital Group** is an insurance company based in Pembroke, Bermuda. It sells three things: insurance for businesses (property, liability, cyber, marine and more), reinsurance — insurance for other insurers — and mortgage insurance, which protects lenders if a home buyer stops paying. People rarely hear the name, but in 2025 Arch earned about $4.4 billion of net income on roughly $19.3 billion of revenue.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: about $19.3 billion in 2025, up from $9.6 billion in 2022; net income about $4.4 billion.
> - Three engines: Insurance, Reinsurance and Mortgage — Arch is one of the largest US private mortgage insurers since buying United Guaranty from AIG in 2016.
> - In the third quarter of 2026 Arch reported net income of about $1.0 billion.
> - Arch pays no regular dividend; in late 2024 it paid a one-time special dividend of $5 per share.

## How it makes money

An insurer earns money twice. First from underwriting: it collects premiums today and pays claims later, and if claims plus costs are lower than premiums, it keeps the difference. The key measure is the combined ratio — claims and expenses as a share of premiums; below 100% means underwriting is profitable. Second from investing: Arch holds tens of billions of dollars of "float" (premiums not yet paid out as claims) in bonds and other assets and keeps the investment income.

Arch's **Insurance** segment writes specialty cover for companies — property, casualty, professional liability, cyber, marine, travel and more — mostly in the US, UK and Europe. In August 2024 it added Allianz's US MidCorp and Entertainment business, which insures mid-sized companies and film and TV productions. **Reinsurance** sells protection to other insurers against large losses such as hurricanes and earthquakes. **Mortgage** insures home loans in the US (as Arch MI), plus Australia and Europe, and is usually the steadiest part of the business.

Arch's profit grew fast after insurance prices rose across the industry from 2019. Net income was about $1.5 billion in 2022, $4.4 billion in 2023, $4.3 billion in 2024 and $4.4 billion in 2025, while revenue doubled from $9.6 billion to $19.3 billion over the same years. In the third quarter of 2026 the company reported net income of about $1.0 billion. Arch does not give a formal profit forecast — results depend heavily on how many disasters happen in a given year.

## The stock's story

Arch in its current form dates back to 2001, when the investment firms Warburg Pincus and Hellman & Friedman recapitalised a small listed company (formerly Risk Capital Holdings) and built a new specialty insurer and reinsurer in Bermuda. It grew by hiring underwriting teams and by being selective: Arch is known for writing less business when prices are poor and more when they are good. The 2016 purchase of United Guaranty from AIG made it a major US mortgage insurer. The shares were split three-for-one in 2018. In October 2024 longtime chief executive Marc Grandisson retired and Nicolas Papadopoulo, who had run the insurance and reinsurance businesses, became CEO. The stock trades on Nasdaq under ACGL and has been part of the S&P 500.

## Competitors

In specialty insurance Arch competes with [Chubb]({lang}/cb/), [W. R. Berkley]({lang}/wrb/), [Travelers]({lang}/trv/), [AXIS Capital]({lang}/axs/) and [AIG]({lang}/aig/). In reinsurance its rivals include [RenaissanceRe]({lang}/rnr/), [Everest Group]({lang}/eg/), Munich Re and Swiss Re. In US mortgage insurance it competes with [MGIC]({lang}/mtg/), Radian, Enact and Essent.

## Main risks

- **Catastrophes:** a big hurricane, earthquake or wildfire season can wipe out a quarter's or a year's profit in reinsurance and property lines.
- **The insurance cycle:** after several years of high prices, competition can push premiums down, and profit margins usually follow.
- **Housing and jobs:** mortgage insurance does well when people keep paying their loans; a recession with rising unemployment would raise claims.
- **Reserves and "social inflation":** if past claims, especially US liability lawsuits, turn out bigger than set aside, earnings are revised down.
- **Interest rates and markets:** investment income and the value of the bond portfolio move with rates and markets.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Arch is a profitable, carefully run insurer, but insurance stocks can still fall sharply after a heavy disaster year or when the pricing cycle turns. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your purchases across good and bad years, so you buy more shares when the price is low and fewer when it is high. Since Arch pays no regular dividend, any return comes from the share price. Many people hold one company only as part of a wider portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
