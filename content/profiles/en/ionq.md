**IonQ** builds quantum computers — machines that use single charged atoms (ions) instead of ordinary chips to attack certain problems in chemistry, logistics and security. Most people never touch one: companies and governments rent time on IonQ machines through Amazon Web Services, Microsoft Azure and Google Cloud, or buy whole systems. Founded in 2015 in Maryland, it listed on the New York Stock Exchange in 2021 through a merger with a SPAC (a listed shell company).

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: $173.8 million in Q3 2026, up 336% a year, partly thanks to the SkyWater chip factories bought in July.
> - The reported net income of $564 million is not real profit: it came from an $896 million non-cash gain on warrants. The business lost $433 million from operations.
> - The stock peaked at $82.09 in October 2025 and was about 70% below that in November 2026.
> - It has a lot of cash — about $3.7 billion after a $2.0 billion share sale in October 2026 — but every share sale dilutes existing owners.

## How it makes money

IonQ now reports two segments. The **IonQ Platform** — quantum computers, cloud access, research contracts, quantum networking, sensing and space projects — brought in $90.3 million in Q3 2026, up 127% a year. **SkyWater Foundry Services** added $83.5 million: SkyWater is the largest chip foundry working only in the United States, with plants in Minnesota, Florida and Texas, and it makes chips for defence and commercial clients. IonQ paid $15 in cash plus 0.4883 IonQ shares for each SkyWater share, and the deal closed on 31 July 2026.

## The numbers right now

In the quarter to 30 September 2026 IonQ booked $173.8 million in revenue, against $39.9 million a year earlier. On paper it earned $564 million, or $1.35 per diluted share — but only because the value of its warrants (rights to buy shares at a set price) fell, which counts as a gain under accounting rules. Without that one-off effect the picture is different: an operating loss of $433.1 million and an adjusted EBITDA loss of $133.6 million. Remaining performance obligations — signed work not yet delivered — more than doubled to $1.2 billion. IonQ raised its 2026 revenue forecast to $505–515 million and also bought Elisity, a cybersecurity company.

## The stock's story

IonQ is one of the most volatile stocks on the market. After listing in 2021 it fell about 90% from its November 2021 peak to the end of 2022. Then the quantum hype of 2024–2025 lifted it from under $10 to a record $82.09 on 13 October 2025. Since then it has slid back, including a 31.6% drop in July 2026 amid insider selling and a sell-off in high-risk tech stocks.

## Competitors

IonQ competes with tech giants that build their own quantum computers — IBM, Google and Microsoft — and with Quantinuum (majority-owned by Honeywell), which also uses trapped ions. Among smaller listed rivals are Rigetti Computing and D-Wave Quantum. IonQ's edge: the most revenue among pure quantum companies, a big cash pile and its own US chip factories.

## Main risks

- **No proven mass market yet:** useful, large-scale quantum computing is still years away, and nobody knows exactly when demand will become big and steady.
- **Heavy losses:** the business loses hundreds of millions of dollars a quarter; headline profit can swing wildly with warrant values.
- **Dilution:** IonQ pays for growth and acquisitions by issuing new shares, so each existing share owns a smaller slice.
- **Acquisition risk:** SkyWater is a very different, lower-margin factory business, and merging it with a research company is hard.
- **Valuation:** a market value of about $11 billion rests on hopes for the future, so sentiment shifts move the price fast.

## What it means if you buy regularly

IonQ has already fallen about 90% once and about 70% again from its 2025 record. Buying regularly spreads purchases over high and low months, but it cannot rescue a company whose technology does not pay off. For a stock this speculative, many people keep only a small slice in a wider portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
