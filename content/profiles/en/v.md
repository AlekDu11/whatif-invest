**Visa** is the world's largest card payment network: when you tap a Visa card, its system checks, routes and settles the payment in seconds — 281 billion times in fiscal 2026. It was born in 1958 as Bank of America's BankAmericard and became a public company in 2008.

## How it makes money

Visa does not lend money or issue cards — banks do that. It charges small fees for running the network: service fees based on how much is spent on Visa cards, data processing fees for each transaction, and higher fees when a payment crosses a border. Newer "value-added services" such as fraud tools and Visa Direct money transfers grow even faster. In the year to September 2026 net revenue rose 15% to a record $45.9 billion; in the fourth quarter alone it earned about $6 billion of net income on $12.1 billion of revenue.

## Why it matters

Because Visa takes a tiny cut of a huge flow, it keeps about half of its revenue as net profit — rare for any business. Growth comes from cash turning into cards and phones worldwide: processed transactions rose 9% and cross-border volume 13% in fiscal 2026. Visa Direct moved $1.5 trillion, up 23%. One surprising fact: Visa never takes credit risk — if a cardholder doesn't pay, it's the bank's problem, not Visa's.

## Main risks

Merchants and regulators push hard against card fees, and Visa set aside $474 million for litigation in the last quarter alone. New rules on interchange or network competition in the US or Europe could squeeze its pricing. Instant bank payments, stablecoins and local systems such as India's UPI or Brazil's Pix compete for the same transactions. Spending also slows in recessions. Buying a fixed amount regularly smooths price swings but does not remove the risk.
