**Tezos (XTZ)** is a smart-contract blockchain built to upgrade itself: holders of its token, tez, vote on changes to the rules, and approved upgrades switch on without splitting the network. ## How it works

Tezos uses proof of stake, which it calls "baking". Validators, called bakers, lock up tez and take turns producing and confirming blocks; ordinary holders can delegate their tez to a baker and share the rewards while keeping the coins in their own wallet. Since the Tallinn upgrade in January 2026 a new block arrives about every 6 seconds. The special part is governance: anyone can propose a new version of the protocol, bakers vote on it in several rounds, and if it passes the network adopts it automatically. Upgrades are named after cities in alphabetical order, from Athens in 2019 to Seoul in September 2025 and Tallinn in January 2026. Smart contracts run in Tezos's own language, Michelson, and Etherlink, a Layer 2 network on Tezos, runs Ethereum-style apps.

## What the token is used for

Tez pays the fees for transactions and smart contracts, and it is what bakers stake to secure the chain and vote on upgrades. People who stake or delegate earn new tez as rewards. Tezos has been used for NFT art platforms, tokenised assets and projects by banks and public institutions, especially in France. Its value depends on whether enough developers and users choose Tezos over larger chains.

## Its story so far

Arthur and Kathleen Breitman described Tezos in a 2014 white paper, and in July 2017 its fundraiser brought in about $232 million in bitcoin and ether. A public dispute between the founders and the head of the Swiss Tezos Foundation then delayed the launch and led to investor lawsuits, which were settled for $25 million in 2020. The network went live in 2018. XTZ reached about $9.12 in October 2021, during the NFT boom, and then fell for years as attention moved to other chains. The protocol kept upgrading: Seoul (the 19th upgrade) added built-in multisig accounts in September 2025, and Tallinn (the 20th) cut block time to 6 seconds in January 2026. In November 2026 XTZ fell to new lows.

## Alternatives

Tezos competes with other proof-of-stake smart-contract platforms: [Ethereum]({lang}/eth/), the biggest; [Cardano]({lang}/ada/), which also focuses on formal research and on-chain governance; [Polkadot]({lang}/dot/), which can likewise upgrade without hard forks; and [Algorand]({lang}/algo/).

## Main risks

- **Adoption:** Tezos has far fewer apps and users than the biggest chains; without growth, demand for tez stays weak.
- **Inflation:** with no supply cap, new tez from rewards dilute holders who do not stake.
- **Big swings:** XTZ has lost most of its 2021 value and can still drop sharply within days.
- **Competition:** larger and faster-growing chains attract the developers and money Tezos needs.
- **Regulation and exchanges:** crypto rules differ by country and can change, and smaller coins risk losing exchange listings.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your entry price over time, so you get more tez when the price is low and less when it is high. Staking or delegating can add rewards, but they do not protect you from a falling price. Steady buying only works out if the network's use and the price recover, so keep XTZ to a small share of money you could afford to lose. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
