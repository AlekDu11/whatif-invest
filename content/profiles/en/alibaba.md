**Alibaba** runs Taobao and Tmall, China's biggest online shopping sites, and is now betting its future on AI: it owns China's largest cloud business and the Qwen family of AI models. Jack Ma and 17 co-founders started it in a Hangzhou flat in 1999.

## How it makes money

Most profit still comes from Chinese e-commerce: merchants pay Alibaba for ads, search placement and commissions on Taobao and Tmall (it calls this "customer management revenue"). Its second engine is Alibaba Cloud, which rents computing power and AI services to companies. It also runs fast grocery delivery ("quick commerce"), international shops such as AliExpress and Trendyol, and logistics through Cainiao.

## Why it matters

In the quarter to June 2026 revenue rose 9% to about $39.6 billion. AI cloud revenue grew 45% to $7.1 billion, its fastest pace in 22 quarters, and quick commerce grew 45%. Alibaba spent nearly $10 billion on AI infrastructure in that quarter alone. One surprising fact: Qwen models have been downloaded more than 3 billion times, with over 300,000 versions built on top of them — one of the most-used open AI model families in the world.

## Main risks

Heavy spending on AI and delivery subsidies has cut profits, and it is not yet clear when that will pay off. Competition is fierce: PDD (Temu, Pinduoduo) and JD fight for shoppers, and Tencent, ByteDance and Huawei compete in AI and cloud. Beijing's regulation can change suddenly — Alibaba was fined a record 18.2 billion yuan in 2021 — and US chip export limits restrict the hardware it can buy. The shares are still below their 2020 peak. Buying a fixed amount regularly smooths price swings but does not remove the risk.
