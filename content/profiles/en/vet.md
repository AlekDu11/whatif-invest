**VeChain** is a blockchain built for companies: it is used to record where products come from, prove that goods are genuine and track sustainability data. Its main network went live in June 2018, it is led by Sunny Lu, a former IT chief of Louis Vuitton China, and its VET token is worth {{mcap:vet}} in total ({{rank:vet}} among cryptocurrencies). It is unusual because it runs on two tokens: VET to hold and stake, and VTHO to pay fees.

> - VeChain uses two tokens: VET stores value and secures the network, VTHO pays for transactions.
> - At most about 86.7 billion VET can exist, and almost all of it is already in circulation.
> - In 2025 the network moved from 101 approved "authority" nodes to open staking, and only staked VET now earns VTHO.

## How it works

VeChainThor, the main network, adds a new block about every 10 seconds. For years blocks were made by 101 "Authority Masternodes" — known operators approved by the VeChain Foundation, each holding 25 million VET. The Hayabusa upgrade in December 2025 replaced this with delegated proof of stake: anyone with at least 25 million VET can run a validator, and smaller holders can delegate their VET through the StarGate staking app, launched in July 2025. Fees are paid in VTHO, and since the Galactica upgrade of July 2025 the base part of every fee is burned.

## Why people think it has value

VeChain's pitch is real-world use rather than speculation. Brands and partners have used it to trace food, prove that luxury and sports goods are genuine, and keep digital product passports of the kind the EU is introducing. Its VeBetter platform rewards people for eco-friendly actions through apps on the network. Holding VET matters because staked VET earns VTHO, which every app and company on the network needs to pay fees, so more use means more demand for VTHO and more of it burned.

## Its story so far

VeChain started in 2015 as a project inside the Chinese company BitSE, and in 2017 it sold its first token, VEN, on Ethereum. In June 2018 its own blockchain launched, and VEN was swapped for VET at 1 to 100. In 2019 it powered a food-tracing platform for Walmart China. Like most coins, VET rose sharply in the 2021 boom, peaking in April 2021 at about $0.28, then fell more than 90% over the following years. In 2025 the "Renaissance" upgrades — Galactica in July and Hayabusa in December — rebuilt its fees and staking.

## Alternatives

Other networks that court businesses include [Hedera]({lang}/hbar/), [Algorand]({lang}/algo/), [XDC Network]({lang}/xdc/) and [IOTA]({lang}/iota/). Many companies also build on [Ethereum]({lang}/eth/) or use private databases instead of any public blockchain, which is VeChain's biggest competition.

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** VET has lost more than 90% from its peak before; deep drops can happen again.
- **Adoption gap:** partnerships make headlines, but they do not always turn into heavy, paying use of the network.
- **Concentration:** large validators and the VeChain Foundation hold big stakes, so a few players shape the network.
- **Complex token model:** the VET/VTHO split and the new staking rules are hard for beginners, and the rules can change again.
- **Regulation and competition:** crypto rules differ by country, and rival chains fight for the same enterprise clients.

## What it means if you buy regularly

VET has gone through a long fall after the 2021 boom. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your entry price over time, but it does not protect you from a coin that keeps losing value. If you hold VET, staking it is the only way to earn VTHO now. Many people keep coins like this to a small part of their savings. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
