**Akash Network** is a marketplace for computing power: people who need servers or graphics cards (GPUs) for websites, apps or artificial intelligence rent them from independent providers around the world, instead of from Amazon, Google or Microsoft. It is built by the US company Overclock Labs, led by founder Greg Osuri, and its token AKT is worth {{mcap:akt}} in total today ({{rank:akt}} among cryptocurrencies). People follow it as one of the oldest "DePIN" projects — decentralised physical infrastructure — riding the demand for AI computing.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Akash matches buyers of computing with providers in an open auction; GPUs joined the marketplace in August 2023.
> - Since March 2026 every lease is paid through AKT under a "Burn-Mint Equilibrium" model, so more usage means more AKT burned.
> - In November 2026 the community approved moving Akash from its own Cosmos chain to Solana over about 24 months, with AKT swapped 1:1.

## How it works

A developer describes what they need — for example, one GPU and some memory to run an AI model — and providers bid to host it. The cheapest suitable offer wins, and the app runs in containers on that provider's hardware. Providers range from data centres to, with the Homenode beta opened in 2026, people with a powerful home graphics card such as an RTX 4090. The network itself has been its own blockchain built with the Cosmos SDK, secured by validators who stake AKT. Akash also runs AkashML, a service for running open AI models, which is offered through the OpenRouter platform.

## What the token is used for

AKT secures the network through staking, gives holders votes on upgrades, and since the Mainnet 17 upgrade on 23 March 2026 it settles all payments. Under Burn-Mint Equilibrium (BME) a customer burns AKT to get ACT, a credit worth about one dollar that cannot be traded; when the job is paid out, providers receive newly minted AKT at the price of that moment. If AKT rose in between, less is minted than was burned and the supply shrinks; if it fell, more is minted. Customers can also pay by card, which buys AKT on the market first. Before BME, people could pay in the stablecoin USDC, and part of the fees went to stakers.

## Its story so far

Akash's mainnet went live in September 2020, and in March 2021 the marketplace for renting computing opened. AKT reached its record of about $8 in April 2021, then lost almost all of it in the 2022 crypto crash, falling below $0.20 in November 2022. On 31 August 2023 the Mainnet 6 upgrade added Nvidia GPUs and USDC payments, and the AI boom lifted AKT back to around $5 in early 2024. Prices slid again in 2025, to about $0.35 by the end of the year. In October 2025 Osuri said Akash would give up its own chain; BME went live in March 2026, and on 2 November 2026 Akash confirmed the move to Solana after governance proposal 346 passed with 97% in favour.

## Alternatives

The real competition is the big clouds — Amazon Web Services, Microsoft Azure and Google Cloud — and specialised GPU clouds. Among crypto projects, [Render]({lang}/render/) and [io.net]({lang}/io/) also rent out GPUs, [Bittensor]({lang}/tao/) rewards AI models themselves, [Internet Computer]({lang}/icp/) offers on-chain hosting, and [Filecoin]({lang}/fil-crypto/) and [Arweave]({lang}/ar-crypto/) do the same for storage rather than computing.

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** AKT has already fallen more than 95% from its record once; such drops can happen again.
- **Migration:** moving to Solana over two years is a large technical project; delays, bugs or a messy token swap could hurt holders.
- **New token model:** BME only went live in 2026; if usage stays small, newly minted AKT can outweigh what is burned.
- **Competition:** the big clouds have far more money, and other crypto GPU networks chase the same customers.
- **Concentration and regulation:** the core company, large holders and a few providers carry a lot of weight, and rules for tokens differ by country.

## What it means if you buy regularly

AKT's price has followed waves of excitement about AI and crypto more than the network's actual usage. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your entry price over those waves, but it does not remove the risk of large losses. Many people keep tokens like this to a small part of their savings that they can afford to see fall sharply. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
