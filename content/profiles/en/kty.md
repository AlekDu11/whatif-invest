**Grupa Kęty** is a Polish industrial group that turns aluminium into things you see every day: window and door frames, glass facades of office buildings, external blinds and pergolas, parts for cars and trucks, and printed plastic packaging for food. Its best-known brand is Aluprof. Based in Kęty in southern Poland, it has been listed on the Warsaw Stock Exchange since 1996.

> **Key takeaways**
> - 2026 forecast: revenue 5.89 billion zł, EBITDA 1.11 billion zł and net profit 636 million zł (+14%).
> - After nine months net profit was about 587 million zł — roughly 92% of the full-year target.
> - Agreed to buy Italian extrusion maker Metra for €645 million; closing is expected in Q1 2027.
> - Paid a 2025 dividend of 48.97 zł per share in two parts; the payout policy is 60–100% of profit.

## How it makes money

Kęty works in four segments. Extruded Products squeezes heated aluminium through shaped dies to make profiles for construction, cars, transport and electrical equipment. Architectural Systems (Aluprof) designs complete window, door and facade systems sold to building firms. Sun Shading Systems makes roller shutters, blinds, garage doors and pergolas. Flexible Packaging (Alupol Packaging) prints food packaging and produces BOPP plastic film. Aluminium prices are passed on to customers, so higher metal prices lift revenue; profit depends more on volumes and on how much processing Kęty adds to each kilogram.

## The numbers right now

The second quarter of 2026 was a record: revenue 1.65 billion zł (+15%) and net profit 241 million zł (+42%), with plants running at more than 90% of capacity. In the third quarter the company estimated revenue of about 1.62 billion zł (+11.6%), EBITDA of about 325 million zł (+10%) and net profit of about 201 million zł (+14%), in line with analysts' expectations. Demand cooled over the summer as some customers delayed orders. Net debt was about 1.18 billion zł at the end of September. Over the last twelve months revenue was about 5.91 billion zł and net profit about 696 million zł, and the shares trade at roughly 18 times earnings.

## The stock's story

Kęty has been one of the long-term growth stories of the Warsaw exchange, with a strong uptrend since autumn 2022, when the stock traded below 600 zł. Rising profits and generous dividends then pushed it to a record of about 1,317 zł in July 2026, and over the past 52 weeks it traded between 877 and 1,362 zł — giving the company a market value of about 12.5 billion zł. In September 2026 it announced its biggest deal ever: Metra, based near Brescia in Italy, with nine plants and about 1,400 employees serving rail, industry and construction in Europe and North America.

## Competitors

In extrusion Kęty competes with large European producers such as Norway's Hydro and with many local extruders. In window and facade systems its rivals include Schüco from Germany and Reynaers from Belgium. In packaging it faces global groups such as Amcor and Constantia Flexibles. Its strengths are a strong position in Poland, high plant utilisation and a habit of moving into higher-value products.

## Main risks

- **Construction cycle:** most products go into new buildings and renovations; a slowdown in European construction hits volumes quickly.
- **Bigger debt after Metra:** the deal is partly financed with a loan of up to 2 billion zł, and net debt could rise to about 2.5–2.7 times EBITDA from around 1.3.
- **Integration:** Metra is a large foreign business; mistakes in merging it could eat into the expected gains.
- **Aluminium and energy prices:** sharp moves in metal or energy costs can squeeze margins or swing revenue.
- **Liquidity and currency:** the stock trades in złoty on a smaller exchange; for foreign investors the exchange rate adds or removes return.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Kęty combines industrial growth with a large dividend — the 2025 payout was close to 4% of the share price. Regular buying with reinvested dividends spreads purchases over good and bad years, but the stock is tied to the construction cycle and was below 600 zł as recently as 2022. The Metra deal raises both the potential and the risk. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
