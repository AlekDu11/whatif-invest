**Gnosis (GNO)** is one of the oldest projects in the Ethereum world: it started in 2015 as a prediction-market platform and today runs Gnosis Chain, a cheaper sister network of Ethereum, plus the Gnosis Pay card that lets people spend crypto from their own wallet. ## How it works

Gnosis Chain works like Ethereum: the same wallets, apps and smart contracts run on it, but transactions cost a fraction of a cent and blocks arrive every few seconds. Since a "merge" in December 2022 it has used proof of stake: validators lock GNO, confirm blocks and earn rewards, and anyone can stake through a validator or a staking pool. Because the entry ticket is only 1 GNO, the network has a large number of independent validators spread around the world. GnosisDAO, where GNO holders vote, decides on upgrades and how the project's treasury is spent.

## What the token is used for

GNO is the staking token of Gnosis Chain: validators must lock it, and they can lose part of it if they misbehave. It is also the voting token of GnosisDAO, which controls a large treasury and the direction of products such as Gnosis Pay. Since transaction fees are paid in xDAI, demand for GNO depends mainly on how many people want to stake it and how much the network and its payment products are used.

## Its story so far

Martin Köppelmann and Stefan George founded Gnosis in 2015. In April 2017 it sold about 5% of its tokens in a sale that raised roughly $12.5 million in under 15 minutes. Its multi-signature wallet, Gnosis Safe, became the standard way to guard crypto treasuries and was spun off as the separate project Safe in 2022, as was the CoW Swap exchange. In late 2021 Gnosis merged with the xDai chain to form Gnosis Chain, and GNO reached about $644 in November 2021. Gnosis Pay, a Visa debit card linked to a self-custody wallet, rolled out in Europe in 2024. After the Balancer exploit in November 2025, Gnosis Chain froze the attacker's funds on its network and later moved about $9.4 million back through a hard fork — a step that drew both praise and criticism.

## Alternatives

As a cheaper network for Ethereum apps, Gnosis Chain competes with Ethereum's layer-2 networks such as [Arbitrum]({lang}/arb/), [Optimism]({lang}/op/) and Base, and with other compatible chains such as [Polygon]({lang}/pol/). For crypto payment cards it competes with offers from big exchanges. Many people simply stake [Ethereum]({lang}/eth/) itself.

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** GNO has fallen far from its 2021 high and is thinly traded on most exchanges.
- **Competition:** Ethereum's layer-2 networks are larger, cheaper than before and attract most new apps.
- **Value capture:** fees are paid in xDAI, so heavy use of the chain does not directly create demand for GNO.
- **Governance:** the DAO and a few large holders can change rules; the 2025 hard fork showed the chain can be rewritten when they agree.
- **Regulation and security:** payment cards face financial rules, and bridges to other chains are a common target for hackers.

## What it means if you buy regularly

GNO's history since 2021 shows a sharp peak, a deep fall and long sideways years. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your entry price instead of betting on one moment, and staking can add a small yield, but neither protects you from a falling price. Many people keep smaller coins to a small part of their savings. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
