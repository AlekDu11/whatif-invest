**Salesforce** makes the software companies use to keep track of their customers: who bought what, which deal is close, which complaint is still open. Sales teams, call centres and marketing departments log in every day and the company pays a subscription per user. Since 2024 Salesforce has been adding AI "agents" that do part of that work on their own — and in 2026 investors argued about whether AI helps it or makes it less needed.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: $11.35 billion in the quarter to 31 July 2026, +11% a year; full-year guidance raised to $46.1–46.4 billion.
> - AI is growing fast from a small base: Agentforce and Data 360 recurring revenue nearly $3.9 billion, +210% a year.

## How it makes money

Almost everything is subscriptions: $10.82 billion of the $11.35 billion in the latest quarter came from "subscription and support". Salesforce now groups it into two blocks. Agentforce Apps ($7.19 billion, +8%) are the classic tools for sales, customer service and marketing, now with AI built in. Data 360, the platform and other products ($3.62 billion, +20%) cover data tools, the Slack messenger, Tableau charts and Informatica, a data-management company it bought for about $8 billion. Customers are mostly large and mid-sized businesses that sign multi-year contracts, which makes revenue fairly predictable.

In its second quarter of fiscal 2027 (to 31 July 2026) Salesforce earned $11.35 billion, up 11%, of which $456 million came from Informatica. GAAP operating margin was 20.5% and earnings per share were $4.29, more than double a year earlier. Operating cash flow was $1.27 billion. Contracted future revenue for the next 12 months (cRPO) reached $33.5 billion, up 14%. For the third quarter the company guided to $11.42–11.5 billion. In fiscal 2027 it paid a dividend of about $1.76 per share a year and was carrying out a $25 billion accelerated buyback. It also agreed to buy Fin, formerly Intercom, for $3.6 billion, and the content platform Contentful.

## The stock's story

Salesforce listed in 2004 and was one of the great cloud software winners. A wave of excitement about AI agents pushed the shares to a then-record close of $367.87 in December 2024. Then the mood flipped: in 2026 investors began to fear that AI tools — including coding agents such as Anthropic's Claude Code — would let companies build or replace software themselves, a sell-off nicknamed the "SaaS-pocalypse". The stock fell 40.9% in the first half of 2026 even though Salesforce beat forecasts each quarter. In the year to November 2026 it traded between $146.32 and $269.11.

## Competitors

In customer software Salesforce competes with Microsoft (Dynamics 365), Oracle, SAP, HubSpot for smaller firms and ServiceNow in service and IT workflows. In AI agents the new rivals are the AI model makers themselves, such as OpenAI and Anthropic. Salesforce's advantage is that it already holds years of customer data inside thousands of companies, and switching systems is slow and expensive.

## Main risks

- **AI could shrink the need for seats:** Salesforce has long charged per user; if AI agents replace some staff, fewer licences may be needed.
- **Slower core growth:** the classic apps grew 8% in the latest quarter; much of the headline growth now depends on acquisitions and the newer data business.
- **Big deals to digest:** Informatica, Fin and Contentful must be integrated, and acquisitions add cost and execution risk.
- **Cash spent on buybacks:** the $25 billion buyback lifts earnings per share but leaves less cash for a rainy day; in August 2026 full-year cash-flow growth was guided at only about 4–5%.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Salesforce is a profitable company with growing cash flow, but its stock can swing hard on sentiment: in the first half of 2026 it lost about two fifths of its value while results kept beating forecasts. Regular buying spreads purchases over such swings, yet one company can stay out of favour for years, so many people hold it as one part of a wider portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
