**Amgen** is one of the world's largest biotech companies: it makes medicines from living cells for heart disease, osteoporosis, cancer, asthma and rare diseases. Its drugs include Repatha (lowers "bad" cholesterol), Prolia and Evenity (bone loss), Enbrel (arthritis) and Tezspire (severe asthma). Based in Thousand Oaks, California, it employs about 31,500 people and trades on Nasdaq.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: $10.4 billion in Q3 2026, +8% a year, driven by more patients rather than higher prices.
> - Repatha sold $1.09 billion in the quarter (+37%), while Prolia fell 45% after cheaper copies (biosimilars) arrived.
> - Dividend: $2.52 a share per quarter, $10.08 a year, up 6%.
> - Debt is large: $54.0 billion against $13.2 billion in cash, mostly from the $27.8 billion Horizon deal of 2023.

## How it makes money

Amgen sells branded medicines to hospitals, pharmacies and health systems, mostly in the US. Growth now comes from newer drugs: in Q3 2026 Evenity rose 39%, Tezspire 43%, the leukaemia drug Blincyto 27% and Uplizna, a rare-disease drug that came with the Horizon purchase, almost tripled. Amgen also sells its own biosimilars — cheaper copies of other companies' biologic drugs — such as Pavblu (eye disease), which made $300 million in the quarter. The other side: older blockbusters are losing patent protection. Prolia and Xgeva sales roughly halved after copies launched, and Enbrel's net price fell 14% because Medicare now sets its price.

In the quarter to 30 September 2026 Amgen's revenue was $10.4 billion, with 17 products growing by double digits. GAAP earnings per share were $7.08 (+19%) and the adjusted figure $6.45 (+14%). Free cash flow was $3.7 billion, and the company bought back no shares that quarter. For full-year 2026 Amgen expects $38.9–39.7 billion of revenue and adjusted earnings of $22.90–23.70 per share. Net profit over the twelve months to September 2026 was about $9.4 billion, a 24% margin.

## The stock's story

Amgen is a mature, slow-moving stock by tech standards, but 2026 was a strong year: the shares set a new high in March 2026 and kept climbing, trading between about $312 and $484 in the 12 months to November 2026. Investors were rewarding growth from Repatha and Evenity and the hope pinned on MariTide, a once-a-month obesity injection that was in large Phase 3 trials in late 2026, including a head-to-head study against semaglutide (the ingredient in Ozempic and Wegovy). MariTide had no sales as of November 2026.

## Competitors

In cholesterol and bone drugs Amgen faces Novartis, Regeneron and makers of biosimilars such as Sandoz. In inflammation it competes with AbbVie, Johnson & Johnson and Pfizer. In obesity, the race MariTide wants to join, Eli Lilly and Novo Nordisk are far ahead. Amgen's strengths are its manufacturing scale in biologic drugs and a broad portfolio, so no single drug dominates.

## Main risks

- **Patent cliffs:** biosimilars are already halving Prolia and Xgeva, and more Amgen drugs will lose protection over the coming years.
- **US drug pricing:** Medicare price setting already cut Enbrel's net price by 14%; more drugs can be added to the list.
- **Obesity bet:** much of the 2026 rally rests on MariTide; weak Phase 3 data or side-effect issues would hurt.
- **Regulators:** the FDA has asked Amgen to withdraw Tavneos, a drug for a rare vasculitis, from the US market; Amgen is contesting it.
- **Debt:** $54 billion of borrowing limits buybacks and leaves less room for big new deals.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Amgen is a steady, dividend-paying company: $10.08 a share a year, with regular increases. Reinvesting that dividend adds up over a decade. But a single drugmaker can stall for years when patents expire or a key trial fails, and the strong 2026 run meant the shares priced in a lot of good news. Regular buying spreads your entry price over time; it does not remove those risks. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
