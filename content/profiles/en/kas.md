**Kaspa** is a proof-of-work cryptocurrency, mined like bitcoin, that was built to be much faster: instead of one chain of blocks it uses a "blockDAG", where many blocks can be added at the same time. ## How it works

In bitcoin, if two miners find a block at the same moment, one of the blocks is thrown away, so blocks must be slow (about every 10 minutes) to keep this rare. Kaspa keeps all such parallel blocks and links them into a graph — a **blockDAG** (directed acyclic graph). Its **GHOSTDAG** protocol, based on academic research by Yonatan Sompolinsky and Aviv Zohar, then puts all blocks and transactions into one agreed order. This lets Kaspa create blocks very often and confirm payments in seconds while keeping bitcoin-style mining with its own kHeavyHash algorithm. The coin supply falls smoothly: the mining reward shrinks a little every month and halves about once a year.

## Why people think it has value

Kaspa's supporters see it as "fast digital silver": it keeps what people like about bitcoin — proof of work, a fixed supply, no company and no pre-allocated coins — but payments settle in seconds instead of minutes. The fair launch matters to many holders, because no team or fund received cheap coins to sell later. In 2026 the network also began adding programmability: the Toccata upgrade brought covenants (rules for how coins can be spent), zero-knowledge proof checks and native tokens, which could let developers build apps on Kaspa.

## Its story so far

Kaspa grew out of the research and the company DAGlabs; its mainnet started on 7 November 2021. For the first year it traded only on small exchanges for fractions of a cent. Larger exchanges listed it in 2023, and the price rose many times over, reaching its record of about $0.21 in mid-2024. Developers rewrote the node software in Rust ("Rusty Kaspa"), which made the jump in speed possible: on 5 May 2025 the Crescendo hard fork raised the network from one to ten blocks per second. The price fell sharply over 2025 and 2026, and in 2026 the Toccata hard fork turned Kaspa into a programmable layer 1.

## Alternatives

Kaspa competes most directly with other proof-of-work payment coins such as [Bitcoin]({lang}/btc/), [Litecoin]({lang}/ltc/) and [Bitcoin Cash]({lang}/bch/). For fast, cheap transfers and apps people also use proof-of-stake networks such as [Solana]({lang}/sol/), and for everyday payments many simply use dollar stablecoins.

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** KAS has fallen by more than 80% from its 2024 record before; such drops can happen again.
- **Mining concentration:** a few large mining pools and ASIC makers control much of the hash power.
- **Small team:** development relies on a group of core developers funded mostly by community donations, not a large company.
- **Adoption:** speed alone does not create demand; Toccata apps and real payments still have to catch on.
- **Market access:** much of the trading happens on mid-size exchanges, and rules for crypto can change by country.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Kaspa is a young coin with very sharp ups and downs. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your entry price over good and bad months, but it does not protect you from a long decline. Most new KAS has already been mined, so future price moves depend mainly on demand. Many people keep such coins to a small part of their crypto. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
