**HSBC** is one of the world's largest banks: it takes deposits, lends to people and companies, manages wealth and moves money across borders for businesses. It is headquartered in London, but it was born in Hong Kong in 1865 and still earns most of its profit in Asia, especially Hong Kong. Its shares trade in London as HSBA and in Hong Kong as 0005.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Profit before tax: $9.6 billion in the third quarter of 2026, up 32% on a year earlier; $29.2 billion in the first nine months.
> - Return on tangible equity was 18.0% in January–September 2026; HSBC aims for 17% or better each year from 2026 to 2028.
> - It declared a third interim dividend of $0.10 per share for 2026 and planned another share buyback of up to $1 billion.
> - CEO Georges Elhedery is simplifying the bank: about $1.9 billion of yearly cost savings were actioned by September 2026.

## How it makes money

Most of HSBC's money is interest: it pays savers less than it charges borrowers. In the third quarter of 2026 net interest income was $9.3 billion out of $18.6 billion of revenue. The rest is fees — from wealth management, insurance, cards, trade finance and payments for multinational companies, and markets and banking services for big corporate clients. Wealth fees and other income from that business were $8.2 billion in the first nine months of 2026, up from $7.2 billion a year earlier.

HSBC has four main businesses: Hong Kong, the UK ring-fenced bank, corporate and institutional banking, and international wealth and premier banking. Its strength is connecting money between Asia, the Middle East, Europe and the Americas; a factory in China and a buyer in Germany can bank with the same group.

## The stock's story

The Hongkong and Shanghai Banking Corporation was founded in 1865 to finance trade between Asia and Europe. In 1992 it bought Britain's Midland Bank, and in 1993 the group moved its headquarters to London. In the 2008 financial crisis HSBC avoided a government bailout, but its shares fell sharply and in 2009 it raised about £12.5 billion from shareholders. In 2012 it paid $1.9 billion to settle US money-laundering charges.

In March 2020, at the request of the Bank of England, HSBC cancelled its dividend during the Covid crisis, and later that year the shares fell to their lowest level since the 1990s. As interest rates rose from 2022, profits jumped and dividends returned. HSBC sold its Canadian bank to RBC in 2024, Georges Elhedery became CEO in September 2024 and started a big simplification, and in January 2026 HSBC completed the buyout of the rest of Hang Seng Bank, taking it off the Hong Kong stock market.

## Competitors

In Hong Kong HSBC competes with Standard Chartered, Bank of China (Hong Kong) and Chinese state banks. In the UK it fights Lloyds, Barclays and NatWest. In global transaction banking and wealth its rivals are JPMorgan, Citigroup, UBS and Standard Chartered, and in Asian wealth management also DBS.

## Main risks

- **Interest rates:** lower rates shrink the gap between what HSBC earns on loans and pays on deposits.
- **Hong Kong and China:** a large part of profit comes from Hong Kong; a weak Chinese economy or more losses on Hong Kong commercial property would hurt.
- **Geopolitics:** HSBC sits between the US, the UK and China, so sanctions and political tension create legal and business risk.
- **Fines and rules:** banks are tightly regulated; past compliance failures cost HSBC billions.
- **Dividends are not fixed:** as in 2020, regulators or a crisis can stop payouts.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Many people buy HSBC for its dividend, but bank shares can fall hard in a crisis, and the 2020 cut showed that payouts can stop. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more shares when the price is low and fewer when it is high, without trying to time interest rates or the Chinese economy. Many people hold one bank only as part of a wider portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
