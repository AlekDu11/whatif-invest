**Tokyo Electron** (TEL) does not make chips — it makes the machines that chipmakers use to build them: tools that coat, etch, clean and lay down the ultra-thin layers on silicon wafers. Its customers are TSMC, Samsung, SK hynix, Intel, Micron and Chinese chip factories, so its sales rise and fall with how much they spend on new plants. The shares trade in Tokyo under the code 8035; on 1 October 2026 every old share was split into five.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Sales: ¥1,667.5 billion in April–September 2026, +41% on the year; operating profit ¥506.8 billion, a 30.4% margin.
> - First full-year forecast for the year to March 2027: sales ¥3.7 trillion (+51%), operating profit ¥1,169 billion, net income ¥865 billion.
> - In July–September 2026 China bought about 36% of everything TEL sold; logic and foundry chips took 56% of new-machine sales, memory (DRAM) 34%.
> - For shareholders: a five-for-one split on 1 October 2026, a buyback of up to ¥150 billion (November 2026 – March 2027) and a dividend policy of paying out about half of profit.

## How it makes money

A modern chip factory needs hundreds of machines, and TEL sells several of the key types. It is best known for coater/developers — the tracks that spread light-sensitive film on a wafer before and after the lithography scanner prints the pattern — and it also sells etching, deposition (film-making) and cleaning tools plus wafer probers that test chips. In July–September 2026 about four-fifths of sales came from new equipment; the rest came from servicing, parts, upgrades and refurbished tools for the thousands of TEL machines already installed in factories, a steadier income stream. The 2026 boom was driven by artificial intelligence: AI accelerators need the newest logic chips, and they come with large amounts of fast DRAM memory.

In the first half of the year to March 2027 (April–September 2026) net income rose 56% to ¥376.9 billion. Results beat TEL's own July forecast, which had pencilled in ¥1.62 trillion of sales and ¥458 billion of operating profit. On 30 October 2026 the company published its first forecast for the whole year: ¥3.7 trillion of sales and ¥865 billion of net income, which means it expected a stronger second half. TEL estimated that the world would spend about $160 billion on wafer-fab equipment in 2026 and more than $210 billion in 2027. The interim dividend was ¥415 per pre-split share; the year-end dividend was forecast at ¥108 per new share (¥540 before the split).

## The stock's story

TEL was founded in Tokyo in 1963 as a small trading company that imported chip equipment, and later began building its own machines. Because chipmakers cut orders sharply in downturns, the stock has always moved in big waves: it fell hard in the 2000–01 dot-com bust and again in the 2008–09 financial crisis. In 2013 TEL agreed to merge with its US rival Applied Materials, but the two dropped the deal in April 2015 after US competition regulators objected. From 2023 the AI spending boom lifted chip-equipment shares worldwide, while US limits on selling advanced tools to China became a recurring worry. In 2026 TEL posted a record first half, split its shares five-for-one and announced its biggest buyback plan of the year.

## Competitors

TEL's main rivals are Applied Materials, Lam Research and KLA in the US and, in Japan, Screen Holdings (cleaning and coating) and Kokusai Electric. ASML of the Netherlands makes the lithography scanners that TEL's tracks work with, so it is both a partner and a fellow supplier. In China, local makers such as Naura and AMEC are growing with government support.

## Main risks

- **The chip cycle:** when chipmakers stop building factories, equipment orders can fall by a third or more within a year.
- **China and export rules:** China bought about 36% of TEL's sales in July–September 2026; tighter US or Japanese export controls, or Chinese rivals, could cut that.
- **A few huge customers:** a handful of chipmakers place most orders, so one delayed factory matters.
- **The yen:** a stronger yen lowers the value of overseas sales in TEL's yen-based results.
- **High expectations:** after a record half-year a lot of growth is already expected, and the shares fell on results day in October 2026 even though the numbers beat the forecast.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Tokyo Electron is a highly profitable world leader, but its shares swing much more than the market because they follow the chip cycle. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more shares when the cycle turns down and fewer near the peaks, without trying to guess the turning point. Many people hold a cyclical stock like this only as part of a wider portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
