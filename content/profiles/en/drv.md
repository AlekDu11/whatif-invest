**Derive** is an on-chain exchange for crypto options and perpetual futures — the kind of trading usually done on big centralized platforms — where users keep control of their own funds. It started in 2021 as Lyra Finance and renamed itself Derive in August 2024.

## How it works

Orders are matched off-chain by a fast order book, while accounts, margin and final settlement are recorded on blockchain infrastructure built on Ethereum. Traders can use one account with several kinds of collateral and "portfolio margin", which looks at the risk of all positions together. Besides options and perpetuals, Derive offers spot trading, vaults and structured products. The team is led by co-founder and CEO Nick Forster, a former options trader at Susquehanna.

## What DRV is for

DRV launched in January 2025, swapped 1:1 for the old LYRA token. Holders can stake it to get stDRV, which gives a vote on upgrades and earns weekly rewards; unstaking takes 7 days, or is instant with a 20% penalty. According to Derive's docs, 35% of protocol revenue is used to buy DRV on the open market. Total supply is fixed at 1.5 billion, and no new tokens will be minted.

## Why it matters

On-chain options are still a small niche next to centralized venues such as Deribit, and Derive is one of its best-known players. Surprising fact: on November 21, 2026, DRV jumped about 26% in a day while bitcoin fell to a two-year low — with no confirmed news behind the move.

## Main risks

DRV is a small token (market value of roughly $330 million), so it can move 20–30% in a day either way. About 1.06 billion of 1.5 billion DRV circulate; no detailed unlock schedule for the rest is public, and new supply could weigh on the price. Derivatives trading is risky in itself, and bugs or a failure of the exchange's infrastructure could cause losses. Rules for crypto derivatives are strict in many countries. Buying a fixed amount regularly smooths the swings but does not remove the risk.
