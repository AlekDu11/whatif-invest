**Derive** is an on-chain exchange for crypto options and perpetual futures — the kind of trading usually done on big centralized platforms — where users keep control of their own funds. It started in 2021 as Lyra Finance and renamed itself Derive in August 2024. Today all DRV in circulation is worth {{mcap:drv}} ({{rank:drv}} among cryptocurrencies).

## How it works

Orders are matched off-chain by a fast order book, like on a regular exchange, while accounts, margin and final settlement are recorded on Derive's own network built on Ethereum. Traders can post several kinds of collateral in one account and use "portfolio margin", which looks at the risk of all positions together instead of one by one. Besides options and perpetuals, Derive offers spot trading, vaults and structured products. The team is led by co-founder and CEO Nick Forster, a former options trader at Susquehanna.

## What the token is used for

Holders can stake DRV to get stDRV, which gives a vote on upgrades and earns weekly rewards. Unstaking takes 7 days, or is instant with a 20% penalty. Part of the exchange's revenue goes into buying DRV on the market, so more trading means more buying. No new tokens beyond the 1.5 billion will be minted, but the tokens that do not yet circulate can still come onto the market.

## Its story so far

Lyra launched in 2021 as one of the first options markets built on Ethereum's layer-2 networks, letting users buy and sell options against shared pools of money. Over time it moved to an order-book design and its own chain, and in August 2024 it rebranded as Derive. The DRV token replaced LYRA in January 2025. After a low in April 2025 the price rose sharply through 2025 and 2026 and set its record in October 2026. On 21 November 2026 DRV jumped about 26% in a day while bitcoin fell to a two-year low, with no confirmed news behind the move.

## Alternatives

For on-chain perpetuals the biggest rival is [Hyperliquid]({lang}/hype/), with [dYdX]({lang}/dydx/) and [GMX]({lang}/gmx/) also popular. For crypto options most volume still goes through centralized venues such as Deribit, now owned by Coinbase, so on-chain options remain a small niche.

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** DRV is a small token and can move 20–30% in a day either way.
- **Unlocks:** about 440 million DRV are not yet in circulation, and no detailed public schedule shows when they arrive.
- **Competition:** larger exchanges, both on-chain and centralized, fight for the same traders.
- **Technology:** bugs in smart contracts or a failure of the order book or chain could cause losses.
- **Regulation:** many countries restrict crypto derivatives for ordinary investors, which limits who can use the platform.

## What it means if you buy regularly

DRV has less than two years of history, with a huge rise from a very low starting point and sharp drops along the way. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more when the price is low and less when it is high, without trying to guess the bottom — but it does not remove the risk of losses. Many people keep small tokens like DRV to a tiny part of their savings that they can afford to see fall sharply. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
