**Cisco** makes the equipment that moves internet traffic: routers, switches, Wi-Fi access points and the optical gear that links data centres. Almost every large company, telecom operator and cloud provider uses it. On top of the hardware it sells security software, the Webex video-call service and Splunk, which watches computer systems for problems and attacks. In 2026 a new source of growth arrived: the networks inside AI data centres.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: $63.3 billion in fiscal 2026 (to 25 July 2026), +12%; GAAP net income $13.3 billion, +30%.
> - In the August–October 2026 quarter revenue jumped 24% to $18.5 billion, Cisco's fastest growth in over 15 years.
> - Cisco took $9.3 billion of AI infrastructure orders from cloud giants in fiscal 2026.
> - In December 2025 the shares closed above their March 2000 dot-com record for the first time in more than 25 years.

## How it makes money

Most of Cisco's money comes from selling products — about three-quarters of revenue in fiscal 2026. Networking is by far the largest part: $34.7 billion in fiscal 2026, up 22%, as cloud companies such as Microsoft, Google and Meta bought high-speed switches, routers and optics for AI clusters. Security brought in $8.2 billion, collaboration (Webex, meeting-room devices, call-centre software) $4.3 billion and observability (tools that monitor apps and networks, including part of Splunk) $1.1 billion. The other quarter, $15.0 billion, is services — support contracts and subscriptions that customers renew every year, which makes the income steadier.

In the first quarter of fiscal 2027 (to 24 October 2026) revenue was $18.5 billion, up 24%. Product revenue rose 32% and networking alone 43%, to $11.1 billion, while services grew only 1%. GAAP net income was $4.6 billion, or $1.14 per share (non-GAAP $1.40, +40%). Gross margin slipped to 63.7% from 65.5% because AI networking hardware is less profitable than software. Customers had $46.2 billion of contracted but not yet delivered business (remaining performance obligations), up 8%.

After that quarter Cisco raised its fiscal 2027 forecast to $74.8–75.8 billion of revenue and $5.47–5.53 of non-GAAP earnings per share, and said it expects about $9 billion of AI infrastructure revenue from cloud giants in fiscal 2027, more than double fiscal 2026. It pays a dividend of $0.42 per share a quarter and spent $2.0 billion on buybacks in the quarter.

## The stock's story

Cisco was founded in 1984 by two Stanford University computer staff, Len Bosack and Sandy Lerner, and listed its shares in 1990. Its routers became the plumbing of the early internet, and in March 2000, at the height of the dot-com bubble, it was briefly the world's most valuable company. When the bubble burst, the stock lost most of its value within about two years and then spent a quarter century below that peak, even though the company kept growing and began paying a dividend in 2011. Chuck Robbins became CEO in 2015 and shifted the business toward software and subscriptions; in March 2024 Cisco completed its biggest-ever purchase, the data-analytics company Splunk. AI then revived demand for networking hardware, and on 10 December 2025 the shares closed above their 2000 record for the first time.

## Competitors

In data-centre and AI networking Cisco competes with **Arista Networks**, **Nvidia** (which sells its own networking equipment alongside its AI chips), **Juniper**, now part of **Hewlett Packard Enterprise**, and Chinese maker **Huawei** in many markets. In security it faces **Palo Alto Networks**, **Fortinet**, **CrowdStrike** and **Zscaler**; in collaboration, **Microsoft Teams** and **Zoom**.

## Main risks

- **AI spending cycle:** much of the recent growth depends on a few cloud giants; if they cut their data-centre budgets, orders could fall quickly.
- **Lower margins:** AI hardware earns less per dollar than software, so faster sales do not always mean equally faster profit.
- **Competition:** Arista and Nvidia are strong in the fastest-growing AI networks, and big customers can design their own equipment.
- **Slow parts of the business:** services grew just 1% and security 7% in the first quarter of fiscal 2027.
- **History lesson:** the 2000 crash showed that a great company can be a bad investment if the share price runs far ahead of profits.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Cisco is a mature, very profitable company with a dividend, now growing faster thanks to AI. But its own history is the warning: people who bought at the 2000 peak waited more than 25 years to get back to even on price. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more shares when the price is low and fewer when it is high, so you never put all your money in at the top. Many people hold one company only as part of a wider portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
