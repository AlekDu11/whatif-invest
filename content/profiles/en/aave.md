**Aave** is the largest lending app in crypto: a set of smart contracts where people deposit coins to earn interest and others borrow against their own crypto, with no bank in between. It grew out of ETHLend, a project started by Stani Kulechov in 2017, and has run under the Aave name since 2020. ## How it works

Lenders put coins such as USDC or ETH into shared pools and earn interest. Borrowers must first deposit collateral worth more than the loan; if the value of that collateral falls too far, the contracts sell part of it automatically (a "liquidation") so lenders get paid back. Interest rates move by formula: the more of a pool is borrowed, the higher the rate. Aave runs on Ethereum and many other networks, and it also issues its own dollar stablecoin, GHO, launched in July 2023. V4 (March 2026) pools liquidity in "hubs" with different risk levels that lend to separate markets under set limits.

## What the token is used for

AAVE is the governance token: holders vote on which coins can be used, on risk settings and on how the treasury is spent. Holders can also stake AAVE as a backstop: they earn rewards, but part of the stake can be used to cover losses if the protocol runs short. Since 2025 the Aave DAO has used protocol income to buy AAVE on the market, and in October 2026 founder Stani Kulechov presented a plan to keep doing so, with part of the bought tokens going to stakers and the rest burned.

## Its story so far

ETHLend raised about $16 million in a token sale in November 2017 and was renamed Aave — Finnish for "ghost" — in 2018. The Aave protocol launched on Ethereum in January 2020 and became known for "flash loans", loans that are borrowed and repaid within a single transaction. In October 2020 the old LEND token was swapped for AAVE at 100 to 1. During the 2021 boom AAVE peaked at about $660 in May, then fell more than 90% by 2022. Version 3 arrived in March 2022 and GHO in July 2023. In December 2025 the SEC ended its probe, and on 30 March 2026 V4 went live after a governance vote that passed with about 60% support.

## Alternatives

Other lending protocols compete for the same deposits: [Compound]({lang}/comp/), one of the first DeFi lenders, [Morpho]({lang}/morpho/), which builds lending markets on its own design, and Spark, part of the [Sky]({lang}/sky/) ecosystem (formerly Maker). Centralized exchanges also offer interest and loans, but there you trust a company with your coins.

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** AAVE has lost more than 90% of its value before, and DeFi tokens often fall harder than bitcoin.
- **Technology:** a smart-contract bug, a faulty price feed or a fast crash could cause losses; in November 2022 a large attack on the CRV market left Aave with bad debt.
- **Governance:** big holders and Aave Labs have strong influence, and holders do not always agree, as the split vote on V4 showed.
- **Regulation:** the SEC probe ended, but rules for DeFi lending and stablecoins such as GHO can still change by country.
- **Competition:** if deposits move to rival lenders, fees and buybacks shrink.

## What it means if you buy regularly

AAVE's history shows a fast rise in 2021 and a long period far below its peak. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more when the price is low and less when it is high, without trying to guess the bottom — but it does not remove the risk of losses. Many people keep tokens like AAVE to a small part of their savings that they can afford to see fall sharply. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
