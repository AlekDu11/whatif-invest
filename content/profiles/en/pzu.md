**PZU** (Powszechny Zakład Ubezpieczeń) is Poland's largest and oldest insurer: it insures cars, homes, farms and companies, sells life and health cover, and runs pension and investment funds. Through big stakes in Bank Pekao and Alior Bank it is also part of Polish banking. Its roots go back to 1803, it has been listed on the Warsaw Stock Exchange since May 2010, and the Polish state owns about 34% of it.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Net profit: a record 6.7 billion zł in 2025 (+25.4%); 3.0 billion zł in the first half of 2026 (−6.8%).
> - Dividend: 4.80 zł a share, 4.14 billion zł in total, paid on 8 October 2026.
> - The stock rose more than 57% in 2025 and set new records in 2026, trading near 77 zł at its 52-week high.
> - The planned merger with Bank Pekao, first aimed for mid-2026, is stuck waiting for law changes.

## How it makes money

The core is **insurance**: customers pay premiums, PZU pays claims and keeps the difference plus investment income on the money. In Q2 2026 it paid out and spent 87.9 zł for every 100 zł of non-life premiums earned (a combined ratio of 87.9%) — below 100 means underwriting is profitable. Motor insurance is the biggest mass product, alongside property, farm, corporate, group life and a growing health business with 3.6 million contracts. PZU also operates in Lithuania, Latvia, Estonia and Ukraine. Its fund managers (TFI PZU and PTE PZU) look after 88.5 billion zł of clients' money. Finally, it consolidates **banking** results: it owns about 20% of Pekao and 31.9% of Alior, and the banks added 801 million zł to group profit in the first half of 2026.

## The numbers right now

In 2025 the group earned 6.7 billion zł for shareholders, with gross insurance revenue up 5% to 30.9 billion zł and an adjusted return on equity of 20.7%. The first half of 2026 was weaker: profit of 3.0 billion zł versus 3.23 billion a year earlier, because of lower interest rates, higher taxes, court rulings hitting its banks, rising claim costs and tough price competition in motor insurance. Still, Q2 2026 alone grew 12% to 1.647 billion zł. Insurance revenue in the half was 15.65 billion zł, and the Solvency II capital ratio was a comfortable 230% at the end of March. PZU's Q3 2026 report is scheduled for 19 November 2026.

## The stock's story

PZU is a classic dividend stock: much of the return has come from regular payouts rather than price jumps. In 2025 the share price climbed from just above 42 zł to about 67 zł, a record at the time, and it went higher in 2026, reaching about 77 zł. The year was also turbulent at the top: after the change of government three different people led the company in 2025, and Bogdan Benczak became CEO at the end of that year. ## Competitors

In Polish insurance PZU competes with Warta (owned by Germany's Talanx), Ergo Hestia (part of Munich Re), Allianz, Generali and UNIQA. Its edge is size: more customers than any other Polish insurer, a national agent network and banks inside the group.

## Main risks

- **Merger uncertainty:** the Pekao deal needs new laws, and a political standoff between the government and President Karol Nawrocki has stalled them; an alternative structure is being studied.
- **State control:** the government is the largest shareholder and has replaced management several times, so political goals can weigh on decisions.
- **Lower interest rates** reduce investment income and bank profits.
- **Claims inflation and motor price wars** squeeze insurance margins.
- **Currency:** for a non-Polish investor the zloty's moves add another layer of gains or losses.

## What it means if you buy regularly

PZU is a large, profitable company that pays out much of its profit, so regular buying is usually a calmer ride than with young tech stocks. But it is one country's insurer and bank group, exposed to Polish politics, rates and the zloty. Dividends can be cut if profits fall. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
