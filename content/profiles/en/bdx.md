**Becton, Dickinson and Company (BD)** is one of the world's largest makers of medical supplies, based in Franklin Lakes, New Jersey. If you have had an injection, a blood test or an IV drip in hospital, there is a good chance the needle, syringe, catheter or infusion pump came from BD. It also makes prefilled-syringe parts for drug companies, surgical products such as hernia mesh, and robots that sort and dispense medicines in hospital pharmacies. In February 2026 it handed its laboratory and diagnostics business to Waters, so today's BD is a purer medical-device company.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Sales: $19.5 billion in fiscal 2026 (year to 30 September), up 4.9%; adjusted earnings per share were $12.70.
> - On 9 February 2026 BD completed the combination of its Biosciences and Diagnostic Solutions business with Waters and received about $3.9 billion in cash.
> - For fiscal 2027 the company expected low single-digit sales growth and adjusted EPS of $13.10–13.30.
> - BD has paid a dividend for decades and has raised it every year for more than 50 years.

## How it makes money

After the Waters deal BD reports four segments (fiscal 2026 sales):

- **Medical Essentials** ($6.7 billion): syringes, needles, IV catheters and blood-collection tubes — cheap items hospitals use by the billion every year.
- **Interventional** ($5.6 billion): products from the former C. R. Bard, bought in 2017 for about $24 billion — vascular stents and catheters, urology and hernia repair.
- **Connected Care** ($4.7 billion): Alaris infusion pumps, Pyxis medication cabinets and pharmacy automation.
- **BioPharma Systems** ($2.4 billion): prefillable syringes and other parts that drug makers use to package injectable medicines, including the new weight-loss and diabetes drugs.

Much of this is repeat business: hospitals keep buying the same disposable products, which makes sales steadier than at many tech companies.

## The stock's story

Maxwell Becton and Fairleigh Dickinson founded the company in New York in 1897 to sell thermometers and syringes. BD grew mostly through steady products and large deals: CareFusion (Alaris, Pyxis) in 2015 and Bard in 2017. In WhatIfStack's data the shares climbed for most of the 2000s and 2010s, then went sideways from 2020 to 2024 while BD dealt with an FDA recall and sales halt of Alaris pumps and with the cost of the Bard deal. In 2025 the shares dropped sharply after BD cut its outlook because of US tariffs and weaker demand in China and in research labs. In July 2025 BD agreed to combine its lab unit with Waters; after that closed in February 2026 and results improved, the shares recovered strongly. Tom Polen has been CEO since 2020.

## Competitors

In medical supplies BD competes with Medtronic, Baxter, B. Braun, Cardinal Health and Terumo; in vascular and surgical products with Boston Scientific and Medtronic; in pharmacy automation with Omnicell; and in prefilled syringes with suppliers such as Gerresheimer, Stevanato and West Pharmaceutical.

## Main risks

- **Slow growth:** many of BD's products are mature, so sales usually grow only a few percent a year.
- **Prices and tenders:** hospital buying groups push prices down, and China's volume-based procurement tenders have cut prices for medical products there.
- **Tariffs and supply chains:** BD makes and buys products worldwide; trade rules changed several times in 2025–26.
- **Product quality and regulators:** the Alaris recall showed how an FDA problem can stop sales of a key product for years.
- **Lawsuits:** BD has faced large product-liability cases, including over Bard hernia mesh.

## What it means if you buy regularly

BD is a slow, steady business that still had a long flat stretch: in mid-2025 the shares were lower than in early 2018. For someone buying a fixed amount every month, years like that mean buying more shares at similar prices, and the rising dividend adds to the return while the price waits. A single medical-supply company is still one company with its own recalls and lawsuits, so many people hold it as part of a wider portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
