**ING Bank Śląski** is one of Poland's largest banks: about 4.9 million people keep their salary account, card, mortgage or savings there, and hundreds of thousands of Polish firms use it for business banking. It is listed on the Warsaw Stock Exchange, but 75% of the shares belong to the Dutch group ING, so it works as ING's Polish arm. In Q3 2026 it earned PLN 1.13 billion (zł) of net profit.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Net profit: PLN 1.13 billion in Q3 2026, +1.9% a year; PLN 3.15 billion in nine months, −3% because of a higher tax on banks.
> - Return on equity of 19.7% — very high for a European bank.
> - Dividend for 2025: 26.71 zł a share, 75% of profit, PLN 3.48 billion in total.
> - Deposits of PLN 264.7 billion and loans of PLN 189.5 billion at the end of September 2026.

## How it makes money

Most of the money comes from the gap between what the bank earns on loans and bonds and what it pays on deposits. Net interest income was PLN 2.44 billion in Q3 2026, up 11.5% a year, with a net interest margin of 3.14%. Fees and commissions — cards, accounts, investment funds, brokerage — added PLN 692 million, up 15.7%. Corporate loans grew 9%, mortgages 8% and cash loans 14% year on year.

Investing is a growing part of the business. In April 2026 the bank completed the purchase of Goldman Sachs TFI, a fund manager with PLN 56 billion of assets and more than 778,000 clients, for PLN 405 million; it is being renamed ING TFI.

## The numbers right now

In the quarter to 30 September 2026 total income was PLN 3.16 billion. Costs including the Polish bank tax were PLN 1.34 billion, so the cost-to-income ratio was 42.4%. Loan-loss provisions fell more than 71% from a year earlier because the bank set aside much less for corporate defaults. The total capital ratio — the safety cushion regulators watch — was 15.09%.

Profit for the first nine months was 3% lower than a year earlier. The main reason, according to the bank, is that Poland raised corporate income tax for banks from 19% to 30% in 2026. CEO Michał Bolesławski called Q3 a quarter of "high uncertainty", yet the results beat analysts' forecasts.

## The stock's story

ING Bank Śląski has been listed in Warsaw since 1994. Like other Polish banks, its shares fell hard in 2022, when the war in Ukraine started and the government made banks offer mortgage "credit holidays". As interest rates stayed high, profits jumped, and in 2025–2026 the shares rose to the highest levels in their history. Generous dividends — 33.35 zł for 2023, 25.18 zł for 2024 and 26.71 zł for 2025 — are a big part of the return.

## Competitors

It competes with PKO Bank Polski, Bank Pekao, Santander Bank Polska and mBank for the same customers. Its strengths are the backing of a global group, fast growth in retail clients — almost 100,000 new ones in each of the last two quarters — and a strong position in mortgages and business banking.

## Main risks

- **Lower interest rates:** most profit comes from interest, so cuts by the National Bank of Poland would squeeze income.
- **Taxes and politics:** Poland already taxes bank assets and has raised banks' income tax to 30%; governments can add new burdens, as with credit holidays in 2022.
- **Lawsuits:** at the end of September the bank faced 412 cases over WIBOR-linked mortgages and 124 "free loan" cases over consumer credit terms.
- **Controlled by ING Groep:** the parent decides strategy and dividends; only about 13% of shares are held by ordinary market investors.
- **Currency:** for investors outside Poland, a weaker zloty cuts returns in dollars or euros.

## What it means if you buy regularly

ING Bank Śląski is a mature, profitable bank, not a fast-growth story, and much of its return comes from dividends. Buying monthly spreads purchases across good and bad years for Polish banks, but shares like this can fall sharply when rates drop or the state changes the rules. Many people hold a single bank as a small part of a wider portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
