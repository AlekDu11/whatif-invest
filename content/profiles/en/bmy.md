**Bristol Myers Squibb** (BMS) is a US drugmaker based in Princeton, New Jersey. It sells medicines for cancer, blood diseases, heart conditions, immune disorders and schizophrenia — its best-known products are the blood thinner Eliquis, made with Pfizer, and the cancer immunotherapy Opdivo. The big question for the company is what replaces these two when their key patents expire around 2028.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: $13.1 billion in the September 2026 quarter, +7%; the company expects about $49.75–50.25 billion for 2026.
> - Eliquis alone brought in $4.7 billion in the quarter (+25%), more than a third of all sales.
> - Newer "growth" drugs earned $7.7 billion (+12%), while older ones grew only 1%.
> - Revlimid sales fell 47% in a year after generic copies arrived — a preview of what patent expiry does.

## How it makes money

BMS splits its products into two groups. The Growth Portfolio — newer medicines with long patent life left — earned $7.66 billion in the quarter: cancer drugs Opdivo ($2.37 billion), the under-the-skin version Opdivo Qvantig ($334 million), Yervoy and Opdualag; the anaemia drug Reblozyl ($750 million); the CAR-T cell therapy Breyanzi ($526 million); the heart drug Camzyos ($420 million); and Cobenfy for schizophrenia. The Legacy Portfolio earned $5.42 billion, mostly from Eliquis ($4.69 billion), plus older cancer drugs Revlimid, Pomalyst and Sprycel that are losing sales to generics. About 70% of revenue comes from the United States.

In the September 2026 quarter BMS had revenue of $13.07 billion, up from $12.22 billion a year earlier. Profit per share was $1.05 under standard accounting and $1.35 on the company's adjusted basis, both lower than a year before because of about $0.58 per share of charges for buying drugs still in development. In October it raised its 2026 revenue forecast to about $49.75–50.25 billion but cut its adjusted profit forecast to $6.35–6.55 per share, again because of those deal costs. At the end of September it held about $13 billion in cash and securities against roughly $43 billion of debt. BMS pays a quarterly dividend.

## The stock's story

Bristol-Myers and Squibb, two old American drug companies, merged in 1989. In 2019 BMS bought Celgene for about $74 billion, gaining the blood-cancer drug Revlimid — then one of the world's best-selling medicines — and the drugs that became Reblozyl and Breyanzi. In 2024 it paid about $14 billion for Karuna Therapeutics; its drug Cobenfy, approved in the US that September, was the first schizophrenia medicine with a new mechanism in decades. Since Revlimid generics arrived from 2022, the company has been racing to grow new medicines faster than old ones fade. In 2026 the FDA approved Zenbexus (iberdomide) for multiple myeloma, and BMS reported positive late-stage results for several lung- and breast-cancer drugs developed with partners BioNTech and SystImmune.

## Competitors

In cancer immunotherapy BMS's main rival is [Merck](/en/mrk/), whose Keytruda outsells Opdivo; [AstraZeneca](/en/azn/) and [Johnson & Johnson](/en/jnj/) compete in lung and blood cancers. In blood thinners Eliquis competes with Xarelto from J&J and Bayer, and its US profits are shared with [Pfizer](/en/pfe/). In immune diseases and new treatments it faces [AbbVie](/en/abbv/) and [Eli Lilly](/en/lly/).

## Main risks

- **The patent cliff:** Eliquis and Opdivo, more than half of sales today, face generic or biosimilar competition from around 2028; Revlimid shows how quickly sales can fall.
- **US drug pricing:** Medicare price negotiation has already picked Eliquis, and new US pricing rules can cut revenue.
- **Pipeline bets:** BMS is spending billions on drugs still in testing; a failed trial can wipe out years of work.
- **Debt from deals:** past purchases such as Celgene left large debt, and new deals add more.
- **Slow new launches:** Cobenfy earned only $57 million in the quarter, so new drugs may take years to matter.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Big drugmakers are steady businesses, but their shares can stay flat or fall for years when a best-seller is about to lose patent protection. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more shares when the price is low and fewer when it is high, without trying to guess whether the new medicines will fill the gap in time. Many people hold one company only as part of a wider portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
