**GateToken (GT)** is the token of Gate (known as Gate.io until 2025), a crypto exchange that started in 2013. Holders get perks on the exchange, GT pays the network fees on Gate's own blockchains, and Gate regularly destroys part of the supply. ## How it works

GT exists in several forms: as an ERC-20 token on Ethereum, as the native coin of GateChain (Gate's own blockchain, started together with GT in 2019), on GateChain's Ethereum-compatible side and on Gate Layer. In September 2025 Gate launched Gate Layer, a Layer 2 network built on the OP Stack (the same software as Coinbase's Base) that settles on GateChain; GT is the only token used to pay fees there. Supply shrinks in two ways. Every quarter Gate burns a portion of GT based on how the platform performed, and on GateChain the base part of every transaction fee is destroyed automatically (the EIP-1559 model used by Ethereum). Burned tokens go to an address no one controls. Gate said 180.6 million GT had been burned by the second quarter of 2025, and its GT page showed about 191.9 million in November 2026.

## What the token is used for

GT works like a loyalty token for the exchange. It counts toward Gate's VIP tiers, can be used to pay trading fees, and gives access to new tokens: Launchpad sales, Launchpool (stake GT to earn new tokens) and HODLer Airdrops for people who hold GT. Outside the exchange, it pays for transactions on GateChain and Gate Layer. The burns aim to make each remaining token a slightly larger share of a shrinking supply. None of this is a legal claim on Gate's profits: the perks are set by the company and can change.

## Its story so far

Han Lin started the exchange in China in 2013 under the name Bter. In 2015 it lost about 7,000 BTC from its cold wallets in a hack. In 2017, after China banned crypto trading for local money, it was relaunched as Gate.io. GT was issued in April 2019 through a token sale on the exchange; in August 2019 Gate burned 19 million GT. The token stayed below $1 through 2019 and 2020 (it closed 2020 at about $0.45), then rose in the 2021 bull market to a monthly close of about $6.9 in November 2021. After the FTX collapse it fell to about $3 at the end of 2022. It climbed again in 2024 and peaked in January 2025, then fell back during 2025 and 2026. In May 2025 the exchange renamed itself Gate and moved to gate.com, and in September 2025 it launched Gate Layer.

## Main risks

- **One company:** if Gate loses customers, is hacked or fails, GT can lose most of its value fast — as FTX's token did in 2022.
- **Regulation:** Gate needs licences country by country and does not serve users in some countries, including the US from its main platform; new actions can cut its business.
- **Perks can change:** fee benefits, Launchpool rewards and burn sizes are company decisions, not guaranteed rights.
- **Concentration:** part of the supply is frozen or reserved, and Gate decides when and how much to burn.
- **Big swings:** most GT trading happens on Gate itself, so prices can move sharply.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Buying GT regularly is, in effect, a bet on one exchange staying healthy for years. A fixed amount each month buys more when the price is low and less when it is high, but it cannot protect you if the company runs into trouble. Many people keep exchange tokens to a small part of a crypto portfolio and never hold more on an exchange than they can afford to lose. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
