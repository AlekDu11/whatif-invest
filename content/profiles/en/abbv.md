**AbbVie** is a US drug company that makes medicines for long-term illnesses such as psoriasis, Crohn's disease and arthritis, plus cancer and brain drugs, and Botox. It was split off from Abbott Laboratories at the start of 2013 and built its first decade on Humira, an injectable anti-inflammatory drug. Its story since then is about replacing Humira before cheaper copies took its sales.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: $17.4 billion in Q3 2026, +10.3% a year.
> - Skyrizi ($5.8 billion, +22.7%) and Rinvoq ($2.7 billion, +23.9%) made up 48.7% of sales.
> - Humira fell to $683 million in the quarter, down 31% as biosimilars took its place.
> - The quarterly dividend rises 5.2% to $1.82 per share from February 2027.

## How it makes money

AbbVie sells prescription drugs and is paid mostly by US insurers and government programmes: the United States brought in $13.3 billion of the $17.4 billion in Q3 2026, about 77%. Immunology is the core, $9.2 billion in the quarter, led by Skyrizi and Rinvoq, newer drugs for psoriasis, bowel diseases and arthritis. Neuroscience ($3.4 billion, +19%) includes Vraylar for schizophrenia and bipolar disorder, migraine pills and Botox for medical use. Oncology ($1.7 billion) relies on the blood-cancer drugs Imbruvica and Venclexta. Aesthetics ($1.2 billion) is Botox Cosmetic and Juvederm fillers, which came with the purchase of Allergan.

## The numbers right now

In the quarter to 30 September 2026, reported on 30 October, revenue rose 10.3% to $17.4 billion. GAAP earnings per share were $2.37 and adjusted EPS $3.82, up from $1.86 a year earlier, when large research-deal costs had pulled profit down. AbbVie raised its 2026 adjusted EPS forecast to $13.95–13.99. It also closed the purchase of Apogee Therapeutics for about $10.6 billion in cash, adding a late-stage eczema drug. The buying has a cost: at 30 September 2026 AbbVie had about $80.9 billion of debt against $10.8 billion in cash.

## The stock's story

AbbVie began trading in January 2013. The share price rose 60% in 2017 and 24% in 2022, a year when the wider market fell. In March 2025 it closed at $216.66, a record at that time, then slipped back. Through 2026 it climbed to new highs as Skyrizi and Rinvoq grew faster than Humira shrank: in late October 2026, just before the Q3 report, it closed at $290.79, a record at that time, and it rose further the day after the report. The real test was Humira losing US exclusivity to biosimilars; the stock held up because the replacements were already selling.

## Competitors

In immunology AbbVie competes with Johnson & Johnson (Stelara, Tremfya), Eli Lilly, Pfizer, Bristol Myers Squibb and Amgen, plus makers of Humira biosimilars. In cancer it faces AstraZeneca, BeiGene and others in blood cancers. In aesthetics, Galderma and Revance sell rival toxins and fillers.

## Main risks

- **Concentration:** almost half of revenue comes from two drugs; any safety issue or strong new rival would hit hard.
- **Drug-price pressure:** in January 2026 Medicare picked Botox for government-set prices from 2028; a court vacated that choice in October 2026, but the ruling can be appealed.
- **Debt from deals:** about $80.9 billion of borrowing (September 2026) limits room for mistakes.
- **High expectations:** after the 2026 rally, a weak 2027 forecast could move the stock sharply.
- **Patent clocks:** the drugs that sell best in 2026 will also face copies one day, as Humira did.

## What it means if you buy regularly

AbbVie paid and raised its dividend while growing through the loss of its biggest drug. In 2026 the shares traded well above their levels of a few years earlier, so people who started buying later paid more per share than early buyers; the record high and today's distance from it are in the list above. A single drugmaker can drop fast on trial or pricing news. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
