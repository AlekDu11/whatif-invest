**Bitcoin Cash** is the version of bitcoin that chose to be everyday cash rather than digital gold. It split from bitcoin on 1 August 2017 after a long fight over block size, and every bitcoin holder received the same number of BCH. All BCH coins together are worth {{mcap:bch}} today ({{rank:bch}} among cryptocurrencies).

> **Key takeaways**
> - Like bitcoin, BCH is capped at 21 million coins; about 20.1 million had been mined by November 2026.
> - Blocks can hold at least 32 MB and grow with demand, so a normal payment usually costs well under one cent.
> - Its record high is {{ath:bch}} ({{athd:bch}}); today the price is {{fromath:bch}} from it.
> - Its community split twice more: Bitcoin SV left in November 2018 and eCash in November 2020.

## How it works

BCH keeps bitcoin's core design: proof-of-work mining with the same SHA-256 machines, a new block about every 10 minutes and a block reward that halves roughly every four years — most recently in April 2024, to 3.125 BCH. The big difference is capacity. Bitcoin kept small blocks and moves fast, cheap payments to extra layers such as Lightning; Bitcoin Cash made the blocks themselves bigger, and since May 2024 the limit adjusts automatically to demand. The network upgrades once a year in May: CashTokens (2023) added its own tokens, and later upgrades made smart contracts much more powerful. Developers have also accepted a plan to cut the block time to one minute, proposed for May 2027.

## What the token is used for

BCH is meant to be spent: it pays for goods and services, transfers and fees on its own network, and many wallets, payment processors and exchanges support it because it is one of the oldest coins. Holders also see it as a cheaper, "bigger-block" bet on bitcoin's original idea of peer-to-peer electronic cash. In practice most demand comes from trading rather than shopping.

## Its story so far

From 2015 bitcoin users argued over whether to raise the 1 MB block limit. When no deal was reached, supporters of bigger blocks, including miners and the investor Roger Ver, launched Bitcoin Cash in August 2017. In the December 2017 boom BCH set its record of about $3,786, then lost more than 95% by the end of 2018, when a "hash war" with the Bitcoin SV camp shook the market. Another split in November 2020 created eCash. BCH rose again in 2021 and 2024, but its value relative to bitcoin kept falling, and in November 2026 it traded near $200.

## Alternatives

As payment coins, BCH competes with [Litecoin]({lang}/ltc/), [Dogecoin]({lang}/doge/) and [Dash]({lang}/dash/), and with its own offshoots [Bitcoin SV]({lang}/bsv/) and [eCash]({lang}/xec/). For storing value most investors choose [Bitcoin]({lang}/btc/), and for everyday crypto payments many people now use dollar stablecoins.

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** BCH has lost more than 90% from its peak; such drops can happen again.
- **Security:** it uses the same mining machines as bitcoin but has less than 1% of bitcoin's computing power, so a large miner could in theory attack the chain.
- **Little real use:** payments with BCH are modest; most demand comes from trading.
- **Splits and competition:** disagreements have already divided the coin twice, and stablecoins and bitcoin's Lightning network compete for payments.
- **Regulation:** crypto rules differ by country and can change quickly.

## What it means if you buy regularly

BCH's history shows a fast peak in 2017 and long years far below it. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more when the price is low and less when it is high, without trying to guess the bottom — but it does not remove the risk of losses. Many people keep crypto to a small part of their savings that they can afford to see fall sharply. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
