**Bitcoin Cash** is the version of bitcoin that chose to be everyday cash rather than digital gold: it split from bitcoin on August 1, 2017, after a long fight over how big blocks should be, and gave every bitcoin holder the same number of BCH.

## How it works

BCH keeps bitcoin's core rules: proof-of-work mining with the same SHA-256 machines, a new block about every 10 minutes and a hard cap of 21 million coins, with the block reward halving roughly every four years (most recently in April 2024, to 3.125 BCH). The big difference is capacity. Bitcoin kept small blocks and moved fast payments to extra layers such as Lightning; Bitcoin Cash made blocks much bigger — at least 32 MB, adjusting automatically to demand since 2024 — so ordinary transactions stay cheap, usually well under a cent.

## Why it matters

BCH is one of the oldest and most widely listed cryptocurrencies, accepted by many payment processors and wallets. Since 2023 it also supports its own tokens and simple smart contracts (CashTokens). A surprising fact: the community split again in November 2018, when a faction left to create Bitcoin SV — a reminder that in crypto, disagreements can literally divide a coin in two.

## Main risks

BCH shares mining hardware with bitcoin but has only a tiny fraction of its computing power, so a large miner could in theory switch over and attack the chain. Real-world use for payments is modest, and most demand comes from trading. Its value relative to bitcoin has fallen sharply since 2017, and the price is far below its 2017 record. Crypto rules differ by country. Regular buying smooths out the swings but does not remove the risk.
