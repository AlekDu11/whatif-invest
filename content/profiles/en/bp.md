## What Backpack does

Backpack runs a centralized exchange for spot and futures trading in crypto, a self-custody wallet for Solana and other networks, and a staking program. The company says it holds licences in the UAE, the UK and several European countries. In January 2025 it took over FTX EU, the former European arm of the collapsed FTX exchange, with its licence from Cyprus's regulator CySEC, which gave it a regulated base in the European Union. Members of its founding team worked at FTX and Alameda before the 2022 collapse, and FTX Ventures and Jump Crypto led a $20 million round in September 2022.

## What the BP token is for

BP is mainly a way to share in the exchange's growth. People who stake BP for at least a year can convert staked tokens into company equity at a fixed rate: up to 20% of Backpack's shares are set aside for such stakers, with priority access if the company ever goes public. Unstaking is free in the first seven days, after that it takes a seven-day wait. The 75% held by the treasury is released on milestones such as new licences, launches in new regions (the EU, Japan, the US) and new products, and treasury tokens stay locked until at least a year after a future stock-market listing.

## Its story so far

Ferrante's earlier company, Coral, built developer tools for Solana and launched the Mad Lads NFTs in April 2023, which became one of Solana's best-known collections. After FTX failed in November 2022, the team rebuilt itself around the exchange and wallet. In February 2024 it raised $17 million at a $120 million valuation in a round led by Placeholder, and in February 2026 Axios reported talks about new money at a valuation of more than $1 billion. The token launch was delayed several times; before it, the team removed more than 50 million "fake points" and required identity checks for the airdrop. BP started trading at about $0.31 on a day of heavy market liquidations.

## Alternatives

Exchange tokens are a crowded field. [BNB]({lang}/bnb/) belongs to Binance, the largest exchange, and [OKB]({lang}/okb/) to OKX; both are far bigger and more established. [Hyperliquid]({lang}/hype/) runs its own futures exchange on its own blockchain and has become the main rival for traders who want a token tied to an exchange's business. Inside Solana, the native coin [SOL]({lang}/sol/) is still the main way to bet on the ecosystem as a whole.

## Main risks

- **Very new:** BP has less than a year of price history, and young tokens can lose most of their value quickly.
- **Unlocks:** three quarters of the supply is still in the treasury; as milestones are reached, new tokens enter the market.
- **One company:** the token's value depends on a single exchange; hacks, outages or a lost licence would hit it directly.
- **Regulation:** a token that can be turned into company shares may be treated as a security in some countries, and the rules are still unsettled.
- **Competition:** Binance, OKX, Bybit and Hyperliquid are much larger and fight for the same traders.

## What it means if you buy regularly

With so little history, BP gives no long track record to learn from — only a few months of sharp swings. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your purchases over high and low prices without guessing the bottom, but it does not protect you from losses if the token keeps falling. Many people keep new exchange tokens to a very small part of their savings. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
