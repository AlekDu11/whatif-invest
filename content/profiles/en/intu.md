**Intuit** makes the software millions of Americans use to do their taxes (TurboTax), run a small business's books (QuickBooks), check their credit score (Credit Karma) and send marketing emails (Mailchimp). In the year to July 2026 it took in $21.4 billion and earned $4.6 billion — yet its stock fell by more than half from its 2025 peak as investors started to fear that AI could do much of this work for free.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: $21.4 billion in fiscal 2026 (year to 31 July), +14%; net income $4.6 billion.
> - QuickBooks and the rest of the small-business side brought in $12.9 billion, about 60% of the total.
> - The stock reached an all-time high of $790.75 in July 2025, then fell 20% in a single day in May 2026 — its worst day since 2003.
> - Guidance for fiscal 2027: $23.3–23.5 billion of revenue (+9–10%) and about 3,000 job cuts (17% of staff) already announced.

## How it makes money

Intuit has two big halves. **Global Business Solutions** ($12.9 billion in fiscal 2026, +16%) is QuickBooks — accounting, payroll, payments, bill pay and loans for small and mid-sized companies — plus Mailchimp. Most of it is paid monthly online, so it repeats every year. **Consumer** ($8.6 billion, +11%) is TurboTax ($5.3 billion), Credit Karma ($2.6 billion, +20%) and ProTax software for accountants ($647 million).

TurboTax is seasonal: almost all of its money arrives between January and April. More people now pay for TurboTax Live, where a human expert checks or files the return; it grew 37% and is now 53% of TurboTax revenue. Credit Karma is free for users and earns a fee when someone takes a card or loan it recommends. From fiscal 2027 Mailchimp, whose sales are flat, is reported as its own segment.

## The numbers right now

In fiscal 2026 Intuit's GAAP operating income rose 20% to $5.9 billion and earnings per share rose 20% to $16.46. The fourth quarter alone included a $293 million restructuring charge for the job cuts. The company spent $5.5 billion buying back its own shares, raised the quarterly dividend 15% to $1.38, and ended July with $7.2 billion in cash and investments against $7.7 billion of debt.

For fiscal 2027 management expects revenue of $23.28–23.51 billion and GAAP earnings per share of $20.12–20.36. TurboTax is expected to grow only 2–3%, Credit Karma 11–13%.

## The stock's story

Intuit was one of the steadiest winners of the past decade: Macrotrends data show the shares rose every year from 2016 to 2021, fell 39% in 2022 and jumped 62% in 2023. They hit a record $790.75 in July 2025. Then the mood turned. Software stocks sold off on fears that AI tools could replace paid apps, and in May 2026, after Intuit announced the 17% job cuts and slower TurboTax sales, the stock dropped 20% to about $307 in one session. By then it was down 54% for the year.

## Competitors

In taxes, Intuit's main rival is H&R Block, both online and in its offices, plus free government filing options. In small-business software it competes with Xero, Sage, FreshBooks and payments-and-banking platforms such as Block (Square). Credit Karma competes with free credit-score services from banks and card issuers. A new wave of AI-native bookkeeping startups is the challenger investors watch most.

## Main risks

- **AI disruption:** if chatbots and AI agents can prepare taxes or keep books cheaply, Intuit's pricing power could shrink — this fear drove most of the 2026 fall.
- **TurboTax slowing:** US TurboTax units fell 2% to 39 million in fiscal 2026, and guidance points to only 2–3% growth.
- **Credit cycle:** Credit Karma earns when banks want to lend; in a recession that income can drop fast.
- **Execution:** cutting 17% of staff while pushing into AI and mid-market clients is a lot of change at once.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Intuit has shown both faces: years of steady gains, then a fall of more than half in about a year while the business still grew 14%. Regular buying spreads purchases across such swings, but there is no guarantee the price returns to its peak. Many people hold a single stock like this as part of a broader portfolio. Past results are not a forecast.
