**Render** is a marketplace for computing power: artists, studios and AI developers pay to use other people's graphics cards (GPUs), and the owners of those GPUs are paid in the RENDER token. It grew out of OTOY, the Los Angeles graphics-software company led by Jules Urbach, and it is one of the best-known "AI and compute" tokens, worth {{mcap:render}} today ({{rank:render}} among cryptocurrencies).

> - The supply is capped at about 644 million RENDER; about 86% of it was in circulation in November 2026.
> - Since December 2023 the network runs on a burn-and-mint model: tokens paid for jobs are burned, and new ones are minted for GPU owners.
> - In November 2023 the token moved from Ethereum (ticker RNDR) to Solana (ticker RENDER).

## How it works

Rendering turns a 3D scene into finished images or video frames, and it needs a lot of GPU power. Instead of buying expensive hardware, a creator sends the job to the Render Network. Independent "node operators" with idle GPUs pick up the work, mostly using OTOY's OctaneRender software, and are paid once it is checked. Since 2025 the network has also been opening up to AI and general GPU computing, for example through a platform called Dispersed. Rule changes go through public Render Network Proposals (RNPs) voted on by token holders; the Render Network Foundation, spun out of OTOY in 2023, oversees governance.

## What the token is used for

Jobs are priced in ordinary money. Under the Burn-and-Mint Equilibrium (RNP-001), the creator pays with RENDER and that RENDER is burned — removed from supply — once the work is done. Every epoch (about a week) the network mints new RENDER on a fixed, declining schedule and shares it among node operators and other contributors. If real demand burns more than is minted, supply shrinks; if demand is weak, it grows. RENDER is also used to vote on proposals.

## Its story so far

Jules Urbach first described the idea in 2009. The token was sold to the public in October 2017, with a private sale from January to May 2018, and the network opened to everyone on 27 April 2020. It rose many times over in 2023 as artificial intelligence became the market's favourite theme. Holders voted to move to Solana (RNP-002), the move was completed on 2 November 2023, and burn-and-mint emissions began in December 2023. The price peaked in March 2024 during the AI boom, and over 2024 exchanges switched from RNDR to RENDER. In November 2026 Render fell about 12% in a week as AI-linked tokens slid together.

## Alternatives

Other decentralized GPU and computing networks include [Akash]({lang}/akt/), [io.net]({lang}/io/), [Aethir]({lang}/ath/) and [Golem]({lang}/glm/); [Bittensor]({lang}/tao/) is one of the largest crypto-AI projects. The real competition, though, is big clouds such as Amazon Web Services and Google Cloud.

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** small AI tokens can fall much further than bitcoin; after its March 2024 peak, Render lost most of its value.
- **Emissions:** new RENDER is minted every epoch; if real jobs do not burn enough tokens, the supply keeps growing.
- **Competition:** big cloud companies offer GPUs at huge scale and with support that a decentralized network may struggle to match.
- **Dependence:** the network relies heavily on OTOY's software and on the Solana blockchain, so problems at either would hit it.
- **Regulation:** crypto rules differ by country and can change quickly, including how tokens like RENDER are classified.

## What it means if you buy regularly

RENDER's price has had sharp rises and deep falls and moves with the mood around AI. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more when the price is low and less when it is high, without trying to guess the bottom — but it does not remove the risk of losses. Many people keep tokens like RENDER to a small part of their savings that they can afford to see fall sharply. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
