**Sony** is no longer mainly a TV and Walkman company: most of its profit now comes from entertainment — PlayStation games and subscriptions, Sony Music's songs and artists, and Sony Pictures' films and anime (Crunchyroll). It also makes the image sensors inside many of the world's smartphone cameras. The shares trade in Tokyo under the code 6758 and in New York as SONY.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Sales: ¥3,429.4 billion in July–September 2026, +10% on the year; operating income ¥622.3 billion (+45%), net income ¥449.6 billion (+44%).
> - The games business (PlayStation) had sales of ¥1,208.5 billion and operating income of ¥220.1 billion in that quarter — the biggest part of Sony.
> - On 5 November 2026 Sony raised its forecast for the year to March 2027: sales ¥13 trillion, operating income ¥1,870 billion, net income ¥1,330 billion.
> - Planned dividend for that year: ¥35 per share, ¥10 more than a year earlier.

## How it makes money

Sony has five main businesses. **Games & Network Services** sells PlayStation 5 consoles, its own games (Spider-Man, God of War, Wolverine), a cut of other publishers' games and PlayStation Plus subscriptions; PlayStation had 127 million monthly active accounts in September 2026. **Music** earns from streaming and publishing rights to a huge catalogue of songs. **Pictures** makes films and TV series and owns Crunchyroll, the biggest anime streaming service outside Japan. **Imaging & Sensing Solutions** makes image sensors for phones such as the iPhone, plus sensors for cars and factories. **Entertainment, Technology & Services** is the old electronics arm: TVs, headphones, cameras and professional video gear. In October 2025 Sony spun off its financial business (Sony Financial Group), so banking and insurance are no longer part of the company.

In the July–September 2026 quarter every main business except electronics grew. Games operating income rose 83% to ¥220.1 billion (a year earlier it had been cut by about ¥50 billion of one-off losses, mostly on Bungie). Pictures' sales jumped 48% to ¥513.7 billion and its operating income rose to ¥92.6 billion from ¥13.9 billion, thanks to the film Spider-Man: Brand New Day, which took more than $2.5 billion at the box office worldwide. Music made ¥122.8 billion of operating income and image sensors ¥154.6 billion, even though sensor sales fell 6%. Sony said operating income for April–September topped ¥1 trillion for the first time in a half-year.

For the full year to March 2027 Sony now expects sales of ¥13,000 billion (up from ¥12,479.6 billion the year before), operating income of ¥1,870 billion and net income of ¥1,330 billion. The forecast assumed about ¥153 to the dollar for the rest of the year.

## The stock's story

Sony was founded in Tokyo in 1946 by Masaru Ibuka and Akio Morita and became famous for the transistor radio, the Trinitron TV and, in 1979, the Walkman. The first PlayStation came out in 1994 and turned games into Sony's most important business. The shares soared in the 1999–2000 tech bubble and then crashed; in April 2003 a weak forecast set off the "Sony shock" that dragged down the whole Tokyo market. In the 2008–2012 years Sony lost money on TVs and phones and its shares fell to multi-decade lows. A turnaround that cut weak electronics and leaned into games, music, films and sensors lifted the stock through the 2010s and 2020s. In October 2024 Sony split each share into five, and Hiroki Totoki became CEO in April 2025.

## Competitors

In games Sony fights Microsoft (Xbox) and Nintendo, and increasingly Tencent and mobile gaming. Sony Music competes with Universal Music and Warner Music. Sony Pictures competes with Disney, Warner Bros. Discovery, Netflix and other studios. In image sensors the main rivals are Samsung and the Chinese maker OmniVision; in TVs and headphones, Samsung, LG, TCL, Apple and Bose.

## Main risks

- **The console cycle:** the PS5 launched in 2020 and its unit sales are slowly falling; profits depend on the next console and big game hits.
- **Memory chip prices:** the company said rising memory costs were a challenge for console profits in the following year.
- **Hits are unpredictable:** a year like 2026 with a billion-dollar Spider-Man film may not repeat; films and games can flop.
- **Smartphone demand:** image sensors rely on a few phone makers, above all Apple.
- **The yen:** much of Sony's money is earned in dollars and euros, so a stronger yen lowers its yen-based profits.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Sony is a large, profitable company with several separate engines, but its shares have still lost half their value or more in bad periods. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more shares when the price is low and fewer when it is high, without trying to time the market. Many people hold one company only as part of a wider portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
