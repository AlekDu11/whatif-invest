**Sony** is no longer mainly a TV and Walkman maker: today it is an entertainment giant built on PlayStation, Sony Music, Sony Pictures and the image sensors inside many of the world's smartphones. In the year to March 2026 it earned ¥1.45 trillion in operating profit on ¥12.5 trillion of sales.

## How it makes money

Games are the biggest business: PlayStation consoles, games and the PlayStation Plus subscription brought ¥4.69 trillion of sales. Music (Sony Music, plus a huge catalogue of song rights) earned ¥447 billion of operating profit, up 25%. Imaging & Sensing — camera chips for phones and cars — grew sales 20% and profit 37%. Pictures (films, TV series and the anime service Crunchyroll) and Entertainment, Technology & Services (TVs, cameras, headphones) complete the group. In October 2025 Sony spun off most of its bank and insurance arm, Sony Financial Group.

## Why it matters

Sony was founded in 1946 in a bombed-out Tokyo department store by Masaru Ibuka and Akio Morita. It is now one of the few companies that owns both the "pipes" (consoles, sensors) and the content (games, songs, films) people pay for. One surprising fact: last year music made almost as much operating profit as the whole PlayStation business.

## Main risks

Game profits swing with the console cycle and big releases; Sony took a ¥120 billion write-down on Bungie, the studio behind Destiny. The sensor business depends on a few large smartphone customers. For this year Sony expects sales to slip 1%, and a stronger yen would shrink overseas earnings. Films and TV are hit-driven. Buying a fixed amount regularly smooths price swings but does not remove the risk.
