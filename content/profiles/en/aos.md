**A. O. Smith** makes the tank in the basement or garage that gives you hot water for a shower, plus boilers that heat schools and hospitals and filters that clean tap water. Based in Milwaukee, it sells mostly in North America, where water heaters are usually bought when the old one breaks — so demand is steadier than for most building products.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Sales: $940.5 million in Q3 2026, flat on a year earlier; earnings per share $0.93.
> - North America is about 78% of sales, with a segment margin of 23.6% in the quarter.
> - It agreed on 27 October 2026 to sell 81% of its China business at a value of about $400 million.
> - The stock trades well below its July 2024 record of about $91 and has raised its dividend for more than 30 years in a row.

## How it makes money

A. O. Smith sells gas and electric water heaters and heat pumps, commercial boilers under the Lochinvar brand and water treatment products such as softeners and filters (Aquasana and others). In North America it sells through about 800 independent plumbing wholesalers and has a long exclusive relationship with Lowe's; its five largest customers made up about 41% of 2025 sales. The Rest of World segment — mainly China, India and Europe — brought the other 22%, with China alone about 18% of 2025 sales. Most demand comes from replacing worn-out units rather than from new buildings. In January 2026 it bought Leonard Valve for about $470 million, paid with a new loan.

## The numbers right now

In the quarter to 30 September 2026 sales were $940.5 million, almost unchanged from $942.5 million, and net earnings slipped 4% to $126.3 million. North America grew 3% to $761.6 million (Leonard Valve added $15 million), while Rest of World fell 7% to $192.8 million as China sales dropped 17% in local currency. Free cash flow for nine months was $396.2 million. The company held $234.5 million of cash against $673.1 million of debt and bought back $260.6 million of its own shares in nine months. Its 2026 outlook, which excludes China, is sales of $3.27–3.29 billion and adjusted EPS of $3.42–3.52.

## The stock's story

For years A. O. Smith was a quiet winner. Its weakest years were 2018 (−29%) and 2022 (−32%). In May 2019 short seller J Capital published a report questioning its China sales; the company called the claims unfounded. On adjusted prices the stock reached a record of about $91 in July 2024, then slid as sales stopped growing and China kept shrinking. The China sale, announced in October 2026, is meant to turn that problem into cash: buyer CPE takes 81%, A. O. Smith keeps 19% and a brand licence, and proceeds go to buybacks. Closing is expected in the first half of 2027.

## Competitors

In North American water heaters and boilers it competes with Rheem, Bradford White, Rinnai, Navien and Aerco; in water treatment with Culligan, Kinetico, Pentair and Franklin Electric. In China its rivals have been Haier and Midea, and in India Racold, Bajaj and Havells.

## Main risks

- **Few big customers:** five customers make up about 41% of sales, so losing one would hurt.
- **Slow growth:** sales were flat in 2026 so far; replacement demand is steady but rarely grows fast.
- **China deal:** the sale still needs regulatory approval, and after it the company will depend even more on one market.
- **Leftover China exposure:** the 19% stake and brand licence keep some link to a market where sales fell 17% in Q3 2026.

## What it means if you buy regularly

A. O. Smith is a steady, profitable business with a long record of dividend increases, but its share price still fell about a third in single years like 2022. Regular buying spreads purchases across those swings; reinvested dividends add to the result over long periods. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
