**Sei (SEI)** is a layer-1 blockchain built for speed, aimed first of all at trading apps: it confirms transactions in under a second and runs apps written for Ethereum. It was founded by Jeff Feng and Jayendra Jog of Sei Labs and launched its main network in August 2023. Its market value is {{mcap:sei}} ({{rank:sei}} among cryptocurrencies).

> - The total supply is fixed at 10 billion SEI; about 6.7 billion were in circulation in November 2026.

> - Since the Sei V2 upgrade in 2024 it runs Ethereum-style (EVM) smart contracts in parallel; in 2026 it is dropping its older Cosmos side to become EVM-only.
> - Team members and early investors received 40% of all tokens, released gradually after launch.

## How it works

Sei began as a chain built with Cosmos technology and uses proof of stake: validators lock SEI, check transactions and add blocks, and SEI holders can delegate their coins to them to share in the rewards. Blocks arrive in roughly 400 milliseconds. Its main idea is parallel processing: transactions that do not touch the same data are executed at the same time instead of one after another, which suits exchanges with many orders. A plan approved by the community in May 2025 (SIP-3) removes the old Cosmos transactions in stages during 2026, so only Ethereum-style accounts remain, clearing the way for a planned upgrade called Giga that aims for much higher capacity.

## What the token is used for

SEI pays the fees for every transaction on the network, is staked by validators and delegators to secure it, and gives holders a vote on proposals such as upgrades. Demand therefore depends on how many people actually use apps on Sei — trading sites, lending, games and stablecoin payments — and on how much SEI is locked in staking.

## Its story so far

Sei Labs raised money from venture funds in 2022 and launched the main network on 15 August 2023, when SEI also started trading, with part of the supply handed out through an airdrop and a Binance Launchpool. The price climbed in the early-2024 crypto rally and reached about $1.14 in March 2024. In July 2024 the Sei V2 upgrade added the parallel EVM, letting Ethereum apps and wallets work on Sei. In May 2025 the community approved the move to an EVM-only network, rolled out in steps through 2026. The price fell sharply in 2025 and 2026, and in November 2026 it traded near its lowest levels since launch.

## Alternatives

Sei competes with other fast smart-contract networks: [Solana]({lang}/sol/), the leader among speed-focused chains, [Sui]({lang}/sui/) and [Aptos]({lang}/apt/), and [Monad]({lang}/mon/), another parallel EVM chain. For trading in particular, [Hyperliquid]({lang}/hype/) and [Injective]({lang}/inj/) chase the same users. Many apps also simply stay on [Ethereum]({lang}/eth/) and its layer-2 networks.

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** SEI has fallen far from its 2024 high; small-cap coins can drop much further.
- **Token unlocks:** team and investor tokens are released over several years, adding supply to the market.
- **Concentration:** the foundation, Sei Labs and early investors hold a large share and influence decisions.
- **Competition:** many fast chains fight for the same apps and traders, and users move easily.
- **Technology and regulation:** big upgrades such as the EVM-only switch can bring bugs, and crypto rules can change quickly.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Sei's short history so far is a rise in its first months followed by a long fall, so regular buying has mostly meant collecting coins at ever lower prices. A fixed amount bought regularly evens out your entry price, but it only pays off if the network wins lasting use. Many people keep small, young coins to a tiny part of their savings. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
