**Alphabet** is the parent company of Google: the search box, YouTube, Android, Chrome, Gmail, Maps and the Gemini AI assistant. Most of these are free for you; advertisers pay to appear next to your searches and videos. A fast-growing second business, Google Cloud, rents computing power and AI to companies.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: $127.6 billion in Q3 2026, +25% a year; Google Cloud more than doubled (+103%) to $30.7 billion.
> - Operating income rose 30% to $40.6 billion, but net income fell to $26.2 billion from $35.0 billion because of a $12.4 billion paper loss on shares Alphabet owns.
> - AI spending: $51.4 billion of capex in one quarter and $195–205 billion planned for 2026 — free cash flow turned negative ($−9.1 billion) in Q3.
> - Alphabet is now selling shares instead of buying them back: no buybacks in 2026, about $50 billion raised from new common and preferred shares in June.

## How it makes money

Search is still the core: $63.4 billion in Q3 2026, +12%, half of all revenue. YouTube ads added $11.2 billion. Subscriptions, platforms and devices — YouTube Premium, Google One storage, Play Store fees and Pixel phones — brought $14.3 billion. Google Cloud, at $30.7 billion, is now almost a quarter of revenue and earned $11.1 billion of operating profit, up from $3.6 billion a year earlier. Cloud customers have signed contracts worth $561 billion that have not yet been billed (the backlog). Other Bets, such as Waymo self-driving taxis, had $372 million of revenue and lost $2.0 billion.

## The numbers right now

In the quarter to 30 September 2026 Alphabet earned $2.12 per share, down from $2.87. The drop came from below the operating line: Alphabet holds stakes in other companies, including SpaceX shares worth $75.2 billion, and their value fell in the quarter. The business itself made a 32% operating margin.

The big story is spending. Capital expenditure — mostly servers, chips and data centres — was $132 billion in the first nine months of 2026, more than double the $63.6 billion a year earlier, and management expects a significant further increase in 2027. To pay for it Alphabet raised $20.5 billion in a public stock offering and $10 billion from an affiliate of Berkshire Hathaway in June, $19 billion from convertible preferred shares and $28.7 billion from bonds. It still had $265 billion of cash and securities on 30 September. The quarterly dividend is $0.22 per share.

## The stock's story

Alphabet's shares fell 39% in 2022, when ad budgets shrank and interest rates rose, then gained 58% in 2023 and 36% in 2024. In 2026 AI demand pushed its market value past $4 trillion, and the stock closed at $354.97 on 21 September 2026. After the Q3 results it dropped about 10% in after-hours trading, from $322.22 to $288.35, as investors worried about the profit miss and the spending bill.

## Competitors

In AI assistants Alphabet competes with OpenAI's ChatGPT, Microsoft's Copilot and Meta AI. In online ads its rivals are Meta, Amazon and TikTok; in cloud it is number three behind Amazon Web Services and Microsoft Azure. Its edge: it owns every layer — its own AI chips (TPUs), models (Gemini, whose app has 1 billion users) and data centres.

## Main risks

- **Spending may not pay off:** if AI demand cools, $200+ billion a year of capex leaves expensive, fast-ageing hardware.
- **Dilution:** new shares and convertible preferred stock spread profits over more shares; buybacks are paused.
- **Antitrust:** a US court ordered search remedies in December 2025 (appeals pending); in the ad tech case the court in September 2026 refused a break-up but imposed rules. In the EU a €4.1 billion Android fine became final, a €3.0 billion ad tech fine is under appeal, and €890 million of Digital Markets Act fines came in July 2026.
- **AI could change search:** chat answers may reduce clicks on ads; search ad growth (+12%) is far slower than cloud.
- **Investment swings:** large stakes such as SpaceX make reported profit jump up and down from quarter to quarter.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Alphabet is one of the world's largest companies, but its shares still fell 39% in a single year in 2022, and they now carry a bet that AI spending will earn its money back. Regular buying spreads purchases across those swings; many people hold a single tech giant as one part of a broader portfolio rather than the whole of it. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
