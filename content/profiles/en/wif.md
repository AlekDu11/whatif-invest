**dogwifhat** (WIF) is a memecoin on the Solana blockchain built around a viral photo of a Shiba Inu dog wearing a pink knitted hat. It has no product, company or cash flows behind it: people buy it for the meme, the community and the hope that others will buy after them. ## How it works

WIF is a standard token on Solana, a fast and cheap blockchain. There is no WIF network of its own: transactions are confirmed by Solana validators and cost a fraction of a cent. Anyone can hold WIF in a Solana wallet such as Phantom, trade it on decentralized exchanges like Raydium or Jupiter, or buy it on large centralized exchanges. The token has no tax on trades and its supply cannot be increased, so the number of coins is known in advance.

## Why people buy it

A memecoin's value comes only from what buyers are willing to pay. WIF's appeal is a simple, funny image that spreads easily on social media, a large community of holders and listings on major exchanges, which make it easy to buy. For traders it is a way to bet on the mood of the Solana market: in good times memecoins often rise faster than bigger coins, and in bad times they usually fall harder.

## Its story so far

The dog photo, of a Shiba Inu called Achi, had circulated online for years before an anonymous team launched WIF on Solana in November 2023. Early in 2024 the coin rose many times over, and Binance listed it on 5 March 2024. That month holders raised about $700,000 in a crowdfund to show the dog on the Las Vegas Sphere, and the price peaked at about $4.80 on 31 March 2024. The Sphere ad never happened — in January 2025 the venue said it had no deal — and WIF lost most of its value during 2025 as interest in memecoins faded.

## Main risks

- **Extreme swings:** WIF fell more than 90% from its 2024 peak; memecoins can lose almost everything.
- **Attention only:** there is no product or income, so the price depends on hype that can disappear quickly.
- **Concentration:** large wallets hold a big share of the supply and can move the price by selling.
- **Anonymous team:** the creators are unknown and nobody is responsible for promises made by the community, as the Sphere story showed.
- **Regulation and scams:** rules for memecoins are unclear, and fake WIF tokens and phishing sites copy the name.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Buying a memecoin regularly spreads your entry price, but it cannot fix the core problem: the coin is worth only what the next buyer pays. Many people who buy memecoins at all keep them to a very small amount they could lose completely without harm. Check the official contract address before buying. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
