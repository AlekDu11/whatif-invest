**AppLovin** decides which ad you see inside a free mobile game — and gets paid when that ad brings the advertiser a new player or a sale. Its AI system, AXON, buys ad space across thousands of apps and aims for the return each advertiser sets. The company is based in Palo Alto, California, and has traded on Nasdaq since April 2021.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: $2.18 billion in Q3 2026, +55% a year; net income $1.44 billion — 66 cents of every revenue dollar.
> - Free cash flow of $1.42 billion in the quarter; $1.4 billion went on buying back 4.1 million shares.
> - The shares closed 2025 at $673.82 and fell sharply in 2026 after a short-seller report and a slight revenue miss in August.
> - Q4 2026 outlook: $2.365–2.395 billion in revenue, with an adjusted EBITDA margin of 84–85%.

## How it makes money

Almost all revenue comes from **AppLovin Ads**, the company's AI-powered advertising platform. Game studios and, increasingly, online shops pay to reach people; AppLovin places their ads inside other apps and takes a cut. It reports revenue after paying app owners for the ad space, which helps explain its very high margins.

Mobile games are still the core. The next bet is e-commerce and other non-gaming advertisers, which the CEO, Adam Foroughi, said in 2026 were growing faster than gaming from a much smaller base. AppLovin used to make games too, but sold that apps business to Tripledot on 30 June 2025 for $400 million in cash plus about 22% of Tripledot. About half of revenue comes from users in the US.

In the quarter to 30 September 2026 AppLovin made $2.18 billion in revenue, up from $1.41 billion a year earlier. Net income rose 72% to $1.44 billion, and adjusted EBITDA (profit before interest, tax and some non-cash costs) reached $1.84 billion, an 84% margin. Free cash flow was $1.42 billion.

The company ended that quarter with $3.17 billion in cash against $3.52 billion of long-term debt. The board added $3.2 billion to the buyback programme, bringing it to $3.6 billion. For Q4 2026 AppLovin expected $2.365–2.395 billion in revenue.

## The stock's story

AppLovin listed at $80 a share in April 2021. In 2022 growth stalled and the stock collapsed to about $9 (28 December 2022). Then profits took off as its AI ad engine improved: the shares soared through 2024, rose about 97% in 2025, joined the S&P 500 in September 2025 and closed at $745.61 on 29 September 2025, a record at that time.

2026 was rough. A new short-seller report made accusations the company called false, investors worried about new competition from Meta, and on 5 August a slight revenue miss sent the shares sharply lower — even though the CFO said the SEC had closed its inquiry with no recommended action. Strong Q3 results brought buyers back, but on 20 November 2026 the stock closed at $348.29, about 48% below its 2025 close of $673.82.

## Competitors

AppLovin competes for in-app ad budgets with **Unity** (which merged with ironSource in 2022), **Google AdMob** and **Meta Audience Network**, and with Meta and Google themselves for e-commerce budgets. Its edge is the reach of its network inside games and the results AXON delivers.

## Main risks

- **Gaming dependence:** most revenue still comes from mobile games; if game studios cut ad budgets, AppLovin feels it fast.
- **Expectations:** with this growth, even a small miss — as in August 2026 — can wipe out a big part of the share price in days.
- **Platform rules:** Apple and Google can change privacy and tracking rules at any time; in 2026 the company said the impact had been muted.
- **Lawsuits and short sellers:** securities class actions from 2025 and another filed on 16 September 2026 were pending in late 2026; the company called the claims without merit.
- **Competition:** Meta and Google have far more data and money to fight for in-app ads.

## What it means if you buy regularly

AppLovin has shown both extremes: about −89% from its IPO price by the end of 2022, more than 80 times up from that low by 2025, then more than half of the peak value lost by November 2026. Regular buying spreads purchases over highs and lows, but a stock this volatile can halve your balance in months — many people hold a company like this only as a small part of a broader portfolio. Past results are not a forecast.
