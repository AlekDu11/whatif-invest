**Fluid** is a decentralized finance (DeFi) protocol where people lend, borrow and swap crypto from one shared pool of money. It was built by the team behind Instadapp, one of the oldest DeFi apps on Ethereum, and in early 2025 the old INST token was renamed FLUID. ## How it works

Most DeFi apps keep lending and trading in separate pools. Fluid puts all deposits into one "liquidity layer", and different apps — a money market, vaults and a decentralized exchange (DEX) — draw on it. With "smart collateral", the coins you put up for a loan are also used as trading liquidity on the DEX and earn swap fees. With "smart debt", what you borrow is also put to work as liquidity, so trading fees can pay part of your interest. Everything runs on smart contracts, programs on the blockchain, mainly on Ethereum and also on layer-2 networks such as Arbitrum and Base. In 2025 the Solana exchange Jupiter launched its lending app, Jupiter Lend, built with Fluid's technology.

## What the token is used for

FLUID is a governance token: holders vote on fees, new markets and how the treasury is spent. The rebrand proposal of December 2024 also planned token buybacks once the protocol reached about $10 million in yearly revenue, so that part of the fees Fluid earns would flow back to the token. FLUID does not pay a dividend, and its value depends on how much the protocol earns and how much of that reaches holders.

## Its story so far

Instadapp was founded in 2018 by brothers Sowmay and Samyak Jain as a simple dashboard for using several DeFi apps at once. Its INST governance token launched in June 2021, at the height of the DeFi boom, and hit about $24 that month before losing more than 95% by the end of 2022. After about two years of development the team launched Fluid's lending protocol in 2024 and its DEX in November 2024, which quickly became one of the largest exchanges on Ethereum by volume. Holders then approved converting INST into FLUID one to one, and the token traded as FLUID from 2025, mostly between about $1.50 and $6.

## Alternatives

[Aave]({lang}/aave/) is the largest DeFi lending protocol, and [Morpho]({lang}/morpho/) is a fast-growing rival. For trading, Fluid's DEX competes with [Uniswap]({lang}/uni/) and [Curve DAO]({lang}/crv/). On Solana, [Jupiter]({lang}/jup/) is both a partner and a competitor.

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** the token lost more than 95% of its value once before; DeFi tokens can fall fast in bear markets.
- **Smart-contract risk:** a bug or hack in one shared liquidity layer could hit lending and trading at the same time.
- **Liquidations:** loans backed by volatile coins are sold automatically when prices drop, which can cause chain reactions in a crash.
- **Value to holders:** buybacks depend on revenue targets and governance votes; fees may not reach the token as expected.
- **Concentration and regulation:** the team and early investors hold a large share, and DeFi rules are still being written in many countries.

## What it means if you buy regularly

As FLUID, the token has a short history, and regular buys since 2025 have mostly been at falling prices. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads out the timing risk, but the result still depends on whether the protocol keeps growing its fees. Many people keep DeFi tokens to a small part of their savings that they can afford to see fall sharply. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
