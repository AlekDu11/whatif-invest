**Lido** is the biggest service for staking ether: you hand it ETH, it runs validators through professional node operators, and you get **stETH**, a token that grows with the staking rewards and that you can still trade or use in other apps. Lido is run by a DAO, and its governance token **LDO** is worth {{mcap:ldo}} today ({{rank:ldo}} among cryptocurrencies).

> **Key takeaways**
> - Lido launched in December 2020; on 30 June 2026 it held about 9.13 million staked ETH, roughly 21% of all staked ether.
> - LDO has a fixed supply of 1 billion tokens. It gives a vote on the protocol, not a direct share of its fees.
> - Since the end of June 2025 stETH holders can delay or block DAO decisions through "dual governance".

## How it works

To run an Ethereum validator yourself you need 32 ETH, a server that is always online and the know-how to avoid penalties. Lido pools deposits of any size and spreads them across many node operators. In return you get **stETH** one to one, and its balance in your wallet rises every day with the rewards. **wstETH** is the "wrapped" version with a fixed balance whose value grows instead; it is the form most DeFi apps and other blockchains use. Lido keeps 10% of the staking rewards and splits it between the node operators and the DAO treasury; stakers get the other 90%. Since Lido V2 in May 2023 you can also withdraw stETH back to ETH directly. In January 2026 Lido V3 added **stVaults**: separate staking setups for institutions, funds and networks such as Linea that still give access to stETH.

## Why people think it has value

LDO holders vote on how the protocol is run: fees, which node operators take part, upgrades and how the treasury is spent. So the bet on LDO is a bet that Lido stays at the centre of Ethereum staking. For years the fees went to the treasury and holders got nothing directly. That started to change in 2026: the DAO swapped about 1,591 stETH for about 10 million LDO in a one-off buyback, and it approved an automatic buyback mechanism (NEST) that buys LDO only when yearly staking revenue is above $40 million. As of mid-2026 that threshold had not been reached. Dual governance also means LDO votes no longer have the last word: stakers can veto changes they do not like.

## Its story so far

Lido was founded by Konstantin Lomashuk and Vasiliy Shapovalov and went live on 19 December 2020, when ETH staked on the new Beacon Chain could not yet be withdrawn — stETH was a way to stay liquid. It grew fast in the 2021 DeFi boom, and LDO set its record in August 2021. In June 2022, during the collapses of Celsius and Three Arrows Capital, stETH traded below ETH for weeks. After withdrawals became possible in 2023, Lido's share of staked ETH peaked at close to a third, then slowly fell as exchanges, custodians and rivals grew. Lido wound down its versions for other blockchains to focus on Ethereum. Revenue fell 23% in 2025 to $40.5 million, LDO slid to the lowest prices in its history in 2026, and the DAO answered with buybacks and the stVaults launch.

## Alternatives

In liquid staking Lido competes with [Rocket Pool]({lang}/rpl/), [Ether.fi]({lang}/ethfi/) and the staking services of exchanges such as Coinbase and Binance, as well as staking ETFs and custodians for big investors. In restaking and validator tools there are [EigenCloud (EigenLayer)]({lang}/eigen/) and [SSV Network]({lang}/ssv/). Many people simply hold [Ethereum]({lang}/eth/) itself, and the fortunes of LDO depend heavily on the price of ETH.

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** by 2026 LDO had fallen more than 95% from its 2021 record, and it can fall further.
- **Smart contracts and operators:** a bug, a hack or a large penalty on Lido validators could cost stakers money and hurt the token.
- **Losing share:** Lido's slice of staked ETH has been shrinking since 2023; less ETH means less revenue.
- **Weak token value capture:** LDO mainly gives votes; buybacks start only when revenue is high enough.
- **Concentration and regulation:** a single protocol holding a large part of staked ETH worries part of the Ethereum community, and staking rules differ by country.

## What it means if you buy regularly

LDO's history since 2022 has been mostly a long fall, with short rebounds. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your purchases over highs and lows without guessing the bottom, but it does not remove the risk of losses, and a governance token can stay cheap even while the protocol is large. If you mainly want staking rewards, stETH and LDO are very different things: one tracks ETH, the other is a bet on Lido itself. Many people keep a single-project token like this to a small part of their savings. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
