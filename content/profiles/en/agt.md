**Agroton** is a Ukrainian farming company that grows wheat, sunflower and other crops and stores and sells the grain. Its story is the war in one company: it farmed in Luhansk region, lost its entire land bank after Russia's full-scale invasion, and is now rebuilding on a much smaller scale in Kharkiv region. Its shares trade on the Warsaw Stock Exchange in Polish zloty (PLN).

> **Key takeaways**
> - Agroton lost all of its roughly 110,000 hectares of farmland after February 2022.
> - It is rebuilding: 7,500 hectares in 2025, about 21,500 hectares after buying the Mriia farm (about 14,000 hectares) from Kernel.
> - The last full-year figures available are from 2021: revenue $57.3 million, net profit $26.6 million.
> - The shares listed in 2010 at PLN 27 and after 2022 traded far below that price.

## How it makes money

Agroton earns money by growing crops on land it leases and selling the harvest. When it listed in 2010 its main products were sunflower seeds and wheat, with corn, barley and other grains on the side, and it also ran livestock and food processing. It owned grain elevators with 235,000 tonnes of storage. Revenue depends on three things: how many hectares it farms, the weather and yields, and world prices for grain and oilseeds, which are set in US dollars. The company reports in dollars, while the shares trade in zloty.

This is a small company with limited disclosure. The last full-year results published in standard databases are for 2021, a strong year: revenue $57.3 million and net profit $26.6 million. After the invasion Agroton told the Warsaw exchange that it no longer controlled its property or land in occupied Luhansk region, that its staff had left, and that full financial reporting would come after the war ends. In 2023 it held about 5,000 hectares in Kharkiv region and was clearing mines from the fields before sowing.

In 2025 the company started to grow again by buying land from Agroprosperis, reaching 7,500 hectares. It then took control of LLC Mriia, a Kharkiv-region farm of about 14,000 hectares from Kernel, one of Ukraine's largest agricultural groups; Ukraine's competition regulator approved the deal in 2026. That lifts the land bank to about 21,500 hectares — still only about a fifth of what it farmed before the war.

## The stock's story

Agroton raised $54 million in a Warsaw IPO in 2010 at PLN 27 per share, when it farmed 134,000 hectares in Luhansk and Kharkiv regions. The full-scale invasion in 2022 cut it off from its main land: in August that year the head of the Luhansk regional administration said occupiers had taken the grain harvested on Agroton's fields and 40 of its vehicles. After that the shares traded at a small fraction of the IPO price.


## Competitors

Agroton sells into the same grain and oilseed markets as much larger Ukrainian groups such as Kernel, MHP, Astarta and IMC, and smaller Warsaw-listed peers such as KSG Agro. With about 21,500 hectares it is a small player that takes world prices rather than setting them.

## Main risks

- **War:** its land is now in Kharkiv region, where fields had to be cleared of mines; the company has already lost its land to occupation once.
- **Little information:** full recent financial statements are hard to find, which makes the company hard to value.
- **Prices and weather:** a bad harvest or a fall in world grain prices hits revenue directly.
- **Small and illiquid stock:** with a market value of {{mcap:agt}}, few shares change hands and prices can jump on small trades.
- **Currency:** the company reports in US dollars, but the shares are priced in zloty, so exchange rates move the share price too.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Agroton shows how much a single company can lose when events go against it: the business built over decades was cut off almost overnight. Regular buying spreads purchases over time, but it cannot protect against a permanent loss of assets, so a stock like this, if held at all, usually makes up only a very small part of a broad portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
