**Canton** is a blockchain built for Wall Street: banks and market infrastructure firms use it to move tokenized bonds, funds and cash while each deal stays visible only to the parties in it. ## How it works

Canton was created by Digital Asset, a New York company founded in 2014, and apps on it are written in Digital Asset's Daml language. Instead of one public ledger that everyone can read, each transaction is shared only with the firms involved. A shared layer called the Global Synchronizer puts encrypted messages in the right order without reading them. It is run by "Super Validators" — large operators that also vote on the network's rules. So banks get the benefits of a shared ledger without showing their trades to competitors.

## What the token is used for

CC works on a "burn-mint" model. Every transaction pays a fee by burning CC, so coins are destroyed as the network is used. At the same time new CC is minted on a set schedule and handed to the Super Validators, validators and app builders that bring real activity. Every coin in circulation was earned for running or using the network rather than sold to early investors. If usage grows faster than minting, supply could stop growing; so far it still grows.

## Its story so far

Digital Asset spent years building ledger software for banks and exchanges. The Canton Network was presented in May 2023 as a "network of networks" for regulated finance, and its public layer and Canton Coin went live in July 2024. Large financial firms brought real flows, such as repo trades and tokenized Treasuries. CC became widely tradable on big crypto exchanges in late 2025, set its record of $0.194 in February 2026 and then lost more than half of its value as the wider crypto market fell.

## Alternatives

For tokenized bonds, funds and payments Canton competes with public networks such as [Ethereum]({lang}/eth/) and [Avalanche]({lang}/avax/), with the [XRP]({lang}/xrp/) Ledger, and with private bank chains like JPMorgan's Kinexys. Investors who want exposure to tokenized real-world assets also look at tokens like [Ondo]({lang}/ondo/).

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** CC fell about 60% from its February 2026 record within months.
- **Growing supply:** there is no cap, and as long as minting beats burning the number of coins keeps rising.
- **Usage vs. token:** banks using Canton does not automatically mean demand for CC.
- **Concentration:** rewards and votes sit with a small group of Super Validators, and reward rules have changed several times.
- **Fakes and regulation:** look-alike "CC" tokens exist on other chains, and rules for tokenized securities differ by country.

## What it means if you buy regularly

CC has only about a year of trading history, and most of it has been a decline from an early peak. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more when the price is low and less when it is high, without trying to guess the bottom — but it does not remove the risk of losses. Many people keep young tokens like CC to a small part of their savings that they can afford to see fall sharply. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
