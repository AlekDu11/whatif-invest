**Hyperliquid** is a crypto exchange that runs entirely on its own blockchain — and spends almost all of the fees it earns buying back its own token, HYPE. It is one of the largest on-chain markets for perpetual futures (bets on a price going up or down without an expiry date).

## How it works

Traders deposit stablecoins and trade bitcoin, ether, hundreds of other coins and, since 2026, even oil, silver and stock-style markets, with an order book that works like a normal exchange but settles every trade on the Hyperliquid blockchain. HYPE is the network's coin: holders stake it to secure the chain and vote on changes, and it is used for fees and as gas on HyperEVM, the part of the chain where other apps run. Up to 99% of trading fees go to the Assistance Fund, which buys HYPE on the market; in December 2025 validators voted to treat those tokens as burned.

## Why it matters

HYPE launched on November 29, 2024 with no venture-capital investors: 31% of the 1 billion tokens went straight to users in one of crypto's biggest airdrops. By mid-2026 the fund had bought back well over $1 billion of HYPE, and US spot HYPE ETFs launched in May 2026. One surprising fact: in April 2026 oil futures on Hyperliquid briefly traded more in a day than its bitcoin futures.

## Main risks

Less than half of the supply circulates; about 24% belongs to core contributors and is being released over several years, which can add selling pressure. The Hyper Foundation controls a large share of staked HYPE, and the validator set is small. Buybacks depend on trading volume, which can drop fast, and rules for leveraged crypto trading are tightening in many countries. HYPE can lose much of its value quickly; regular buying smooths swings but does not remove risk.
