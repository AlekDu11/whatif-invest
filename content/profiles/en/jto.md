**Jito** is a set of tools on the Solana blockchain: a liquid staking token, JitoSOL, and software that lets validators earn extra income from the order of transactions in a block (called MEV). JTO is the governance token of the Jito DAO, launched in December 2023, and it is worth {{mcap:jto}} in total today ({{rank:jto}} among cryptocurrencies). People follow it as a bet on how much activity flows through Solana.

## How it works

On Solana, people stake SOL with validators to secure the network and earn rewards. With JitoSOL you stake SOL through Jito and receive a token that keeps earning staking rewards while you can still trade it or use it in DeFi. Validators that run Jito's software can also accept "tips" from traders who want their transactions placed in a particular order; part of those tips is shared with stakers. In 2025 Jito introduced BAM (Block Assembly Marketplace), a new way of building blocks; by the end of June 2026 validators running it held about a third of all staked SOL.

## What the token is used for

JTO lets holders vote in the Jito DAO on fees, treasury spending and new products. The DAO collects fees from JitoSOL staking and withdrawals and a share of MEV tips; in July 2025 the core team proposed sending 100% of protocol fees to the DAO treasury. Under a 2026 proposal (JIP-38), 80% of fees from JTX, Jito's new trading platform, go to buying back and burning JTO for at least a year. JTO does not give a legal claim on profits — its value depends on what holders decide and on how much Solana activity runs through Jito.

## Its story so far

Jito Labs was founded in 2021 and raised a $10 million Series A in 2022, with Solana Ventures and Solana co-founder Anatoly Yakovenko among its backers. JitoSOL launched in 2022 and became one of the largest liquid staking tokens on Solana. JTO was announced on 27 November 2023 and airdropped in early December 2023, with about 115 million tokens circulating at the start. The price was highest in its first months of trading and then fell for long stretches as more tokens were unlocked. In the second quarter of 2026 JitoSOL held about 9.9 million SOL, down 20% from the quarter before.

## Alternatives

Jito's main business depends on [Solana]({lang}/sol/), so its fortunes follow that network. In Solana liquid staking it competes with Marinade, Sanctum and Jupiter's JupSOL, and with exchanges that offer staking. On Ethereum, [Lido DAO]({lang}/ldo/) plays a similar role with stETH.

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** JTO has spent long periods far below its early highs; drops of 70–90% are possible for tokens like this.
- **Token unlocks:** tokens of core contributors and investors were released gradually, and more supply can weigh on the price.
- **Falling revenue:** MEV tips and protocol revenue dropped sharply in 2025–2026 and depend on Solana trading activity.
- **Dependence on Solana:** outages, rule changes or lower use of Solana hit Jito directly.
- **Governance and regulation:** a few large holders can sway DAO votes, and rules for staking and tokens can change.

## What it means if you buy regularly

JTO has a short history, so even one year of regular buying says little about the long run. Buying a fixed amount over time spreads out the timing risk, but it does not protect you if the protocol's revenue and Solana activity keep shrinking. Many people keep tokens like this to a very small part of their crypto. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
