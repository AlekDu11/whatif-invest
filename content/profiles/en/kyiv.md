**Kyivstar** is Ukraine's largest telecom operator: about 23 million people used its mobile network at the 2025 listing and 1.3 million homes got broadband from it. Founded in 1994, it also owns the ride-hailing app Uklon, the medical booking service Helsi and the pharmacy search site Tabletki. In August 2025 it became the first Ukrainian company to trade on a US exchange, Nasdaq, under the ticker KYIV.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Q3 2026 revenue: $349 million, +17.5% in dollars and +26.5% in hryvnias.
> - EBITDA (profit before interest, tax and depreciation) was $189 million in Q3 2026, a 54% margin; net profit $99 million.
> - Digital services grew 68.9% to $76 million in Q3 2026 — 21.7% of revenue.
> - VEON kept 83.6% of the company after selling 14.38 million shares at $10.50 in February 2026.

## How it makes money

Most money still comes from mobile and home internet: telecom and infrastructure revenue was $273 million in Q3 2026, up 8.4% in dollars. The average mobile customer paid about $4.0 a month in that quarter, 8.6% more than a year earlier, and 71.5% of customers used 4G. People who take several services at once (mobile plus home internet or Kyivstar TV) made up 40.3% of active customers and paid about 1.5 times more than average.

The fast-growing part is digital. Uklon had $32.8 million of revenue in Q2 2026 (+50.8%) and was expanding into more Ukrainian cities and Uzbekistan. Helsi passed 109,000 premium subscribers by mid-2026, and Tabletki joined the group in February 2026. In total, 29.5 million people used Kyivstar's digital services in Q3 2026. In 2026 the company also agreed to buy the Ukrainian cloud provider GigaCloud.

In the quarter to 30 September 2026 Kyivstar earned $349 million in revenue and $189 million of EBITDA (+10.3%). Net profit was $99 million, against an $89 million loss a year earlier that included a one-time $162 million non-cash charge linked to the Nasdaq listing. Higher energy costs cut the telecom margin by 3.1 points to 57.7%. At the end of September 2026 Kyivstar had $436 million of cash, and it generated $274 million of free cash flow in the first nine months of the year. With the Q3 2026 results it raised its 2026 forecast to revenue growth of 15–17% in dollars.

## The stock's story

Kyivstar reached Nasdaq on 15 August 2025 by merging with a SPAC (a listed shell company), Cohen Circle Acquisition Corp. I, raising $178 million. Our price history starts in December 2024 at about $10 — that is the SPAC's share price before the merger. On the first trading day the stock opened at $14 and slipped to about $11.5. It climbed above $16 in June 2026, then gave most of those gains back in the following months. Earlier, in December 2023, a cyberattack linked to Russian military intelligence knocked out its network for days — a reminder of how exposed the business is.

## Competitors

In mobile, Kyivstar competes with Vodafone Ukraine and lifecell; in home internet, with Ukrtelecom, Datagroup-Volia and many local providers. Uklon competes with Bolt and Uber. Kyivstar's edge is the largest network and customer base in the country, plus satellite backup through Starlink Direct-to-Cell, which more than 6 million of its customers had used by mid-2026.

## Main risks

- **The war:** Russian strikes damage towers and the power grid; blackouts raise energy costs and can interrupt service.
- **The hryvnia:** Kyivstar earns in hryvnias but reports in dollars — growth of 26.5% in hryvnias became 17.5% in dollars in Q3 2026.
- **A controlling shareholder:** in 2026 VEON owned 83.6% and planned to cut its stake gradually toward 80%; further share sales can weigh on the price, and small shareholders have little say.
- **Cyberattacks and regulation:** the 2023 attack showed the cost of being a target, and Ukrainian taxes and rules can change quickly in wartime.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Kyivstar has traded on Nasdaq only since August 2025, so there is no long history to learn from, and our early data reflects the SPAC price. The company's results grew in 2026, but the share price depends heavily on news from the war and on the hryvnia. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your entry points; it does not reduce country risk, which is why many people hold a single-country stock like this as a small part of a broader portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
