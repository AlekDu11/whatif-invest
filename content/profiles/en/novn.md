**Novartis** is a Basel-based maker of prescription medicines — from the breast-cancer drug Kisqali to the heart pill Entresto — and one of the two giants, with Roche, that make Switzerland's stock market a pharma market.

## How it makes money

Novartis researches new medicines, wins patents and sells the drugs to hospitals, insurers and health systems worldwide. In 2025 it sold $54.5 billion (+8%) and earned $14.0 billion in net profit, keeping about 40 cents of every dollar of sales as core operating profit. Its biggest products are Entresto (heart failure, $7.7 billion in 2025), Cosentyx (psoriasis and arthritis, $6.7 billion), Kisqali ($4.8 billion, +58%) and Kesimpta (multiple sclerosis, $4.4 billion).

The company was born in 1996 from the merger of Ciba-Geigy and Sandoz. It later spun off its eye-care unit Alcon (2019) and its generics arm Sandoz (2023), so today it is a pure "innovative medicines" company.

## Why it matters

Novartis is one of the heaviest stocks in the Swiss SMI index and a classic dividend payer: for 2025 it proposed CHF 3.70 per share, its 29th increase in a row since it was created. One surprising fact: in the third quarter of 2026 Entresto sales fell 38% after US generics arrived — yet total sales still grew 4%, carried by Kisqali (+37%) and the cholesterol shot Leqvio (+63%).

## Main risks

Patents expire: every blockbuster eventually faces cheaper copies, and new drugs must replace the lost sales. Research is risky — in Q3 2026 Novartis wrote down $1.9 billion on one experimental drug. US drug-price pressure and tariffs hit all big pharma. Prices on our site are in US dollars, so the Swiss franc also moves the return. Buying a fixed amount regularly smooths price swings but does not remove the risk.
