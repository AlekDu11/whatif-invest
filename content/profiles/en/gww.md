**W. W. Grainger** sells the unglamorous things businesses need every day to keep running: safety gloves, light bulbs, motors, pumps, tools, cleaning supplies and spare parts. Its customers are factories, contractors, hospitals, schools and government agencies, not shoppers. The company was incorporated in Illinois in 1928 and is based in Lake Forest, near Chicago.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Sales: $5.18 billion in Q3 2026, +11.3% a year (+14.9% on a comparable daily basis).
> - Adjusted earnings per share rose 22.8% to $12.54; the 2026 forecast was raised to $47–48.
> - The dividend was raised 10% to $2.49 a quarter — the 55th year in a row of increases.
> - The stock hit a record of about $1,521 on 4 November 2026 and closed at $1,359 on 20 November, about 11% lower.

## How it makes money

Grainger is a distributor: it buys from thousands of manufacturers, stores the goods in warehouses and delivers them fast. This is called MRO — maintenance, repair and operating supplies. It has two businesses.

**High-Touch Solutions** serves large and mid-sized customers in the US, Canada, Mexico and Puerto Rico with about 2 million products, sales reps, 245 US branches and 21 US distribution centres. Sales grew 13.8% in Q3 2026.

**Endless Assortment** is the online side for smaller buyers: **Zoro** in the US (about 13 million products) and **MonotaRO** in Japan, a majority-owned listed company offering access to about 29 million. Sales rose 19.3% on a comparable basis; Zoro US grew 25.2% and MonotaRO 13.6% in local currency. Grainger left the UK at the end of 2025, selling Cromwell and closing Zoro UK. In total it serves more than 4.6 million customers with about 25,000 employees.

## The numbers right now

In the quarter to 30 September 2026 Grainger had $5.18 billion of sales at a 38.8% gross margin, and an adjusted operating margin of 16.1%. Net earnings were $587 million. Operating cash flow was $661 million; $278 million went on buybacks and $145 million on dividends. Debt was about $2.7 billion against $768 million in cash.

Management said demand improved across all major end markets, helped by data-centre and power-plant construction. For 2026 it now expects $19.8–20.0 billion of sales and adjusted earnings of $47–48 per share. At the 20 November price, that is roughly 29 times this year's expected profit (our estimate) — a premium price for a distributor.

## The stock's story

Its worst stretch came in 2017: fearing Amazon Business, it cut prices sharply, profits fell and the stock sank to about $155 in August 2017. The cheaper prices won more orders, profits recovered, and the stock climbed for years.

In 2025 it slipped about 3% and hit a 52-week low of about $916 on 21 November 2025. Then growth sped up: on 4 November 2026, after the third beat-and-raise quarter in a row, it reached a record of about $1,521. It has since given back part of that gain.

## Competitors

Grainger competes with other industrial distributors such as Fastenal and MSC Industrial Direct, with Amazon Business, with manufacturers selling directly and with thousands of local and regional suppliers.

## Main risks

- **Economic cycle:** when factories and builders slow down, they buy fewer supplies; strong data-centre demand may not last forever.
- **Online price pressure:** Amazon Business and others can push prices down, as in 2017.
- **Costs:** freight, diesel and tariffs squeeze margins; some supplier rebates that helped in 2026 reset in 2027.
- **Valuation:** a high price for steady growth means disappointing results can hit the stock hard.
- **Japan and currency:** MonotaRO's growth slowed, and a weaker yen lowers its value in dollars.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Grainger is a steady, cash-generating business with a long record of rising dividends, but its stock has had bad years too, as in 2017, and can drop sharply after a weak quarter. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads purchases across expensive and cheap periods, and dividends can be reinvested. It is still a single company, so many people hold it as one part of a broader portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
