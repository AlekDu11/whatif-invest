**PancakeSwap** is a decentralised exchange (DEX): a site and app where people swap crypto tokens straight from their own wallets, with no company holding their money. It launched in September 2020 on Binance Smart Chain, now called BNB Chain, and has since spread to Ethereum, Arbitrum, Base and other networks. ## How it works

PancakeSwap has no order book. Instead, users called liquidity providers put pairs of tokens into pools, and a program — an automated market maker (AMM) — sets the price from the ratio of the two tokens in each pool. Every swap pays a small fee, and most of it goes to the liquidity providers. Version 3, launched in April 2023, let them put their money into a chosen price range. PancakeSwap Infinity (formerly v4) went live on BNB Chain at the end of April 2025 and on Base in July 2025; it added "hooks", plug-ins that let developers build pools with their own rules.

## Why people think it has value

CAKE is used to reward liquidity providers, for staking and for voting on how the protocol is run. Part of the fees from trading and other PancakeSwap products is used to buy CAKE on the market and burn it — remove it for good. Since September 2023 more CAKE has been burned than created, so the supply has been shrinking; media reports put the net reduction in 2025 at about 8%. The idea for holders is simple: the more people trade on PancakeSwap, the more CAKE disappears.

## Its story so far

PancakeSwap grew fast in late 2020 and 2021, when high fees on Ethereum pushed many traders to the cheaper Binance Smart Chain. CAKE traded below $0.20 in November 2020 and set its record in April 2021, at the peak of that boom. In the 2022 bear market it lost most of its value, together with the rest of DeFi. In December 2023 voters cut the maximum supply from 750 million to 450 million, with about 98% in favour. In April 2025 Tokenomics 3.0 retired veCAKE — a system in which users locked CAKE for extra votes and rewards — and cut emissions; projects built on veCAKE, such as Cakepie, criticised the change. In a vote on 16–19 January 2026 the cap was cut again, to 400 million.

## Alternatives

The best-known rival is [Uniswap]({lang}/uni/), the largest DEX on Ethereum, whose design PancakeSwap first copied. Other DEXs — SushiSwap, Curve, Raydium and Jupiter on Solana, Aerodrome on Base — compete for the same traders, and many people simply swap on centralised exchanges such as Binance. CAKE is also closely tied to [BNB]({lang}/bnb/) and BNB Chain: when that network is busy, PancakeSwap usually is too.

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** CAKE is far below its 2021 record and can fall sharply again.
- **Competition:** traders can move quickly to other DEXs, networks or apps, and fewer trades mean fewer fees and burns.
- **Rule changes by vote:** the tokenomics have changed several times; future votes can change emissions, burns and rewards again.
- **Smart contracts and scams:** bugs, hacks and fake tokens in open pools can cost users money, and the team is anonymous.
- **Ties to BNB Chain:** much of PancakeSwap's story depends on BNB Chain and the Binance ecosystem, so problems there would hit it too.
- **Regulation:** rules for DEXs and DeFi differ by country and can change quickly.

## What it means if you buy regularly

CAKE's history is one huge rise in 2021 followed by a long, deep fall. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your purchases over highs and lows without guessing the bottom, but it does not remove the risk of losses. Burns shrink the supply, yet the price still depends on how many people want to use PancakeSwap and hold CAKE. Many people keep a single-project token like this to a small part of their savings. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
