**TSMC** (Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company) makes the chips inside most iPhones and the AI processors Nvidia sells — the world's largest contract chipmaker and one of the most valuable companies in Asia. Morris Chang founded it in Taiwan in 1987.

## How it makes money

TSMC does not sell chips under its own name. Companies such as Apple, Nvidia and AMD design them, and TSMC manufactures them in its factories ("fabs") for a fee per silicon wafer. This model, called a pure-play foundry, was TSMC's own invention. The newest, smallest production processes are the most profitable, because few rivals can make them at scale.

In 2025 revenue rose about 36% to roughly $122.4 billion. High-performance computing — mostly AI and data-centre chips — made up 58% of sales, up from 51% a year earlier, while smartphones fell to 29%. The company expects revenue to grow nearly 30% in 2026.

## Why it matters

Most of the world's most advanced chips come out of TSMC's fabs in Taiwan, which makes it a choke point for the whole AI boom. To spread the risk, it is building factories abroad, with total planned investment in the US of $165 billion, including six advanced fabs in Arizona. One surprising fact: a company almost no consumer has heard of makes the brains of products used by billions of people.

## Main risks

Tension between China and Taiwan is the biggest risk: most production is on the island. A handful of customers bring in a large share of revenue, so a slowdown in AI spending would hit fast; even the CEO has said careless investment would be "a disaster". US tariffs and export controls can change, overseas fabs cost more to run, and a stronger Taiwan dollar trims profits. Chipmaking is cyclical. Buying a fixed amount regularly smooths price swings but does not remove the risk.
