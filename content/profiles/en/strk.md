**Starknet** is a "layer 2" network on top of [Ethereum]({lang}/eth/): it bundles thousands of transactions together, proves with cryptography that they are all correct and posts that proof to Ethereum, so using apps costs a fraction of what it does on Ethereum itself. It was built by the Israeli company StarkWare and went live in November 2021. Its token STRK is worth {{mcap:strk}} in total ({{rank:strk}} among cryptocurrencies).

## How it works

Starknet is a "ZK rollup". Transactions run on Starknet, and a prover then creates a STARK — a short mathematical proof that every step was computed correctly. Ethereum only has to check that proof, not repeat the work, so many transactions share one cheap check while still relying on Ethereum's security. StarkWare's co-founder Eli Ben-Sasson is one of the scientists who invented STARK proofs; the project says they are also resistant to future quantum computers. Developers write Starknet apps in Cairo, a language designed to make programs easy to prove.

## What STRK is used for

STRK pays transaction fees on Starknet, is used to vote on network upgrades, and is staked: holders lock it with validators to help secure the network and earn new STRK as a reward. Staking started in November 2024. In August 2025 an on-chain vote (SNIP-31, about 93.6% in favour) allowed wrapped bitcoin — WBTC, tBTC, LBTC and SolvBTC — to be staked alongside STRK, and bitcoin staking went live on 30 September 2025. In February 2026 Starknet announced strkBTC, a bitcoin-backed token with private balances and confidential transfers.

## Its story so far

StarkWare was founded in 2018 and first sold its technology as StarkEx, an engine used by exchanges such as dYdX and Immutable. The general-purpose Starknet network opened in November 2021. The STRK token launched on 20 February 2024 with a large airdrop to users and developers; it traded highest in its first days and then fell for most of the following two years as big amounts of tokens held by early investors and the team were gradually unlocked. In 2025–2026 the project re-positioned Starknet as a place to use bitcoin, not only Ethereum.

## Alternatives

Starknet competes with other Ethereum layer 2 networks: [Arbitrum]({lang}/arb/) and [Optimism]({lang}/op/) (optimistic rollups), Coinbase's Base, and other ZK rollups such as [ZKsync]({lang}/zk/), Scroll and Linea. Its distinctive points are STARK proofs and the Cairo language; the downside is that Ethereum developers must learn a new language.

## Main risks

- **Competition:** many layer 2 networks fight for the same users and apps, and the most-used ones get most of the activity.
- **Token supply:** staking adds new STRK, and tokens of early investors and contributors were unlocked over several years.
- **Technology:** complex cryptography, bridges and wrapped-bitcoin issuers can fail or be hacked; parts of the network are still run by a small set of operators.
- **Value capture:** cheap fees are good for users but leave less revenue that could support the token's price.
- **Big swings:** like most young tokens, STRK has lost most of its value from its peak before.

## What it means if you buy regularly

STRK is a young, volatile token with only a short price history, so a regular plan here has seen mostly falling prices so far. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads the risk of a bad entry point, but it does not protect against a long decline. Many people keep tokens like this to a small part of their crypto holdings. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
