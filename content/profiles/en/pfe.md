**Pfizer** is one of the world's biggest drugmakers: the company behind the Eliquis blood thinner, the Prevnar pneumonia vaccines and, during the pandemic, the Comirnaty COVID vaccine and the Paxlovid pill. Now it is trying to replace fading COVID money and older drugs that lose patent protection soon with new cancer medicines, vaccines and an obesity program. The stock trades on the New York Stock Exchange.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: $16.6 billion in Q3 2026, flat on a year ago; without COVID products, +7%.
> - Newly launched and acquired drugs brought in $3.8 billion, +32% a year.
> - 2026 forecast raised to $62–64 billion in revenue and $2.95–3.05 adjusted earnings per share.
> - The stock was about 57% below its late-2021 high of about $59 in June 2026.

## How it makes money

Almost all of Pfizer's sales come from prescription medicines and vaccines sold to hospitals, pharmacies, wholesalers and governments. Last quarter **Primary Care** brought in $6.97 billion (Eliquis, Prevnar, the RSV vaccine Abrysvo, Comirnaty, Paxlovid), **Oncology** $4.33 billion, **Specialty Care** $3.35 billion (including the Vyndaqel family for a rare heart disease) and **Hospital & Biosimilars** $1.64 billion.

The cancer business grew largely through the 2023 purchase of Seagen, which added Padcev, Adcetris, Tukysa and Tivdak. In the third quarter Padcev sales rose 55% and Eliquis 25%, while Paxlovid fell 63% and Comirnaty 29%.

## The numbers right now

For the quarter to September 2026 Pfizer reported $16.58 billion of revenue, versus $16.65 billion a year earlier. Net profit was $3.24 billion, or $0.56 a share (down 9%); adjusted earnings per share were flat at $0.87. In the first nine months it spent $8.3 billion on research and paid $7.3 billion in dividends — $1.29 per share, or $0.43 a quarter — and bought back no shares.

On 3 November Pfizer raised its 2026 outlook to $62–64 billion in revenue and $2.95–3.05 adjusted earnings per share. That already counts about $1.1 billion of sales lost to cheaper generic and biosimilar copies this year.

## The stock's story

For years Pfizer was a slow, steady dividend stock; then the pandemic made it a winner. Vaccine and pill sales pushed 2022 revenue to a record $100.3 billion, and the stock topped out at about $59 at the end of 2021. When COVID demand collapsed, so did the share price: by mid-2026 it was around $25, roughly 57% below the peak.

To refill its pipeline, Pfizer closed the purchase of obesity-drug developer Metsera on 13 November 2025 after a bidding war with Novo Nordisk — $65.60 a share in cash plus up to $20.65 more if milestones are met, for a deal worth up to about $10 billion. Its lead drug is a weekly or monthly GLP-1 injection heading into late-stage trials.

## Competitors

Pfizer competes with the other giants: Merck in vaccines and cancer, GSK in RSV and other vaccines, AstraZeneca, Johnson & Johnson and Novartis in cancer and heart drugs, and Moderna in COVID shots. Eliquis is shared with Bristol Myers Squibb. In obesity it starts far behind Eli Lilly and Novo Nordisk, which already sell blockbuster weight-loss drugs.

## Main risks

- **Patent cliff:** US protection for Eliquis and part of the Prevnar family ends between 2025 and 2028, with cancer drugs Ibrance and Xtandi following in 2027 — cheaper copies can cut sales quickly.
- **COVID keeps shrinking:** Paxlovid and Comirnaty sales are still falling sharply.
- **Pipeline bets:** obesity and new cancer drugs must win approval and then win share from strong rivals.
- **Dividend pressure:** dividends take a large part of cash flow; if earnings fall, the payout may come under strain.
- **US drug pricing** and policy changes can lower what Pfizer earns per pack.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Pfizer shows that a big, profitable company can still lose more than half of its value when a boom ends. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your entry over good and bad months, and the dividends add to the total, but they do not protect you if the price stays low for years. Many people hold one stock like this only as a small part of a broader portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
