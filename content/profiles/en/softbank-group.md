**SoftBank Group** is less a phone company than a giant investment fund run by its founder, Masayoshi Son. It owns most of the chip designer Arm, controls the Japanese mobile operator SoftBank Corp., runs the Vision Funds that hold stakes in hundreds of tech start-ups, and has become one of the biggest backers of OpenAI, the maker of ChatGPT. Its shares trade in Tokyo under the code 9984, and they move like a leveraged bet on artificial intelligence.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Net income was ¥5.0 trillion in fiscal 2025 (to 31 March 2026) — according to the company, the highest ever recorded by a Japanese company.
> - In the April–September 2026 half it swung to a net loss of ¥768.0 billion, even though net sales rose 13.3% to ¥4.24 trillion.
> - By 1 October 2026 SoftBank had invested $64.6 billion in OpenAI for a stake of about 13% (fully diluted).
> - The shares were split 4-for-1 on 1 January 2026.

## How it makes money

SoftBank Group earns mostly from the rising (or falling) value of what it owns, not from selling products. Its big pieces are Arm, whose chip designs are inside almost every smartphone and a growing number of AI servers; SoftBank Corp., a Japanese telecom company that also controls the LINE Yahoo internet group and the PayPay payments app (listed on Nasdaq in March 2026); the Vision Funds, with stakes in companies such as ByteDance; and its OpenAI shares. When these holdings go up in value, SoftBank books investment gains; when they fall, it books losses — so profits jump around from quarter to quarter. The company also borrows heavily against its shares, so interest costs and debt matter a lot.

In the six months to 30 September 2026 SoftBank made ¥1.73 trillion of investment gains — mostly from its Intel shares (about ¥1.06 trillion) and the Vision Funds, led by ByteDance. But that was outweighed by a ¥597.2 billion loss on power-equipment orders for US AI data centres, ¥790.7 billion of finance costs and ¥433.3 billion of derivative losses, leaving a net loss of ¥768.0 billion after a profit of ¥2.92 trillion a year earlier. In the July–September quarter alone the net loss was ¥1.12 trillion.

At the end of September SoftBank carried its OpenAI shares at a fair value of $99.6 billion, against $54.6 billion invested by then. In fiscal 2025 its net income was ¥5.0 trillion, up from ¥1.15 trillion a year earlier, and its loan-to-value ratio was 17.0% at 31 March 2026.

## The stock's story

Masayoshi Son founded SoftBank in 1981 as a software distributor. In 2000 it put about $20 million into a young Chinese company called Alibaba — one of the most profitable start-up bets ever — but the same year the dot-com crash wiped out most of SoftBank's share price. It became a mobile operator by buying Vodafone's Japanese business in 2006, bought the US carrier Sprint in 2013 and the British chip designer Arm in 2016 for about £24 billion. The $100 billion Vision Fund launched in 2017; losses on WeWork and other start-ups hit the shares hard in 2019–2022. Arm listed on Nasdaq in 2023, and from 2025 Son bet big on OpenAI, adding a $30 billion follow-on commitment in February 2026. In September 2026 SoftBank completed the purchase of DigitalBridge for $3.1 billion; in November 2026 China blocked its planned purchase of ABB's robotics business there.

## Competitors

As an investor SoftBank competes for AI deals with Microsoft, Nvidia, Amazon, Google, sovereign funds from the Gulf and big venture firms. Arm competes with x86 chips from Intel and AMD and with the free RISC-V design. SoftBank Corp. fights NTT Docomo, KDDI (au) and Rakuten Mobile in Japan.

## Main risks

- **One big AI bet:** a large part of its value is OpenAI and Arm; if AI spending slows or OpenAI's valuation falls, SoftBank's value can drop sharply.
- **Debt:** SoftBank borrows against its Arm, OpenAI and Intel shares; falling prices can force sales at bad moments.
- **Profits swing:** investment gains and losses make results unpredictable — a record year can be followed by a loss, as in 2026.
- **Key person:** the strategy depends heavily on Masayoshi Son.
- **Discount:** the shares often trade below the value of the assets SoftBank owns, and that gap can widen.

## What it means if you buy regularly

SoftBank Group is one of the most volatile big stocks in Japan: it has lost more than half its value several times and then recovered. Buying a fixed amount regularly smooths out the price you pay, but it does not remove the risk of a concentrated, debt-funded AI bet. Many people hold a stock like this only as a small part of a wider portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
