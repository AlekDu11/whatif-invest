**Zama** is a privacy protocol that lets blockchains work with data that stays encrypted the whole time — balances, bids or votes can be checked and calculated without anyone seeing them. It comes from Zama, a cryptography company in Paris founded in 2020 by Rand Hindi and Pascal Paillier, and its ZAMA token pays for the encryption work and secures the network. ## How it works

On a normal blockchain like Ethereum everything is public: anyone can see how much you hold and what you send. Zama adds a confidential layer on top of existing chains. Smart contracts handle encrypted numbers, while a network of operators does the heavy encrypted calculations off-chain and manages the decryption keys jointly, so no single party can read the data. The mainnet went live on Ethereum in December 2025, starting with confidential transfers of tokens such as stablecoins.

## What the token is used for

Apps pay protocol fees in ZAMA when they encrypt, compute on or decrypt data. Operators who run the encryption servers and key management stake ZAMA, and holders can delegate to them for a share of the rewards. The bet on ZAMA is that banks, payment apps and DeFi want on-chain privacy that still follows the rules, and that Zama becomes the standard way to get it.

## Its story so far

Zama spent its first years building open-source FHE libraries used by researchers and companies. In June 2025 it raised a $57 million Series B led by Blockchange Ventures and Pantera Capital, which took its valuation above $1 billion. The Zama Confidential Blockchain Protocol launched on Ethereum in December 2025. In January 2026 Zama sold tokens in an on-chain sealed-bid auction, where the bids themselves were encrypted with its own technology; it attracted about $118 million. ZAMA was listed on major exchanges on 2 February 2026.

## Alternatives

Privacy coins such as [Monero]({lang}/xmr/) and [Zcash]({lang}/zec/) hide payments on their own blockchains rather than adding privacy to Ethereum apps. [Aztec]({lang}/aztec/) builds a private layer-2 network for Ethereum using zero-knowledge proofs. [Oasis]({lang}/rose/) and [Secret]({lang}/scrt/) offer confidential smart contracts based on secure hardware, and [Mind Network]({lang}/fhe/) also uses FHE.

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** ZAMA is a young token with a short price history; deep drops are common for new coins.
- **Unlocks:** most of the supply was not in circulation at launch; tokens for the team and investors unlock over time.
- **Speed and cost:** FHE is still much slower and more expensive than normal computing, which limits what apps can do.
- **Adoption:** the protocol needs real apps and institutions to use it, not just interest from traders.
- **Regulation:** privacy tools draw extra attention from regulators, and rules can change quickly.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Zama has traded only since February 2026, so its price history is short and says little about the long run. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your entry price over time and can soften sharp swings, but it does not remove the risk that a young project fails to find enough users. Many people keep such tokens to a small share of their savings they can afford to see fall sharply. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
