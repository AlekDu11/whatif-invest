**VEON** is a mobile operator for emerging markets, headquartered in Dubai: it runs Jazz in Pakistan, Kyivstar in Ukraine, Beeline in Kazakhstan and Uzbekistan, and Banglalink in Bangladesh. Together these networks have about 152 million telecom subscribers. Until 2023 VEON's biggest business was Beeline in Russia; it sold it and now owns 83.6% of Kyivstar, which is listed separately on Nasdaq.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Q3 2026 revenue: $1.31 billion, +17.2%; EBITDA (profit before interest, tax and depreciation) $577 million, +10.1%.
> - Digital services — mobile money, video, apps — brought in $355 million, +45.9%, or 27.2% of revenue.
> - Pakistan is the largest market ($522 million, +28.9%); Ukraine is second ($352 million, +17.4%).
> - The share fell about 64% in February 2022 and has since risen roughly tenfold from its 2022 low in our adjusted data.

## How it makes money

VEON earns most of its money from prepaid mobile plans: calls, data and SMS in countries where many people go online only by phone. The average customer pays little — mobile ARPU (average revenue per user) was $1.8 a month in Q3 2026 — so growth comes from more data use, price rises and moving customers to 4G.

The second engine is digital: 221.1 million people use VEON's apps and services, including 59.7 million customers of its financial services such as mobile wallets. Digital revenue grew 45.9% in Q3, much faster than classic telecom (+9.2%). VEON is adding pieces: it bought Perfectum in Uzbekistan, Kyivstar agreed to buy the cloud provider GigaCloud, and it received approval for a digital banking licence in Bangladesh.

## The numbers right now

In the quarter to 30 September 2026 VEON had $1.307 billion of revenue and $577 million of EBITDA. Net profit was $225 million, of which $187 million belonged to VEON shareholders ($2.67 per ADS). Revenue by market: Pakistan $522 million, Ukraine $352 million, Kazakhstan $230 million (+8.5%), Bangladesh $117 million (+0.7%) and Uzbekistan $86 million (+11.0%). VEON held $2.03 billion of cash, with leverage of 1.05 times EBITDA, and spent $112.5 million on buying back ADSs under its current programme. It raised the floor of its 2026 forecast to revenue growth of 16–18% and EBITDA growth of 10–12%.

## The stock's story

VEON began as VimpelCom, the Russian Beeline operator, and in our price history (adjusted for ADS ratio changes) it traded above $500 in 2007. The real turning point was Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine: in February 2022 the share lost about 64% in one month, and by October 2022 it was near $8 in adjusted terms. In March 2023 VEON combined every 25 old ADSs into one new ADS — our history is adjusted for this. It completed the sale of Beeline Russia to local managers on 9 October 2023, moved its headquarters from Amsterdam to Dubai, and since 25 November 2024 has traded only on Nasdaq. After years of growth and buybacks, the price climbed back above $70 in autumn 2026.

## Competitors

VEON fights a different rival in each country: Zong and Ufone in Pakistan, Grameenphone and Robi in Bangladesh, Kcell in Kazakhstan, Ucell in Uzbekistan, and Vodafone Ukraine and lifecell in Ukraine. Its strengths are large networks and the digital services built on top of them.

## Main risks

- **Currencies:** VEON reports in dollars but earns in Pakistani rupees, hryvnias, tenge, taka and sums; a weaker local currency shrinks dollar results.
- **The war in Ukraine:** Kyivstar brings about a quarter of revenue and depends on power supply and the security situation.
- **Emerging-market politics:** taxes, licence fees and regulation in Pakistan, Bangladesh and Central Asia can change quickly.
- **Quality of profit:** part of recent profit and cash flow came from one-off items such as asset sales; free cash flow fell 33.8% in Q3 because of a high base from such proceeds a year earlier.

## What it means if you buy regularly

VEON shows both sides of buying a single stock: someone who bought before February 2022 saw most of their money vanish in weeks, while someone who kept buying through the crash collected cheap shares. Regular buying spreads your entry points, but it cannot remove country, currency or war risk, so many people hold a stock like this only as a small part of a broader portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
