**Decred** is a cryptocurrency built around one idea: the people who hold the coins, not miners or a company, should decide how the network changes. It launched in February 2016, was started by developers who had worked on bitcoin software, and has the same 21 million coin limit as bitcoin. ## How it works

Decred mixes two systems. Miners still produce blocks with computing power (proof of work), as in bitcoin. But each block must also be approved by holders who have staked DCR in tickets (proof of stake). A ticket costs a set amount of DCR, which is locked until the ticket is picked at random to vote; then the holder gets the coins back plus a reward. Over time stakers' share of the reward grew: in 2022 most of it moved to stakers, and in 2023 holders voted to switch mining to a new algorithm, BLAKE3, and cut miners' share to 1%. Today stakers receive 89% of each block reward and the treasury 10%.

## Why people think it has value

Decred offers what its fans call "secure, adaptable and self-funding" money. Because stakeholders must approve blocks, an attacker would need both huge computing power and a large share of coins. Rule changes are voted on openly, so the project can upgrade without the splits seen in bitcoin's history. The treasury pays developers through Politeia, a public proposal system, without relying on donations or a company. Staking also attracts holders who want to earn more DCR while keeping a say in the network.

## Its story so far

Decred started in February 2016 without a public token sale: a small share of coins went to the founders and a free "airdrop" went to early community members. In 2017 holders approved the first rule change by stakeholder vote. The decentralized treasury followed in 2020, after a vote. In the 2021 crypto boom DCR reached about $250 in April 2021, then lost most of its value in the 2022 crash. The 2023 switch to BLAKE3 was meant to push out large specialised mining machines and shift security further toward stakers.

## Alternatives

As scarce digital money, Decred competes with [Bitcoin]({lang}/btc/) and [Litecoin]({lang}/ltc/). Among coins with privacy features and their own treasury, [Zcash]({lang}/zec/) and [Dash]({lang}/dash/) are its closest rivals; Dash also uses a funding system for development voted by its holders.

## Main risks

- **Small market:** trading volume is low, so prices can jump or crash on modest orders.
- **Attention:** Decred is far less known than bitcoin, and fewer big exchanges list it.
- **Concentration:** a few large stakers can carry a big weight in votes.
- **Security:** with only 1% of rewards going to miners, the system relies heavily on staking working as designed.
- **Big swings and regulation:** drops of 80–90% have happened, and rules for staking and privacy features vary by country.

## What it means if you buy regularly

DCR has moved in long cycles of booms and deep falls. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your entry price over time, and some holders also stake to grow their coins — but staking locks your DCR for a while and does not protect against a falling price. Keep coins like this to a small part of savings you can afford to see fall sharply. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
