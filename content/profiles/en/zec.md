**Zcash** is a cryptocurrency that works like Bitcoin but lets you hide who paid whom and how much — a digital version of paying in cash. Launched in October 2016, it became one of the biggest crypto stories of 2025–2026 as interest in financial privacy came back.

## How it works

Zcash runs on its own blockchain, secured by miners, with the same hard cap as Bitcoin: 21 million coins, and a block reward that halves roughly every four years. The difference is choice. A "transparent" address is public, like Bitcoin. A "shielded" address uses zero-knowledge proofs (zk-SNARKs) — maths that proves a payment is valid without revealing the sender, the receiver or the amount. ZEC itself pays network fees and rewards miners; part of new issuance funds development grants.

## Why it matters

For years few people used the private side. That changed: by April 2026 about 31% of all ZEC sat in shielded pools, up from 11% a year earlier. Easier wallets, listings on big platforms and Zcash funds in the US and Europe brought new buyers. One surprising fact: on its first trading day in 2016 ZEC changed hands above $3,000 — a price it has never reached since.

## Main risks

Privacy coins face regulatory pressure: some exchanges have delisted them in parts of Europe and Asia, and rules may tighten. The price is extremely volatile — ZEC rose more than tenfold in 2025, then fell about 60% from its November peak, and swung from about $1,690 in September 2026 to roughly $900 now. The network is small (around a hundred full nodes) and depends on a few developer organisations. Buying a fixed amount regularly smooths the swings but does not remove the risk.
