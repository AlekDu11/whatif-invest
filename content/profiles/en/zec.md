**Zcash** is a cryptocurrency that works much like Bitcoin but lets you hide who paid whom and how much — a digital version of paying in cash. It launched in October 2016 and became one of the biggest crypto stories of 2025–2026 as interest in financial privacy returned; in November 2026 all ZEC together were worth about $13.5 billion.

> **Key takeaways**
> - There will never be more than 21 million ZEC, the same cap as Bitcoin; about 17 million already circulate.
> - By April 2026 about 31% of all ZEC sat in private "shielded" pools, up from about 11% a year earlier.
> - The NU7 upgrade went live in early November 2026: blocks every 25 seconds instead of 75, and 60% of fees set aside for future rewards.
> - On its first trading day in October 2016 ZEC traded above $3,000 — a price it has never reached since.

## How it works

Zcash runs on its own blockchain, secured by miners, with a block reward that halves roughly every four years. The difference from Bitcoin is choice. A "transparent" address is public, like Bitcoin's. A "shielded" address uses zero-knowledge proofs (zk-SNARKs) — maths that proves a payment is valid without revealing the sender, the receiver or the amount. Users can share a "viewing key" with an auditor or tax office if they want to prove their payments. Part of new issuance funds development grants. Since NU7, blocks arrive three times faster, and most of each transaction fee is recycled into a pool that pays future miners.

## Why people think it has value

ZEC is needed to pay network fees and is what miners earn, so its value rests on demand for private, censorship-resistant payments with a fixed supply. Supporters see it as "Bitcoin with privacy". Easier wallets such as Zashi, cross-chain swaps through NEAR Intents, listings on big platforms and Zcash investment products in the US and Europe brought new buyers in 2025–2026.

## Its story so far

Zcash grew out of the academic Zerocoin and Zerocash research and was launched on 28 October 2016 by the Electric Coin Company, led by Zooko Wilcox. Because very few coins existed on day one, the price spiked above $3,000 and then fell for years. For a long time only a small share of users used shielded addresses, and some exchanges delisted privacy coins. In 2025 the story changed: ZEC rose more than tenfold as privacy came back into fashion, then fell about 60% from its November 2025 peak. In 2026 it swung sharply again, and NU7 went live in November 2026.

## Alternatives

Zcash's closest rival is [Monero]({lang}/xmr/), where every payment is private by default. [Dash]({lang}/dash/) offers optional coin mixing. As a store of value with a 21 million cap it is compared with [Bitcoin]({lang}/btc/), and for faster, cheaper payments with [Litecoin]({lang}/ltc/).

## Main risks

- **Regulation:** privacy coins face pressure; some exchanges have delisted them in parts of Europe and Asia, and new EU anti-money-laundering rules bar exchanges from handling them from 2027.
- **Big swings:** ZEC rose more than tenfold in 2025 and lost about 60% within months afterwards.
- **Small network:** a limited number of full nodes and a few developer organisations carry most of the work.
- **Concentration of mining:** a few large pools produce most blocks.
- **Upgrade risk:** big changes such as NU7 can bring bugs or wallet incompatibilities.

## What it means if you buy regularly

ZEC has spent long years falling and short periods rising very fast. Buying a fixed amount regularly averages the price you pay and spares you from guessing the top or the bottom, but it does not protect you from long declines or from a delisting that makes the coin harder to sell. Many people keep coins like this to a small share of savings they can afford to lose. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
