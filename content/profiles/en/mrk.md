**Merck & Co.** — called MSD outside the US and Canada — is one of the world's biggest drug companies, based in Rahway, New Jersey. Its star product is **Keytruda**, an immunotherapy that helps the body's immune system attack cancer and is used for dozens of tumour types; it alone brings in close to half of Merck's sales. Merck also sells the HPV vaccine Gardasil, other vaccines, the lung-blood-pressure drug Winrevair and a large animal-health business. The big question for investors is what happens when Keytruda's main patents begin to expire in 2028.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Sales: $65.0 billion in 2025; the company expected $66.8–67.3 billion for 2026 (outlook of 29 October 2026).
> - In the July–September 2026 quarter sales rose 5% to $18.1 billion; Keytruda (including the new under-the-skin version Keytruda Qlex) earned $8.6 billion.
> - Newer drugs are growing fast: Winrevair sales almost doubled to $694 million in that quarter.
> - Keytruda's patent protection begins to expire in 2028, so Merck has been buying companies to fill the gap.

## How it makes money

Merck has two segments. **Pharmaceuticals** — prescription medicines and vaccines — made $16.3 billion of the $18.1 billion of sales in the September 2026 quarter. Inside it, oncology is dominated by Keytruda; vaccines include Gardasil ($2.0 billion in the quarter, +14%) and childhood and adult vaccines; and newer drugs include Winrevair for pulmonary arterial hypertension and Ohtuvayre for the lung disease COPD, which came with the purchase of Verona Pharma in October 2025. **Animal Health** — vaccines and medicines for farm animals and pets, such as the Bravecto flea and tick tablets — brought in $1.7 billion.

Drug companies earn high margins on patented medicines (Merck's non-GAAP gross margin was 81.4% in the quarter) and then lose most of a drug's sales when cheaper copies arrive. That is why the older diabetes pill Januvia fell 39% in the quarter.

For the full year 2026 Merck raised its sales outlook to $66.8–67.3 billion. Its non-GAAP EPS outlook of $2.77–2.82 looks low because it includes one-off research charges of about $9.0 billion and $5.7 billion for the purchases of Cidara Therapeutics and Terns Pharmaceuticals. In 2025 sales were $65.0 billion and non-GAAP EPS was $8.98.

## The stock's story

The US Merck started in 1891 as the American branch of the German Merck family company and became fully independent after the First World War; the German Merck KGaA is a separate company today. In September 2004 Merck withdrew the painkiller Vioxx over heart risks, and the shares fell by about a quarter in a single day and stayed weak for years amid lawsuits. In 2009 it merged with Schering-Plough, which brought in the antibody that became Keytruda; Keytruda was first approved in the US in 2014 and grew into the world's best-selling medicine. In WhatIfStack's data the shares peaked in mid-2024, then fell by more than a third into mid-2025 on worries about the Keytruda patent cliff and weak Gardasil sales in China, and recovered strongly in 2026 as new drugs grew and the outlook was raised. Rob Davis became CEO in 2021 and also chairman in late 2022.

## Competitors

In cancer immunotherapy Merck's main rival is Bristol Myers Squibb (Opdivo), along with Roche, AstraZeneca and a growing number of Chinese drug makers. In vaccines it competes with Pfizer, GSK and Sanofi; in animal health with Zoetis and Elanco. After 2028 it will also face copies of Keytruda itself.

## Main risks

- **The Keytruda cliff:** one drug is close to half of sales; when copies arrive from 2028, those sales will start to fall, and new products must replace them.
- **Pipeline and deals:** costly acquisitions only pay off if the drugs win approval and sell well; trials can fail.
- **Drug prices:** the US government and other payers keep pushing for lower prices, including Medicare price negotiation.
- **Vaccines in China and politics:** Gardasil demand in China dropped, and changes in US vaccine policy can affect sales.
- **Lawsuits and safety:** as Vioxx showed, a safety problem can cost billions and hit the share price hard.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Big drug companies often look steady because people need medicines in any economy, but their shares can swing a lot on one trial result or one patent date — Merck lost more than a third of its value between 2024 and 2025. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your entry price over those swings, and the dividend is part of the return. Many people hold a single drug maker only as part of a wider portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
