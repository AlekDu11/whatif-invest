**Mantle** is a network built on top of Ethereum that makes transactions much cheaper and faster, and **MNT** is the token that pays its fees and runs its governance. It grew out of BitDAO, a crypto investment collective closely tied to the exchange Bybit, and launched in July 2023. > - Mantle started as an optimistic rollup and in September 2025 switched its mainnet to zero-knowledge proofs from Succinct.

## How it works

Mantle is a "layer 2": it collects many transactions, processes them on its own chain and then posts a compact record back to Ethereum, which acts as the final judge. That keeps fees at a fraction of Ethereum's while apps work the same way, because Mantle is compatible with Ethereum's software. It was first built on Optimism's OP Stack and stores transaction data with EigenDA instead of on Ethereum itself, which cuts costs further. In September 2025 it moved to OP Succinct, so each batch of transactions now comes with a mathematical (zero-knowledge) proof that it is correct, instead of a waiting period in which anyone can challenge it.

## What the token is used for

On the network, MNT pays transaction fees and gives holders a vote on upgrades, grants and how the treasury is spent. It is also a common trading pair and collateral in Mantle apps, alongside products such as mETH, a liquid form of staked ether. Outside the chain, Bybit has turned MNT into a kind of exchange token: since 2025 it can cut trading fees, fund the Bybit Card and be used in Bybit savings and launch programmes. That gives the token real uses, but it also ties its fortunes to one company.

## Its story so far

BitDAO launched in 2021 with a treasury of more than $2 billion, much of it pledged by Bybit. In May 2023 its members voted to merge with Mantle and swap BIT for the new MNT token one for one; the mainnet went live on 17 July 2023 with a $200 million ecosystem fund. MNT fell to its lowest level, about $0.31, in October 2023, then rose with the 2024 market. The zero-knowledge upgrade and Bybit's new uses for the token in September 2025 pushed it to a record in October 2025, after which it fell back sharply with the rest of the market in 2026.

## Alternatives

Mantle competes with other Ethereum layer 2 networks for users and apps: [Arbitrum]({lang}/arb/) and [Optimism]({lang}/op/) are the largest optimistic rollups, while [ZKsync]({lang}/zk/) and [Starknet]({lang}/strk/) use zero-knowledge proofs like Mantle now does. Coinbase's Base has no token but draws many users. As an exchange-linked token MNT can also be compared with [BNB]({lang}/bnb/) and [OKB]({lang}/okb/).

## Main risks

- **Dependence on Bybit:** much of the demand and the treasury story is linked to one exchange; trouble there would hit MNT.
- **Treasury and supply:** almost half of all tokens started in the treasury, and governance decisions to spend or move them can add selling pressure.
- **Competition:** there are dozens of layer 2 networks with similar technology, and users can move between them easily.
- **Technology:** new proof systems, bridges and outside data storage add points where something can break.
- **Regulation:** rules for exchange tokens and exchange-run networks differ by country and can change.

## What it means if you buy regularly

MNT has swung from about $0.31 to its record and back down within three years. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your entry price over those swings instead of betting on one moment, but it does not protect you from a long decline. A token like this is a bet on one network and one exchange, so many people keep it to a small part of their crypto holdings. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
