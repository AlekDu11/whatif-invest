**Plasma** is a blockchain designed for one job: moving stablecoins — digital dollars such as USDT — quickly and cheaply. It launched in September 2025 with backing from Bitfinex, Founders Fund and Framework, and offered free USDT transfers from day one. ## How it works

Plasma is a separate "layer 1" blockchain that runs the same smart contracts as Ethereum, so wallets and apps can move over easily. Its own consensus system, PlasmaBFT, confirms transactions in about a second. The key feature is a built-in sponsor for fees: simple USDT transfers cost the user nothing, and other fees can be paid in USDT or bitcoin instead of XPL. Plasma also plans to anchor its records to the Bitcoin blockchain for extra security and to offer a bridge for bitcoin. In September 2025 the team announced Plasma One, an app with a card for spending stablecoins, aimed at countries where dollars are hard to get.

## What the token is used for

XPL pays for transactions that are not sponsored, and validators — the computers that run the network — must stake XPL. Under the published plan, rewards for validators start at about 5% a year and fall gradually, while part of the fees is burned. XPL is also used as collateral and liquidity in Plasma's DeFi apps such as Aave and Ethena. Note that the free transfers mean ordinary stablecoin users do not need to buy XPL at all.

## Its story so far

Plasma raised $24 million in early 2025 in rounds led by Framework and Bitfinex, then sold 10% of its tokens to the public in July 2025 for $50 million, at a $500 million valuation; the sale was heavily oversubscribed. A June 2025 deposit campaign hit its $1 billion limit in about 30 minutes. When the network and XPL launched on 25 September 2025, the token jumped to about $1.68 within days. The excitement faded quickly, more tokens came onto the market, and by November 2026 XPL traded below $0.05 — about 97% under its peak.

## Alternatives

Most USDT still moves on [TRON]({lang}/trx/) and [Ethereum]({lang}/eth/), and [Solana]({lang}/sol/) is a fast, cheap rival. [Stable]({lang}/stable/) is another new chain built for USDT, and Circle, the issuer of USDC, is building its own network, Arc. Payment companies also run stablecoin chains of their own.

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** XPL lost more than 95% in its first year; new tokens like this can stay volatile.
- **Token unlocks:** large team, investor and ecosystem allocations keep entering the market until about 2028.
- **Value capture:** free USDT transfers are good for users but mean the network's success does not automatically create demand for XPL.
- **Competition:** TRON, Ethereum, Solana and other stablecoin chains fight for the same money.
- **Concentration and regulation:** at launch the validators were run by the Plasma team, and stablecoin rules are changing fast in the US, the EU and elsewhere.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Plasma has a very short history that started with a peak and a deep fall. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more when the price is low and less when it is high, without trying to guess the bottom — but it does not remove the risk of losses, and a young token can fall much further. Many people keep crypto to a small part of their savings that they can afford to see fall sharply. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
