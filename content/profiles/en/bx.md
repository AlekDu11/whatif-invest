**Blackstone** is the world's largest manager of "alternative" investments — things that do not trade on a stock exchange every day: office towers, warehouses, apartments, data centres, whole private companies and private loans. It raises money from pension funds, sovereign wealth funds, insurers and, more and more, wealthy individuals, invests it for years and takes fees and a share of the profits. The firm is based in New York and its shares trade on the New York Stock Exchange as BX.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Assets under management: $1.37 trillion on 30 September 2026, up 10% in a year; $990 billion of it earns fees.
> - New money: $63.3 billion in July–September 2026 and $271.6 billion over 12 months.
> - Distributable earnings — the cash profit it pays out — were $1.7 billion, or $1.32 per share, 13% less than a year earlier.
> - The dividend for the quarter was $1.12 per share; Blackstone pays out most of its distributable earnings, so the dividend changes every quarter.

## How it makes money

Blackstone has two main kinds of income. **Management fees** are a yearly percentage of the money in its funds; they are steady and grew into fee-related earnings of $1.6 billion in the quarter ($1.26 per share, +5%). **Performance fees** ("carried interest") are a share of the profit — often around 20% — that it gets only when it sells an investment at a gain; they swing a lot from year to year. At the end of September 2026 Blackstone had $7.3 billion of such profits earned on paper but not yet cashed. Its money is spread across four businesses: credit and insurance ($480 billion), private equity including secondaries and infrastructure ($465 billion), real estate ($309 billion) and multi-asset investing, its hedge-fund solutions arm ($114 billion). About $562 billion is "perpetual capital" that clients cannot withdraw on a fixed date, such as its funds for wealthy individuals (BREIT for property, BXPE for private equity, BXINFRA for infrastructure).

In the third quarter of 2026 GAAP net income attributable to Blackstone was $662 million ($0.83 per share). Distributable earnings fell mainly because fewer big asset sales happened than a year earlier; Blackstone sold $33.1 billion of investments in the quarter and deployed $30.1 billion into new ones. It has $228.9 billion of "dry powder" — money promised by clients but not yet invested. Infrastructure funds gained 28.9% over 12 months and corporate private equity 14.7%, while opportunistic real estate lost 4.0%. Credit was the biggest source of new money ($35.8 billion in the quarter), and private wealth funds kept raising cash: $2.9 billion for BXPE, $1.3 billion for BREIT and $1.1 billion for BXINFRA.

## The stock's story

Stephen Schwarzman and Peter G. Peterson founded Blackstone in New York in 1985 with $400,000, first as a merger-advisory boutique, then as a buyout firm. It became a real-estate giant and one of the best-known private-equity houses — its 2007 takeover of Hilton Hotels, later floated on the stock market, became one of the most profitable deals of its kind. Blackstone listed on the New York Stock Exchange in June 2007, just before the financial crisis, and the shares lost most of their value by early 2009. In 2019 it converted from a partnership into an ordinary corporation, which let many more funds own the stock; it crossed $1 trillion of assets in 2023 and joined the S&P 500 that September. Schwarzman remains chairman and CEO, with Jon Gray as president.

## Competitors

Blackstone's closest rivals are other listed alternative managers: KKR, Apollo, Brookfield, Carlyle and Ares, plus TPG. In private credit it also competes with banks and with traditional fund firms moving into private markets, above all BlackRock. For wealthy clients' money it competes with private-markets funds from almost every big asset manager.

## Main risks

- **Deals and timing:** performance fees and much of distributable earnings depend on selling assets at good prices; when markets for deals or IPOs freeze, payouts fall.
- **Real estate:** higher interest rates and empty offices hurt property values; BREIT limited withdrawals in 2022–23 when too many investors asked for their money at once.
- **Private credit:** fast growth in private loans has not yet been tested by a deep recession.
- **Interest rates:** high rates make borrowing for buyouts more expensive and make bonds a tougher competitor for investors' money.
- **A variable dividend:** the payout follows earnings, so it can fall sharply in a weak year.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Blackstone's shares tend to rise more than the market when deals and asset sales boom and fall harder when they stall, and the dividend moves with them. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more shares when the price is low and fewer when it is high, without trying to time the market. Many people hold one alternative manager only as part of a wider portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
