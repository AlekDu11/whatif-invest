**Conflux (CFX)** is a public blockchain developed by a team in China that works within Chinese rules, which makes it one of the few open networks that local companies and city governments have openly partnered with. It runs smart contracts like Ethereum, but orders blocks in a structure called a Tree-Graph to process more transactions. CFX is its native token, used for fees, staking and governance; all CFX in circulation is worth {{mcap:cfx}} today ({{rank:cfx}} among cryptocurrencies).

> **Key takeaways**
> - Conflux was co-founded by computer scientist Fan Long, with Turing Award winner Andrew Yao as an academic adviser; its main network launched in October 2020.
> - It has two spaces on one chain: its own Core Space and an Ethereum-compatible eSpace, so Ethereum apps and wallets can move over easily.

## How it works

Most blockchains keep only one chain of blocks and throw away competing blocks produced at the same time. Conflux keeps them: blocks point to several earlier blocks, forming a Tree-Graph, and the network then agrees on one order for all of them. This lets it accept blocks faster without losing security. Blocks are created by proof-of-work miners, while CFX holders who stake their coins run a proof-of-stake "finality" layer that confirms the order, a hybrid added in the 2022 Hydra upgrade. The 2022 upgrade also introduced eSpace, which runs the same smart-contract code as Ethereum, and in 2025 the Conflux 3.0 upgrade aimed to raise speed further.

## Why people think it has value

China banned crypto trading for its residents, but it supports blockchain technology, and Conflux positioned itself as a compliant public chain that Chinese firms can use. It has announced projects with partners such as China Telecom (a SIM card with a built-in crypto wallet, announced in 2023) and, in 2025, plans for AxCNH, a stablecoin pegged to the offshore Chinese yuan aimed at cross-border trade. CFX pays for every transaction and is staked to secure the network, so more real use would mean more demand for the token.

## Its story so far

The Conflux Foundation was set up in 2018 and raised money from well-known Chinese and international investors before the main network, called Tethys, launched in October 2020. CFX reached about $1.70 in March 2021 during the crypto boom, then fell more than 90% over the following year. In early 2023 it rose several times over within weeks on hopes for crypto-friendly rules in Hong Kong and the China Telecom news, then gave back much of the gain. In July 2025 the team announced the offshore-yuan stablecoin and the 3.0 upgrade, and CFX again jumped sharply before falling back.

## Alternatives

Among smart-contract platforms, [Ethereum]({lang}/eth/) is the leader, with faster rivals such as [Solana]({lang}/sol/) and [Avalanche]({lang}/avax/). Other Asia-focused networks include [NEO]({lang}/neo/) and [VeChain]({lang}/vet/). For companies in mainland China, permissioned blockchains under the state-backed BSN network are the main competitor, since they do not need a public token at all.

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** CFX has lost more than 90% from its peak and its rallies have often faded quickly.
- **China policy:** a change in Chinese or Hong Kong rules could help or hurt the project overnight.
- **Partnership hype:** announced deals do not always turn into heavy real use or fees.
- **Supply:** there is no fixed cap, and new CFX is issued as mining and staking rewards.
- **Competition:** larger chains have far more apps, developers and money locked in them.

## What it means if you buy regularly

CFX tends to move in short bursts on news, followed by long declines. Buying a fixed amount regularly removes the temptation to chase those spikes and spreads your entry price, but it does not protect you if interest in the project fades. Many people keep a specialised token like CFX to a small part of their savings. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
