**Aptos** is a "layer-1" blockchain — a base network for apps, payments and tokens, like Ethereum or Solana — built to be fast and cheap. It was founded by engineers who had worked on Meta's abandoned Diem stablecoin project, and it went live in October 2022. ## How it works

Aptos is a proof-of-stake network: validators lock up APT and take turns confirming blocks, and transactions are usually final in under a second. Its execution engine, Block-STM, runs many transactions at the same time and only re-does the few that clash, instead of processing them one by one. Smart contracts are written in Move, a language built to make digital assets harder to copy or lose by mistake. Accounts can also change their keys without moving funds, which helps users recover access.

## What the token is used for

APT pays the network's transaction ("gas") fees and is staked by validators and their delegators to secure the chain; stakers earn rewards in new APT. Holders also vote on governance proposals. Under the 2026 model, 100% of base gas fees are burned, so heavy use can take tokens out of circulation, and the same plan lowers staking rewards from about 5.2% to about 2.6% a year. Demand for APT therefore depends on how much the network is actually used.

## Its story so far

Mo Shaikh and Avery Ching founded Aptos Labs in 2021 after Meta wound down Diem. In 2022 the company raised about $350 million from investors including a16z and Jump Crypto. The mainnet launched in October 2022 with 1 billion APT at the start, a large share reserved for the team, investors, the community and the Aptos Foundation. APT climbed to about $20 in January 2023 and then fell for most of the following years as tokens were unlocked every month. In November 2024 BlackRock's tokenised money-market fund BUIDL expanded to Aptos. In February 2026 the Aptos Foundation announced the 2.1 billion APT cap and said it would permanently lock and stake 210 million APT, and in October 2026 the investor and contributor unlocks ended.

## Alternatives

[Sui]({lang}/sui/) is its closest twin — also founded by ex-Diem engineers and also built on a version of Move. The big general-purpose rivals are [Solana]({lang}/sol/) and [Ethereum]({lang}/eth/), which have far more apps, users and money locked in them. Other fast layer-1 networks such as [Sei]({lang}/sei/) compete for the same developers.

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** APT has lost most of its value since early 2023; deep falls can continue or repeat.
- **Token unlocks:** monthly releases from the community and Foundation pools continue until 2032, adding new supply.
- **Concentration:** the Foundation, Aptos Labs and early investors hold a large share, so their decisions matter for the price.
- **Competition:** Solana, Ethereum and Sui fight for the same users, and many fast networks have struggled to keep them.
- **Regulation:** rules for tokens, staking and stablecoins differ by country and can change quickly.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Aptos is a bet that a newer, faster network can win lasting users against bigger rivals, and its price has been very volatile. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your purchases over highs and lows without guessing the bottom — but it does not remove the risk of losses. Many people keep smaller tokens like APT to a small part of their crypto. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
