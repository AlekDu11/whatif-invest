**Falcon Finance** is a DeFi protocol that lets people deposit crypto, stablecoins or tokenized real-world assets and get **USDf**, a synthetic dollar, in return. It calls itself "universal collateral infrastructure": your coins keep working as collateral while you get dollars to spend or earn yield on. **FF** is its governance and rewards token, worth {{mcap:ff}} in total ({{rank:ff}} among cryptocurrencies).

> **Key takeaways**
> - FF has a fixed supply of 10 billion tokens; about 2.34 billion were in circulation at launch in September 2025.
> - Its record high is {{ath:ff}} ({{athd:ff}}), reached on its first trading day; today the price is {{fromath:ff}} from it.
> - FF itself is not a stablecoin — USDf is the dollar product, FF is a volatile token tied to the protocol's success.
> - Most of the supply belongs to the ecosystem fund, the foundation and the team, and unlocks over several years.

## How it works

You deposit collateral and mint USDf. Stablecoins such as USDT or USDC mint USDf one to one; volatile coins like BTC or ETH must be over-collateralized, so you deposit more value than the dollars you receive. If you stake USDf you get **sUSDf**, a token whose value slowly rises as the protocol earns from strategies such as funding-rate arbitrage, staking and providing liquidity. Locking for three or six months in "boosted" vaults pays more. The protocol keeps an on-chain insurance fund for bad periods and plans to accept more tokenized Treasury bills, bonds and other real-world assets as collateral.

## What the FF token is used for

FF holders vote on protocol decisions, such as which assets are accepted and how the treasury is used. People who stake FF get better terms: higher USDf yields, lower collateral requirements ("haircuts") and cheaper swaps, plus ecosystem rewards and early access to new products. In short, FF is a bet that USDf grows and the protocol earns more — not a claim on those dollars.

## Its story so far

Falcon Finance launched USDf in 2025, with Andrei Grachev of the market maker DWF Labs as a founding partner, and grew quickly as traders looked for yield on their dollars. In July 2025 USDf briefly slipped below one dollar amid questions about its reserves, and the team responded with more reserve reporting. In September 2025 it published the FF tokenomics; Binance chose FF for its HODLer Airdrops, giving 150 million FF to BNB holders, and trading began on 29 September 2025. The price hit its high on that first day and then lost most of its value within weeks as airdropped tokens were sold.

## Alternatives

The closest rival is [Ethena]({lang}/ena/), whose USDe synthetic dollar also earns from funding-rate strategies. [Sky]({lang}/sky/) (formerly Maker) issues the USDS and DAI stablecoins against crypto collateral, [Aave]({lang}/aave/) lets you borrow dollars against your coins, and [Ondo]({lang}/ondo/) focuses on tokenized Treasury bills. For simply holding dollars, many people use plain fiat-backed stablecoins such as USDT or USDC.

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** FF fell sharply after launch; young DeFi tokens can lose most of their value quickly.
- **Token unlocks:** ecosystem, foundation and team tokens keep unlocking, adding supply that can push the price down.
- **USDf peg and strategy risk:** if trading strategies lose money or collateral crashes, USDf can lose its dollar peg, as it briefly did in July 2025.
- **Concentration and trust:** close ties to one market maker and a small group controlling much of the supply mean their decisions matter a lot.
- **Regulation and smart contracts:** synthetic dollars face new rules in many countries, and any bug or hack can freeze or lose funds.

## What it means if you buy regularly

FF has only a short history, and most of it is a fall from its launch-day price. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your entry over time, so you buy more when the price is low without guessing the bottom — but it does not remove the risk of losses, which is high for a new token with large unlocks ahead. Many people keep such tokens to a small slice of their savings. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
