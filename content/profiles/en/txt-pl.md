**Text S.A.**, known until 2023 as LiveChat Software, makes the chat window you see in the corner of many online shops and websites. Founded in 2002 in Wrocław, Poland, it sells subscriptions to more than 35,000 businesses worldwide, earns mostly in US dollars and keeps over a third of its revenue as net profit — yet its sales have stopped growing, and the stock trades on the Warsaw Stock Exchange (GPW) under the ticker TXT.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue for the year to 31 March 2026: PLN 329.1 million, down 7.1%; net profit PLN 116.6 million, down 29.1%.
> - Latest quarter (April–June 2026): revenue PLN 83.2 million (−1.9%), net profit PLN 29.1 million, EBITDA margin 46.4%.
> - Monthly recurring revenue was $7.29 million at the end of September 2026, +3.3% a year; the goal is $100 million a year.
> - Dividend for 2025/26: PLN 4.26 per share; the company aims to pay out as much profit as possible.

## How it makes money

Customers pay a monthly or yearly subscription for each product. **LiveChat** is the original chat tool for websites and online stores. **ChatBot** builds automated bots that answer common questions, **HelpDesk** handles support tickets, **KnowledgeBase** lets a company publish help articles, and **OpenWidget** is a free widget that brings in new users. Since May 2026 the company has also pushed **Text**, an AI customer-service suite with an AI selling agent, a WhatsApp integration and an "Enterprise AI Agent" listed on Google Cloud Marketplace; Text also became an app in the ChatGPT marketplace in August 2026. Named clients include Unilever, Atos, Wembley Stadium, MIT and Stanford. Most revenue is in dollars, while results are reported in Polish zloty.

## The numbers right now

In the first quarter of fiscal 2026/27 (April–June 2026) revenue was PLN 83.2 million, 1.9% lower than a year earlier, but in dollars it rose 2.1% to $22.61 million — the gap is the stronger zloty. EBITDA fell 7.4% to PLN 38.6 million and net profit 6.2% to PLN 29.1 million, still a 35% net margin. Operating cash flow grew 22.2% to PLN 41.5 million and deferred revenue hit a record PLN 77.2 million. Preliminary data for July–September show monthly recurring revenue (MRR) of $7.29 million, up 3.3% a year but down 2.3% from June; API usage fees roughly doubled to about $450,000. Full second-quarter results are still to come.

## The stock's story

In 2021, during the boom in online shopping, investors valued LiveChat like a fast-growing tech company, at about 33.5 times earnings. Then customer growth stalled, the dollar weakened and fear grew that AI chatbots would replace classic chat. By June 2024 the stock was at its lowest since March 2022 and valued at about 11 times earnings, even though earnings per share had almost tripled in four years. In August 2026 the shares fell almost 8% in one morning after first-quarter results came in below analysts' forecasts.

## Competitors

Text competes with Intercom, Zendesk, Tidio, Freshworks and HubSpot's service tools, and increasingly with AI agents built on top of large language models. Its strengths are low costs, very high margins and a large base of small and mid-sized online businesses; its weakness is that bigger rivals spend much more on AI and sales. In June 2026 Text closed its classic sales team and moved fully to product-led growth, where customers sign up on their own.

## Main risks

- **AI disruption:** generative AI makes chat bots cheap to build, so customers may switch to newer platforms or need fewer paid seats.
- **Growth has stalled:** yearly revenue and profit fell in 2025/26, and MRR dipped again in the September quarter.
- **Currency:** revenue is in dollars but results and dividends are in zloty; a weaker dollar cuts reported profit.
- **Dividend depends on profit:** the payout follows earnings, so falling profit means smaller dividends.
- **Small company:** a single product shift or one weak quarter can move the share price sharply.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Text pays out most of its profit, so for a regular buyer a large part of the return has come from dividends rather than price growth. Buying in small steps spreads the risk of entering at a bad moment, but it cannot fix a business whose sales shrink. Investors are waiting to see whether the new AI products bring MRR back to steady growth. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
