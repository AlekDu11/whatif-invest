**ASML** builds the machines that print the world's most advanced computer chips — and it is the only company on Earth that can make the extreme ultraviolet (EUV) systems needed for the newest ones. Based in Veldhoven in the Netherlands, it was founded in 1984 as a joint venture of Philips and ASM International.

## How it makes money

Chipmakers such as TSMC, Samsung and Intel buy ASML's lithography systems, which use light to draw billions of tiny circuits onto silicon wafers. ASML earns from two streams: selling new and used machines, and servicing and upgrading the large fleet already installed in factories (about €3.3 billion in the third quarter of 2026 alone). In 2025 net sales grew 16% to €32.7 billion, and EUV made up 48% of system sales.

## Why it matters

Without ASML's EUV machines, there are no cutting-edge AI processors or iPhone chips. In the third quarter of 2026 the company reported a record €11.7 billion of sales and €5.0 billion of net profit, with a 57.5% gross margin, and it expects €44–45 billion of sales for the whole year. One surprising fact: demand is so strong that ASML says its planned 2027–2028 capacity will not fully cover customer orders, and some customers already want to book machines for 2029 and 2030.

## Main risks

ASML depends on a handful of huge customers, so when chipmakers cut spending, orders drop fast. Dutch and US export controls already block EUV sales to China, and stricter rules could cut a market that was still expected to bring about 20% of system sales in 2026. The machines are extremely complex, so supplier problems or delays hurt deliveries. The share price usually reflects high expectations and can fall sharply when growth slows. Buying a fixed amount regularly smooths price swings but does not remove the risk.
