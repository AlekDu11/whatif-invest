**ASML** builds the machines that "print" circuits onto silicon wafers — the most important step in making computer chips. It is the only company in the world that makes extreme ultraviolet (EUV) lithography systems, which every maker of the most advanced chips needs, from TSMC and Samsung to Intel. ASML is based in Veldhoven in the Netherlands; its shares trade in Amsterdam and on Nasdaq under the ticker ASML.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Net sales: a record €11.7 billion in the third quarter of 2026 (to 27 September); net income €5.0 billion.
> - ASML expected €44–45 billion of sales for the full year 2026 with a gross margin of about 56%.
> - In October 2026 it said planned production for 2027 and 2028 would not fully meet customer demand, mainly because of AI chips.
> - It bought back about €4.1 billion of its own shares in that one quarter and paid an interim dividend of €1.88 per share.

## How it makes money

ASML sells two things. The first is the machines themselves: EUV systems for the smallest, most advanced chip layers, and older but still essential DUV (deep ultraviolet) systems for the rest. A single EUV machine is the size of a bus and costs well over €100 million; the newest High-NA versions cost even more. In the third quarter of 2026 ASML sold 113 new systems and 8 used ones.

The second is "installed base management": servicing, upgrading and adding options to the thousands of machines already working in chip factories. That brought in €3.3 billion in the third quarter of 2026 and keeps coming even when chipmakers slow their spending on new equipment. ASML's customers are a small group of chipmakers — mostly in Taiwan, South Korea, the US and China — so a few big orders can move its results a lot.

In the third quarter of 2026 ASML had net sales of €11.66 billion, a record and near the top of its own forecast. Gross margin rose to 57.5% from 54.0% in the quarter before, and net income was €4.96 billion, or €12.95 per share. At the end of September it held €12.7 billion in cash and short-term investments. For the fourth quarter of 2026 it guided to €14.2–15.2 billion of sales with a gross margin of about 57%. ASML employs more than 45,500 people.

## The stock's story

ASML was founded in 1984 as a joint venture of Philips and ASM International, starting out in a leaky shed next to a Philips building in Eindhoven. It listed on the stock market in 1995. Over the next two decades it bet on EUV, a technology many in the industry doubted: in 2012 Intel, TSMC and Samsung co-invested to speed up its development, and in 2013 ASML bought Cymer, the US maker of the light sources. EUV machines went into high-volume chip production in the late 2010s, and from then on ASML had no direct rival for the most advanced chips.

The stock also had sharp falls. From 2023 Dutch and US export rules stopped ASML from selling its most advanced machines to China. Christophe Fouquet replaced Peter Wennink as CEO in April 2024. In October 2024 ASML published weak orders early by mistake and the shares fell about 16% in a day. In 2026 the AI boom pushed chipmakers to raise their equipment plans, and in October 2026 ASML said some customers already wanted to place orders for 2029 and 2030.

## Competitors

No one else makes EUV machines. In older DUV lithography ASML competes with Japan's Nikon and Canon, and Chinese companies such as SMEE are trying to build their own tools with state support. Other chip-equipment makers — Applied Materials, Lam Research, Tokyo Electron and KLA — sell different steps of chipmaking, so they compete for the same customer budgets rather than for the same machines.

## Main risks

- **Few customers:** a handful of chipmakers buy most of ASML's machines, so if one of them cuts spending, sales drop fast.
- **The chip cycle:** chip demand swings between booms and slumps; after the AI boom, equipment orders could fall for a while.
- **China and export rules:** Dutch and US restrictions already limit what ASML can sell in China, and they could get tighter.
- **Production limits:** the machines are hugely complex and rely on suppliers such as Zeiss for optics; delays mean lost sales.
- **A high price:** investors pay a lot for ASML's monopoly, so disappointing orders can hit the shares hard, as in October 2024.

## What it means if you buy regularly

ASML is a profitable company with a unique product, but its share price follows the ups and downs of the chip industry, and drops of a third or more have happened. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more shares when the price is low and fewer when it is high, without trying to guess the next turn of the cycle. Many people hold one company only as part of a wider portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
