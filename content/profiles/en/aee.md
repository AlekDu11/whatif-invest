**Ameren** is the company that keeps the lights on and the gas flowing for millions of homes and businesses in Missouri and Illinois. It owns power plants, high-voltage lines and the local wires and pipes, and state regulators decide how much it may charge. Founded in 1881 and based in St. Louis, it trades on the New York Stock Exchange.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Serves 2.5 million electric and more than 900,000 natural gas customers across 64,000 square miles.
> - Q3 2026 earnings: $2.42 per share, up from $2.17 on an adjusted basis a year earlier; 2026 guidance raised to $5.35–5.55.
> - Plans $38.1 billion of infrastructure investment and targets 8–10% yearly earnings growth from 2027 to 2031.
> - Pays a dividend of about $3.00 a share a year and aims to pay out 45–55% of its earnings.

## How it makes money

Ameren is a regulated utility. It invests in power plants, lines and pipes, regulators add that investment to its "rate base" (the asset value it may earn a return on), and customer bills pay it back with an allowed profit. The biggest part is Ameren Missouri, which generates and sells electricity from coal, nuclear, gas, hydro, wind and solar plants; it earned $582 million in Q3 2026. Ameren Illinois delivers electricity and gas but does not own the power plants, and Ameren Transmission builds long-distance power lines. Growth comes from building more: the company expects its rate base to grow about 13.3% a year from 2026 to 2031.

## The numbers right now

In the quarter to 30 September 2026 Ameren earned $676 million, or $2.42 per share, against $2.35 a year earlier ($2.17 excluding a one-off tax benefit). Over nine months it earned $4.83 per share and spent $4.09 billion on new infrastructure, up from $3.12 billion. Over the last twelve months revenue was about $8.58 billion and net profit about $1.6 billion, and the stock trades at roughly 20 times earnings. The price of growth is debt: long-term debt rose to $20.4 billion from $18.2 billion at the end of 2025. For 2027 the company guided to $5.70–5.90 per share.

## The stock's story

Ameren is a classic slow-and-steady utility stock. It rose in almost every year of the last decade, but it is sensitive to interest rates: in 2023, when rates jumped, it lost about 16%. It recovered with gains of about 27% in 2024 and 15% in 2025 and set new records in 2026, reaching $118.32 at the top of its 52-week range. Data centres asking for large amounts of power in Missouri are part of the new growth story — large-load customers signed electric service agreements with Ameren Missouri this year.

## Competitors

Within its service area Ameren is a monopoly, so its real "competition" is for investors' money and for regulators' approval. Similar listed US utilities include Evergy and Xcel Energy in the Midwest, WEC Energy, and Exelon, whose ComEd serves northern Illinois. Ameren's advantages are steady demand, a large planned investment pipeline and its position in a region attracting new data centres.

## Main risks

- **Regulators:** a rate review for Ameren Missouri filed in June 2026 and several Illinois cases are still pending; a tougher decision would cut allowed returns.
- **Interest rates:** high rates make borrowing for the $38.1 billion plan more expensive and make bonds look more attractive than utility dividends.
- **Debt and new shares:** heavy spending is funded by debt and share issuance — diluted shares rose to 278.9 million from 272.2 million in a year.
- **Data centre demand** may arrive later or smaller than planned, leaving the company with costly projects.
- **Weather:** a mild summer or winter lowers sales; guidance assumes normal temperatures.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Utilities like Ameren usually move less than the overall market, and part of the return comes from the dividend, which the company expects to keep raising. Regular buying with reinvested dividends can compound steadily, but the stock can still fall 15–30% in a bad year, usually when interest rates rise. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
