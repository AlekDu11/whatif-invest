**Pendle** is a DeFi app that lets people trade future yield. It takes a token that earns interest — for example staked ether or a yield-bearing stablecoin — and splits it into two parts: one that locks in a fixed return and one that bets on the yield. ## How it works

You deposit a yield-bearing token and Pendle splits it until a fixed date, the maturity. The PT works like a zero-coupon bond: it trades below the value of the underlying asset and can be redeemed for the full amount at maturity, so buying it locks in a fixed yield. The YT receives all the yield the asset earns until maturity: if the real yield turns out higher than what you paid, you gain; if lower, you lose. Both trade on Pendle's own exchange. Pendle runs on [Ethereum]({lang}/eth/) and many other networks, including Arbitrum, BNB Chain and Base. In August 2025 it launched Boros on Arbitrum, a separate app for trading the funding rates of perpetual futures, starting with Binance's bitcoin and ether contracts.

## Why people think it has value

PENDLE is a claim on the business, not money. Pendle takes a cut of the yield earned through YT and of trading fees; in 2025 the protocol earned more than $37 million in revenue, according to The Block. Since January 2026 holders can stake PENDLE as sPENDLE: up to 80% of revenue is used to buy back PENDLE, and the rewards go to stakers who take part in governance votes. Withdrawing takes 14 days, or instantly for a 5% fee. The more money flows through Pendle, the more revenue there is to share — and the reverse.

## Its story so far

Pendle was started by a team led by TN Lee, formerly at Kyber Network, with backing from Binance Labs and others; the first version went live in June 2021. Version 2 arrived in late 2022 with a new exchange and vePENDLE, a model where holders locked tokens for up to two years to vote and earn fees. In July 2023 Binance listed PENDLE. In 2024 demand jumped as people used Pendle to fix or bet on the yields of restaking tokens and Ethena's USDe, and the token set its record that spring. In September 2025 Pendle said over $13 billion was deposited. Only about 20% of the supply had been locked as vePENDLE, so in January 2026 Pendle replaced it with sPENDLE. By October 2026 deposits had fallen to about $1.1 billion.

## Alternatives

Pendle is the largest app of its kind; smaller fixed-yield protocols such as Spectra compete with it. Many people who simply want interest use lending markets such as [Aave]({lang}/aave/) or [Morpho]({lang}/morpho/), or staking via [Lido]({lang}/ldo/). Much of Pendle's activity depends on yield sources like [Ethena]({lang}/ena/) — they are partners and a risk at the same time.

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** PENDLE has fallen sharply more than once — DeFi tokens drop hard when yields and deposits shrink.
- **Smart contracts:** a bug in Pendle or in the asset behind a PT or YT can cause losses.
- **YT can go to zero:** a YT is worth nothing at maturity, so if the yield disappoints, the money is gone.
- **Dependence on others:** if a large yield source (a stablecoin, a restaking token) fails, Pendle's markets suffer too.
- **New supply:** there is no fixed limit like bitcoin's; new PENDLE is still issued as rewards.

## What it means if you buy regularly

PENDLE is a small, volatile DeFi token whose price follows how much money uses the protocol. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your entry price over time without trying to guess the bottom — but it does not remove the risk of large losses. Many people keep tokens like this to a small part of their crypto. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
