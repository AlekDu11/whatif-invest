**Capital One** is one of the biggest credit card lenders in the United States, and since buying Discover in 2025 it also owns its own card payment network — among big US card lenders only American Express also has one. Based in McLean, Virginia, it also makes car loans, runs an online-first bank with branches and cafés, and lends to businesses. Its shares trade on the New York Stock Exchange under the ticker COF.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Third quarter of 2026: total net revenue $16.3 billion and net income $3.5 billion, or $5.61 per share ($6.69 excluding deal-related costs).
> - Credit card loans were $276.4 billion of its $462.5 billion loan book at the end of September 2026 — about 60%.
> - It closed the Discover deal in May 2025 and the $5.15 billion purchase of business-payments fintech Brex on 7 April 2026.
> - Founder Richard Fairbank has run the company since it began in 1994.

## How it makes money

Capital One is mainly a lender. Most of its revenue is interest: it borrows cheaply through customer deposits ($480.7 billion at the end of September 2026) and lends at higher rates, above all on credit cards, where interest rates are high. That is why its net interest margin — the gap between what it earns on loans and pays on deposits — was 8.41% in the third quarter of 2026, far above a typical big bank. It also earns fees each time a card is used (interchange) and now network fees from the Discover network. The loan book has three parts: **Credit Card** ($276.4 billion of loans, US and a smaller UK and Canada business), **Consumer Banking** ($94.0 billion, almost all car loans) and **Commercial Banking** ($92.1 billion of loans to companies). The price of high interest rates is losses: when customers stop paying, the bank writes the loans off, and it sets aside money for that in advance.

## The stock's story

Capital One began in 1994, when Richard Fairbank and Nigel Morris spun the credit card unit out of Signet Bank in Virginia. Their idea was to use data and testing to decide who gets which card at what rate, and it turned a small card business into a national lender. Capital One later bought regional banks and, in 2012, the online bank ING Direct USA and HSBC's US card portfolio. The shares fell hard in the 2008–2009 financial crisis, when card losses jumped, and again in 2020 at the start of the pandemic. In 2019 a hacker stole data on about 100 million people from its cloud systems, which led to regulatory fines. In February 2024 it agreed to buy Discover Financial Services in an all-stock deal valued at about $35 billion; the deal closed in May 2025, adding Discover's cards and its payment network. Capital One has moved its debit cards onto that network and plans to issue new credit cards on it and switch existing ones step by step. In April 2026 it completed the purchase of Brex, which sells corporate cards and expense software.

## Competitors

In credit cards it competes with JPMorgan Chase, American Express, Citigroup, Bank of America and Synchrony Financial; in car loans with Ally Financial, Wells Fargo and carmakers' own finance arms. With Discover it now also takes on the card networks Visa and Mastercard, which are far larger. In digital banking it competes with other online banks and fintech apps.

## Main risks

- **Credit losses:** card and car loans go bad first when unemployment rises; a recession could push write-offs sharply higher.
- **Integration:** combining Discover and Brex is costly and complex — the Discover integration alone cost $338 million in the third quarter of 2026 — and the network plan takes years.
- **Regulation:** card late fees, interest rates and interchange are political topics in the US; new limits could cut profit.
- **Interest rates:** lower rates can shrink what it earns on loans, while higher rates raise its deposit costs and strain borrowers.
- **Competition from Visa and Mastercard:** moving customers to the smaller Discover network only pays off if enough shops accept the cards.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Capital One is a profitable lender, but bank stocks that focus on credit cards swing with the economy: in past downturns this one lost more than half its value before recovering. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more shares when fears about the economy push the price down and fewer when it is high, without trying to time the cycle. Many people hold one bank only as part of a wider portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
