**Ethereum** is a blockchain you can program. Bitcoin mainly moves coins; Ethereum also runs small programs called smart contracts, which power stablecoins, crypto exchanges without a company in the middle, lending apps and NFTs. Its coin is ether (ETH). ETH is worth {{mcap:eth}} today ({{rank:eth}} among cryptocurrencies).

> **Key takeaways**
> - Ethereum went live on 30 July 2015; since September 2022 it runs on proof of stake instead of mining.
> - There is no fixed maximum supply: about 122 million ETH exist, and part of every fee is burned.
> - Its record high is {{ath:eth}} ({{athd:eth}}); today the price is {{fromath:eth}} from it.
> - US spot ether ETFs have traded since July 2024, so ETH can be bought through an ordinary brokerage account.

## How it works

Thousands of computers keep a shared copy of Ethereum's ledger and run the same programs, so everyone agrees on the result. Since "the Merge" in September 2022, the network is secured by validators who lock up (stake) at least 32 ETH and earn rewards for confirming blocks; dishonest validators lose part of their stake. Every action — a transfer, a trade, a loan — costs a fee in ETH called "gas". Since 2021 the base part of each fee is burned, which removes those coins for good. Cheaper "layer 2" networks such as [Arbitrum]({lang}/arb/), Optimism and Base bundle many transactions and settle them on Ethereum.

## Why people think it has value

ETH is the fuel of the largest smart-contract ecosystem: you need it to pay for anything on the network. Holders can stake it and earn a yield for securing the chain, a bit like interest. Most dollar stablecoins and much of decentralised finance (DeFi) live on Ethereum or its layer 2s, so more activity means more demand for block space. Investors also see it as a bet on the "internet of money" in general — through ETFs and some public companies that hold ETH on their balance sheets.

## Its story so far

Vitalik Buterin described Ethereum in 2013, then aged 19; a 2014 crowd sale sold ETH for about $0.31. In 2016 a hack of "The DAO" fund led to a split: Ethereum rolled the theft back and the original chain lives on as [Ethereum Classic]({lang}/etc/). ETH passed $1,400 in January 2018, fell more than 90% by the end of that year, and reached about $4,870 in November 2021 during the DeFi and NFT boom. The Merge in 2022 cut its energy use by about 99.9%. Upgrades in 2024 (Dencun), May 2025 (Pectra) and December 2025 (Fusaka) made layer 2s cheaper. ETH set its record in August 2025 and then fell sharply in 2026; the next big upgrade, Glamsterdam, was still in testing in November 2026.

## Alternatives

Ethereum's main rivals are other smart-contract platforms that promise lower fees and higher speed: [Solana]({lang}/sol/), [BNB]({lang}/bnb/) Chain, [Cardano]({lang}/ada/), [Avalanche]({lang}/avax/) and [Sui]({lang}/sui/). For investors who want "digital gold" rather than a platform, [Bitcoin]({lang}/btc/) is the usual comparison.

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** ETH fell more than 90% in 2018 and about 75% in 2022; deep drops can happen again.
- **Competition:** cheaper chains and Ethereum's own layer 2s can take activity — and fees — away from the main network.
- **Supply:** with no hard cap, ETH can become inflationary again when activity and burning are low.
- **Technology:** smart-contract bugs, hacks and complex upgrades can cost users money; Glamsterdam was delayed in November 2026 after auditors found problems.
- **Regulation:** rules for staking, DeFi and stablecoins differ by country and can change quickly.

## What it means if you buy regularly

ETH has swung harder than bitcoin in both directions. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your purchases over highs and lows, so you do not have to guess the right moment — but it does not remove the risk of losses, and a long fall can keep the average in the red for years. Many people keep crypto to a small part of savings they can afford to see drop sharply. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
