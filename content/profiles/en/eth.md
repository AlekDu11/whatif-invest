**Ethereum** is a blockchain you can program: instead of only moving coins, it runs small programs called smart contracts. Most stablecoins, decentralised exchanges, lending apps and NFTs live on it or on networks built around it.

## How it works

Ethereum launched in July 2015; Vitalik Buterin proposed it in 2013. Since "the Merge" in September 2022 the network is secured by proof of stake: validators lock up ETH and earn rewards for confirming transactions, instead of miners burning electricity. Every action on the network costs a fee paid in ETH ("gas"), and part of every fee is burned, permanently removing those coins.

## Why it matters

ETH is the fuel of the largest smart-contract ecosystem. Demand comes from three places: fees for using apps, staking (holders earn a yield for securing the network) and investors, including through US spot ether ETFs. Many cheaper "layer 2" networks, such as Arbitrum and Base, settle their transactions on Ethereum.

## Main risks

ETH swings harder than bitcoin and has fallen more than 80% from its peak before. Rival blockchains such as Solana compete for the same apps, and much activity has moved to layer 2 networks, which can mean less fee income for Ethereum itself. Smart contracts can have bugs that get exploited, and staking ETH through third parties adds their risk too.
