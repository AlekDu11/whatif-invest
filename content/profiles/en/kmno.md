**Kamino** is a DeFi app on the Solana blockchain where people lend and borrow crypto, take leveraged positions and put their coins into automated vaults that earn fees. It was launched in 2022 by the team behind Hubble Protocol and grew into the largest lending protocol on Solana. ## How it works

Users deposit crypto such as SOL or USDC into Kamino Lend and earn interest paid by borrowers. Borrowers must lock more collateral than they take out; if the value of the collateral falls too far, the position is automatically sold off ("liquidated") to repay the loan. Kamino's liquidity vaults take coins, place them in trading pools on Solana exchanges and rebalance and reinvest the fees automatically. "Multiply" products loop borrowing and depositing to give leveraged exposure in one click.

## What the token is used for

KMNO does not represent a share of a company. Holders use it to vote on proposals that set the protocol's rules, and those who stake it earn boosted rewards in Kamino's points programs. The interest and fees users pay go to lenders and the protocol, not directly to every KMNO holder. The bet on KMNO is that Kamino stays at the center of borrowing and lending on Solana and that governance over it becomes more valuable.

## Its story so far

Hubble Protocol, a Solana stablecoin project, launched Kamino in 2022 as a tool that automatically manages liquidity positions for its users. In 2023 the team added Kamino Lend and leverage products, and deposits grew quickly as Solana recovered from the 2022 crash. In April 2024 the Kamino Foundation launched KMNO and handed 7.5% of the supply to early users through a points-based airdrop. KMNO set its record in December 2024, during the Solana memecoin boom, and then fell sharply as more tokens unlocked and the market cooled.

## Alternatives

On Solana, Kamino competes with [Jupiter]({lang}/jup/), which offers lending and leveraged trading, and [Drift]({lang}/drift/), a trading and borrowing platform. On Ethereum and other networks, [Aave]({lang}/aave/) is the largest lending protocol, and [Morpho]({lang}/morpho/) is a fast-growing rival. Outside crypto, banks and brokers play the same role for ordinary loans and margin trading.

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** KMNO has lost most of its value since its 2024 peak; deep drops can happen again.
- **Smart-contract risk:** a bug or hack in Kamino or in the Solana apps it relies on could cause losses for users.
- **Liquidations:** borrowers and "Multiply" users can lose their collateral quickly in a sharp market fall.
- **Token unlocks:** a large part of the 10 billion supply is still to be released, which can weigh on the price.
- **Regulation:** rules for DeFi lending and governance tokens differ by country and can change quickly.

## What it means if you buy regularly

KMNO has only traded since 2024, so its history is short and mostly shows a rise and a long fall. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your entry price over time, but it does not protect you if the protocol loses users or the token keeps losing value. Many people keep DeFi tokens to a tiny share of their savings that they can afford to see fall sharply. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
