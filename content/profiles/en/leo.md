**UNUS SED LEO** is the exchange token of Bitfinex, one of the oldest crypto exchanges, and one of the strangest top-20 coins: it is worth billions but trades only a few hundred thousand dollars a day. The name is Latin for "one, but a lion".

## How it works

iFinex, the company behind Bitfinex, sold 1 billion LEO in May 2019 at $1 each, raising $1 billion after it lost access to about $850 million held by a payment processor. In return it promised to buy LEO back every month with at least 27% of its revenue and burn (destroy) the tokens until none are left. LEO used to pay Bitfinex fees is burned too, and holders get lower trading and withdrawal fees on the exchange. The token runs on Ethereum.

## Why it matters

LEO is a bet on one company's business. Bitfinex also pledged to burn LEO with 80% of the net money it recovers from its 2016 hack, when about 120,000 BTC were stolen. US authorities seized most of those coins in 2022, and in 2026 the first tranches started moving back, which could fund large burns. One surprising fact: after seven years of buybacks, about 920 million LEO still exist — roughly 8% of the supply has been burned.

## Main risks

Almost all trading happens on Bitfinex, so selling a large amount can be hard and the price can stay flat for months. The value depends on iFinex's profits, its honesty about burns and its legal position; Bitfinex and Tether share owners and have both settled cases with US regulators. LEO is not authorised for sale in some regions, including under the EU's MiCA rules. Regular buying smooths swings but does not remove risk.
