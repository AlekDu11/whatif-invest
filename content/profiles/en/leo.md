**UNUS SED LEO** is the exchange token of Bitfinex, one of the oldest crypto exchanges. It was sold in May 2019 to raise $1 billion, and Bitfinex's owner promised to buy it back and destroy it with part of its income. LEO's market value was about $8.4 billion in November 2026, a top-20 coin that changes hands far less than its size suggests. The name is Latin for "one, but a lion".

> **Key takeaways**
> - 1 billion LEO were sold at $1 each in May 2019; about 920 million still existed in October 2026, so roughly 8% has been burned.
> - iFinex, the company behind Bitfinex, pledges at least 27% of its monthly gross revenue to buy back and burn LEO.
> - Bitfinex also pledged 80% of the net money it recovers from its 2016 hack, when about 119,754 BTC were stolen; US authorities seized about 94,636 BTC in 2022.
> - Daily trading is tiny for its size — often a few hundred thousand dollars against a market value of over $8 billion.

## How it works

LEO is not a blockchain of its own: it is a token that runs on Ethereum (and earlier also on EOS). iFinex issued it in 2019 after it lost access to about $850 million held by a payment processor, Crypto Capital. Every month iFinex buys LEO on the market — or directly, for example by swapping bitcoin for LEO — and burns (destroys) it, until no tokens are left. LEO used to pay fees on Bitfinex is burned too. A separate pledge covers money recovered in the Crypto Capital case: 95% of the net amount goes to burns.

## What the token is used for

Holders get lower trading, lending and withdrawal fees on Bitfinex and other iFinex platforms. But for most buyers LEO is a bet on one company's cash flow: the more Bitfinex earns, the more LEO is bought and destroyed. The 2016 hack adds a second story. If the seized bitcoins return to Bitfinex, 80% of the net proceeds — after redeeming the recovery tokens given to users in 2016 and paying legal costs — must buy and burn LEO within 18 months.

## Its story so far

LEO started at $1 in May 2019. In February 2022, when US authorities arrested two people over the 2016 hack and seized the coins, LEO jumped by more than half in a day. Since then the price has climbed slowly with few deep falls, unusual for crypto, and set a record of $10.61 in May 2026. In April 2026 a first 8.2 BTC moved from a US government wallet, and on 6 October 2026 about 265 BTC followed — a small part of the total, with no public timetable for the rest.

## Alternatives

Other exchange tokens work in a similar way: [BNB]({lang}/bnb/) (Binance), [OKB]({lang}/okb/) (OKX), [GT]({lang}/gt/) (Gate), [KCS]({lang}/kcs/) (KuCoin) and [WhiteBIT Coin]({lang}/wbt/). Most of them also power their own blockchains, while LEO is almost purely a buyback token.

## Main risks

- **One company:** LEO's value depends on iFinex's profits, its honesty about burns and its legal position.
- **Thin trading:** almost all volume is on Bitfinex, so selling a large amount quickly can be hard.
- **Concentration:** iFinex and early buyers hold most of the supply and decide the pace of buybacks.
- **Hack money uncertain:** how many seized bitcoins return, when, and how much is left after costs is not settled.
- **Regulation:** Bitfinex and Tether share owners and have both settled cases with US regulators; LEO is not offered in some regions, including under the EU's MiCA rules since July 2026.

## What it means if you buy regularly

LEO has moved more steadily than most coins, but that partly reflects how little it trades — and a calm chart can turn quickly if confidence in the company breaks. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your entry price over time, but it does not remove the risk of losses. Many people keep crypto to a small part of their savings that they can afford to see fall sharply. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
