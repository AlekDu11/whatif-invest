**Garanti BBVA** is one of Turkey's largest private banks: it takes deposits, lends to families and companies, issues credit cards (its Bonus card is one of the best known in the country) and runs a popular mobile app. Spain's BBVA owns about 86% of it, so only a small part of the shares trades freely on Borsa Istanbul under the ticker GARAN. Its profits depend heavily on Turkey's inflation, interest rates and the lira.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Net income: TL 98.2 billion in January–September 2026, about +16% on the year; TL 33.8 billion in the third quarter alone.
> - Return on average equity was 27.5% and total assets TL 5.6 trillion at the end of September 2026.
> - 31.8 million customers, 18.9 million of them active digital users.
> - Non-performing loans rose to 3.9% from 3.2% at the end of the first quarter, and credit costs ran above the bank's own 2026 guidance.

## How it makes money

Like any bank, Garanti BBVA earns the difference between what it charges on loans and what it pays on deposits — the net interest margin, which was 6.0% in the first nine months of 2026, almost one point higher than a year earlier as Turkish rates came down. The second engine is fees: TL 141.2 billion in nine months (+36%), two-thirds of it from payment systems — cards, merchant terminals and transfers. It also has insurance, pension, leasing and investment arms and a bank in Romania. Customer deposits were TL 3.6 trillion and cash and non-cash loans TL 4.2 trillion at the end of September 2026.

In the third quarter of 2026 Garanti BBVA earned TL 33.8 billion, 10% more than in the second quarter; the net interest margin rose to 6.3%. The capital adequacy ratio was 16.1% and the core (CET1) ratio 12.0%. Fee growth (+36%) and loan growth in lira (+39% since January) beat the bank's plan, while cost of risk was 2.6% for nine months against a 2.0–2.5% target. Remember that Turkish inflation was still around 30% in 2026, so big lira growth numbers are much smaller in real terms.

## The stock's story

Garanti was founded in 1946 and was controlled by Turkey's Doğuş Group from 1983. BBVA bought a 25% stake in 2011, raised it to about 40% in 2015 and to 49.85% in 2017, and in 2019 the bank was renamed Garanti BBVA. In 2022 BBVA made a tender offer to the other shareholders and lifted its stake to about 86%. For investors who count in dollars, the stock has been a roller coaster: it lost about two-thirds of its value in 2008, fell by more than half in the 2018 lira crisis and stayed low until 2022. After Turkey returned to high interest rates and tighter policy in mid-2023, the shares rose about fivefold in dollar terms by mid-2024, then gave part of that back in 2026.

## Competitors

Garanti BBVA competes with the other big private banks — İş Bankası, Akbank and Yapı Kredi — and with the state banks Ziraat Bankası, Halkbank and VakıfBank, which can lend at cheaper rates when the government wants. In payments and apps it also faces fintechs and digital banks.

## Main risks

- **The lira and inflation:** profits are in Turkish lira; when the lira falls, a foreign investor's return falls with it, as in 2018 and 2021.
- **Rates and policy:** the bank's margin swings with the central bank's rate and with regulators' rules on loan growth and deposits.
- **Bad loans:** high real rates squeeze borrowers; non-performing loans and credit costs were already rising in 2026.
- **Little free float:** with BBVA holding about 86%, few shares trade, and decisions follow the parent's strategy.
- **Country risk:** politics, the current-account deficit and Turkey's credit rating move foreign money in and out quickly.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Garanti BBVA is a very profitable bank by Turkish standards, but its share price in dollars has dropped by half or more several times in the past 20 years. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you get more shares when the price is low and fewer when it is high, without trying to time the market.
