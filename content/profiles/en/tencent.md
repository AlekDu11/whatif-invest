**Tencent** runs WeChat, the "everything app" that 1.42 billion people use to chat, pay, order food and book doctors — and it is also the world's biggest video game company by revenue. Pony Ma (Ma Huateng) and four partners founded it in Shenzhen in 1998; it has traded in Hong Kong since 2004.

## How it makes money

Tencent has four engines. **Games** — hits like Honor of Kings and Peacekeeper Elite at home, plus Riot Games (League of Legends) and a majority stake in Supercell abroad. **Advertising** inside WeChat, its video feeds and mini-programs. **Fintech and business services** — WeChat Pay, wealth products and Tencent Cloud. And **subscriptions** to video and music.

In 2025 revenue grew 14% to ¥751.8 billion (about $105 billion) and profit attributable to shareholders rose 16% to ¥224.8 billion. Domestic games brought ¥164.2 billion; international games jumped 33% to ¥77.4 billion. Ad revenue rose 19% to ¥145 billion, helped by AI that targets ads better.

## Why it matters

WeChat is the operating system of daily life in China, which gives Tencent a stream of users that rivals cannot easily copy. It spends heavily on AI — its own Hunyuan models — and on buying back shares: about HK$80 billion in 2025. One surprising fact: Tencent owns stakes in hundreds of companies worldwide, from game studios to e-commerce and electric cars.

## Main risks

Chinese regulators can act fast: the 2021 tech crackdown and limits on gaming for minors cut the share price sharply. US–China tension is a constant threat; the US Defense Department put Tencent on its list of "Chinese military companies" in January 2025, which Tencent disputes. Game revenue depends on new hits, AI spending pushes costs up, and a slowing Chinese economy hurts ads. Prices are in Hong Kong dollars. Regular buying smooths swings but does not remove the risk.
