**Berkshire Hathaway** is the company Warren Buffett turned from a failing textile mill into one of the biggest businesses in the world. Today it owns dozens of companies outright — the GEICO car insurer, the BNSF railroad, a large energy group, factories and household brands — plus a stock portfolio worth about $300 billion. Since January 2026 it has been run by Greg Abel, its first new CEO in six decades.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Q3 2026: revenue $105.7 billion (+11%), but operating earnings fell 6% to $12.7 billion as insurance underwriting profit dropped 43%.
> - Insurance "float" — money held between collecting premiums and paying claims — was about $176 billion at the end of 2025.
> - Berkshire bought back $9.6 billion of its own shares in Q3 2026 and held more than $350 billion in cash and Treasury bills at the end of September 2026.

## How it makes money

Insurance is the engine. GEICO, General Re and Berkshire's other insurers and reinsurers collect premiums up front and pay claims later, sometimes years later. That pool of money, called "float", reached about $176 billion at the end of 2025, and Berkshire invests it. When underwriting is profitable, the float costs nothing — it is like a loan that pays you. In 2025 insurance underwriting earned $7.3 billion after tax and insurance investment income $15.3 billion before tax.

The second leg is a group of big, steady businesses: BNSF, one of the largest railroads in North America; Berkshire Hathaway Energy (utilities, wind farms and gas pipelines); industrial firms such as Precision Castparts and Lubrizol; and brands like Duracell, See's Candies, Dairy Queen and Fruit of the Loom. In early 2026 it added OxyChem, the chemicals arm of Occidental Petroleum, for $9.7 billion.

The third leg is the stock portfolio. Its US-listed holdings were worth $304 billion on 30 September 2026, led by [Apple]({lang}/aapl/) (about $76 billion, a quarter of the total), [American Express]({lang}/axp/) ($46 billion), Alphabet, [Coca-Cola]({lang}/ko/) and Bank of America. Berkshire does not pay a dividend: it keeps the profits to buy companies or its own shares.

In the third quarter of 2026 (July–September) revenue rose 11% to $105.7 billion, the second quarter in a row of double-digit growth. Operating earnings — the profit from the businesses, without gains on stocks — fell 6% to $12.7 billion. Insurance underwriting profit dropped 43% to $1.35 billion: GEICO paid out 76.6% of its premiums in claims, against 71.5% a year earlier, as it cut prices to win back customers. BNSF earned $1.73 billion, up 19%, and Berkshire Hathaway Energy earned 8% more. Net earnings, which include paper gains on the stock portfolio, were $22.1 billion.

At the end of September Berkshire held more than $350 billion in cash and short-term Treasuries, down from a record $373 billion at the end of 2025. Buybacks sped up to $9.6 billion in the quarter, more than double the second quarter, plus about $2 billion in October. For the whole of 2025 operating earnings were about $44.5 billion, 6% lower than in 2024. One Class B share (BRK.B) is worth 1/1,500 of a Class A share (BRK.A).

## The stock's story

Buffett started buying Berkshire Hathaway, a struggling New England textile maker, in 1962 and took control in 1965. He used its cash to buy National Indemnity, an insurer, in 1967 — the same year Berkshire paid its only dividend — and later took full ownership of GEICO in 1996 and bought BNSF in 2010. Over 1965–2024 Berkshire's market value per share grew by about 19.9% a year, almost twice the 10.4% of the S&P 500 with dividends. In August 2024 it became the first non-tech US company worth more than $1 trillion.

At the annual meeting in May 2025 Buffett announced that Greg Abel, who ran the energy business and later all non-insurance operations, would become CEO; Abel took over on 1 January 2026. His first shareholder letter in February 2026 said the large cash pile was "a strategic asset", not a retreat from deal-making. On 18 September 2026 Buffett, 96, also stepped down as chairman after 56 years, becoming chairman emeritus; his son Howard Buffett became chairman. In early November 2026 the shares set new record highs.

## Competitors

Berkshire has no single twin. In insurance it competes with [Progressive]({lang}/pgr/) and State Farm for car insurance, and with [Chubb]({lang}/cb/), Munich Re and Swiss Re in reinsurance. BNSF's main rival is [Union Pacific]({lang}/unp/) in the western US. As an investment, the closest alternative is a broad index fund such as the [Vanguard S&P 500 ETF]({lang}/voo/): it also spreads money across the US economy, but with no manager making big bets.

## Main risks

- **Life after Buffett:** Abel and a new team must prove they can allocate hundreds of billions as well as Buffett did; Todd Combs, one of the two stock pickers, left for JPMorgan.
- **Insurance shocks:** a big hurricane, wildfire or legal shift can wipe out a year of underwriting profit, and GEICO's margins are shrinking.
- **Size:** at this scale only very large deals move the needle, so fast growth is hard.
- **Cash drag:** if interest rates fall, the cash pile earns less until it is spent.
- **Concentrated portfolio:** about a quarter of the stocks are Apple, so a fall in a few holdings hits net earnings.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Berkshire is often described as "the US economy in one share", but it is still one company, and its price has fallen sharply in past crises. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more shares when the price is low and fewer when it is high, without trying to time the market. Because Berkshire pays no dividend, all of the return comes from the share price. Many people hold it next to a broad index fund rather than instead of one. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
