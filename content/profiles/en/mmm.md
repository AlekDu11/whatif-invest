**3M** is the company behind Post-it notes and Scotch tape, but most of its money comes from things you rarely see: sandpaper and adhesives for factories, safety masks and ear protection, films for phone screens and car parts. It is one of America's oldest industrial names, now smaller and more focused after spinning off its health care business in 2024.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Sales: $6.58 billion in Q3 2026, +1% a year (+3.3% organic on an adjusted basis).
> - Adjusted earnings per share: $2.50, +12%; full-year 2026 guidance raised to $9.10–9.15.
> - Legal bills are large: about $2.0 billion of litigation and PFAS cash payments in the first nine months of 2026.
> - On adjusted prices the stock lost about 55% from January 2018 to October 2023, then rose 45% in 2024 and 25% in 2025.

## How it makes money

In 2026 3M reported three segments. Safety & Industrial — abrasives, tapes and adhesives, electrical products, respirators and hearing protection — brought $3.18 billion in Q3 2026, about 48% of sales, and grew 7% organically. Transportation & Electronics — films, materials for cars, electronics and data centers — added $2.0 billion (about 30%). Consumer — Post-it notes, Scotch tape and other household products — added $1.31 billion (about 20%). In the same quarter 3M expanded its data-center relationship with Amazon, including a warrant for up to 3.2 million 3M shares tied to $6 billion of sales over seven years.

In the quarter to 30 September 2026 3M had GAAP sales of $6,584 million and a GAAP operating margin of 23.7%. GAAP earnings per share jumped 70% to $2.63, partly thanks to a $0.60 gain on its remaining Solventum stake; adjusted EPS was $2.50. Adjusted free cash flow was $1.2 billion for the quarter. The company ended September with $4.8 billion of cash against roughly $15 billion of debt, and its share count fell to 516.7 million from 530.3 million at the end of 2025. In October 2026 it guided to adjusted EPS of $9.10–9.15 and about $4.8 billion of adjusted free cash flow.

## The stock's story

For decades 3M was a steady dividend stock. That changed after 2018: growth stalled and two waves of lawsuits arrived. About 260,000 lawsuits, mostly from US service members, said 3M's Combat Arms earplugs failed to protect their hearing, and water utilities sued over PFAS — "forever chemicals" 3M made for decades. In 2023 3M agreed to pay $6 billion over the earplugs (through 2029) and $10.3 billion to US public water systems. Adjusted for dividends and the spin-off, the shares lost about 55% from January 2018 to October 2023. On 1 April 2024 3M spun off its health care arm as Solventum (one SOLV share per four 3M shares) and kept 19.9% to sell later. As the big settlements removed part of the legal uncertainty, the stock rose 45% in 2024 and 25% in 2025, and on 4 November 2026 it reached an adjusted close of $193.95, a record at the time.

## Competitors

3M fights a different rival in each business: Honeywell in safety equipment, Henkel and Avery Dennison in adhesives and tapes, Saint-Gobain in abrasives, DuPont in specialty materials, and store brands in consumer goods. Its strength is breadth — tens of thousands of products sold to almost every industry.

## Main risks

- **PFAS:** 3M said it would exit PFAS manufacturing by the end of 2025, but claims beyond the US water settlement could keep costing money.
- **Settlement payments:** the earplug and water deals are paid over years, taking cash that could otherwise go to shareholders.
- **Slow growth:** sales rose only 1% in Q3 2026; much of 3M's business follows factory, car and electronics cycles.
- **Debt:** about $15 billion of borrowing against $4.8 billion of cash.

## What it means if you buy regularly

3M shows how a famous, profitable company can still lose more than half its value when legal risks pile up. Someone buying every month through 2018–2023 kept getting cheaper shares and benefited when the stock recovered — but nobody knew in advance that it would. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
