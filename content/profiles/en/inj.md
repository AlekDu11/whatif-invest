**Injective** is a blockchain designed for finance: exchanges, derivatives, tokenized stocks and other markets can run directly on it, with a shared order book built into the network. It was founded in 2018 by Eric Chen and Albert Chon, incubated by Binance Labs, and its main network went live in November 2021. ## How it works

Injective is built with the Cosmos SDK and connects to other chains through IBC and bridges. Unlike most blockchains, where every exchange writes its own smart contracts, Injective has ready-made modules for finance: an on-chain order book that many apps share, so a trade placed in one app can be matched with orders from another, and batch auctions in every block to reduce front-running. Since November 2025 it also runs Ethereum-style smart contracts natively next to its WebAssembly ones, so Ethereum developers can deploy their apps without changing tools. Apps on Injective offer perpetual futures, tokenized stocks and real-world assets; native USDC arrived in May 2026.

## What the token is used for

INJ is staked with validators to secure the network, and stakers earn newly created INJ; new issuance moves within a range depending on how much INJ is staked. Holders vote on upgrades and on economic changes. Fees on the network are paid in INJ. The special part is the burn: until October 2025 a weekly auction sold a basket of app fees for INJ and destroyed that INJ, removing about 6.8 million tokens. It was replaced by a monthly Community BuyBack: people commit INJ, receive a share of ecosystem revenue in return, and the committed INJ is burned. A "Supply Squeeze" proposal approved in January 2026 sped up the planned cut in issuance. Even so, in September 2026 new issuance was larger than the burns.

## Its story so far

Injective began in 2018 as a decentralized exchange project in Binance Labs' first incubation program and sold tokens on Binance Launchpad in October 2020. The main network launched in November 2021. In 2023–2024 interest in fast finance chains and the burn grew, and INJ reached about $52 in March 2024. It then lost more than 90% over the next two years. In April 2026 Bitnomial launched the first US-regulated INJ futures, and in May 2026 INJ almost doubled in a month, from about $3 to above $5.80. On 5 November 2026 Canary Capital's staked INJ ETF began trading on Nasdaq, and in October 2026 the team published a new whitepaper, "Injective: A Finance-Native Layer-1 Blockchain".

## Alternatives

For on-chain trading, Injective competes with [Hyperliquid]({lang}/hype/), [dYdX]({lang}/dydx/) and [Aster]({lang}/aster/), and with trading apps on [Solana]({lang}/sol/), [Ethereum]({lang}/eth/) and [Sei]({lang}/sei/). As a chain built with Cosmos technology, it is also part of the [Cosmos]({lang}/atom/) ecosystem, which shares its tools with many other networks.

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** INJ has lost more than 90% from its 2024 peak; sharp rises and falls are common.
- **Competition:** Hyperliquid and other trading platforms attract most on-chain derivatives volume.
- **Supply:** new INJ is still created for stakers, and burns depend on how much revenue the apps earn.
- **Concentration:** early investors, the team and large holders own a big share and can move the price.
- **Regulation:** derivatives and tokenized stocks are tightly regulated, and rules differ by country.

## What it means if you buy regularly

INJ shows how quickly a small token with a strong story can rise and fall. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more when the price is low and less when it is high, without trying to guess the bottom — but it does not remove the risk of losses. Many people keep crypto to a small part of their savings that they can afford to see fall sharply. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
