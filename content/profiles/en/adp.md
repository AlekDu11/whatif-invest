**Automatic Data Processing (ADP)** runs payroll, taxes, benefits and HR paperwork for more than 1.1 million employers in over 140 countries. Companies pay it a fee for every employee it handles, and ADP also earns interest on the billions of dollars of wages and taxes it holds for a few days before paying them out. In fiscal 2026 (to 30 June) revenue reached $21.9 billion, and in November 2026 the company raised its dividend for the 52nd year in a row.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: $21.9 billion in fiscal 2026, +7% on the year; net earnings $4.41 billion, or $10.94 per share.
> - Interest on clients' money brought in $1.35 billion; ADP held an average of $40.4 billion of client funds.
> - In November 2026 ADP raised its quarterly dividend 12% to $1.90 a share — the 52nd yearly increase in a row.
> - The shares fell to about $201.50 in March 2026, their lowest since April 2023, on job-market and AI worries, then recovered in the second half of the year.

## How it makes money

ADP has two businesses. **Employer Services** ($14.8 billion of revenue in fiscal 2026) sells payroll, tax filing, time tracking and HR software: RUN for small businesses, Workforce Now for mid-sized firms and Lyric HCM for large ones. Clients usually pay per employee per pay run, so ADP's revenue grows when its clients hire. **PEO Services** ($7.1 billion) is HR outsourcing under the ADP TotalSource name: ADP becomes the "co-employer" of a small company's staff and gives them big-company benefits and insurance. It covered an average of 762,000 worksite employees in fiscal 2026.

The third, quieter engine is the "float". Between the day a client sends wages and taxes and the day they are paid out, ADP invests the money in safe bonds. In fiscal 2026 that interest was $1.35 billion, up 14% — almost pure profit, but it depends on interest rates. ADP is also known for the monthly ADP National Employment Report on US private-sector jobs, which markets watch ahead of official data.

In the first quarter of fiscal 2027 (July–September 2026), reported on 28 October 2026, revenue rose 6% to $5.49 billion. Net earnings grew 11% to $1.13 billion and diluted earnings per share rose 14% to $2.83. Employer Services revenue was $3.69 billion (+6%) and PEO $1.81 billion (+7%). The number of employees on US clients' payrolls grew 2%, and interest on client funds jumped 15% to $329 million as the average yield rose to 3.6%. The adjusted operating margin widened to 26.8% from 25.5%.

ADP raised its fiscal 2027 outlook: revenue growth of about 6% (from 5–6%) and adjusted earnings per share growth of 10–12% (from 9–11%). In the quarter it paid $677 million in dividends and spent $759 million on buybacks, about twice as much as a year earlier. At the end of September it had $3.28 billion of cash and $4.97 billion of long-term debt.

## The stock's story

ADP was founded in New Jersey in 1949 as a payroll service and is still based there, in Roseland. For decades it was one of the steadiest stocks in the S&P 500: earnings and the dividend grew almost every year, and the share price rose through most of the 2010s and early 2020s. The shares set a monthly closing record in May 2025.

Then came a sharp fall. US hiring slowed through 2025 and 2026, which means fewer paychecks for ADP to process, and investors began to worry that AI could reduce the number of workers companies need or let cheaper software replace parts of ADP's service. On 25 March 2026 the stock touched about $201.50, its lowest since April 2023 and about a third lower than a year earlier. The mood turned after fiscal 2026 results on 29 July 2026 beat analysts' estimates, with management pointing to AI-driven efficiency and a margin outlook of 70–90 basis points of expansion; the shares jumped and kept recovering into the autumn. Maria Black has been CEO since 2023.

## Competitors

ADP's closest rival is [Paychex](/en/payx/), which serves mostly small and mid-sized US businesses. Cloud HR platforms such as [Workday](/en/wday/), [Paycom](/en/payc/) and [Paylocity](/en/pcty/) compete for mid-sized and large employers, along with Dayforce, UKG and Oracle. Younger players such as Gusto, Rippling and Deel target startups and international hiring. ADP's advantages are its size, its decades of tax and compliance know-how in many countries, and clients' reluctance to switch payroll systems.

## Main risks

- **Jobs:** ADP earns more when clients add workers; fiscal 2027 guidance assumes only about 1% growth in employees per US client, so a recession would hit revenue directly.
- **Interest rates:** $1.35 billion of yearly client-funds interest falls if rates come down.
- **AI and new rivals:** AI tools and fast-growing software firms could squeeze prices or make HR outsourcing less needed.
- **Slow growth:** revenue grows around 6–7% a year; the stock depends on steady margin gains and buybacks to grow earnings faster.
- **Client churn:** small businesses fail or switch providers often, so ADP must keep winning new clients just to stand still.

## What it means if you buy regularly

ADP is a mature business with a long record of rising dividends, but 2025–2026 showed that even a "boring" stock can lose about a third of its value in a year. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more shares when the price is low and fewer when it is high, and reinvested dividends add to the result over time. Many people hold one company only as part of a wider portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
