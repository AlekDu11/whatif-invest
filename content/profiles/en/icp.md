**Internet Computer (ICP)** is a blockchain built by the Swiss DFINITY Foundation that tries to run whole apps and websites on-chain, like a decentralised cloud, instead of only recording payments. ICP is its token: it is used to vote on the network's rules and is burned to pay for computing. All ICP in circulation is worth {{mcap:icp}} today ({{rank:icp}} among cryptocurrencies).

> - In January 2026 DFINITY published "Mission 70", a plan to cut ICP's yearly inflation, then about 9.7%, by 70% by the end of 2026.
> - Usage is still small next to the biggest chains: in early 2026 DeFi apps on ICP held only about $11–16 million.

## How it works

Apps on the Internet Computer live in "canisters" — smart contracts that hold both code and data and can serve a web page straight to your browser. Independent node providers run the hardware in data centres around the world, grouped into "subnets". Canisters pay for computing and storage with "cycles", and cycles are created by burning ICP, so every unit of real usage removes some ICP from supply. Users usually pay nothing per click: the app owner covers the cost, a so-called reverse gas model.

## Why people think it has value

ICP has three jobs. It is locked in "neurons" to vote in the Network Nervous System (NNS), the on-chain body that approves upgrades and new node providers, and voters earn rewards for it. It is burned for cycles. And it pays node providers. The bet is that real demand — DFINITY's AI app builder Caffeine, which writes apps from plain-language prompts, and "Cloud Engines" for business and AI workloads, rolled out to waitlisted users in November 2026 — will burn more ICP than voting and node rewards create. Under a model DFINITY described in February 2026, 20% of Cloud Engine revenue burns ICP and 80% goes to node providers.

## Its story so far

DFINITY was founded in 2016 and raised money from early backers and venture funds before launch. When ICP started trading on 10 May 2021 it briefly ranked among the ten largest cryptocurrencies, then lost more than 90% within weeks as early holders sold. A 2021 report by research firm Arkham alleged insiders had dumped tokens; DFINITY rejected it, and a US class action by investors followed. The price kept sliding through the 2022 bear market. On 14 January 2026 DFINITY published Mission 70: lower voting and node rewards should cut new supply from about 9.72% a year to about 5.42% by January 2027, and burning from usage should do the rest. ICP jumped more than 30% that day but gave the gain back by February, and in November 2026 it fell to new lows near $2.

## Alternatives

As a general platform for apps ICP competes with [Ethereum]({lang}/eth/), [Solana]({lang}/sol/), [NEAR]({lang}/near/) and [Polkadot]({lang}/dot/). For decentralised storage and computing it overlaps with [Filecoin]({lang}/fil/), [Arweave]({lang}/ar/) and [Akash Network]({lang}/akt/) — and, above all, with ordinary cloud providers such as Amazon Web Services and Google Cloud.

## Main risks

- **Inflation:** voting and node rewards create new ICP every year; Mission 70 is a plan, and DFINITY itself says the final result is not guaranteed.
- **Adoption:** the inflation cut depends on apps burning cycles, and developer and DeFi activity has stayed far below Ethereum's.
- **Big swings:** the token lost more than 99% from its 2021 launch level — a fall that has not been recovered.
- **Concentration:** DFINITY and early backers hold large voting power in the NNS, so a few players weigh heavily on decisions.
- **Competition:** big cloud companies and faster blockchains chase the same developers and the same AI workloads.

## What it means if you buy regularly

ICP's history is mostly a long decline from a very high starting price, with sharp short bounces along the way. Buying a fixed amount regularly averages your price, but it cannot turn a falling asset into a winner — it only spreads the timing. Many people keep coins like this to a small share of money they can afford to lose. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
