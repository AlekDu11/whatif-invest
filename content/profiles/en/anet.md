**Arista Networks** makes the high-speed switches that connect thousands of servers and AI chips inside giant data centers — the "traffic police" that move data between machines. Its biggest buyers are cloud companies such as Microsoft and Meta, and the AI boom has pushed its sales to the fastest growth since it listed on the New York Stock Exchange in June 2014.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: $3.66 billion in Q3 2026, +58.5% a year; net profit $1.56 billion.
> - Two customers brought in 42% of 2025 revenue (26% and 16%); in 2024 Arista named them as Microsoft and Meta.
> - Management targets about $18.5 billion of revenue in 2027, with AI networking roughly doubling to about $7 billion.
> - One IPO share from 2014 ($43) is now 16 shares after two 4-for-1 splits.

## How it makes money

Arista sells network switches and routers plus the software that runs them, called EOS. In 2025 about 65% of revenue came from its core business — AI, cloud and data-center networking. Another 18% came from "adjacencies": office and campus networks (Wi-Fi, switches for company buildings) and routing. The remaining 17% was software and services.

The AI part is the story now. Training and running AI models means linking huge numbers of chips, and the links have to be fast. Arista bets on Ethernet, an open standard, and in 2026 it launched rack-scale designs with AMD, Arm, Broadcom, Meta, Microsoft and Qualcomm, including one that connects up to 144 AI chips in a rack.

In the quarter to 30 September 2026 Arista earned $3.66 billion in revenue, up 58.5% from a year earlier and 20.5% from the previous quarter. Net profit was $1.56 billion, or $1.22 per share, and the operating margin was 44.9% — very high for a hardware company.

Arista held $14.36 billion in cash and investments and bought back no shares in the first nine months of 2026. For the fourth quarter it guided to $3.9–4.0 billion of revenue. For comparison, all of 2025 brought in $9.01 billion (+28.6%). In September 2026 it joined the S&P 100 index.

## The stock's story

Arista priced its IPO at $43 a share in June 2014. It split its stock 4-for-1 in November 2021 and again in December 2024, so our prices are split-adjusted.

The ride has been bumpy. In our monthly data the stock lost about a third between January and March 2025. It then hit a 52-week high of about $217 in October 2026, but fell 14% in one week in early November during a wider sell-off in AI hardware stocks.

## Competitors

In its annual report Arista lists Cisco, Dell, Extreme Networks, HPE (which now owns Juniper), Huawei and Nvidia, plus cheap "white box" switches that big clouds build themselves. Inside AI clusters, Nvidia's own InfiniBand and NVLink links compete with Arista's Ethernet.

## Main risks

- **Few huge customers:** two clients gave 42% of 2025 revenue; if one cuts spending, sales can drop fast.
- **AI spending cycle:** the stock falls hard whenever investors doubt big-tech data-center budgets.
- **Suppliers:** Arista relies mostly on Broadcom for its chips, with no written supply guarantee, and memory shortages have raised costs.
- **Big commitments and tariffs:** it had $11.4 billion of purchase commitments at the end of September, and its products are made in Malaysia, Vietnam and Mexico.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Arista shows how much a single stock can grow — and how fast it can drop: a third in a few months in 2025, 14% in a week in 2026. Buying a fixed amount regularly gets you more shares after such falls, but it does not protect you if the business itself slows. Many people hold a stock like this as one part of a broader portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
