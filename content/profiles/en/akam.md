**Akamai Technologies** is the company that makes websites and apps load fast and stay online when millions of people hit them at once: it keeps copies of videos, games and web pages on servers close to you and blocks attacks before they reach a bank or a shop. Banks, shops and streaming services pay it every month, and now it is spending heavily to become a cloud for AI models.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: $1.122 billion in Q3 2026, +6% a year; security alone brought $622 million (+9%).
> - Cloud infrastructure, the AI bet, grew 34% to $109 million, and Akamai says it signed more than $14 billion of multi-year cloud commitments this year.
> - The price of that bet: capital spending of $3.46–3.66 billion planned for 2026 and an adjusted operating margin down to 25% from 31%.
> - The stock fell more than 99% from its 2000 dot-com peak to 2002, and is down about 39% from its May 2026 high (monthly closes).

## How it makes money

Akamai sells three things, mostly on long contracts. **Security** is now the biggest: firewalls for websites and APIs, protection against floods of fake traffic (DDoS attacks) and tools that stop hackers moving inside a company network — about 55% of revenue in Q3 2026. **Delivery** is the original business: pushing web pages, software updates and video to users through thousands of servers around the world; it is about 35% of revenue and shrinking (−3% a year). **Cloud infrastructure services** (about 10%) rent computing power, built on Linode, which Akamai bought in 2022. This is where AI comes in: Akamai pitches its spread-out network as a place to run AI models close to users, and in 2026 launched Akamai Inference and Akamai Agent Sandbox. Half of revenue comes from outside the US.

## The numbers right now

In the quarter to 30 September 2026 Akamai earned $1.122 billion of revenue (+6%) and $117 million of GAAP net profit (−16%). Adjusted earnings per share were $1.77, down 5%. Spending is the big story: Q3 capital spending was 41% of revenue, and full-year 2026 capex is guided at $3.46–3.66 billion. Buybacks were paused in Q3 after $616 million in the first nine months. It holds $4.24 billion in cash and securities against about $7.57 billion of convertible bonds.

For 2026 it expects $4.44–4.465 billion of revenue, for 2027 growth of 17–22%, and more than $7 billion of revenue in 2028.

## The stock's story

Akamai was founded in 1998 by MIT mathematician Tom Leighton, still its CEO, and listed on Nasdaq in 1999. It was a dot-com darling: the monthly close hit about $261 in February 2000, then collapsed below $1 by September 2002 — a fall of more than 99%. Then came two decades of steady, slow growth. In May 2026 the stock jumped about 26% in a day when Akamai announced a $1.8 billion, seven-year cloud deal with a frontier AI model company, widely reported to be Anthropic. After the Q3 report investors focused on the falling margins and the spending bill, and the shares slid back from about $150 to around $92.

## Competitors

In delivery and security Akamai competes with Cloudflare, Fastly, Zscaler and Palo Alto Networks, and with the content networks of Amazon Web Services, Microsoft and Google. In AI cloud it is small next to those giants and GPU clouds such as CoreWeave. Its strengths are a network in about 130 countries and a growing security business.

## Main risks

- **Spending before revenue:** billions go into hardware now, while most of the new cloud revenue is expected in 2027–2028; if customers delay, margins stay low for longer.
- **Customer concentration:** a large part of the new commitments comes from a few AI companies, and the largest AI deal is priced with the thinnest returns among its big contracts, according to analysts.
- **Shrinking core:** delivery revenue keeps falling as prices drop.
- **Debt:** about $7.57 billion of convertible bonds; conversion into shares can dilute existing holders.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Akamai shows both sides of a long history: a near-total crash after 2000, then years of slow, steady gains, and now a sharp swing on an expensive AI bet. Regular buying spreads purchases over calm and wild months, but it cannot remove the risk that the AI spending does not pay off. Many hold a stock like this only as a small part of a portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
