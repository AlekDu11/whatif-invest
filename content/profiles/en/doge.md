**Dogecoin** is the cryptocurrency that started as a joke — a Shiba Inu meme turned into money in December 2013 — and grew into the biggest meme coin and one of the most traded coins in the world. Since late 2025 Americans can buy it through spot ETFs. In November 2026 all DOGE together were worth about $8.6 billion.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Dogecoin has no maximum supply: about 5 billion new coins are added every year on top of roughly 157 billion in circulation.
> - Because that number is fixed, yearly inflation falls slowly; it is now a little over 3%.
> - The record price so far is $0.73 (May 2021); in November 2026 DOGE traded more than 90% below it.
> - US spot Dogecoin ETFs started trading in late 2025 (Grayscale GDOG) and January 2026 (21Shares TDOG).

## How it works

Dogecoin was created by programmer Billy Markus and Adobe engineer Jackson Palmer as a lighthearted copy of Bitcoin's idea; its code came from Litecoin. It runs on a public blockchain secured by miners. A new block arrives about every minute instead of every ten, so transfers confirm faster and fees are usually tiny. Every block pays a fixed 10,000 DOGE. Since 2014 it has been "merge-mined" with Litecoin: the same machines secure both networks at once.

## What DOGE is used for

DOGE is mostly used for tipping, small payments and trading. It has no smart contracts and no company behind it; volunteers and the Dogecoin Foundation maintain the code. Its value rests on a large, loyal community, wide availability on exchanges and in payment apps, and attention: Elon Musk's posts fuelled the 2021 boom. ETFs now give it another source of demand from ordinary brokerage accounts.

## Its story so far

Dogecoin launched on 6 December 2013 and quickly became known for internet charity drives, such as sponsoring the Jamaican bobsled team for the 2014 Winter Olympics. For years it was worth a fraction of a cent. In early 2021 social media hype and Elon Musk's posts sent it up by thousands of percent, to a record of $0.73 on 7 May 2021; it then lost most of that gain in 2022. Musk's government cost-cutting body, nicknamed DOGE, brought the name back into the news in 2025. Grayscale's spot ETF opened in late 2025 and 21Shares' TDOG followed on Nasdaq in January 2026, but inflows stayed modest, and DOGE fell heavily with the rest of the market in 2026.

## Alternatives

Among meme coins, Dogecoin's main rivals are [Shiba Inu]({lang}/shib/), [Pepe]({lang}/pepe/) and [Bonk]({lang}/bonk/). For cheap, fast payments with a longer technical track record people also use [Litecoin]({lang}/ltc/), its "sister" chain, and the original, [Bitcoin]({lang}/btc/).

## Main risks

- **Hype-driven price:** demand depends on mood, social media and celebrities, and can vanish quickly.
- **Endless supply:** about 5 billion new DOGE every year keep diluting holders.
- **Concentration:** a few large wallets hold a big share of all coins.
- **No cash flow:** there is no revenue or business behind the price.
- **Big swings:** DOGE has lost more than 90% from its record.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Dogecoin's history is a few short, explosive rallies and long declines. Buying a fixed amount regularly averages the price you pay and removes the temptation to chase a hype spike, but it does not protect you from losses if interest fades. Many people keep meme coins to a very small share of savings they can afford to lose. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
