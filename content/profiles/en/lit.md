**Lighter** is a decentralised exchange for perpetual futures — contracts that let you bet on the price of bitcoin, ether and other assets with leverage. It runs on its own zk-rollup on top of Ethereum, ordinary traders pay no trading fees, and every match of orders is checked with zero-knowledge proofs. ## How it works

Lighter is a layer 2 on Ethereum. Trades happen on Lighter's own fast order book, and custom zero-knowledge circuits prove that orders were matched fairly by price and time, and that liquidations, funding payments and risk checks followed the rules. Users' money sits in Ethereum smart contracts, and the state only moves forward after a proof is verified on Ethereum. If the operator stalls or censors users, they can send their requests straight to Ethereum or leave through an emergency exit called "Desert Mode".

## How it makes money and what LIT does

Lighter's business model is unusual: people trading through the front end pay nothing, while API and high-frequency flow — market makers and trading firms — pays "premium" fees. The team says exchange revenue and the revenue of future products are visible on-chain and go to growth and LIT buybacks, depending on market conditions; fees and buybacks can be tracked in a treasury account on Lighter's block explorer.

LIT is issued by Lighter's US company. In January 2026 staking became its first big use: to deposit into the Lighter Liquidity Pool (LLP), which earns yield and acts as an insurance fund in liquidations, you must stake 1 LIT for every 10 USDC. Stakers with at least 100 LIT pay no withdrawal or transfer fees, and staking gives discounts on premium fees. The team also plans to tie its transaction and fairness-verification infrastructure to LIT staking.

## Its story so far

Lighter was founded in 2022 by Vladimir Novakovski, who entered Harvard at 16 and started working at Citadel at 18. It grew out of Lunchclub, an AI networking app he co-founded in 2017; most of that team moved to Lighter. In 2024 it raised $21 million in a round led by Haun Ventures and Craft Ventures. After about eight months of private testing, with around 188,000 accounts, the public mainnet opened in October 2025.

Growth was fast: in mid-November 2025 weekly volume peaked at $74.9 billion, and for November 2025 Lighter reported $292.5 billion, ahead of Aster and Hyperliquid. That month it raised $68 million in a round led by Founders Fund and Ribbit Capital, with Haun Ventures and Robinhood, reportedly at about a $1.5 billion valuation. Spot trading was added in early December 2025.

LIT started trading on 30 December 2025; the airdrop to holders of points from the first two 2025 seasons was worth about $675 million at the time. In early January 2026 the treasury account began accumulating LIT, which the market read as buybacks, and on 14 January 2026 the LLP staking requirement was announced. Once the airdrop was over, many points farmers left: by mid-January 2026 weekly volume had fallen to about $25.3 billion, roughly a third of the peak, and Hyperliquid was back in first place.

## Alternatives

The main rival is [Hyperliquid]({lang}/hype/), which runs on its own layer-1 blockchain rather than on Ethereum and has its own token HYPE. [Aster]({lang}/aster/) competed with Lighter for first place by volume in late 2025, and [dYdX]({lang}/dydx/) is one of the oldest decentralised perpetuals exchanges. Big centralised exchanges such as Binance and OKX still handle most crypto futures trading.

## Main risks

- **Volume driven by rewards:** a large part of the 2025 activity came from points farming before the airdrop; after it, volume fell about threefold in a few weeks.
- **Token unlocks:** half of the supply belongs to the team and investors; after the one-year lock it unlocks over three years, which can add selling pressure.
- **Competition:** Hyperliquid, Aster and centralised exchanges fight for the same traders, and fee-free trading is easy to copy.
- **Technology:** a young zk-rollup with a custom order book and proof system can have bugs or outages, even with an Ethereum exit.
- **Regulation:** leveraged derivatives are tightly regulated in many countries, and the rules for decentralised exchanges can change.
- **Big swings:** LIT is a young token; large drops after launch are part of its history.

## What it means if you buy regularly

LIT has traded for less than a year, so there is no long "$10 a day" track record to look at — any result covers only a short and very volatile period. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your entry price over time instead of betting on one moment, but it does not remove the risk of losses, especially with a young token and upcoming unlocks. If you buy at all, many people keep such tokens to a small part of their savings that they can afford to see fall sharply. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
