**Cadence Design Systems** makes the software that engineers use to design computer chips — from Nvidia's AI processors to the chips in phones and cars — and to test them before a single one is made. This kind of software is called EDA (electronic design automation), and Cadence and its rival Synopsys dominate it. Based in San Jose, California, it also sells ready-made chip building blocks (IP), simulation software for whole electronic systems and hardware that emulates chips before they exist. Its shares trade on Nasdaq under the ticker CDNS.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Third quarter of 2026: revenue $1.633 billion, up 22% on the year; GAAP net income $289.7 million, or $1.05 per share ($2.11 non-GAAP).
> - Backlog — signed contracts not yet booked as revenue — reached a record $8.2 billion at the end of September 2026.
> - About 79% of revenue is recurring, mostly multi-year software licences.
> - In October 2026 it raised its 2026 revenue forecast to $6.387–6.417 billion, about 21% growth, after $5.297 billion in 2025.

## How it makes money

Chip companies pay Cadence for licences, usually three-year contracts, so most revenue repeats and the company can see years ahead. In the third quarter of 2026 **Core EDA** — tools to draw, check and verify chip designs, plus Palladium and Protium hardware that emulates a chip so its software can be tested early — brought in 69% of revenue. **Semiconductor IP** (15%) means ready-made, pre-tested blocks such as memory and connection interfaces that customers drop into their own chips. **System Design and Analysis** (16%) covers software that simulates heat, electromagnetic signals, mechanics and fluid flow in circuit boards, cars and aircraft — an area it expanded by buying BETA CAE in 2024 and Hexagon's design and engineering business (MSC Software) for about €2.7 billion in 2026. By region, the Americas gave 43% of quarterly revenue, China 16%, other Asia 19%, Europe, Middle East and Africa 15% and Japan 7%.

In the quarter to September 2026 revenue grew 22% to $1.633 billion from $1.339 billion; Core EDA rose 20%, IP 24% and System Design and Analysis 30%. GAAP operating margin was 26.2% (44.1% non-GAAP, which leaves out costs such as stock pay and acquisitions). Operating cash flow was $424 million, and the company spent $400 million buying back its own shares; the board added another $2.5 billion to the buyback plan. For 2026 Cadence expects GAAP earnings of $4.84–4.90 per share ($8.37–8.43 non-GAAP) and about $2.0 billion of operating cash flow. At the end of September it had $1.45 billion of cash and $2.65 billion of debt. It does not pay a dividend.

## The stock's story

Cadence was formed in 1988 by the merger of two chip-design software firms, SDA Systems and ECAD. For years it was a solid but unexciting software business; it went through a tough patch around the 2008 crisis. Lip-Bu Tan, CEO from 2009 to 2021, refocused it on recurring software and IP, and profit margins rose. Anirudh Devgan took over as CEO in December 2021. As chips became more complex and AI drove demand for custom processors, the stock became one of the market's long-term winners: our data show it rising many times over the past decade. In July 2025 Cadence agreed to pay about $140 million to settle US charges over exports to a Chinese military university, and in mid-2025 the US briefly restricted sales of chip-design software to China. The company is now adding AI to its own tools so engineers can design chips faster.

## Competitors

Its main rival is Synopsys, which bought simulation software maker Ansys in 2025; together the two control most of the EDA market. The third player is Siemens, whose EDA unit grew out of Mentor Graphics. In simulation it competes with Ansys (now Synopsys) and Dassault Systèmes, and in IP with Synopsys and Arm Holdings. Chinese firms such as Empyrean are growing at home.

## Main risks

- **A two-player market:** if Synopsys gains with a new tool or a big customer switches, growth can slow fast.
- **China and export rules:** China gave 16% of quarterly revenue; US restrictions can cut sales overnight, as in 2025.
- **The chip cycle:** customers spend on design when they are confident; a downturn in chips or a pause in AI spending would delay new contracts.
- **Acquisitions:** large deals such as Hexagon's unit raise debt and lower GAAP margins until they are integrated.
- **A high price:** the shares usually trade at a high multiple of earnings, so disappointing growth can mean a sharp fall even if the business stays healthy.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Cadence earns most of its money from long software contracts, which makes its revenue steadier than that of chipmakers, but its stock can still fall sharply when investors cool on chips or AI. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more shares after those drops and fewer at the peaks, without trying to time the market. Many people hold one technology company only as part of a wider portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
