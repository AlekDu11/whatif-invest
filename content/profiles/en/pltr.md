**Palantir** makes software that pulls an organisation's scattered data into one place and lets people — and now AI models — act on it: an army planning a mission, a factory spotting a supply problem, a hospital managing beds. Built first for US intelligence agencies, it still earns a lot from governments, but in Q3 2026 US companies paid it more than the US government for the first time. Sales nearly doubled in a year, yet the stock is one of the most expensive on Wall Street.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: $2.31 billion in Q3 2026, +96% in a year; US commercial revenue +148% to $984 million.
> - Very profitable: net income $1.13 billion in the quarter, no debt, $10.4 billion in cash and US Treasuries.
> - The stock went from a closing low near $5.92 in December 2022 to a record near $207 in November 2025.
> - The price assumes years of fast growth: about 109 times the profit of the past 12 months.

## How it makes money

Palantir sells subscriptions to its platforms. **Gotham** is the original defence and intelligence tool, **Foundry** is an "operating system" for company data, and **AIP** (Artificial Intelligence Platform) connects large language models to that data so they can do real work. Clients usually start with a pilot and then expand, so most growth comes from existing customers signing bigger deals.

In Q3 2026 the US brought in $1.94 billion of the $2.31 billion total. US government revenue rose 96% to $953 million, helped by Pentagon programmes such as the Maven Smart System. CEO Alex Karp links demand to "sovereign AI" — organisations that want to control their own AI and data.

## The numbers right now

In the quarter to 30 September 2026 Palantir made $1.085 billion of GAAP operating income (a 47% margin) and $1.22 billion of adjusted free cash flow. Its "Rule of 40" score — growth plus profit margin, a yardstick for software firms — was 158%, far above the 40% seen as healthy. New contracts signed were worth $2.92 billion, up only 6% a year. Stock-based pay reached $319 million in the quarter, almost double a year earlier.

Management raised its 2026 forecast to $8.53 billion of revenue (about +91%), including more than $3.56 billion from US companies, and expects $2.649–2.653 billion in Q4.

## The stock's story

Palantir listed through a direct listing on 30 September 2020 at a reference price of $7.25. Early excitement lifted it to about $45 in January 2021, then rising rates and losses dragged it to a closing low near $5.92 in December 2022. The AI boom turned it around: it joined the S&P 500 in September 2024, moved to the Nasdaq in November 2024 and set a record near $207.52 on 3 November 2025. In 2026 it was down about 26% by mid-May as investors cooled on pricey AI stocks, then bounced about 80% from the year's low by late September.

## Competitors

In business software Palantir competes with data platforms such as Snowflake and Databricks, with C3.ai, and with Microsoft, Amazon and Google, which sell their own data and AI tools. AI labs such as OpenAI and Anthropic are a newer threat. In defence it faces big contractors and governments' in-house systems. Its edge: software proven in the most sensitive settings.

## Main risks

- **Valuation:** the stock began 2026 at about 155 times past earnings; in May 2026 it fell even after strong results.
- **Government dependence:** a big share of revenue comes from the US government, so budgets and politics matter.
- **Slower deal signing:** contract value grew 6% in Q3 while revenue grew 96%.
- **Dilution and insider selling:** rising stock-based pay and insider sales weigh on shareholders.
- **Reputation:** privacy rows, such as the one over its UK NHS data work, can cost contracts.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Palantir has shown both extremes: a fall of more than 85% from the 2021 high to the 2022 low, then a rise of more than 30 times. Regular buying spreads purchases across such swings, but a stock priced for perfection can drop hard even when the business does well. Many people hold a stock like this only as a small slice of a broader portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
