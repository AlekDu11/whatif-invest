**Allstate** is one of the biggest car and home insurers in the United States: you pay a monthly premium, and it pays when your car is hit or a storm takes your roof off. Its profit depends on two things: charging more in premiums than it pays out in claims, and investing the money it holds until those claims come in. It also sells device protection plans, roadside help and identity protection.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: $17.3 billion in Q3 2026, +0.5% a year; premiums earned rose 3.8% to $15.1 billion.
> - Catastrophe losses almost tripled to $1.65 billion (from $558 million), so quarterly net income fell 44.8% to $2.05 billion.
> - Over nine months, net income still rose 21.3% to $7.72 billion and the combined ratio improved to 85.1 from 89.4.
> - Share count is down 5.1% in a year; about $1.5 billion went to buybacks and dividends in Q3 alone.

## How it makes money

The core is property-liability insurance: 39.3 million policies, of which 26.2 million are car policies and 7.9 million homeowners policies. Insurers measure success with the combined ratio — claims plus costs per $100 of premium. Allstate's was 86.6 in Q3 2026, meaning it kept about $13.40 of every $100 before investment income; above 100 means an underwriting loss. Car insurance did better than home this quarter (83.2 vs 89.5), because storms hit homes hardest.

The second engine is a $87.4 billion investment portfolio that earned $1.18 billion in the quarter. A smaller Protection Services arm (device protection plans, roadside, identity protection) brought in $964 million of revenue, +6.9%. Arity, its driving-data unit, earned $76 million and bought the traffic-data firm INRIX in October 2026.

## The numbers right now

In the quarter to 30 September 2026 Allstate earned $8.03 per share under standard accounting (GAAP), down from $13.95 a year earlier; the year-ago figure also included a $720 million gain from selling its group health business. Adjusted earnings, which strip out investment gains and losses, were $9.76 per share. The investment portfolio lost 2.1% in the quarter, with $545 million of net losses on investments and derivatives.

For the first nine months, underwriting income — profit from insurance itself — jumped 47.5% to $6.69 billion. Book value, roughly what the company would be worth on paper per share, rose 25.3% to $120.23. Of the $4.0 billion buyback programme started in February 2026, $1.3 billion was left at the end of September.

## The stock's story

Allstate is a slow-and-steady stock with some deep holes. In the 2008 financial crisis it fell 78.6% from its December 2006 peak to March 2009 and did not regain that level until November 2014. It dropped 41.7% in the March 2020 Covid crash and almost 30% between April 2022 and July 2023, when car repair costs jumped faster than insurers could raise prices. As prices caught up, profits returned: the stock set a record of $275.11 in August 2026 and closed at $267.64 on 13 November 2026.

## Competitors

In car insurance Allstate fights State Farm, Progressive, GEICO (owned by Berkshire Hathaway), USAA and Liberty Mutual; in home insurance it meets State Farm and many regional insurers. Price matters most in car insurance, so customers switch easily. Allstate's strengths are its brand, a network of agents and direct sales, and the scale to spread risk across the whole country.

## Main risks

- **Catastrophes:** hurricanes, wildfires, hail and winter storms can wipe out a quarter's profit, as Q3 2026 showed; climate change makes them harder to price.
- **Car repair and medical costs:** if claims costs rise faster than premiums, as in 2022–2023, car insurance can swing to losses.
- **Price competition:** Allstate's average car premiums are already slipping as it moves customers to simpler, cheaper products; rivals can cut prices too.
- **Investment swings:** the portfolio carried $2.6 billion of unrealised losses at the end of Q3; falling markets hit book value.
- **Regulators:** US states approve insurance rate increases, which can delay price rises after costly years.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Allstate's profits move with the weather and with car repair prices, so the stock can stall or fall for years — the 2008 drop took almost eight years to recover. Regular buying smooths out the entry price across good and bad years, and the company returns cash through buybacks and dividends, but one insurer is still one company's risk. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
