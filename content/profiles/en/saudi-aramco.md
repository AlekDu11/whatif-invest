**Saudi Aramco** is the world's largest oil company: in 2025 it pumped about 12.9 million barrels of oil and gas equivalent every day and earned $93.4 billion in net profit — more than almost any company on earth. It is the Saudi state oil company, listed on the Riyadh exchange (Tadawul) since December 2019.

## How it makes money

Most money comes from "upstream": finding, pumping and selling crude oil and natural gas from fields such as Ghawar, among the cheapest to produce anywhere. "Downstream" — refineries and chemical plants in Saudi Arabia, Asia and the US — turns crude into fuel and plastics. In 2025 revenue was $415.8 billion (down 7% as oil averaged $69 a barrel) and free cash flow $85.4 billion. In Q3 2026 adjusted net income rose 9% to $31.2 billion as refining margins jumped.

## Why it matters

Aramco supplies more than half of the Saudi government's income, and it shares profit generously: it declared a base dividend of $21.9 billion for Q3 2026 alone. Debt is low — the gearing ratio was 5.5% at the end of September 2026. It is also expanding gas output and has bought into the Saudi AI company HUMAIN. One surprising fact: the state owns about 81.5% and the Public Investment Fund about 16%, so less than 3% of the shares trade freely, even though the 2019 listing was the largest IPO in history.

## Main risks

Profit rises and falls with oil prices, which Aramco does not control; Saudi output also follows OPEC+ quotas. Total payouts already fell from $124 billion in 2024 to $85.5 billion in 2025 when the extra "performance" dividend was cut. War in the region can disrupt exports. In the long run, electric cars and climate policies could cut oil demand. Minority shareholders have little say. Regular buying smooths price swings but does not remove the risk.
