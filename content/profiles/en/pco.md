**Pepco Group** runs Pepco, the discount chain where many Polish and other European families buy children's clothes, bedding, homeware, toys and seasonal decorations for a few złoty or euros. Listed in Warsaw since May 2021, it used to own the UK chain Poundland and the Dealz shops too, but sold both in 2025–2026 to become a pure Pepco business. Its financial year ends in September.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue for the year to September 2026 is expected above €4.5 billion, about +8% at constant currency, from about 4,260 stores.
> - Underlying net earnings are expected to grow more than 60% in FY26; first-half underlying EBITDA rose 17.5% to €516 million.
> - Pepco returned €400 million through a tender buyback in August 2026 and plans up to €400 million more over FY27–FY29.
> - Listed at 40 zł in May 2021, the share fell below 13 zł in spring 2025, then rose about 64% between April and late July 2026.

## How it makes money

Pepco sells cheap general merchandise and clothing in small-town and city stores across Central, Eastern and Western Europe. Gross margin — what is left from each sale after paying for the goods — is about 51% for FY26, up 2 percentage points, because the group cut low-margin groceries (FMCG) and sources most products directly. Growth comes from opening stores: about 250 net new ones in FY26 and at least 600 more planned in Western Europe (including Spain and Italy) in FY27–FY30. It is also running a careful trial in selected areas of Ukraine. Poundland made 33% of group revenue but only 5% of operating profit before it was sold in June 2025 to Gordon Brothers for a nominal price; Dealz Poland was sold in July 2026.

In the half-year to 31 March 2026 revenue was €2.47 billion (+5%), underlying profit after tax €198 million (+52%) and net debt only €139 million, about 0.2 times yearly EBITDA. In the last quarter of FY26 like-for-like sales — in stores open more than a year — rose 9.4%, partly thanks to planned stock clearance; Western Europe grew 14.9% and Poland 5.9%. Full FY26 results are due on 9 December 2026. The plan is to pay 25% of underlying profit as dividends and move towards 40% over time.

## The stock's story

Pepco listed on the Warsaw Stock Exchange in May 2021 at 40 zł a share, valuing it at about €5 billion. Then came two profit warnings in September 2023 and the debt crisis at its then majority owner Steinhoff, and the shares slid for years, bottoming below 13 zł in spring 2025. Selling Poundland, focusing on the profitable Pepco chain and returning cash turned sentiment: the stock rose about 64% between April and late July 2026, and the August tender buyback was priced at 47.52 zł. In 2026 investment group Ibex was the main shareholder and Stephan Borchert was CEO.

## Competitors

Pepco competes with Action (the Dutch non-food discounter expanding fast across Europe), Sinsay from Poland's LPP, Primark, KiK, TEDi, and supermarkets and online shops such as Temu and Shein that sell similar low-price goods.

## Main risks

- **Consumer spending:** Pepco's shoppers count every złoty; a weaker economy or higher prices hit sales quickly.
- **Expansion in the West:** new markets such as Spain and Italy must keep working as well as Central Europe did.
- **Competition from Action and Asian online platforms** keeps pressure on prices and margins.
- **Costs and currency:** Pepco sources its products directly from suppliers abroad while selling in złoty, euros and other local currencies, so exchange rates, freight and wages all affect margins.
- **Higher debt:** new borrowing for the €400 million buyback lifts leverage towards about 1.0 times EBITDA.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Pepco shows how much a single retailer can swing: someone who bought at the 2021 listing price was down more than two-thirds four years later, while someone buying in 2025 saw the price more than triple. Regular buying spreads purchases across such swings, and dividends are small but growing. Prices are in złoty, so for buyers in other currencies the exchange rate adds or takes away from the result. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
