**MemeCore** is a blockchain made for memecoins: instead of treating joke tokens as a side show, it tries to reward the communities behind them. Its main network went live in September 2025, and its token M quickly became one of the larger new coins. Today M is worth {{mcap:m}} ({{rank:m}} among cryptocurrencies).

## How it works

MemeCore is a layer 1 — its own blockchain, not an app on another one — and it is compatible with Ethereum tools, so wallets and apps written for Ethereum can work on it. Memecoins launched on it use a standard called MRC-20, the local version of Ethereum's ERC-20. Blocks are produced by a small group of validators: the seven with the most M staked take turns and the list is refreshed every 10 blocks, and each validator must lock 7 million M of its own. MemeCore calls this "Proof of Meme"; in practice it is a mix of proof of stake and a permissioned validator set, with extra rewards for meme communities.

## What the token is used for

M pays the transaction fees on the network and is staked by validators and by people who delegate to them. Each new MRC-20 memecoin sets aside a "Meme Vault", usually 5% of its supply, which is paid out over about 1,000 days to people who stake that memecoin — so holders can earn both M and the meme token. A share of block rewards goes to a fund for viral meme projects, and M is the main trading pair on the network's launchpad. According to the project, 58% of the supply is for the community and incentives, 15% for the foundation, 13% for core contributors, 12% for investors and 2% for a meme treasury.

## Its story so far

MemeCore raised money from crypto funds such as IBC Group and Waterdrip Capital in March 2025. M started trading in early July 2025, with Bitget as the first exchange, at around $0.05. The main network launched on 9 September 2025 and the price jumped above $2 within days. In 2026 more exchanges added M; it traded near $3.40 in April and peaked in July 2026, then fell sharply and traded below $1 in November 2026.

## Alternatives

Most memecoins live on bigger general-purpose chains: [Dogecoin]({lang}/doge/) has its own network, while [Shiba Inu]({lang}/shib/) and [Pepe]({lang}/pepe/) run on [Ethereum]({lang}/eth/), and [Bonk]({lang}/bonk/) and [dogwifhat]({lang}/wif/) on [Solana]({lang}/sol/). The closest competitor to MemeCore's launchpad idea is [Pump.fun]({lang}/pump/), the Solana platform where many memecoins are created.

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** M rose more than fifty-fold within a year of launch and then lost most of that gain; such moves can repeat in both directions.
- **Token unlocks:** most of the 10 billion M is not yet in circulation, and the timing for team and investor tokens is not fully public.
- **Centralisation:** a few validators with large stakes produce the blocks, and the founding company still controls key rules.
- **Real use:** activity on the network is small compared with its market value, so the price depends heavily on the memecoin mood.
- **Regulation and scams:** memecoins are a favourite area for rug pulls, and rules for tokens differ by country.

## What it means if you buy regularly

M has only a short history, and memecoin tokens can lose most of their value quickly. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more when the price is low and less when it is high, without trying to guess the bottom — but it does not remove the risk of losses. Many people keep crypto to a small part of their savings that they can afford to see fall sharply. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
