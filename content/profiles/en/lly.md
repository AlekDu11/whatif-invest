**Eli Lilly** is the Indianapolis drugmaker behind Mounjaro and Zepbound, the diabetes and weight-loss injections that made it the fastest-growing big pharmaceutical company in the world. It earns money by inventing medicines, protecting them with patents and selling them in about 90 countries. In November 2025 it became the first healthcare company to pass $1 trillion in stock market value.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: $24.4 billion in the September 2026 quarter, +39% on the year; net income $6.5 billion, or $7.22 per share.
> - Mounjaro brought in $10.7 billion (+65%) and Zepbound $5.4 billion (+49%) — two brands of one molecule, tirzepatide, about two thirds of all sales.
> - In October 2026 Lilly raised its 2026 revenue forecast to $91–92 billion, from $65.2 billion in 2025.
> - Foundayo (orforglipron), its weight-loss pill, was approved in the US in April 2026 and sold $184 million in its first full quarter.

## How it makes money

A successful drug can earn billions a year while its patent lasts; when the patent runs out, cheap copies arrive and sales fall fast. So Lilly spends heavily on research — $4.2 billion in the September 2026 quarter alone, 17% of revenue — and keeps buying smaller biotech companies with promising drugs.

Today one ingredient dominates: tirzepatide, sold as Mounjaro for type 2 diabetes and Zepbound for obesity. Around it Lilly sells newer "key products" — the Alzheimer's drug Kisunla, the blood-cancer drug Jaypirca, the eczema treatment Ebglyss, Omvoh for bowel disease and the breast-cancer pill Inluriyo — which together with Mounjaro, Zepbound and Foundayo made $17.1 billion in the quarter. The older franchise is insulin: Lilly made the first commercial insulin in 1923, and in 2026 the FDA approved Onswik, a once-a-week insulin. Volume rose 53% in the quarter while realized prices fell 15%: Lilly is selling far more medicine at lower prices.

In the September 2026 quarter US sales were $15.1 billion (+34%) and sales outside the US $9.3 billion (+47%). Gross margin was 83.9%. Net income rose 16% to $6.45 billion, even though Lilly booked $4.2 billion of charges for drug projects it bought (mainly AtaiBeckley and Curevo). In the first nine months of 2026 revenue was $67.2 billion, up 46%. Lilly expects non-GAAP earnings of $35–36 per share for 2026. It paid a dividend of $1.73 per share in the quarter, 15% more than a year earlier.

## The stock's story

Colonel Eli Lilly founded the company in 1876. For most of the 2000s the stock went nowhere: on the WhatIfStack chart it cost about $33 in January 2000 and about $23 in January 2010, as blockbusters like Prozac and later Zyprexa lost patent protection. The turn came with the new diabetes drugs. Mounjaro was approved in May 2022 and Zepbound in November 2023, and demand outran supply for months. The shares rose from about $129 in January 2020 to about $1,031 in January 2026 — roughly eightfold. In November 2025 Lilly signed a voluntary agreement with the US government on drug prices and access, and the same month its market value passed $1 trillion. David Ricks has been CEO since 2017.

## Competitors

The main rival is Denmark's Novo Nordisk with Ozempic and Wegovy (semaglutide); in 2026 its next drug, retatrutide, beat Ozempic's highest dose in a head-to-head diabetes trial. Amgen, Pfizer, AstraZeneca, Roche and many smaller biotechs are developing their own obesity drugs, and cheaper copies of semaglutide appeared in some countries where its patent expired in 2026. In cancer and immunology Lilly competes with Merck, AstraZeneca, AbbVie and Johnson & Johnson.

## Main risks

- **One molecule:** Mounjaro and Zepbound are about two thirds of revenue, so any safety issue, supply problem or better rival drug would hit the whole company.
- **Prices:** US politicians and insurers push drug prices down, and prices outside the US fell 29% in the quarter — growth depends on selling much more volume.
- **Trials fail:** expensive research and acquisitions do not always produce approved drugs.
- **Patents:** tirzepatide is protected for years, but every drug eventually loses exclusivity.
- **A high price:** the stock trades at a high multiple of earnings, so even a small disappointment can cause a sharp fall.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Lilly has been one of the best big stocks of the 2020s, but a drugmaker built on one hit can fall hard when the story changes — this one spent a decade going sideways before. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more shares when the price is low and fewer when it is high, without trying to time the market. Many people hold one company only as part of a wider portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
