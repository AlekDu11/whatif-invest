**Bank Pekao** is Poland's second-largest universal bank. It serves millions of Poles and Polish firms with accounts, mortgages, cash loans and business finance. Founded in 1929 and listed in Warsaw, it was controlled by Italy's UniCredit until 2017. Since then the biggest stakes have been held by the state-controlled insurer PZU and the state development fund PFR. For investors it is mostly a dividend story: about three-quarters of its 2025 profit goes back to shareholders.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Net profit: PLN 4.26 billion in the first nine months of 2026, down 18%, mostly because Poland raised the tax rate on bank profits to 30%.
> - Fee income grew 11% to PLN 2.51 billion, while net interest income slipped 1.5% to PLN 10.14 billion.
> - Record dividend: PLN 19.77 per share from 2025 profit, about PLN 5.2 billion in total.
> - The planned merger with PZU, first aimed for mid-2026, is delayed with no new date.

## How it makes money

Like most Polish banks, Pekao earns mainly on the gap between the interest it charges on loans and the interest it pays on deposits (net interest income). Fees from cards, accounts, loans, investment funds and insurance come second. It works in three segments: retail and private banking for individuals; enterprise banking for small and mid-sized firms; and corporate and investment banking for large companies, where it is among the market leaders. It ended 2025 with PLN 352 billion of assets.

## The numbers right now

In the third quarter of 2026 Pekao earned PLN 1.52 billion, about 20% less than a year earlier but above analysts' expectations. For the nine months, the higher bank tax alone cut profit by over PLN 0.7 billion. Without that change the bank says the result would be roughly flat. Return on equity was 16.8%, or 19.4% adjusted for the tax change. Lending grew about 10% year on year, led by corporate loans (+13%) and cash loans (+16%). The total capital ratio stood at 17.8%, well above the regulatory minimum.

2025 was a record year, with PLN 7.02 billion of net profit and an ROE of 21.4%. That paid for the PLN 19.77-per-share dividend, about 75% of profit, the maximum the Polish regulator KNF allowed.

## The stock's story

In 2017 PZU and PFR bought 32.8% of Pekao from UniCredit at PLN 123 a share, about PLN 10.6 billion in total. Like other Polish banks, the shares crashed in the spring 2020 Covid panic, when interest rates fell to near zero. They then rallied strongly as rates rose from 2022 and profits jumped. Over the past year the stock has traded between roughly PLN 187 and PLN 286. Our price data is in US dollars, so it also moves with the zloty.

## Competitors

Pekao's main rival is PKO Bank Polski, the country's largest bank. It also competes with Santander Bank Polska, mBank, ING Bank Śląski and Alior Bank, which is also partly owned by PZU. Its strength is corporate banking.

## Main risks

- **Taxes and politics:** the higher bank tax already costs over PLN 0.7 billion a year. Polish governments have a history of new levies and mortgage payment holidays.
- **Falling interest rates:** as the National Bank of Poland cuts rates, the gap between loan and deposit rates narrows.
- **The PZU deal:** the merger needs new laws, regulator approval and shareholder votes, and the terms for minority shareholders are not set.
- **Currency:** for a dollar or hryvnia investor, swings in the zloty add to or cut returns.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Pekao is a mature, profitable bank that pays large dividends rather than growing fast. Our ten-year "$10 a day" figure includes the 2020 crash, the rate-driven rally and moves in the zloty. Regular buying smooths those swings, but bank shares can fall hard in a crisis or after rule changes. A single bank is usually only one part of a diversified portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
