**Polygon** is a network of blockchains built to make Ethereum-style apps cheap and fast enough for everyday payments. It started in India in 2017 as Matic Network, and in September 2024 its token MATIC was upgraded one-for-one to **POL**, which now pays the fees on Polygon. ## How it works

The main chain, Polygon PoS, is a proof-of-stake network: instead of miners, validators lock up POL and take turns producing blocks, and it regularly saves checkpoints to Ethereum. It works with Ethereum wallets such as MetaMask, but a transfer usually costs a fraction of a cent. Upgrades in 2025 (Bhilai, Heimdall v2 and Rio) pushed it past 1,000 transactions per second and cut the time until a payment is final to about five seconds; the "Gigagas" roadmap aims at 100,000 per second. **Agglayer** is a second piece: a layer that links many separate chains so tokens and messages can move between them as if they were one network.

## What the token is used for

POL pays the gas fees on Polygon PoS. Validators and the people who delegate to them stake POL to secure the network and earn new POL as rewards. POL is also the token for Agglayer and for staking that gives access to airdrops from chains built on it, such as Katana. Holders vote on community treasury grants. Because new tokens are issued every year, the supply grows unless burns and demand from usage outweigh it.

## Its story so far

Jaynti Kanani, Sandeep Nailwal, Anurag Arjun and Mihailo Bjelic founded Matic Network in 2017, and its chain went live in mid-2020. In February 2021 the project renamed itself Polygon; that year cheap fees drew games, NFT markets and DeFi apps, and it bought Hermez Network for about $250 million. In February 2022 it raised $450 million in a token sale led by Sequoia Capital India. Polymarket, the prediction market, was built on Polygon. After the 2022 crash the project focused on zero-knowledge technology, cut about 19% of its staff in February 2024 and swapped MATIC for POL in September 2024. In 2025 Sandeep Nailwal became CEO of the Polygon Foundation and the network repositioned itself around stablecoin payments; in October 2026 Polygon completed the purchase of Coinme.

## Alternatives

Polygon competes with Ethereum's layer-2 networks such as [Arbitrum]({lang}/arb/) and [Mantle]({lang}/mnt/), and with Coinbase's Base, which has no token. For fast and cheap payments, [Solana]({lang}/sol/) and [Tron]({lang}/trx/) are the main rivals — Tron moves a large share of the world's USDT. All of them ultimately compete with [Ethereum]({lang}/eth/) itself as fees there get lower.

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** the price fell sharply after its March 2024 peak, and drops of 80–90% have happened before.
- **Usage does not equal price:** payment volume can grow while the token falls, because fees are tiny and most payments are in stablecoins.
- **Inflation:** about 2% new POL a year dilutes holders unless burns offset it.
- **Competition:** Base, Arbitrum, Solana and Tron fight for the same apps and payment partners.
- **Security and regulation:** a December 2021 bug allowed the theft of about 800,000 MATIC, and payments licenses bring regulators closer to the project.

## What it means if you buy regularly

POL has a short history under its new name, and most of it has been a decline. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your entry price over time, but it cannot turn a falling asset into a winner. Many people keep smaller coins like this to a small slice of a crypto allocation they can afford to lose. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
