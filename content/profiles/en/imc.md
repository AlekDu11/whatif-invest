**IMC** (formerly Industrial Milk Company) is a Ukrainian farming company: it grows corn, sunflower and wheat on about 115,000 hectares in the Poltava, Chernihiv and Sumy regions and stores grain in its own elevators. Founded in 2007 and registered in Luxembourg, it has traded on the Warsaw Stock Exchange since 4 May 2011, so its shares are quoted in Polish zloty.

> **Key takeaways**
> - 2025 was strong: revenue $190.5 million, EBITDA $95.8 million (+11%), net profit $67.5 million (+24%).
> - First half of 2026: revenue $88.9 million (+6%), but net profit fell 33% to $34.3 million.
> - Dividends since 2017, more than $110 million in total; €0.98 a share paid in 2025 and €0.52 in May 2026.
> - Founder Oleksandr Petrov's Agrovalley owns 76.14%; talks to sell control to Kernel were suspended in May 2025.

## How it makes money

IMC is a pure crop farmer. It sells mostly corn, plus sunflower and wheat. In the first half of 2026 corn alone brought 98.1% of revenue: 419,000 tonnes at an average of $208 a tonne.

The second business is storage. IMC's elevators and warehouses hold 554,000 tonnes of grain and oilseeds (three times more than at the IPO), so it can wait for better prices instead of selling right after harvest. About 75% of its sales are exports.

In 2025 IMC sold 768,000 tonnes of crops (−30%, as 2024 included old stocks) and revenue fell 10% to $190.5 million. But prices rose — corn +31%, wheat +16%, sunflower +45% — so EBITDA grew 11% to $95.8 million and net profit rose 24% to $67.5 million. Debt is low: $17.9 million at the end of 2025, down from $23.3 million a year earlier.

The first half of 2026 was weaker: revenue $88.9 million (+6%), normalised EBITDA $50.2 million (−22%) and net profit $34.3 million (−33%). Fuel and energy costs more than doubled, and the company blamed lower corn and sunflower prices, higher logistics costs and the weaker hryvnia.

## The stock's story

IMC listed in Warsaw on 4 May 2011 at PLN 10.8 a share, raising $24.4 million when it farmed only 38,000 hectares. In 2022 part of its land in Chernihiv and Sumy regions was occupied by Russian troops, and the company has since rebuilt operations there. By early May 2026, the share price was PLN 36–37, giving a market value of about $353 million — roughly 2.8 times its value at debut.

The stock is also a dividend story: IMC paid €0.63 a share in June 2025, €0.35 in November 2025 and an interim €0.52 in May 2026 (subject to 15% Luxembourg withholding tax). In 2025 Kernel, another big Ukrainian agroholding, sought control of IMC with Petrov's Agrovalley, and filed for approval with Ukraine's competition authority, but the talks were suspended in May 2025 and IMC said it would not sell a stake. We found no tender offer or delisting plans announced since.


## Competitors

IMC competes with bigger Ukrainian agroholdings such as Kernel, Astarta and MHP for land, people and export logistics, and with farmers worldwide on price, since corn and wheat prices are global. Its advantages are low debt and its own storage.

## Main risks

- **War:** its farms in Chernihiv and Sumy regions lie near the Russian border, and part of its land was occupied in 2022; ports and railways can be hit too.
- **One crop:** with corn at about 98% of half-year sales, a bad corn price or harvest hits the whole company.
- **Costs and currency:** fuel, logistics and wages rose fast in 2026, and results in dollars depend on the hryvnia.
- **Small free float:** only about 24% of shares trade freely, so the price can move sharply.
- **Dividends are not fixed:** they depend on each year's profit and on decisions controlled by the majority owner, and can shrink in a bad year.

## What it means if you buy regularly

IMC has a long record of profits and cash dividends, but it is a small, mostly single-crop company in a country at war. Regular buying spreads your purchases over many prices, and the 10-year figure above includes the 2022 invasion. A stock like this is usually only a small part of a broader portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
