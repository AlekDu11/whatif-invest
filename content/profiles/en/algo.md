**Algorand** is a blockchain designed for fast, cheap payments and digital assets, created by MIT professor Silvio Micali, a winner of computer science's Turing Award. Its network went live in June 2019, and its coin, ALGO, is worth {{mcap:algo}} today ({{rank:algo}} among cryptocurrencies).

## How it works

Algorand uses "pure proof of stake". For every block, the network secretly and randomly picks a small committee of ALGO holders, with the chance of being picked growing with the amount staked. The committee proposes and votes on the block within a few seconds; because no one knows in advance who will be chosen, it is hard to bribe or attack them. Nothing is locked up: coins stay in the owner's wallet while they help secure the network. Algorand has its own standard for tokens (Algorand Standard Assets), smart contracts that developers can write in Python or TypeScript, and fees of a fraction of a cent. In December 2025 it added opt-in peer-to-peer networking, and the first post-quantum transaction on its main network used Falcon signatures.

## What the token is used for

ALGO pays transaction fees and is the stake that secures the network. Since 2025 node runners with enough ALGO online earn block rewards; the stake taking part grew from about 1 billion to 2 billion ALGO in just over a year, and the number of validators rose to nearly 2,000 (December 2025). ALGO holders also vote in governance, including xGov, which since October 2025 funds community grant proposals. Projects use the chain for stablecoins, tokenised assets and payments.

## Its story so far

Micali founded Algorand in 2017, and the main network launched in June 2019 with a token sale. ALGO traded above $3 in its first days and then fell more than 90% within months, partly because large amounts of new tokens came onto the market. It climbed back to about $2.50 in September 2021 and then slid through the 2022 bear market, despite a sponsorship deal with FIFA. In January 2026 the Algorand Foundation announced a return to the United States and a new board. In April 2026 ALGO jumped more than 40% after Google mentioned Algorand's real-world use of post-quantum cryptography, and in June 2026 the foundation set a goal of broad quantum resistance by the end of 2027.

## Alternatives

Algorand competes with other smart-contract chains: [Ethereum]({lang}/eth/), [Solana]({lang}/sol/), [Cardano]({lang}/ada/), [Avalanche]({lang}/avax/), [Aptos]({lang}/apt/) and [Sui]({lang}/sui/). For payments and tokenised assets its closest rivals are [Stellar]({lang}/xlm/), [Hedera]({lang}/hbar/) and [XRP]({lang}/xrp/).

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** ALGO has lost well over 90% from its record and has spent years far below it.
- **Weak use:** good technology has not brought as many users, apps and fees as bigger rivals have.
- **Competition:** Ethereum's layer 2s and fast chains like Solana attract most developers and money.
- **Concentration:** the Algorand Foundation and early backers have held large amounts of ALGO, and their sales can weigh on the price.
- **Regulation and execution:** token rules differ by country, and big upgrades such as the post-quantum move carry technical risk.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Algorand shows that strong science and fast transactions do not by themselves make a token's price rise. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your entry over time — more coins when the price is low, fewer when it is high — but it does not remove the risk that the price stays low for a long time. Many people keep smaller coins like ALGO to a small part of their savings. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
