**The Graph** is often called "the Google of blockchains": it organises raw blockchain data so that apps can find what they need in milliseconds. Wallets, exchanges and DeFi apps use it to show balances, trades and prices without running their own data servers. ## How it works

Reading data straight from a blockchain is slow and clumsy. Developers on The Graph describe the data they need in an open "subgraph", for example every trade on a decentralised exchange. **Indexers** — independent operators running servers — process those subgraphs and answer queries from apps, earning fees and newly issued GRT. **Curators** signal which subgraphs are worth indexing, and **delegators** lend their GRT to indexers they trust in exchange for part of the rewards. Indexers who serve wrong data can lose part of their stake ("slashing"). The Graph now supports dozens of blockchains, and since 2024 its rewards and payments run on Arbitrum, an Ethereum layer 2, to keep costs low. In 2025 the project began rolling out **Horizon**, an upgrade that lets the same staking system secure new kinds of data services.

## What the token is used for

GRT has three jobs. Indexers must stake it before they can work, which ties up a large share of the supply. Apps pay for queries in GRT (often converted automatically from stablecoins or card payments). And holders can delegate it to earn part of the rewards without running a server. A small share of query fees and staking taxes is burned, while new issuance of about 3% a year goes to indexers and delegators. Whether GRT gains in value depends on whether paid demand for data grows faster than new supply.

## Its story so far

The Graph was started in 2018 by Yaniv Tal, Brandon Ramirez and Jannis Pohlmann, whose company later became Edge & Node. For years it ran a free "hosted service" that many Ethereum apps relied on. The decentralised network and the GRT token went live on 17 December 2020, and in the crypto boom GRT rose to about $2.84 in February 2021. It then fell more than 90% over 2021 and 2022. In 2023 and 2024 the project moved its rewards to Arbitrum and shut down the hosted service in June 2024, pushing all users onto the paid network. The AI token rally of early 2024 lifted GRT for a while, but it later fell to new lows.

## Alternatives

The Graph competes with indexing companies and protocols such as Goldsky, SubQuery and Covalent, and with blockchain data APIs from Alchemy and QuickNode, which many developers find simpler. Some large apps simply run their own indexers. Among tokens linked to blockchain data, [Chainlink]({lang}/link/) and [Pyth Network]({lang}/pyth/) are often compared with GRT, although they deliver price feeds to smart contracts rather than search over past data.

## Main risks

- **New supply:** yearly issuance adds new GRT, and indexers often sell rewards to cover their server costs.
- **Weak link to usage:** query fees have long been small next to the rewards, so heavy use does not by itself lift the price.
- **Competition:** centralised data providers are fast and cheap, and apps can switch to them easily.
- **Dependence on one company:** Edge & Node and The Graph Foundation still steer much of the development.
- **Regulation:** rules for staking tokens and their rewards differ by country and can change.

## What it means if you buy regularly

GRT has spent most of its life far below its 2021 peak, so a regular buyer kept getting more tokens at lower prices — which pays off only if the price recovers. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads timing risk but does not remove the risk of losses. Many people keep tokens like this to a small part of their savings. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
