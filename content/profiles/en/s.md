**Sonic (S)** is a layer-1 blockchain — a network of its own, not an add-on to another chain — built for fast, cheap transactions and compatible with Ethereum apps and wallets. It is the successor of the Fantom network: Fantom's team renamed itself Sonic Labs in 2024 and FTM holders could swap their coins for S. ## How it works

Sonic runs the Ethereum Virtual Machine, so developers can move Ethereum apps there with few changes and users can connect with wallets such as MetaMask. Validators who lock up S confirm transactions within about a second, and fees are usually a fraction of a cent. A built-in bridge connects Sonic with Ethereum so tokens can move between the two networks.

## What the S token is used for

S pays the transaction fees on the network, is staked by validators and their delegators to secure it and earn rewards, and is used to vote on proposals. Under the FeeM system announced in November 2025, part of every fee goes back to the app that created it, part to validators, and the rest is burned, so more activity means more S taken out of supply. In February 2026 Sonic Labs said it would also build or buy core apps — for trading, credit and payments — to keep fees inside the ecosystem and fund S buybacks.

## Its story so far

Fantom was one of the faster Ethereum-compatible networks of the 2021 DeFi boom, linked to developer Andre Cronje. In 2024 its foundation rebranded to Sonic Labs, and the Sonic mainnet launched in December 2024 with an airdrop programme for users. S reached its highest price in early January 2025. The money locked in apps on Sonic (TVL) peaked above $1.1 billion in May 2025, then fell to about $26 million by June 2026, when the token traded about 97% below its peak. CEO Mitchell Demeter, appointed in September 2025, resigned in February 2026, and in June 2026 Andre Cronje, Michael Kong and David Richardson left the board; Matt Visser became CEO.

## Alternatives

Sonic competes with other fast, cheap networks for DeFi users: [Solana]({lang}/sol/), [Avalanche]({lang}/avax/) and Ethereum's layer-2 networks such as Base and Arbitrum. [Ethereum]({lang}/eth/) itself remains the main home for DeFi money.

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** S has fallen by more than 90% from its peak; small layer-1 tokens can fall further.
- **Leaving users:** if apps and money keep moving to bigger networks, fees — and the burn — shrink.
- **Leadership:** frequent changes at Sonic Labs make the long-term plan less predictable.
- **Competition:** many networks offer the same speed and low fees, so it is hard to stand out.
- **Bridges and hacks:** cross-chain bridges and DeFi apps are frequent targets for attackers.

## What it means if you buy regularly

S has history only since January 2025, and most of it has been a decline, so regular buying would have meant buying all the way down. Buying a fixed amount over time removes the stress of picking one moment, but it works only if the asset recovers — and for a network still fighting for users that is far from certain. Many people keep a token like this to a very small part of their crypto. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
