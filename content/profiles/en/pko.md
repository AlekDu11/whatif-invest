**PKO Bank Polski** is Poland's largest bank: the place where millions of Poles keep their salary, take a mortgage and pay with the IKO phone app. It earns mostly from the gap between the interest it charges on loans and the interest it pays on deposits. In the first nine months of 2026 it made PLN 8.22 billion of net profit, about 3% more than a year earlier, even though the government sharply raised taxes on banks.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Net profit: PLN 8.22 billion in January–September 2026 (+3.2%), with a return on equity of 19%.
> - Taxes bite: corporate income tax paid rose 76% to over PLN 4.7 billion after the bank tax rate went from 19% to 30% in 2026.
> - Generous payouts: from 2024 profit it paid PLN 5.48 per share, 74.87% of the bank's earnings.
> - The Polish state owns 29.43% of the shares, so politics always plays a part.

## How it makes money

Interest is the engine: net interest income was PLN 18.5 billion in nine months of 2026, and the net interest margin — the bank's average "spread" on its money — was 4.46% in Q3. Fees from cards, accounts, investment funds and insurance added PLN 4.19 billion (+8.2%). PKO lends to everyone: it holds 27.47% of Poland's mortgage market and about 22% of consumer loans, and corporate lending grew 17.6% in a year. It is also very efficient — costs ate only 31% of income.

## The numbers right now

At the end of September 2026 PKO had more than PLN 620 billion of assets and PLN 363 billion of loans to clients (+15.3% a year). Clients kept PLN 747 billion of savings with the group (+13.3%). Bad loans stayed low: the cost of risk was 0.27% of loans, a record low at the time, and impaired loans were 3.39% of the book. The core capital ratio (CET1) of 15.28% leaves room for dividends. Q3 alone brought PLN 2.93 billion of net profit. Besides income tax, the group paid PLN 1.1 billion of Poland's separate tax on bank assets.

For context, in full-year 2025 PKO earned a record PLN 10.68 billion, after setting aside PLN 4.37 billion for lawsuits over old mortgages in foreign currencies such as the Swiss franc.

## The stock's story

PKO shares started trading on the Warsaw Stock Exchange on 10 November 2004, when the state sold part of the bank. The 2008 crisis hit Warsaw hard — the WIG20 index of its largest companies lost almost 70%. Later the bank asset tax and foreign-currency mortgage lawsuits weighed on the bank, and PKO paid no dividend for 2014–2016, 2019, 2020 (a year hit by the cost of those lawsuits) and 2022. A Warsaw rally that began in October 2022 changed the picture: PKO is among the local companies whose shares more than doubled in the three years to August 2026, when the WIG20 finally beat its 2007 record.

## Competitors

PKO's main rivals are Bank Pekao, Santander Bank Polska (being renamed Erste Bank Polska after its sale to Austria's Erste Group), ING Bank Śląski and mBank, plus app-based players such as Revolut. Its strengths are size, low costs and the deposit base that comes with millions of everyday accounts.

## Main risks

- **Falling interest rates:** Poland's central bank has been cutting rates; lower rates squeeze the gap between loan and deposit interest.
- **Taxes and politics:** income tax for banks is set at 30% in 2026, 26% in 2027 and 23% from 2028, on top of the asset tax; the state as top shareholder can influence management and dividends.
- **Swiss-franc and other foreign-currency mortgages:** the legal cost was PLN 4.37 billion in 2025 — lower than in 2024, but still billions of zloty a year.
- **Recession:** a weaker Polish economy would push loan losses up from the record lows of 2026.
- **Currency:** for anyone counting in dollars or euros, a weaker zloty cuts returns.

## What it means if you buy regularly

PKO is a mature, dividend-paying bank, so a big part of the return comes from payouts rather than fast growth. But its history shows years with no dividend and deep falls when crises or new taxes hit. Regular buying spreads purchases across good and bad years and across zloty swings; it does not remove those risks. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
