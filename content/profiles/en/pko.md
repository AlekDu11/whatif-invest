**PKO Bank Polski** is Poland's largest bank: 20.5 million clients, PLN 583 billion in assets and a record PLN 10.7 billion in net profit in 2025. Its roots go back to 1919, when it was founded as the state Postal Savings Bank, and the Polish state still owns 29.4% of it.

## How it makes money

Most profit comes from the gap between what PKO pays on deposits and what it charges on loans — mortgages, consumer credit and business lending. Fees from cards, accounts, investment funds and insurance add a second, steadier stream. Its IKO mobile app is one of the most widely used banking apps in Poland.

In 2025 the group's net profit rose 14.8% to PLN 10.7 billion, and return on equity — profit as a share of shareholders' money — was 19.5%. That was after setting aside PLN 4.4 billion for lawsuits over old mortgages in Swiss francs.

## Why it matters

PKO is one of the heaviest stocks in the WIG20 index of Warsaw's largest companies and a bellwether for the Polish economy, one of the EU's fastest-growing large economies. It pays out much of its profit: for 2025 the board proposed PLN 6.14 per share, about 75% of the bank's profit. One surprising fact: PKO has 20.5 million clients in a country of about 37 million people.

## Main risks

When the central bank cuts interest rates, the bank's lending margin shrinks. Poland taxes bank assets and has raised income tax on banks from 2026, and governments can add new burdens. Swiss-franc mortgage cases still cost billions. State control means politics can influence management and dividends. A recession would raise loan losses.
