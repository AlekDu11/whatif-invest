**Alphabet** is the parent company of Google — the search engine, YouTube, Android, Gmail, Chrome and the Gemini AI assistant that billions of people use every day. Larry Page and Sergey Brin founded Google in 1998; Alphabet was created as its holding company in 2015.

## How it makes money

Most of the money still comes from ads. Companies pay to appear next to search results, on YouTube and across millions of partner sites. In the third quarter of 2026, search ads alone brought in $63.3 billion and YouTube ads $11.2 billion, out of total revenue of $127.6 billion (+25% in a year). The fastest-growing piece is Google Cloud, which rents computing power and AI models to businesses: its revenue more than doubled to $30.7 billion in that quarter.

## Why it matters

Alphabet is one of the few companies that builds every layer of AI itself — its own chips (TPUs), data centres, the Gemini models and products to put them in. Revenue passed $400 billion for the first time in 2025. To keep up, it planned $175–185 billion of capital spending for 2026, nearly double 2025. One surprising fact: the Gemini app went from launch to 1 billion users, which CEO Sundar Pichai called the fastest-growing product in Google's history.

## Main risks

US courts have ruled that Google illegally held monopolies in search and in parts of online advertising, and remedies or fines in the US and Europe could change how it earns money. AI chatbots could pull people away from classic search, the core of its profits. Spending on data centres is enormous, and if AI demand cools, those investments may pay off slowly. Ad revenue also falls when the economy weakens. Buying a fixed amount regularly smooths price swings but does not remove the risk.
