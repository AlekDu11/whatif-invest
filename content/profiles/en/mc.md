**LVMH** is the world's largest luxury group: Louis Vuitton bags, Dior dresses, Tiffany rings, Moët champagne and Sephora shops all belong to it — more than 75 brands that brought in €80.8 billion in 2025. The name stands for Moët Hennessy Louis Vuitton, created by a merger in 1987 and led by Bernard Arnault.

## How it makes money

LVMH sells expensive things at high margins through more than 6,280 stores. Fashion & Leather Goods (Louis Vuitton, Dior, Celine, Loewe) brought €37.8 billion of revenue and €13.2 billion of operating profit in 2025 — about three-quarters of the group's €17.8 billion. Selective Retailing (Sephora, DFS duty-free) added €18.3 billion, Watches & Jewelry (Tiffany, Bulgari, TAG Heuer) €10.5 billion, Perfumes & Cosmetics €8.2 billion and Wines & Spirits (Moët, Hennessy) €5.4 billion.

## Why it matters

LVMH is one of Europe's most valuable companies and a bellwether for wealthy shoppers worldwide, especially in China and the US. Its brands rarely discount, so pricing power is the core of the story. One surprising fact: the group employs more than 211,000 people — more than the population of many European cities.

## Main risks

Luxury demand is cooling: 2025 revenue fell 5% (1% on a like-for-like basis) and net profit dropped 13% to €10.9 billion. Wines & Spirits profit fell 25% as cognac sales weakened. Chinese shoppers, US tariffs and a strong euro can all hit sales. Bernard Arnault was born in 1949, so succession is a long-term question. Buying a fixed amount regularly smooths price swings but does not remove the risk.
