**Toyota** is the world's best-selling carmaker: it sold 10.5 million Toyota and Lexus vehicles in its last fiscal year — about one in every nine new cars on the planet. The company was founded in 1937 by Kiichiro Toyoda, the son of a loom inventor.

## How it makes money

Most revenue comes from selling cars under the Toyota and Lexus brands, plus trucks from Hino and small cars from Daihatsu. A large finance arm lends money to buyers and dealers, and parts and servicing add steady income. In the year to March 2026 Toyota had ¥50.7 trillion in revenue (up 5.5%) and ¥3.76 trillion in operating profit (down 21.5%). US tariffs alone cost it ¥1.38 trillion.

## Why it matters

Toyota bet on hybrids long before electric cars were fashionable: the Prius of 1997 was the first mass-produced hybrid. That bet pays today — electrified models topped 5 million sales for the first time, including 4.62 million hybrids and 243,000 fully electric cars. Its "just-in-time" production system, which keeps only the parts needed right now, is studied by factories worldwide. One surprising fact: Toyota is one of Japan's most valuable companies, and its share is a core holding for millions of Japanese savers.

## Main risks

Toyota expects operating profit to fall again, to about ¥3 trillion, in the year to March 2027. Tariffs and trade rules can change fast, and North America is a key market. A stronger yen shrinks the value of overseas profits. Cheap Chinese electric cars are winning buyers in Asia and beyond, and Toyota must prove it can compete on batteries and software. Car sales also fall in recessions. Buying a fixed amount regularly smooths price swings but does not remove the risk.
