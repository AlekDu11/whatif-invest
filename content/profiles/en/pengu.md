**PENGU** is the token of Pudgy Penguins, a collection of cartoon-penguin NFTs that grew into a consumer brand with plush toys in Walmart and Target stores and billions of social media views. It launched on the Solana blockchain in December 2024 and works mostly as a memecoin — a token whose value comes from community and culture rather than cash flows. ## How it works

PENGU is a standard token on Solana; at launch the team said it would also expand to Ethereum and to Abstract, a consumer-focused Ethereum layer-2 network built by Igloo. Anyone can hold it in a crypto wallet or buy it on exchanges. There is no mining or staking design behind it: the token exists as a fixed supply, and the Pudgy Penguins company uses it in campaigns, games and partnerships.

## Why people think it has value

The value is the brand. Pudgy Penguins started in 2021 as an NFT collection on Ethereum and, under Igloo Inc. and its CEO Luca Netz, turned into toys, animated content and huge social reach. In December 2024 the NFT collection was the second-largest by market value, worth about $1.2 billion according to CoinGecko. Buyers of PENGU are betting that the penguin brand keeps growing and that the token stays its main way for fans to take part — not on profits or dividends, which the token does not pay.

## Its story so far

PENGU went live on 17 December 2024. About 25.9% of the supply went to holders of Pudgy Penguins, Lil Pudgys and Rogs NFTs, about 24.1% to millions of wallets in other crypto communities, 12.35% to liquidity pools and the rest to the team, the company, public goods and expansion. The claim window, first planned for 88 days, was cut short in early February 2025 after about 85% had been claimed; unclaimed tokens were to be burned. The price peaked in its first days, then fell sharply in early 2025 as airdrop recipients sold, before rebounding in mid-2025 and sliding again after that.

## Alternatives

PENGU competes for the same money as other big memecoins: [Dogecoin]({lang}/doge/), [Shiba Inu]({lang}/shib/) and [Pepe]({lang}/pepe/), and on Solana [Bonk]({lang}/bonk/) and [dogwifhat]({lang}/wif/). Unlike most of them, it has a company and real products behind the brand — which helps, but also ties the token to one firm's decisions.

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** memecoins can lose 80–90% of their value, as PENGU's first months showed.
- **Token unlocks:** the team and company allocations (about 29%) unlock gradually after the one-year lock, adding supply.
- **Concentration:** large holders, the company and exchanges hold a big share, so their selling can move the price.
- **Brand risk:** the token depends on the popularity of one brand and one company; a scandal or fading interest would hurt it.
- **Regulation:** rules for memecoins and NFT-linked tokens differ by country and may change.

## What it means if you buy regularly

PENGU has a short and very volatile history, so a "$10 a day" result covers at most one cycle. Buying small, fixed amounts over time avoids putting everything in at a peak, but it cannot turn a meme into a cash-producing asset. Many people treat memecoins as a small, high-risk slice they can afford to lose. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
