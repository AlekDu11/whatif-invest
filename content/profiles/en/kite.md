**Kite** is a blockchain built so that AI agents — programs that act on their own, such as a bot that books a trip or buys data — can prove who they are, follow spending rules set by a human and pay each other in stablecoins. ## How it works

Kite is a layer-1 blockchain that works with Ethereum tools (EVM-compatible). Each AI agent can get a "Kite Passport" — a verifiable identity plus rules such as "spend at most $50 a day". Payments go through fast payment channels with fees in stablecoins, so an agent can pay tiny amounts per request for an API, data or a model. Kite supports the x402 payment standard and works with agent protocols such as Google's A2A and Anthropic's MCP. Developers publish services in an Agentic App Store, grouped into "modules".

## What the token is used for

KITE holders stake their tokens to secure the network: validators and delegators each choose a module to back, which ties rewards to how useful that module is. Holders also vote on upgrades, incentives and module rules. The protocol takes a small commission on AI service payments, and part of the token supply rewards builders and users. The bet behind KITE is that AI agents will one day make billions of small payments and need a network made for them.

## Its story so far

Kite's team came from AI and data companies such as Databricks, Uber and Salesforce, with research roots at UC Berkeley. In 2025 it raised $35 million; the Series A was led by PayPal Ventures and General Catalyst, and Coinbase Ventures later joined as a strategic investor. Before launch, its test network recorded more than 500 million transactions by November 2025. KITE was offered in a Binance Launchpool and began trading on Binance and other exchanges in early November 2025, with 1.8 billion tokens in circulation. The team planned the public main network for the first quarter of 2026.

## Main risks

- **Unproven demand:** paying AI agents is a new idea; real usage may grow slowly or never reach the scale people expect.
- **Token unlocks:** most of the 10 billion KITE was not in circulation at launch; tokens released to investors, the team and the ecosystem can weigh on the price.
- **Big swings:** young AI tokens often move 50% or more in weeks.
- **Competition:** large blockchains and payment companies are building their own tools for AI agents.
- **Regulation and security:** rules for stablecoin payments are still changing, and a bug or hack could hit the network.
