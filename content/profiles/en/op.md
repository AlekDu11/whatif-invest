**Optimism** is a "layer 2" network on top of Ethereum: it makes transactions faster and much cheaper while relying on Ethereum for security. Its free software, the **OP Stack**, also powers other networks, including Coinbase's Base, which together form the **Superchain**. ## How it works

Optimism is an **optimistic rollup**: it executes transactions on its own chain and posts compressed batches of data back to Ethereum. A **sequencer** run by OP Labs orders transactions and confirms them within seconds. Results are assumed correct ("optimistic") unless someone proves otherwise: since June 2024 anyone can submit a **fault proof**, and disputes are settled on Ethereum. That is why withdrawals to Ethereum take about a week. Because the OP Stack is open source, other companies launch their own chains with it — Base (Coinbase), Unichain (Uniswap), World Chain, Ink (Kraken), Soneium (Sony) — and many of them share part of their fee income with the Optimism Collective.

## What the token is used for

OP is mainly a governance token. In the **Token House**, OP holders or their delegates vote on upgrades, grants and the treasury; a second chamber, the **Citizens' House**, decides on rewards for public goods. OP is not needed to use the network: gas is paid in ETH. Its link to usage comes from the Superchain: in January 2026 holders approved a plan to spend 50% of the revenue that chains pay to the Collective on buying OP back.

## Its story so far

Optimism opened its mainnet to selected apps in January 2021 and to everyone in December 2021. The OP token launched on 31 May 2022 with an airdrop to users; the launch was rocky, and days later market maker Wintermute lost 20 million OP sent to a wrong address, most of which was later returned. The **Bedrock** upgrade in June 2023 cut costs, and in August 2023 Coinbase launched Base on the OP Stack, which soon became the busiest layer 2. OP reached its peak of about $4.84 in March 2024, then fell steeply over 2024 and 2025. In January 2026 holders approved the buyback plan.

## Alternatives

Optimism competes with other Ethereum layer 2 networks: [Arbitrum]({lang}/arb/), the largest by value locked; zero-knowledge rollups such as [ZKsync]({lang}/zk/) and [Starknet]({lang}/strk/); plus [Mantle]({lang}/mnt/) and [POL (ex-MATIC)]({lang}/pol/). Base, built on Optimism's own technology, has no token and is both a partner and a rival. [Ethereum]({lang}/eth/) itself is an alternative too, as its upgrades keep making it cheaper.

## Main risks

- **New supply:** monthly unlocks for the team and investors continue, and governance may mint up to 2% more a year.
- **Dependence on Base:** a large share of Superchain revenue comes from Coinbase's chain; if Base left or paid less, OP would lose much of its income.
- **Centralised sequencer:** one operator orders transactions on OP Mainnet; if it fails, users fall back on slower routes.
- **Competition:** Arbitrum and zero-knowledge rollups fight for the same users, apps and fees.
- **Regulation:** rules for tokens, buybacks and DAOs differ by country and can change.

## What it means if you buy regularly

OP has spent most of the last years falling from its 2024 peak, so a regular buyer kept getting more tokens at lower prices — which helps only if the price recovers. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads timing risk but does not remove the risk of losses. Many people keep tokens like this to a small part of their savings. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
