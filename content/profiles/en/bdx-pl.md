**Budimex** is Poland's largest construction company: it builds motorways, railway lines, tunnels, bridges, power substations, hospitals and offices, mostly for the Polish state. Spanish infrastructure group Ferrovial has owned the majority (50.14%) since 2000, and the rest trades on the Warsaw Stock Exchange. Its customers are public agencies such as the roads authority GDDKiA, rail manager PKP PLK, grid operator PSE and CPK, the company building Poland's new central airport.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: PLN 2.83 billion in Q3 2026 (+3.4% a year); net profit PLN 182.4 million (+7%).
> - Order book: PLN 17.3 billion at the end of September — almost two years of work at the 2025 revenue pace of PLN 9.44 billion.
> - Net cash of nearly PLN 1.8 billion, down from PLN 2.6 billion at the end of 2025.
> - Dividend: 32.42 zł a share paid in June 2026, about 4.6% at a price near 700 zł.

## How it makes money

Budimex wins public tenders, signs fixed-price contracts and earns the gap between the contract price and its costs. Most revenue comes from road, rail and airport infrastructure; the rest from buildings (housing, energy, industrial and environmental projects), facility management and waste management. Work is mainly in Poland, with selected projects in Slovakia, the Czech Republic and Latvia. Recent wins show the mix: a PLN 353 million A2 motorway widening near Łódź, a PLN 195 million PSE substation in Ełk, the foundations of the CPK airport terminal, and a place in consortia for a waste-to-energy plant in Łódź and the D35 motorway in the Czech Republic.

## The numbers right now

In the quarter to 30 September 2026 Budimex earned PLN 2.83 billion in revenue, PLN 206.9 million of operating profit (flat year on year) and PLN 182.4 million of net profit. For the first nine months revenue was PLN 7.06 billion (about PLN 6.4 billion a year earlier) and net profit PLN 432.1 million, slightly below the PLN 441 million of 2025. New contracts signed in January–September topped PLN 6.4 billion, about 64% more than a year before, and the company has a further PLN 3.5 billion where its bid is best but not yet signed. In October it adopted a strategy to 2030: revenue growth above 8% a year and an average operating margin above 6%. That margin is lower than the 9.5% it made in 2025, a sign management expects tougher pricing.

## The stock's story

Budimex has paid a dividend every year since 2009, and in 2021 it paid an extra 38.37 zł after selling its property developer, Budimex Nieruchomości. The shares hit a record in spring 2024, then fell about 15% within two months as analysts called them too expensive — at the time they traded at about a 70% premium to global peers on price-to-earnings. They recovered as profit grew: by October 2026 the price was around 700 zł, up about 33% in a year, after closing at 751 zł in August.

## Competitors

In Poland Budimex competes with local listed builders such as Mirbud, Mostostal Warszawa, Erbud and rail specialist Torpol, and with European giants Strabag and Porr. Big tenders also attract Chinese, Turkish and other foreign contractors bidding low prices.

## Main risks

- **Margin pressure:** fixed-price contracts mean that rising wages and material costs can eat profit; the 2030 target already assumes lower margins than in 2025.
- **One main customer:** most work comes from the Polish state, so delays to the CPK, rail or road programmes, or to EU funding, hit the order book.
- **Price competition:** foreign bidders can undercut on large tenders.
- **Shrinking cash pile:** net cash fell from PLN 2.6 billion to PLN 1.8 billion in nine months.
- **Valuation and liquidity:** with Ferrovial holding half the shares, fewer shares trade freely, and the stock has often been priced at a premium.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Budimex rises and falls with Poland's public investment cycle: strong years when EU-funded projects flow, weak ones when tenders dry up. Regular buying spreads purchases across those cycles and collects a sizeable dividend along the way. For anyone who does not earn in zlotys, the exchange rate adds another layer: a stronger or weaker zloty can change the result as much as the share price. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
