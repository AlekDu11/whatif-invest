**LPP** is a Polish clothing company from Gdańsk that owns five high-street brands: Sinsay, Reserved, Cropp, House and Mohito. It designs the clothes, has them made by factories mostly in Asia and sells them in its own shops and online. Founded in 1989, it now runs more than 4,000 stores in 47 markets, which makes it one of the largest fashion retailers in Central and Eastern Europe.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: 6.57 billion zł in May–July 2026, +18.4% a year; net profit 768 million zł.
> - Sinsay, the cheapest brand, has almost 2,700 of LPP's stores and drives most of the expansion.
> - The 2026 plan: 26–27 billion zł of sales and a net margin of 10–11%.
> - On 15 March 2024 the shares fell almost 36% in one day after a short seller's report about Russia.

## How it makes money

LPP earns the gap between factory cost and shelf price. Sinsay is the low-price brand for families and young people and the fastest-growing one: in May–July 2026 LPP opened 230 stores, and 212 of them were Sinsay. Reserved is the main mid-priced brand. Online sales made up 28% of the total in 2025, and at Sinsay most online orders come through the mobile app.

LPP's financial year runs from February to January, so "2025" means February 2025 to January 2026. That year revenue passed 23 billion zł (+19%), net profit was 2.4 billion zł (+36%) and the gross margin (the share of each sale left after the cost of the clothes) was 55.6%.

## The numbers right now

In the second quarter of 2026 (May–July) revenue rose 18.4% to 6.57 billion zł. Store sales grew 18.6% and online sales 16.4%, back to double digits after a 2025 warehouse fire in Romania. Gross margin rose 3.7 points to 57.7%, EBITDA (profit before interest, tax and depreciation) climbed 42% to 1.7 billion zł and net profit was 768 million zł, up 64.5% (47.8% excluding a one-off Russia-related write-off a year earlier).

After these results LPP kept its sales target of 26–27 billion zł and investment plan of 2.5 billion zł for 2026, and raised its margin forecasts: gross margin 56.5–57%, EBITDA margin 24–25%, net margin 10–11%. For 2025 the board recommended a dividend of 900 zł per share, the fifteenth year in a row of sharing profit.

## The stock's story

LPP's long climb on the Warsaw Stock Exchange has not been smooth. In 2022, after Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine, it sold its Russian business, saying the buyer was a Chinese consortium paying about $382 million. In March 2024 the US short seller Hindenburg Research claimed the exit was a sham and that LPP still profited from Russia. On 15 March 2024 the shares fell almost 36%, wiping about 11 billion zł off its value. LPP called the report false or manipulated and notified prosecutors. Fast growth at Sinsay then pushed the stock to a record high in March 2026; over the past 52 weeks it traded between 15,405 zł and 29,000 zł per share.

## Competitors

LPP fights for the same shoppers as Inditex (Zara, Pull&Bear), H&M and Primark, and Sinsay competes on price with Pepco and with Asian online sellers such as Shein and Temu. Its edge is a fast-growing store network and new markets in Eastern Europe and Central Asia.

## Main risks

- **Cheap online rivals:** Shein and Temu sell clothes at very low prices straight from China and could squeeze Sinsay.
- **Consumer spending:** clothes are easy to skip when money is tight.
- **Reputation:** the 2024 report showed how fast questions about Russia can hit the share price.
- **Fast expansion:** opening hundreds of stores a quarter needs heavy investment; if new shops sell less, profits suffer.
## What it means if you buy regularly

LPP's shares have had long rises and sudden drops, such as the crash of March 2024. Regular buying spreads purchases over such moments instead of betting on one date. One practical point: a single share costs well over 15,000 zł, so small regular amounts need a broker with fractional shares, or saving up first. A retailer can also fall out of fashion, so many people hold it as only part of a broader portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
