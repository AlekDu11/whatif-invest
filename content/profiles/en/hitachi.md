**Hitachi** is one of Japan's oldest industrial groups, but today it is mostly a seller of the equipment and software that keep power grids, railways, factories and data centres running. Its biggest growth engine is Hitachi Energy, which makes transformers and grid systems that are in short supply as AI data centres and renewable power need more electricity. Its shares trade in Tokyo under the code 6501.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: ¥10.59 trillion in fiscal 2025 (to 31 March 2026), +8% on the year; net income ¥802.4 billion, up from ¥615.7 billion.
> - Adjusted EBITA margin rose to 12.4% in fiscal 2025; digital "Lumada" businesses made about 40% of revenue.
> - In the April–September 2026 half, revenue grew 17% to ¥5.61 trillion and Lumada reached 44% of sales.
> - In October 2026 Hitachi raised its fiscal 2026 forecast to ¥12.0 trillion of revenue and ¥920 billion of net income.

## How it makes money

Hitachi has four business sectors. **Digital Systems & Services** sells IT systems and consulting to Japanese banks, governments and companies, and runs GlobalLogic, the US software-engineering firm it bought in 2021 (in October 2026 merged with Hitachi Digital Services into "Hitachi Digital"). **Energy** — mainly Hitachi Energy, the former ABB power-grid business — builds transformers, high-voltage lines and grid control systems. **Mobility** is Hitachi Rail: trains, signalling and rail operations software. **Connective Industries** covers elevators and escalators, industrial equipment, measuring and analysis tools from Hitachi High-Tech and other machines. Across all of them Hitachi pushes "Lumada": selling software, data and maintenance services on top of the equipment, which repeats and earns higher margins. In fiscal 2025 Lumada made about 40% of revenue at a 16% adjusted EBITA margin.

In the July–September 2026 quarter revenue was ¥2.90 trillion (+15%) and adjusted EBITA ¥376.1 billion, a 12.9% margin — both records for a second quarter. Net income for the quarter fell 19% to ¥227.1 billion only because a year earlier Hitachi had booked a gain on selling its air-conditioning joint venture. For the year to March 2027 Hitachi now expects revenue of ¥12.0 trillion, adjusted EBITA of ¥1.56 trillion (about 13%) and net income of ¥920 billion. It bought back ¥290 billion of its own shares in the first half and raised the interim dividend by 36%.

## The stock's story

Hitachi began in 1910, when engineer Namihei Odaira built a 5-horsepower electric motor in the repair shop of a copper mine; the company became independent in 1920. For decades it made almost everything, from power plants to TVs and hard drives. That breadth became a burden: in the year to March 2009 Hitachi lost ¥787.3 billion, at the time the biggest loss ever by a Japanese manufacturer, and in dollar terms our data show the shares about 80% below their 2000 level in early 2009. Under Takashi Kawamura, who took over in 2009, and his successors, Hitachi sold or merged away businesses — including its 22 listed subsidiaries such as Hitachi Chemical and Hitachi Metals — for about ¥2.5 trillion, and bought what it wanted to keep: ABB's power-grid business in 2020 (about ¥722 billion), GlobalLogic in 2021 ($9.6 billion) and Thales's rail-signalling unit, completed in 2024. Investors rewarded the change: the shares roughly doubled in dollar terms between January 2024 and January 2026 as demand for grid equipment surged. Toshiaki Tokunaga became CEO in April 2025.

## Competitors

In power grids Hitachi Energy competes with Siemens Energy, GE Vernova, Schneider Electric and Chinese makers. In rail it faces Siemens Mobility, Alstom and China's CRRC. In IT services its rivals include Fujitsu, NEC, NTT Data, Accenture and IBM, and in elevators Otis, Kone, Schindler and Mitsubishi Electric.

## Main risks

- **The grid boom cools:** much of the recent growth and the share-price rise depends on strong spending on power networks and data centres.
- **Big projects:** grid and rail contracts are large and long, and delays or cost overruns can turn them into losses.
- **Currency:** a big part of profit is earned abroad, so a stronger yen lowers results in yen.
- **Japan IT:** the domestic IT business depends on Japanese companies and government budgets.
- **A high price:** after the 2024–2026 rally a lot of growth is already expected in the share price.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Hitachi has changed from a sprawling conglomerate into a focused infrastructure and software group, but the stock has fallen by most of its value before and can swing hard with the economy and the yen. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you get more shares when the price is low and fewer when it is high, without trying to time the market.
