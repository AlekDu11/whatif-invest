**Fast Retailing** is the Japanese company behind **Uniqlo** — simple, well-made basics such as Heattech underwear, fleece, down jackets and T-shirts sold in more than 3,500 stores worldwide. It also owns the cheaper brand GU and fashion labels such as Theory. Its shares trade in Tokyo under the code 9983 and are the biggest weight in the Nikkei 225 index; founder Tadashi Yanai still runs the company.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: ¥3.96 trillion in fiscal 2026 (to 31 August), +16.6% on the year — the fifth record year in a row.
> - Operating profit ¥743.1 billion (+31.7%); net income ¥542.5 billion (+25.3%).
> - Uniqlo outside Japan brought in ¥2.41 trillion, about 61% of sales; North America and Europe together passed Greater China for the first time.
> - The dividend rose to ¥850 per share from ¥500; for fiscal 2027 the company plans ¥900 and revenue of ¥4.45 trillion.

## How it makes money

Fast Retailing designs its clothes, has them made by partner factories (mostly in Asia) and sells them in its own stores and online, so it keeps the whole margin instead of sharing it with department stores. Uniqlo's trick is to sell a small range of basics in huge volumes, improve the fabric every year and keep prices steady. In fiscal 2026 Uniqlo International earned ¥2.41 trillion of revenue (+26.2%), Uniqlo Japan ¥1.08 trillion (+5.7%), GU ¥336.7 billion (+1.8%) and Global Brands — Theory, PLST, Comptoir des Cotonniers — ¥128.5 billion (−2.3%). Abroad, Greater China made about ¥724 billion, Europe ¥512.6 billion (+38.7%) and North America ¥364.9 billion (+34.6%).

## The stock's story

The company began in 1949 as a small men's clothing shop run by Yanai's father in Ube, western Japan. Tadashi Yanai opened the first Uniqlo store in Hiroshima in 1984, renamed the company Fast Retailing in 1991 and took it public in 1994. A nationwide fleece craze around 2000 made Uniqlo famous — and when it faded, the shares lost about 70% of their value in dollar terms between 2001 and 2002. Growth then came from abroad: London in 2001, Shanghai in 2002, New York's SoHo flagship in 2006. Heattech (2003) and the GU brand (2006) followed. The stock fell again from its 2015 peak into 2016 and from early 2021 into 2022, but each time recovered; in March 2024 it split each share into three, and in 2026 it set new records as Western sales boomed. Yanai has said a ¥10 trillion revenue goal is "within sight".

## Competitors

Fast Retailing's main rivals are Inditex (Zara) and H&M — after fiscal 2026 it is close to overtaking H&M as the world's second-largest clothing retailer by sales. It also competes with Gap, with cheap online sellers such as Shein and Temu, with sports brands for everyday wear, and in Japan with Shimamura and Muji.

## Main risks

- **China:** Greater China is still a big market where sales fell in fiscal 2025 and local brands are strong; the company closed dozens of stores there.
- **Currency:** most profit is earned abroad, so a stronger yen lowers the yen value of those sales.
- **Weather and fashion:** a warm winter hurts Heattech and down sales; GU missed its own targets in fiscal 2026.
- **Supply chain and trade:** clothes are made mainly in Asia, so tariffs and questions about where cotton comes from can hit sales and costs.
- **The founder and the price:** Yanai, born in 1949, still leads the company, and after the 2026 rally a lot of growth is already expected in the share price.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Fast Retailing is a fast-growing, very profitable retailer, but clothing sales swing with consumer spending, weather and China, and the stock has fallen by half or more before.
