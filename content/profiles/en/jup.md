**Jupiter** is the app most people on Solana use to swap one token for another: it searches many exchanges at once and sends your trade along the cheapest route. It grew into a "DeFi superapp" with perpetual futures, lending and a launchpad, and its token JUP is worth {{mcap:jup}} today ({{rank:jup}} among cryptocurrencies). People follow it because a large share of Solana's trading passes through it.

> **Key takeaways**
> - JUP launched on 31 January 2024 with a planned total of 10 billion tokens; 3 billion of them were burned in January 2025.
> - Since February 2025 half of Jupiter's protocol fees have gone to buying JUP on the market.
> - About 40% of the supply was set aside for community airdrops, known as "Jupuary".

## How it works

When you swap on Jupiter, its router checks prices across Solana's decentralized exchanges — such as Raydium, Orca and Meteora — and can split one order between several of them to get a better rate. You keep your coins in your own wallet the whole time. On top of swaps Jupiter offers limit orders, regular automatic purchases, perpetual futures with leverage, lending and borrowing, and a launchpad for new tokens. In January 2025 it also bought a majority stake in the memecoin app Moonshot. Jupiter earns fees from these products, mainly from perpetual trading.

## What the token is for

JUP is mainly a governance token: holders who stake it can vote in the Jupiter DAO on things like airdrops and the use of the treasury, and voters have received rewards. It does not give a legal claim on the company's profits. Its link to the business comes from the buyback: since February 2025 Jupiter has used 50% of its protocol fees to buy JUP and lock it in a long-term reserve called the "Litterbox". By October 2026 more than 250 million JUP had been repurchased, and at the end of 2025 the DAO voted to burn about 134 million of them.

## Its story so far

Jupiter started in 2021 as a swap aggregator built by a pseudonymous founder known as "Meow". On 31 January 2024 it launched JUP with one of the largest airdrops in crypto: about 1 billion tokens went to nearly a million Solana wallets. At its "Catstanbul" event in January 2025 the team announced a burn of 3 billion JUP, cutting the planned supply by 30%, and the 50% fee buyback. In June 2025 Jupiter paused DAO voting, citing a breakdown in trust; governance resumed in early 2026, and in February 2026 voters backed a plan to cut the 2026 Jupuary airdrop and stop new emissions.

## Alternatives

On Solana, Jupiter competes with exchanges such as [Raydium]({lang}/ray/) and [Orca]({lang}/orca/) — though it also routes trades through them — and with trading bots and wallets that have built-in swaps. For perpetual futures its main rival is [Hyperliquid]({lang}/hype/). On other blockchains the same role is played by aggregators such as 1inch and by [Uniswap]({lang}/uni/). Its fortunes are closely tied to [Solana]({lang}/sol/) itself.

## Main risks

- **Token unlocks:** team and investor tokens have been released over several years; plans can change by DAO vote, as they did in 2025–2026.
- **Dependence on Solana:** if trading on Solana slows down, Jupiter's fees and buybacks shrink too.
- **Competition:** swaps and perps are easy to copy, and fees can be pushed down by rivals.
- **Governance:** JUP gives no claim on profits, and the 2025 pause showed that the rules can change quickly.
- **Technology and leverage:** bugs, hacks or a crash in leveraged perpetual markets can cause losses.

## What it means if you buy regularly

JUP has traded since early 2024 and has had sharp rises and long falls. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more tokens when the price is low and fewer when it is high, without trying to time the market — but it does not protect you from losses if the project loses users. Many people keep tokens like this to a small part of their savings. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
