**Airbnb** is the app where you book someone else's flat, house or room instead of a hotel. It owns none of those homes: hosts list them, guests pay through the app, and Airbnb keeps a fee on every booking. Founded in 2007 in San Francisco, it listed on Nasdaq in December 2020, in the middle of the pandemic.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: $4.87 billion in Q3 2026, +19% a year, the fastest growth in more than three years.
> - Guests booked 155 million nights and seats in the quarter (+16%), worth $27.7 billion in total.
> - Net income was $1.89 billion, and the board approved a new buyback of up to $12 billion.
> - The stock's record close is $216.84 (February 2021); by December 2022 it was down 62% from there.

## How it makes money

Airbnb is a marketplace. When you book a stay, the full price you pay (the gross booking value) passes through Airbnb, and the company keeps a service fee from guests, hosts or both. In Q3 2026 guests spent $27.7 billion on the platform and Airbnb's revenue was $4.87 billion, so it kept roughly 18 cents of every dollar booked. Homes are still almost the whole business: hotels are only a single-digit percentage of nights. The company is also adding paid services and experiences (for example, a chef or a local tour) to sell more than just a bed.

Summer is the peak season; for October–December 2026 Airbnb expects a much smaller $3.22–3.28 billion.

## The numbers right now

In the quarter to 30 September 2026, revenue rose 19% to $4.87 billion. Nights and seats booked grew 16% to 155 million, up from 10% growth in the previous quarter. Net income reached $1.89 billion and free cash flow (cash left after running the business and investing) was about $1.4 billion; over the past 12 months free cash flow was $4.9 billion.

New markets helped: nights booked by guests from India rose 60%. AI now answers about 40% of support questions without a human. For full-year 2026 management now expects revenue growth in the high teens and an adjusted EBITDA margin of about 36%. Airbnb bought back $0.7 billion of its own shares in Q3 and approved a new plan of up to $12 billion.

## The stock's story

Airbnb priced its IPO at $68 a share in December 2020, and the stock more than doubled on day one, valuing the company at about $100 billion. It hit a record close of $216.84 on 11 February 2021, as investors bet on a fast travel comeback. Then rising interest rates hit growth stocks, and by 28 December 2022 the shares were 62% below the peak. For three more years the stock mostly moved sideways as growth slowed. It jumped more than 11% on 6 November 2026, after the company showed growth speeding up again, but it is still below the 2021 record.

## Competitors

The main rival is Booking.com (Booking Holdings), which also lists many private homes, followed by Vrbo from Expedia Group. Hotels compete too, and so do Google search and new AI travel assistants. Airbnb's strengths are its brand (people say "an Airbnb" for any holiday rental), millions of unique listings and an app people open directly.

## Main risks

- **City rules:** many cities limit or tax short-term rentals to protect housing for locals, which can shrink supply in popular places.
- **Travel cycle:** when people lose jobs or prices rise, holidays are among the first things they cut.
- **Competition and AI search:** if travellers start booking through AI assistants or Booking.com, Airbnb may have to pay more for marketing.
- **Slower growth again:** the speed-up in 2026 still has to last; the Q4 outlook is 16–18% revenue growth, and part of it comes from a weaker dollar.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Airbnb is cash-rich, but its stock has been volatile: it lost 62% from the 2021 peak and took years to win back part of that. Regular buying spreads purchases across good and bad months, so someone who kept buying through 2022 got many shares cheaply. Still, one company can stay below its peak for a long time, and many people hold a stock like this as only one part of a broader portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
