**XDC Network** is a blockchain built for business, above all for trade finance: the letters of credit, invoices and bills of lading that move trillions of dollars of goods around the world and still often travel on paper. Its mainnet went live in 2019, and its coin **XDC** is worth {{mcap:xdc}} today ({{rank:xdc}} among cryptocurrencies).

> - The network is EVM-compatible, so apps written for Ethereum can run on it with few changes, and fees are a tiny fraction of a cent.

## How it works

XDC is a "hybrid" blockchain. The main network is public: anyone can see it and send transactions. Companies can also run private sub-networks for confidential documents and anchor proofs of them on the public chain. Blocks are confirmed by a limited set of masternodes. Each operator has to lock a large amount of XDC (10 million coins per node is the figure used in XDC's own tokenomics papers) and earns new XDC plus fees as a reward. Transactions settle in a few seconds. Because the network speaks the same language as Ethereum (the EVM), developers can reuse familiar tools and wallets such as MetaMask.

## Why people think it has value

The bet behind XDC is that real-world finance moves on-chain. Trade finance is a market of trillions of dollars a year where documents are still faxed, posted and checked by hand. XDC wants invoices, letters of credit and other trade documents to become digital tokens that can be financed, sold or settled quickly. The XDC coin pays transaction fees and is staked by masternode operators, so more use of the network should mean more demand for it. In 2025 XDC Network took over Contour, a trade-finance platform backed by a group of banks, and companies such as Deutsche Telekom joined as validators.

## Its story so far

XinFin, a fintech company founded in Singapore, started work on the network in 2017 and launched the XDC mainnet in 2019. For a long time the coin was little known outside a loyal community. In the 2021 bull market it rose sharply and reached its record in August 2021, then lost most of its value in the 2022 bear market. In the following years the project pushed into tokenised real-world assets and trade finance: it joined trade-finance industry groups, took over Contour in 2025, and Circle announced in late August 2025 that it would bring USDC and its cross-chain transfer system to XDC. In February 2026 the custodian BitGo added support for XDC and USDC on XDC Network, and in July 2026 the network reported a record of about 27.7 million transactions in a month.

## Alternatives

XDC competes with other blockchains that court banks and businesses: [XRP]({lang}/xrp/) and [Stellar]({lang}/xlm/) for payments, [Hedera]({lang}/hbar/) and [Algorand]({lang}/algo/) for enterprise apps, and [Ondo]({lang}/ondo/) for tokenised assets. Many banks also test their own private networks or connect through [Chainlink]({lang}/link/), and the biggest tokenised funds so far have been issued on [Ethereum]({lang}/eth/).

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** XDC is a small coin compared with bitcoin or ether, and it can lose a large part of its value quickly.
- **Adoption may stay slow:** banks and traders change habits slowly; partnerships and pilots do not always turn into heavy, lasting use.
- **Concentration:** blocks are confirmed by a limited number of masternodes, and large holders, including those close to the founding team, own a big share of the coins.
- **Competition:** many networks and banks' own systems fight for the same tokenised-finance business.
- **Regulation:** rules for crypto, stablecoins and tokenised securities differ by country and can change quickly.

## What it means if you buy regularly

XDC's history is a sharp rise in 2021 followed by a long, deep fall, so the timing of a single purchase matters a lot. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your purchases over highs and lows without guessing the bottom, but it does not remove the risk of losses. Its price depends on whether real trade finance really moves to the network. Many people keep a single-project coin like this to a small part of their savings. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
