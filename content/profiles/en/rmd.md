**ResMed** makes the machines that keep people with sleep apnea breathing through the night: a small bedside box (a CPAP device) pushes air through a mask so the airway stays open. Most of these devices are connected to the cloud, so doctors and suppliers can see how the therapy is going. The shares trade in New York under RMD and in Australia on the ASX.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: $1.41 billion in the quarter to 30 September 2026, +6% a year (+7% in constant currency).
> - About 89% of sales come from sleep and breathing products; masks and accessories alone brought $540 million.
> - The company returned more than $1.0 billion to shareholders in one quarter, including $936 million of buybacks.
> - The stock fell about 54% from its 2021 high to its 2023 low on fears that weight-loss drugs would shrink its market.

## How it makes money

ResMed earns in two ways. First, it sells the CPAP devices themselves: $716 million in the latest quarter. Second, and more steadily, it sells masks, cushions and tubing that patients replace regularly: $540 million, growing 10% in constant currency. That repeat business is the core of the model — one device sale brings years of mask orders. A smaller software arm (Brightree and MEDIFOX DAN, programs for home medical equipment suppliers and care providers) added $155 million. In the same quarter ResMed finished selling its MatrixCare software unit for $450.8 million in cash.

In the first quarter of fiscal year 2027 (July–September 2026) ResMed had $1,414.8 million of revenue, a gross margin of 61.5% and net income of $418.4 million, up 20%. Earnings per share were $2.92, up 23%, helped by a share count that fell to 143.4 million. Free cash flow was $374 million. At the end of September it held $1.01 billion in cash against about $410 million of debt, after repaying $250 million during the quarter. The company kept its full-year outlook: revenue of $5.75–5.85 billion. The quarterly dividend is $0.66 per share.

## The stock's story

ResMed shares rose for most of the last decade and got an extra push in 2021, when rival Philips recalled millions of sleep apnea machines. Then came the scare: in 2023 investors feared that GLP-1 weight-loss drugs such as Ozempic would cure many patients of sleep apnea. On adjusted prices the stock fell from about $289 in September 2021 to about $133 in 2023. Sales kept growing anyway — fiscal 2026 revenue rose 10% to about $5.65 billion — and the shares reached a then-record close of $293.73 in August 2025. In August 2026 they dropped about 7% in a day after a weaker-than-expected outlook that included a halt to sales of some Astral ventilators following an FDA recall.

## Competitors

The main rival in machines has been Philips, which agreed in January 2024 to stop selling sleep apnea devices in the US until it meets the terms of a court agreement with regulators. In masks ResMed competes with Fisher & Paykel Healthcare and smaller makers. The less obvious competitor is medicine: weight-loss drugs and other treatments that could reduce how many people need a machine at all.

## Main risks

- **Weight-loss drugs:** so far they have not hurt demand, but the fear can return quickly and move the share price.
- **Philips coming back:** a return of its devices to the US market would bring back price competition.
- **Recalls and regulators:** the Astral ventilator recall shows one product problem can cost tens of millions (ResMed expects about $75 million in fiscal 2027).
- **Costs:** the company has warned about rising prices for electronic components, fuel and freight.
- **Slower growth:** the fiscal 2027 outlook implies growth of only 5–7% in constant currency.

## What it means if you buy regularly

ResMed is a profitable company with repeat sales, but the stock has halved once in recent years on a single fear. Regular buying spreads your entry across calm and nervous months, which is exactly when a story like GLP-1 moves the price. It is still one company, so many people keep a stock like this as part of a wider mix. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
