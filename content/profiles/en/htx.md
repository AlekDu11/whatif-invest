**HTX DAO (HTX)** is the token tied to HTX, the crypto exchange formerly known as Huobi. It arrived in early 2024 to take over from the exchange's old HT token, gives holders a vote on some decisions such as new listings, and the exchange uses part of its income to buy it back and destroy it. > - The maximum supply is close to 1 quadrillion tokens, so one HTX costs only a tiny fraction of a cent.
> - According to HTX DAO, the exchange spends 50% of its quarterly revenue on buying back and burning HTX; about 99.5 trillion tokens had been burned by January 2026.

## How it works

HTX is a TRC-20 token on the TRON blockchain. Holders can keep it on the HTX exchange or in their own TRON wallet. The DAO ("decentralised autonomous organisation") is meant to let token holders vote — so far mostly on which new coins the exchange lists. Every quarter HTX DAO announces a burn: tokens bought on the market are sent to an address nobody controls, which shrinks the supply. In the third quarter of 2025 about 13.1 trillion HTX (over $27 million at the time) were burned, and in the fourth quarter about 13.6 trillion (over $23 million).

## What the token is used for

HTX took over the perks of the old HT token on the exchange: discounts on trading fees, higher membership levels and access to some earn products and token sales. Demand therefore depends mostly on how busy the HTX exchange is: more trading means more revenue, bigger burns and more users who want fee discounts. That makes HTX similar to other exchange tokens, but holders get no legal claim on the exchange's profits — the buyback is a promise, not a contract.

## Its story so far

Huobi was founded in China in 2013 by Leon Li and grew into one of the world's largest exchanges; after China's 2017 crackdown it moved its business abroad and in September 2021 stopped serving mainland Chinese users. In 2022 Justin Sun bought into the exchange, and in September 2023 it was renamed HTX. That autumn it was hacked twice: about $8 million left a hot wallet in September, and in November attackers took about $23 million from the exchange and $86.6 million from the HECO bridge linked to it. In January 2024 HTX said the benefits of its HT token would move to the new HTX token; conversion started in February 2024 at rates that got worse over time, and old HT holders received less than 19% of the new supply. In 2026 the UK (May) and the EU (July) sanctioned HTX over alleged breaches of Russia sanctions.

## Alternatives

Other exchange tokens include [BNB]({lang}/bnb/) from Binance, [OKB]({lang}/okb/) from OKX, [LEO Token]({lang}/leo/) from Bitfinex, [KuCoin]({lang}/kcs/) (KCS), [Gate]({lang}/gt/) (GT) and [WhiteBIT Coin]({lang}/wbt/). HTX also belongs to Justin Sun's [TRON]({lang}/trx/) ecosystem.

## Main risks

- **Dependence on one exchange:** if HTX loses users, suffers another hack or cuts the buyback, demand for the token can fall fast.
- **Concentration and control:** much of the supply and the key decisions sit with the exchange and people around it; The Block reported in March 2024 that DAO voting was not yet active.
- **Regulation and sanctions:** the UK and EU sanctions of 2026 limit where HTX can operate and who can use it, and other regulators may follow.
- **Security:** the 2023 hacks showed that exchange wallets and bridges can be drained.
- **Huge supply:** with nearly 1 quadrillion tokens, even large burns shrink the supply only slowly, and the tiny unit price can make it look "cheap" when it is not.

## What it means if you buy regularly

HTX has traded only since early 2024, so its history is short. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your entry price over time, but it does not protect you if the exchange behind the token runs into trouble. Many people keep exchange tokens to a small part of their savings and do not store them only on that same exchange. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
