**L'Oréal** is the world's largest beauty company: the French group behind Maybelline, Garnier, Lancôme, CeraVe and Kérastase, which sold €44 billion of shampoo, make-up, skincare and perfume in 2025.

## How it makes money

L'Oréal sells about 40 international brands through four divisions. Consumer Products (Garnier, Maybelline, L'Oréal Paris) fills supermarket shelves; Luxe (Lancôme, YSL Beauty, Kiehl's) sells premium skincare and fragrance; Dermatological Beauty (La Roche-Posay, CeraVe, Vichy) is sold with doctors' recommendations; Professional Products (Kérastase, Redken) supplies hair salons. In 2025 sales rose 4.0% like-for-like, and the operating margin was 20.2% — about one euro in five of sales became operating profit. Online sales passed 30% of the total.

## Why it matters

L'Oréal is one of the most valuable companies in France and in the CAC 40 index. It was founded in 1909 by chemist Eugène Schueller, who started with a hair dye, and his descendants — the Bettencourt Meyers family — still own 34.8% of it. A surprising fact: Nestlé, the Swiss food giant, owns another 20.2%. For 2025 shareholders get a dividend of €7.20 per share, up 2.9%. The group is expanding in luxury perfume by buying Kering Beauté, home of the Creed brand.

## Main risks

Beauty is resilient but not immune to weaker consumer spending. China and North Asia, once growth engines, grew only 0.5% in 2025, and travel retail in Asia remains weak. Fast-moving indie and social-media brands compete for young buyers. The stock trades at a premium valuation, so slower growth can weigh on the price. Prices on our site are in US dollars, so the euro also moves the return. Buying a fixed amount regularly smooths price swings but does not remove the risk.
