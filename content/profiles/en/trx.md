**TRON** is the blockchain where much of the world's digital dollar money actually moves: it holds about half of all Tether (USDT) in existence. TRX is the coin that pays for using it. Justin Sun founded the project in 2017.

## How it works

TRON is built for fast, cheap transfers: a new block arrives roughly every 3 seconds. Blocks are produced by 27 "Super Representatives" elected by people who stake (lock up) TRX — every staked coin is one vote, and voters share the rewards. TRX is spent on network resources called bandwidth and energy; when fees are paid in TRX, those coins are burned. Staking TRX gives you these resources instead, so frequent users can send transfers almost free.

## Why it matters

By the end of September 2026, USDT on TRON reached a record of about $92.7 billion — just over 50% of all USDT, according to a TRON-commissioned CoinDesk report. The network averaged 3.7 million daily active users in Q3 2026. One surprising fact: TRON carries 55% of small USDT transfers under $1,000, which makes it an everyday payment rail for freelancers, traders and families sending money across borders.

## Main risks

There is no maximum supply: about 95 billion TRX exist, and new coins issued as rewards roughly balance the coins burned, so TRX is not automatically getting scarcer. The network depends heavily on one product, USDT, and on one issuer, Tether. Block production sits with a small group of 27 validators, and the project is closely tied to its founder. Regulation of stablecoins is changing fast. TRX is about 29% below its December 2024 high and can fall much further. Regular buying smooths the swings but does not remove the risk.
