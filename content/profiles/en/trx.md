**TRON** is a blockchain for fast, cheap transfers and, above all, a highway for digital dollars: roughly half of all Tether (USDT) lives on it. Its coin, TRX, pays for the network and elects the validators who run it. Founded by Justin Sun in 2017, TRX was worth about $29 billion in November 2026.

> **Key takeaways**
> - At the end of September 2026 about $92 billion of USDT sat on TRON — roughly half of all USDT, more than on Ethereum.
> - A block arrives about every 3 seconds, produced by 27 "Super Representatives" elected by TRX stakers.
> - No maximum supply: about 95 billion TRX exist; new coins pay validators and fees paid in TRX are burned.

## How it works

TRON uses "delegated proof of stake": every staked (locked) TRX is one vote, and the 27 candidates with the most votes become Super Representatives, take turns producing blocks and share rewards with their voters. Instead of one gas fee, TRON meters two resources: bandwidth (transaction size) and energy (running smart contracts such as a USDT transfer). Short of resources, you pay by burning TRX; staking TRX gives you bandwidth and energy instead, so heavy users transfer almost free. In August 2025 validators cut the price of energy from 210 to 100 sun (a millionth of a TRX), roughly halving the cost of a typical transfer.

## Why people think it has value

TRX pays fees (or is staked for resources), elects validators and earns staking rewards. Its value rests mainly on demand for block space, mostly from USDT: exchanges, traders, payment firms and families in emerging markets use TRON to move dollars cheaply across borders. Token Terminal counts about $693 million in fees in Q3 2026, even after the cut. TRON also hosts DeFi apps such as JustLend and SunSwap and its own stablecoin, USDD.

## Its story so far

A 2017 token sale raised about $70 million for the TRON Foundation, shortly before China banned such sales. TRX began as a token on Ethereum and moved to TRON's own network in mid-2018, the year TRON bought file-sharing company BitTorrent. Tether started issuing USDT on TRON in 2019, and low fees gradually made it USDT's main chain. In March 2023 the US SEC sued Justin Sun and his foundations over unregistered sales of TRX and BTT and wash trading. It ended in March 2026: the SEC dropped its claims against Sun and the foundations, and BitTorrent developer Rainberry agreed to a $10 million penalty without admitting wrongdoing. In July 2025 Nasdaq-listed SRM Entertainment became Tron Inc. (ticker TRON) and began holding TRX as a treasury reserve, starting with more than 365 million coins.

## Alternatives

For stablecoin transfers TRON competes with [Ethereum]({lang}/eth/) and its layer 2 networks, [Solana]({lang}/sol/), [BNB Chain]({lang}/bnb/) and new stablecoin-only blockchains. For cross-border payments people also look at [XRP]({lang}/xrp/).

## Main risks

- **Dependence on USDT:** most activity is Tether transfers; if Tether, exchanges or users move elsewhere, demand for TRX could fall fast.
- **Concentration:** only 27 validators produce blocks, and the project stays closely tied to Justin Sun and his companies.
- **Regulation:** stablecoin rules are changing fast worldwide, and TRON's popularity for moving dollars also draws scrutiny of illicit flows.
- **No supply cap:** new TRX are issued as rewards; since the 2025 fee cut fewer coins are burned, so supply can grow.
- **Big swings:** after its January 2018 peak TRX lost more than 90% before recovering; it can fall sharply again.

## What it means if you buy regularly

For a crypto coin TRX has a long history and real, measurable usage, but its price still swings hard and leans on one product and one founder. Buying a fixed amount regularly averages your price; it does not remove the risk of a long decline. Many people limit one altcoin to a small share of money they can afford to lose. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
