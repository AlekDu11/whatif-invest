**Walmart** is the world's largest company by revenue: supermarkets and discount stores where millions of Americans buy groceries every week, plus Sam's Club warehouses, stores in Mexico, Canada, China and India's Flipkart. The old model — low prices, huge volume, thin margins — now has a second engine on top: online orders delivered from stores, paid memberships and an advertising business that sells space to brands.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: $713.2 billion in fiscal 2026 (to 31 January 2026), +4.7%; global e-commerce sales were about $150 billion.
> - In the quarter to 31 October 2026 revenue rose 5.2% to $188.8 billion; Walmart U.S. e-commerce grew 23%.
> - Advertising grew 46% to nearly $6.4 billion in fiscal 2026 — a small but very profitable line.
> - John Furner replaced Doug McMillon as CEO in February 2026.

## How it makes money

Walmart has three businesses. Walmart U.S. (about 4,600 stores) brought in $125.0 billion of sales in the October 2026 quarter, about two thirds of the total; groceries are the biggest category. Walmart International (Walmex in Mexico, Canada, China, Flipkart and PhonePe in India and others) sold $35.9 billion, and Sam's Club U.S. $25.9 billion. Profit margins on food are tiny, so the newer lines matter more than their size: membership fees from Walmart+ and Sam's Club, the Walmart Connect ad business (which includes the TV maker Vizio, bought in December 2024), a marketplace for outside sellers, and fast delivery from stores — in the US more than a third of store-fulfilled orders arrived in under three hours.

In the third quarter of fiscal 2027 (August–October 2026) revenue was $188.8 billion, up 5.2%. Walmart U.S. comparable sales rose 2.9% without fuel, Sam's Club 4.9%, and global e-commerce 17%. Operating income grew 16% to $7.8 billion; adjusted earnings were $0.65 per share and reported earnings $0.62. Walmart raised its full-year outlook for the second quarter in a row: sales growth of 4.9–5.2% at constant currency and adjusted earnings of $2.84–2.89 per share.

For fiscal 2026 as a whole, operating income was $29.8 billion and earnings $2.73 per share. Walmart raised its yearly dividend to $0.99 per share for fiscal 2027 and announced a new $30 billion share buyback programme in February 2026. It employs about 2.1 million people.

## The stock's story

Sam Walton opened the first Walmart in Rogers, Arkansas, in 1962, and the company went public in 1970. Through the 1980s and 1990s it became the largest retailer in America and then the world. Amazon's rise made Walmart look old-fashioned for years, and the shares went sideways for much of the 2000s. The turnaround came from spending heavily on e-commerce — Jet.com in 2016, Flipkart in 2018 — and on using its stores as delivery hubs. Higher-income shoppers came to Walmart during the 2022–2023 inflation and stayed. In February 2024 Walmart split its shares 3-for-1, and in December 2025 it moved its listing from the New York Stock Exchange to Nasdaq after more than 50 years. Doug McMillon, CEO since 2014, handed over to John Furner, the former head of Walmart U.S., on 1 February 2026.

## Competitors

Amazon is the main rival online and in delivery, and is growing in groceries. Costco competes with Sam's Club for members; Target, Kroger, Aldi, Dollar General and Dollar Tree fight for the same shopping baskets in the US. In India Flipkart competes with Amazon and Reliance; in Mexico Walmex is the market leader. In advertising Walmart Connect competes with Amazon Ads, Google and Meta for brand budgets.

## Main risks

- **High expectations:** the shares are priced at a high multiple of earnings for a retailer, so a slowdown could hit the price hard.
- **Thin margins:** operating margin was about 4% in fiscal 2026; tariffs, wages and price wars can quickly eat into profit.
- **Tariffs and imports:** many goods come from abroad, and Walmart has to choose between raising prices and accepting lower margins.
- **Competition:** Amazon, Costco and discounters all chase the same shoppers, and online growth costs a lot of money.
- **Pharmacy and regulation:** new US drug price rules cut health and wellness sales in 2026; labour and antitrust rules are other pressure points.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Walmart is a steady business — people buy food in good times and bad — but a steady business does not mean a steady share price: after a strong run the stock can fall a long way if growth disappoints. Buying a fixed amount on a schedule means you get more shares when the price is low and fewer when it is high, without trying to time it. A single company is usually held as one part of a wider portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
