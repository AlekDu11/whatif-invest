**Walmart** is the world's largest company by revenue — the supermarket chain where a huge share of Americans buy groceries every week, now also a fast-growing online store and advertising business. Sam Walton opened the first store in Rogers, Arkansas, in 1962.

## How it makes money

Most revenue comes from selling everyday goods at low prices through three businesses: Walmart U.S. (about 4,600 stores), Walmart International (Mexico, Canada, China, India's Flipkart and others) and Sam's Club, a members-only warehouse chain. Margins on groceries are thin, so the newer, more profitable lines matter: online orders and delivery from stores, membership fees (Walmart+ and Sam's Club) and Walmart Connect, which sells ads to brands that want to reach Walmart shoppers.

## Why it matters

In the quarter to July 2026 Walmart had $187.9 billion in revenue, up 5.9%. Global e-commerce grew 23%, advertising 38% and membership income 17%. People with higher incomes now shop there more, which helped it gain share when prices rose. In December 2025 it moved its share listing from the New York Stock Exchange to Nasdaq after more than half a century. John Furner became CEO in February 2026, succeeding Doug McMillon.

## Main risks

Walmart's shares are priced high compared with its profits, so slower growth could hit the price hard. Tariffs raise the cost of imported goods, and the company must choose between higher prices and lower margins. Amazon, Costco and discount chains compete for the same shoppers, and wages for about 2.1 million employees are a big, rising cost. Buying a fixed amount regularly smooths price swings but does not remove the risk.
