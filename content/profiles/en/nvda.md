**Nvidia** designs the chips that train and run most of the world's artificial intelligence. Founded in 1993 by Jensen Huang and two co-founders to make graphics cards for games, it is now one of the most valuable companies on Earth.

## How it makes money

Nvidia designs graphics processors (GPUs) and the systems around them, and outsources manufacturing mainly to TSMC in Taiwan. Its biggest business by far is the data center: AI chips and networking sold to cloud giants such as Microsoft, Amazon, Google and Meta, and to AI labs. Gaming cards, chips for cars and robots, and professional graphics make up the rest.

## Why it matters

The real moat is software. CUDA, Nvidia's programming platform launched in 2006, is what most AI developers learned on, so switching to a rival chip means rewriting a lot of code. That is why every new wave of AI spending tends to flow through Nvidia first — and why its quarterly results move the whole stock market.

## Main risks

Revenue depends on a handful of very large customers, and several of them are designing their own AI chips. If AI spending slows, sales can drop fast — the stock fell by more than half in 2022. US export rules limit sales to China, and almost all production depends on TSMC in Taiwan.
