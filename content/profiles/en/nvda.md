**Nvidia** designs the chips that train and run most of today's artificial intelligence — the "brains" of the data centers behind chatbots and AI agents. It started in 1993 making graphics cards for games, but almost all of its money now comes from AI; the chips themselves are made mostly by TSMC in Taiwan.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: $115.8 billion in the quarter to 25 October 2026, +103% a year; net profit $65.3 billion.
> - Data centers brought in $107.6 billion — about 93% of all sales.
> - Co-founder Jensen Huang resigned as CEO on 12 November 2026; CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO.
> - The stock closed at a record of about $239 on 6 October 2026, then dropped below a $4 trillion market value by 12 November.

## How it makes money

Nvidia now reports two businesses. **Data Center** ($107.6 billion last quarter, +110% a year) sells AI processors (GPUs), complete server racks such as Vera Rubin NVL72, networking gear that links thousands of chips together, and software. Buyers are cloud giants such as Microsoft, Google, Amazon and Meta, AI labs and "AI clouds" like CoreWeave. **Edge Computing** ($8.2 billion, +42%) covers gaming GeForce cards, PCs, workstations, cars and robots.

The quiet advantage is software. CUDA, Nvidia's programming platform launched in 2006, is what most AI developers learned on, so moving to a rival chip often means rewriting code. In the latest quarter it also agreed to buy Hugging Face, a popular hub for open AI models.

## The numbers right now

In its third fiscal quarter of 2027 (ended 25 October 2026) Nvidia earned $115.8 billion in revenue, up 20% from the previous quarter at a 74.8% gross margin. Free cash flow was $43.8 billion; $28.1 billion went back to shareholders in buybacks and dividends. The dividend is tiny for the share price — $0.25 a quarter.

For the next quarter Nvidia guided to $130 billion of revenue, plus or minus 3%, but a lower 71–72% gross margin. The outlook assumes zero data-center compute sales to China because of US export rules.

## The stock's story

Nvidia listed on Nasdaq in 1999 and was long a gaming-chip company. The AI boom took off after ChatGPT appeared in late 2022 — the same year the stock lost more than half its value. After a 10-for-1 split in 2024 (our prices are split-adjusted) it kept climbing and closed at a record of about $239 on 6 October 2026, valuing the company near $6 trillion.

Then it slid fast. Chip stocks sold off in early November on AI-spending doubts and rising rates; on 9 November investors filed a securities class action; and on 12 November the board announced that Jensen Huang had resigned as president, CEO and director with immediate effect, without giving a reason. CFO Colette Kress became interim CEO and Harvey C. Jones the first-ever board chair. That day the stock fell as much as 14% intraday and its value dropped below $4 trillion, or about 12 times expected earnings — the cheapest since 2012, per LSEG.

## Competitors

AMD is the closest rival in AI GPUs. Broadcom builds custom AI chips for Google (TPUs), Meta and OpenAI, and the cloud giants design their own — Amazon's Trainium, for example. In China, where Nvidia's sales are restricted, Huawei is the main alternative. Nvidia's edge is chips, networking and software that work together.

## Main risks

- **Leadership:** Huang ran Nvidia for 33 years; no permanent successor is named, and an independent board committee is investigating.
- **Few huge customers:** a handful of cloud companies buy most chips, and several build their own.
- **AI spending cycle:** if big tech cuts data-center budgets, revenue can fall quickly — the stock has already halved once, in 2022.
- **China and Taiwan:** export rules block data-center sales to China, and production depends on TSMC in Taiwan.
- **Legal:** a securities class action filed in November 2026 is pending.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Nvidia shows both sides of owning one stock: huge long-term gains and drops of 30–50% within weeks. Buying a fixed amount regularly gets you more shares when prices are low, but does not protect you if a company stays down for years. Many people hold such a stock as one part of a broader portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
