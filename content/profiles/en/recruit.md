**Recruit Holdings** is a Japanese company that most people outside Japan know through its websites: it owns **Indeed**, the world's biggest job-search site, and **Glassdoor**, where employees review their employers. In Japan it also runs everyday sites for housing (SUUMO), restaurants and beauty salons (Hot Pepper), travel (Jalan) and weddings (Zexy), plus one of the world's largest temp-staffing businesses. Its shares trade in Tokyo under the code 6098.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: ¥3.70 trillion in fiscal 2025 (to 31 March 2026); profit attributable to owners ¥496.9 billion.
> - April–September 2026: revenue +19.3% to ¥2.14 trillion and profit +65.7% to ¥411.6 billion — record highs.
> - HR Technology (Indeed and Glassdoor) earned $3.07 billion in the July–September 2026 quarter, +27.5%, at a 47.6% EBITDA+S margin.
> - For the year to March 2027 Recruit guides for ¥4.23 trillion of revenue and ¥755 billion of profit; it is running a ¥350 billion share buyback.

## How it makes money

Recruit has three segments. **HR Technology** is Indeed and Glassdoor: companies pay to promote their job ads ("sponsored jobs") and to reach candidates, mostly in the United States. It is the smallest segment by revenue but by far the most profitable — in the first half of fiscal 2026 it made ¥945.5 billion of revenue at a 47.5% margin. **Marketing Matching Technologies** runs Recruit's Japanese sites that connect shops and companies with customers — SUUMO, Hot Pepper Gourmet and Beauty, Jalan, Zexy — plus the "Air" business software for small shops; shops pay for listings and bookings. **Staffing** places temporary workers in Japan, Europe, the US and Australia; it brings in a lot of revenue (¥917.5 billion in the half) but at a thin margin of about 6%.

## The stock's story

Hiromasa Ezoe founded the company in 1960 as a student at the University of Tokyo, selling job advertisements in a university newspaper. In 1988 the "Recruit scandal" — unlisted shares of a subsidiary given to politicians and officials — became one of Japan's biggest post-war political scandals and contributed to the resignation of Prime Minister Noboru Takeshita in 1989. In the early 1990s, with heavy debts from property and finance ventures, the retailer Daiei took a large stake; Recruit paid down about ¥1.4 trillion of debt by 1994 and later became independent again. It bought Indeed in 2012 and Glassdoor in 2018 for $1.2 billion, and listed on the Tokyo Stock Exchange in October 2014. In dollar terms our data show the shares rising about fivefold from early 2015 to early 2021, then falling roughly a third during 2022 as the post-pandemic hiring boom faded. They dropped again in 2025 amid a slower US job market, then climbed to new highs in the autumn of 2026 as Indeed's growth accelerated. Hisayuki Idekoba is the CEO.

## Competitors

In online recruiting Indeed competes with LinkedIn (Microsoft), ZipRecruiter, Google's job search features and local job boards; Glassdoor also competes with LinkedIn. In staffing the rivals are Randstad, Adecco and ManpowerGroup, and in Japan Persol and Pasona. Its Japanese consumer sites face LINE Yahoo, Rakuten, Tabelog (Kakaku.com) and Booking platforms.

## Main risks

- **The job market:** employers spend less on job ads and temp workers when hiring slows, so profits swing with the US economy.
- **AI:** AI tools could change how people search for and apply to jobs and how companies hire, helping or hurting Indeed.
- **Dependence on Indeed:** most profit now comes from one business in one country, the United States.
- **Currency:** a large part of profit is earned in dollars, so a stronger yen lowers results in yen.
- **A high valuation:** after the 2026 rally a lot of growth is already expected in the share price.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Recruit has grown into a global hiring platform with a very profitable core, but the stock has already lost a third of its value in a hiring downturn once and can do so again. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you pick up more shares when hiring slumps and fewer after rallies, without trying to time the economy.
