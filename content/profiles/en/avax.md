**Avalanche** is a smart-contract blockchain that confirms transactions in about a second and lets companies, games and funds launch their own chains on top of it. It went live in September 2020 and was built by Ava Labs, the company of Cornell computer scientist Emin Gün Sirer. In November 2026 all AVAX in circulation was worth about $3 billion, around number 25 among cryptocurrencies.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Transactions are final in about one second, and the main C-Chain works with Ethereum wallets such as MetaMask.
> - Supply is capped at 720 million AVAX; about 447 million were in circulation in November 2026, and every fee is burned.
> - Tokenized funds from Securitize, WisdomTree and others run on it, and since September 2026 validators can stake for as little as 48 hours.
> - The record price so far is $144.96 (November 2021); in November 2026 AVAX traded about 95% below it.

## How it works

Instead of miners, Avalanche uses a voting method called Snowman: validators repeatedly ask small random groups of other validators which block they prefer until almost all agree, which takes about a second. Most apps run on the C-Chain, which is compatible with Ethereum. The P-Chain keeps track of validators and of "Avalanche L1s" (formerly subnets) — separate chains with their own rules for a company, a game or a fund. Since the Avalanche9000 upgrade in December 2024, running such a chain costs a small monthly fee in AVAX instead of locking 2,000 AVAX per validator.

## What the token is used for

AVAX pays transaction fees on the C-Chain, and those fees are burned — destroyed for good. Validators must stake at least 2,000 AVAX (delegators 25) and earn new AVAX as rewards; since the Helicon upgrade in September 2026 the minimum lock-up is 48 hours instead of two weeks. Avalanche L1s pay their fees in AVAX too. Some companies now hold AVAX as a treasury asset: Nasdaq-listed AVAX One held about 14.2 million AVAX at the end of September 2026, almost all of it staked.

## Its story so far

Ava Labs raised money in a 2020 token sale and launched the mainnet in September 2020. In the 2021 boom large incentive programs pulled DeFi apps onto Avalanche, and AVAX set its record of about $145 on 21 November 2021. In 2022 it fell more than 90% as crypto crashed. From 2023 Avalanche focused on tokenized real-world assets and custom chains, Avalanche9000 cut the cost of launching them in December 2024, and the game Off The Grid moved onto its own Avalanche L1. In September 2026 the Helicon upgrade made staking more flexible, while the price fell again in the autumn of 2026.

## Alternatives

Among smart-contract blockchains Avalanche competes with [Ethereum]({lang}/eth/) and its layer-2 networks, [Solana]({lang}/sol/), [NEAR]({lang}/near/), [Sui]({lang}/sui/) and [Polkadot]({lang}/dot/). For tokenized funds Ethereum still holds the largest share.

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** AVAX has fallen about 95% from its record and lost 28% in four weeks in October–November 2026.
- **Competition:** Solana and Ethereum's layer-2 networks fight for the same apps, users and funds.
- **Value capture:** activity spread across many separate L1 chains may not create much demand for AVAX itself.
- **Token supply:** new coins are still created as staking rewards, and Ava Labs, the foundation and early investors hold large amounts.
- **Regulation and technology:** crypto rules differ by country, and bridges and smart contracts can be hacked.

## What it means if you buy regularly

AVAX's history shows one fast rise in 2021 and a long fall after it. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more when the price is low and less when it is high, without trying to guess the bottom — but it does not remove the risk of losses. Many people keep smaller coins like AVAX to a small part of their savings that they can afford to see fall sharply. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
