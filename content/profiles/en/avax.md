**Avalanche** is a smart-contract blockchain that confirms transactions in about a second and lets companies, games and funds launch their own chains on top of it. It went live in September 2020 and was built by Ava Labs, the company of Cornell computer scientist Emin Gün Sirer.

## How it works

Instead of miners, Avalanche uses a voting method called Snowman: validators repeatedly poll small random groups of other validators until they agree. Most apps run on the C-Chain, which is compatible with Ethereum, so wallets like MetaMask work there. The P-Chain keeps track of validators and of "Avalanche L1s" (formerly subnets) — separate chains with their own rules. Since the Avalanche9000 upgrade in December 2024, running such a chain costs a small monthly fee in AVAX instead of locking 2,000 AVAX per validator.

## What AVAX is for

AVAX pays transaction fees, and every fee is burned — destroyed for good. Validators must stake at least 2,000 AVAX (delegators 25) and earn new AVAX as rewards. Supply is capped at 720 million coins; about 447 million are in circulation.

## Why it matters

Avalanche is a popular home for tokenized real-world assets: Securitize, WisdomTree and others have put funds on it, and the game Off The Grid runs on its own Avalanche L1. A surprising fact: AVAX hit its record of about $145 exactly five years ago, on November 21, 2021.

## Main risks

AVAX trades more than 95% below its 2021 peak. Competition from Solana and Ethereum's layer-2 networks is fierce, and activity spread across many separate chains may not translate into demand for AVAX itself. New coins are still being created as staking rewards, which dilutes holders. Bridges and smart contracts can be hacked, and crypto rules differ by country. Buying a fixed amount regularly smooths the swings but does not remove the risk.
