**Johnson & Johnson** has been in healthcare since 1886. Today it does two things: it sells prescription medicines — for cancer, immune diseases such as psoriasis and Crohn's, and depression — and medical technology, from heart and surgery devices to knee implants and contact lenses. Since it spun off its consumer brands (Band-Aid, Tylenol, Neutrogena) as Kenvue in 2023, it is a pure healthcare company, and in 2025 it sold $94.2 billion worth of products.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Sales: $94.2 billion in 2025, +6.0% on the year; net earnings $26.8 billion ($11.03 per share).
> - Medicines (Innovative Medicine) brought in $60.4 billion, about 64%; MedTech $33.8 billion.
> - In October 2026 J&J raised its 2026 sales forecast to $101.0–101.4 billion — the first year above $100 billion in its history.
> - Profit in the September 2026 quarter fell to $210 million because of a one-time charge for a proposed settlement of the talc lawsuits; adjusted earnings were $2.59 per share.

## How it makes money

About two-thirds of the money comes from medicines that are protected by patents for a limited time. The growth drivers in 2026 were the cancer drugs Darzalex, Carvykti, Tecvayli, Rybrevant and Erleada, the immunology drug Tremfya and the brain-health drugs Spravato and Caplyta. The big drag is Stelara, J&J's former best-seller for psoriasis and Crohn's disease: cheaper copies (biosimilars) arrived in 2025 and alone cut quarterly growth by about 7 percentage points. The other third is MedTech: heart-rhythm (electrophysiology) and Shockwave devices, surgery tools, orthopaedics (knees, hips, trauma) and Acuvue contact lenses. J&J announced in October 2025 that it intends to separate its Orthopaedics business. The United States gives about 59% of sales.

In the September 2026 quarter J&J had sales of $25.4 billion, up 5.9% from a year earlier: medicines $16.8 billion (+7.7%) and MedTech $8.6 billion (+2.5%). Reported net earnings were only $210 million, or $0.09 per share, against $5.2 billion a year before — the drop came from one-time charges for the proposed talc settlement, the purchase of Firefly Bio and a cell-therapy deal with Sail Biomedicines. Without such items, earnings were $6.3 billion, or $2.59 per share. Free cash flow in the first nine months of 2026 was about $15.1 billion.

For the full year 2026 J&J expected adjusted earnings of $11.05–11.15 per share (forecast of October 2026). It has raised its dividend every year for more than 60 years in a row.

## The stock's story

Robert Wood Johnson and his brothers founded the company in New Brunswick, New Jersey, in 1886, making sterile surgical dressings; its shares have traded on the New York Stock Exchange since 1944. For decades J&J was known as one of the steadiest stocks in America — it is one of only two US companies with a top AAA credit rating, together with Microsoft. From the late 2010s tens of thousands of lawsuits claimed its talc baby powder caused cancer; J&J denied this, and its attempts to settle them through a subsidiary's bankruptcy were rejected by courts three times, the last in March 2025. In 2023 it split off the consumer business as Kenvue. It then bought Shockwave Medical in 2024 and Intra-Cellular Therapies (maker of Caplyta) in 2025 to add growth. After a few flat years, the shares rose strongly in 2025 as new medicines more than made up for Stelara's decline.

## Competitors

In medicines J&J competes with AbbVie (Skyrizi and Rinvoq against Tremfya), Merck, Pfizer, Roche, Novartis, Bristol Myers Squibb and AstraZeneca, plus the makers of cheap Stelara copies such as Amgen. In MedTech its rivals are Medtronic, Abbott, Boston Scientific, Stryker, Zimmer Biomet and, in surgical robots, Intuitive Surgical.

## Main risks

- **Patent cliffs:** every blockbuster eventually faces cheap copies, as Stelara did in 2025; new drugs must keep replacing old ones.
- **Talc and other lawsuits:** the proposed settlement still has to work out, and court cases can bring large, unexpected costs.
- **US drug prices:** Medicare price negotiations, tariffs and political pressure on prices can cut margins.
- **Slower MedTech:** devices grew much more slowly than medicines in 2026, and separating Orthopaedics is a big, complex project.
- **Research setbacks:** a failed late-stage trial can wipe out years of expected sales.

## What it means if you buy regularly

J&J is a large, diversified and highly rated company that pays a rising dividend, which is why many people treat it as a "defensive" stock. That does not make it risk-free: lawsuits and patent expiries have hit the shares hard before. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more shares when the price is low and fewer when it is high, without trying to time the market. Many people hold one company only as part of a wider portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
