**CoreWeave** rents out the computing power that AI needs. It fills data centres with Nvidia graphics chips (GPUs) and charges companies like OpenAI, Meta and Microsoft to train and run their models on them, usually under multi-year contracts. It started in 2017 as a crypto-mining operation, switched to AI cloud, and listed on Nasdaq in March 2025 at $40 a share.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: $3.66 billion in Q3 2026, up about 168% from $1.37 billion a year earlier.
> - Signed but not yet delivered work (backlog): about $132.5 billion.
> - Still losing money: a net loss of $709 million in the quarter, mostly because of interest and the cost of chips wearing out.
> - Debt reached almost $47 billion, and CoreWeave plans to spend $37–39 billion on new equipment in 2026.

## How it makes money

CoreWeave sells AI computing as a service. Customers reserve thousands of GPUs for years instead of buying and running the hardware themselves. It also sells cluster-management software and Weights & Biases, a toolkit for AI developers. Most of the money comes from a handful of very large customers. In the quarter to June 2025, Microsoft alone made up 71% of revenue, according to Bloomberg. Since then CoreWeave has signed a $14.2 billion deal with Meta that runs to 2031 and taken its OpenAI contracts to a total of $22.4 billion.

## The numbers right now

In the quarter to 30 September 2026 revenue was $3.66 billion, over $1 billion more than the previous quarter. Adjusted EBITDA, which counts profit before interest, taxes and the wearing-out cost of equipment, was $2.26 billion, a 62% margin. But operating income was only $207 million, and after interest the company lost $709 million, or $1.27 per share.

CoreWeave raised its 2026 revenue forecast to $13.0–13.3 billion and expects to exit the year with an annual revenue run rate of $20–21 billion. Its data centres now draw over 1.7 gigawatts, with more than 4.6 gigawatts contracted for the future. The company had $4.1 billion of cash against roughly $47 billion of debt, and expects $1.05–1.15 billion of interest expense in Q4 alone.

## The stock's story

The stock priced its IPO at $40 on 28 March 2025 and then quadrupled. It hit a record $183.58 on 20 June 2025 as investors bet on AI demand. In October 2025, Core Scientific shareholders voted down CoreWeave's planned takeover of the data-centre company, valued at about $9 billion. Through 2026 worries about debt, rising interest rates and AI spending pulled the shares well below the peak, even though Nvidia invested $2 billion at $87.20 a share in January 2026. Over the past year the price has swung between about $41 and $138.

## Competitors

CoreWeave competes with the big cloud companies, Amazon Web Services, Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud and Oracle (some of them are also its customers). It also competes with other specialist "neoclouds" such as Nebius and Lambda.

## Main risks

- **Debt and interest:** almost $47 billion of debt and over $1 billion of interest a quarter.
- **A few big customers:** losing or shrinking one contract with Microsoft, OpenAI or Meta would hit revenue hard.
- **Chips age quickly:** GPUs lose value as Nvidia releases new generations, and depreciation already eats most of the operating profit.
- **Building on time:** the backlog becomes revenue only if data centres and power arrive on schedule.
- **Dilution:** the company has repeatedly raised money by issuing new shares and debt.

## What it means if you buy regularly

CoreWeave is a young, heavily indebted company whose price has already fallen by more than half from its high. With about 1.5 years of history, our "$10 a day" figure covers a short, unusual period. Regular buying spreads your entry points but cannot protect against one company's debt or customer problems. Many people keep a stock like this as a small slice of a wider portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
