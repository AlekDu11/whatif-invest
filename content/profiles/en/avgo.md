**Broadcom** is the company that builds made-to-order AI chips for tech giants that don't want to rely only on Nvidia — Google's TPUs are its best-known work. It also makes the networking chips that let thousands of processors in a data center talk to each other, plus chips for phones, Wi-Fi and routers. Its second leg is business software: since buying VMware, it sells the programs many corporations use to run their own private clouds.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: $29.6 billion in the quarter to 2 August 2026, +86% a year; AI chips alone $16.7 billion (+221%).
> - Free cash flow $13.7 billion — 46 cents of every dollar of revenue.
> - Management expects about $115 billion of AI revenue in fiscal 2027, up from $58 billion in 2026 — a forecast, not a guarantee.
> - The stock closed at a record $481.57 in June 2026 and was about 34% lower on 10 November.

## How it makes money

**Semiconductor solutions** brought in $20.8 billion last quarter (+127%), about 70% of revenue. The core is custom accelerators — Broadcom calls them XPUs — designed together with each customer: Google's TPUs (also used by Anthropic), OpenAI's first in-house chip, and Meta's MTIA. XPUs made up 73% of AI revenue; the rest is AI networking, such as its Tomahawk Ethernet switch chips, sold even to clouds that use rival accelerators. Phone, Wi-Fi, broadband and storage chips fill out the segment.

**Infrastructure software** earned $8.8 billion (+29%) at an operating margin of about 84%. Most of it is VMware Cloud Foundation, the platform companies use to run servers in their own data centers, plus mainframe and security software from earlier deals.

## The numbers right now

In its fiscal third quarter (ended 2 August 2026) Broadcom earned $29.6 billion of revenue and $13.1 billion of GAAP net profit, up 216% a year. It held $24.0 billion in cash against about $59.4 billion of debt — much of it left from buying VMware. The quarterly dividend is $0.65 a share ($2.60 a year).

For the quarter to early November it guided to about $34.8 billion of revenue (+93%), including $21.7 billion from AI chips, with results due in December. CEO Hock Tan said Google is expected to buy "multi-tens of billions of dollars" of TPUs a year under a long-term agreement, and that Anthropic and OpenAI plan multi-gigawatt deployments in 2027 and 2028.

## The stock's story

Broadcom started life as Avago Technologies, a former Hewlett-Packard chip unit that listed in 2009. Under Hock Tan, CEO since 2006, it grew through deals: Avago bought Broadcom Corp. in 2016 and took its name, later adding VMware in 2023. In 2024 it split its shares 10-for-1 (our prices are split-adjusted).

AI turned a steady dividend payer into one of the world's most valuable companies. The share price closed at a record $481.57 in June 2026. It slid even after record September results — the CFO warned that custom chips weigh on margins — and fell further in the November chip sell-off. On 10 November it jumped 8.8% to $319.48, still about 34% below the peak.

## Competitors

In AI, Broadcom's custom chips compete with Nvidia's ready-made GPUs and with AMD; Marvell and Taiwan's MediaTek also design custom chips for cloud giants. In networking it competes with Nvidia's own switches and with Marvell. VMware faces Microsoft, Nutanix and the public clouds.

## Main risks

- **Concentration:** a few customers — Google above all — drive most of the AI growth; losing or shrinking one order would hurt.
- **Lower margins:** custom chips earn less per dollar than software, so faster AI growth can squeeze overall profitability.
- **AI spending cycle:** the 2027–2028 targets assume huge data-center build-outs; delays in power, memory or customer budgets could push them back.
- **Debt and VMware pricing:** about $59 billion of debt, and some customers have criticised VMware's price rises and may leave.
- **Supply chain:** Broadcom relies on TSMC in Taiwan to make its chips.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Broadcom combines a growing dividend with a stock that can drop by a third in a few months. Regular buying spreads your entries across such swings, but it does not change the fact that one company's results drive the outcome. Many people hold such a stock as part of a broader portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
