**Uniswap** is the largest decentralized exchange: an app where anyone can swap one token for another straight from their own wallet, with no company holding the money. Hayden Adams launched it on Ethereum in November 2018; it now runs on many networks, including its own Unichain. In November 2026 all UNI in circulation was worth about $2.8 billion, around number 30 among cryptocurrencies.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Swaps on Uniswap paid liquidity providers more than $1 billion in trading fees in 2025.
> - In December 2025 holders approved "UNIfication" with 99.9% support: part of the fees now buys and burns UNI, and 100 million UNI were destroyed at once.
> - Only 1 billion UNI were ever created; after the burn the total is below 900 million, and about 607 million circulate.
> - The record price so far is $44.92 (May 2021); in November 2026 UNI traded about 90% below it.

## How it works

There is no order book and no company matching buyers and sellers. Users called liquidity providers deposit pairs of tokens into shared pools, and a formula sets the price from the balance of the two tokens in each pool. Every swap pays a small fee to those providers. Version 3 (2021) let them place money in chosen price ranges, and version 4 (2025) lets developers attach "hooks" — custom code that changes how a pool behaves. The contracts run on Ethereum, many layer-2 networks and Unichain, Uniswap's own layer-2 launched in February 2025.

## What the token is used for

UNI is the governance token: holders vote on changes to the protocol and on how its treasury is spent. Since the UNIfication vote it also has an economic role: part of trading fees goes to the protocol and is used to burn UNI, and sequencer fees from Unichain are burned too, so the supply shrinks as trading grows. Uniswap Labs dropped its own interface fees and took over the Uniswap Foundation's work.

## Its story so far

Hayden Adams, a former mechanical engineer, built the first version with a grant from the Ethereum Foundation and launched it in November 2018. Version 2 arrived in May 2020, just before the "DeFi summer". In September 2020, when a rival tried to lure its users away, Uniswap handed 400 UNI to every wallet that had ever used it — one of crypto's best-known airdrops. UNI set its record of $44.92 in May 2021 and then fell sharply in the 2022 crash. Version 4 and Unichain followed in early 2025, and in December 2025 holders finally switched on the fee mechanism after years of debate.

## Alternatives

Among decentralized exchanges Uniswap competes with [PancakeSwap]({lang}/cake/) on BNB Chain, [Curve]({lang}/crv/) for stablecoin swaps, Aerodrome on Base and fast trading platforms such as [Hyperliquid]({lang}/hype/). Centralized exchanges like Binance and Coinbase remain the main alternative for most buyers.

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** UNI has fallen about 90% from its May 2021 peak.
- **Competition:** rival exchanges and aggregators fight for the same trading volume, and the burn only matters if trading stays high.
- **Concentration:** Uniswap Labs, early investors and the team hold a large share of tokens and voting power.
- **Regulation:** rules for DeFi and for tokens that share fees can change and differ by country.
- **Technology:** smart-contract bugs or a hack of a pool or hook could cost users money.

## What it means if you buy regularly

UNI's history shows a fast rise in 2021 and a long period far below it. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more when the price is low and less when it is high, without trying to guess the bottom — but it does not remove the risk of losses. Many people keep tokens like UNI to a small part of their savings that they can afford to see fall sharply. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
