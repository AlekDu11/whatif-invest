**Uniswap** is the largest decentralized exchange: an app where anyone can swap one token for another straight from their own wallet, with no company holding the money. Hayden Adams launched it on Ethereum in November 2018; it now runs on many networks, including its own Unichain.

## How it works

There is no order book. Users called liquidity providers deposit pairs of tokens into shared pools, and a formula sets the price from the balance in each pool. Every swap pays a small fee to those providers. Version 4 lets developers attach "hooks" — custom code that changes how a pool behaves.

## What UNI is for

UNI is the governance token: holders vote on changes to the protocol and on how its treasury is spent. In September 2020 Uniswap handed 400 UNI to every wallet that had ever used it, one of crypto's best-known airdrops. In December 2025 holders approved "UNIfication" with 99.9% support: part of trading fees now goes to the protocol and is used to burn UNI, sequencer fees from Unichain are burned too, and 100 million UNI were destroyed from the treasury in one go. Uniswap Labs also dropped its own interface fees and took over the Uniswap Foundation's work.

## Why it matters

Uniswap generated more than $1 billion in trading fees in 2025, more than most listed exchanges earn. Surprising fact: of the 1 billion UNI originally created, about 886 million exist today, and the supply now shrinks as trading grows.

## Main risks

UNI trades about 90% below its May 2021 peak of $44.92. Rival exchanges such as Aerodrome, PancakeSwap and Hyperliquid fight for the same volume, and the burn only matters if trading stays high. Uniswap Labs, early investors and the team hold a large share of tokens and voting power. Smart-contract bugs and changing DeFi rules remain risks. Buying a fixed amount regularly smooths the swings but does not remove the risk.
