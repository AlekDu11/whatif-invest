**Ethereum Classic** is the original Ethereum blockchain, which kept running unchanged after the community split in July 2016. It runs the same smart contracts as Ethereum but is still secured by proof-of-work mining, and its supply is capped at 210.7 million coins. ## How it works

ETC runs the Ethereum Virtual Machine (EVM), so wallets and apps built for Ethereum usually work on it with small changes. Roughly every 13 seconds miners add a block, competing with graphics cards and special machines that run the Etchash algorithm; the winner gets new ETC plus fees. Its "5M20" rule cuts the block reward by a fifth every 5,000,000 blocks, keeping the total below 210.7 million.

## Why people think it has value

Supporters see ETC as Bitcoin's ideas — fixed supply, proof of work, "code is law", a history never rewritten by a vote — combined with Ethereum's programmability. Every transaction and contract is paid for in ETC, and ETC is what miners earn for securing the network. Since Ethereum stopped mining, ETC has been the largest proof-of-work network for smart contracts. Real use by apps, however, is far smaller than on Ethereum.

## Its story so far

Ethereum launched in July 2015. In June 2016 an attacker drained millions of ether from The DAO, a large fund built on it. Most of the community backed a July 2016 hard fork that returned the money; those who refused kept the old chain, which became Ethereum Classic. In January 2019 and several times in August 2020, attackers who briefly held most of the mining power double-spent coins on exchanges. The Thanos upgrade in November 2020 let graphics cards with less memory keep mining. ETC climbed to about $167 in May 2021 and fell sharply in 2022. When Ethereum ended mining in September 2022, many miners moved to ETC and its hashrate jumped several-fold. The reward was cut in December 2017, March 2020, April 2022 and May 2024 (to 2.048 ETC per block); a fifth cut, to 1.6384 ETC, was scheduled for block 25,000,001 in 2026.

## Alternatives

The closest rival is [Ethereum]({lang}/eth/) itself: the same roots, but proof of stake and a far larger world of apps, stablecoins and developers. Among proof-of-work coins, ETC is often compared with [Bitcoin]({lang}/btc/), [Litecoin]({lang}/ltc/), [Bitcoin Cash]({lang}/bch/) and [Kaspa]({lang}/kas/), which are mostly digital cash rather than smart-contract platforms.

## Main risks

- **51% attacks:** a group with most of the mining power could again reverse transactions, and exchanges may then slow deposits or delist the coin.
- **Small team:** core software and upgrades depend on a few developer groups and their funders.
- **Competition:** Ethereum and other smart-contract networks attract far more users and apps, so ETC may remain a niche.
- **Big swings:** drops of more than 80% have happened and can happen again.
- **Regulation:** rules on crypto and mining differ by country and can change quickly, from taxes to energy limits or bans.

## What it means if you buy regularly

ETC has had sharp rises and deep falls, and its future depends on a small community and on miners staying. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your entry price over time without guessing the bottom, but it does not protect you if the network loses users or security. Many people keep coins like this to a small part of their savings. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
