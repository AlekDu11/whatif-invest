**Analog Devices** makes the chips that connect electronics to the real world: they turn temperature, sound, motion and electric current into digital data and manage power so devices run safely. Its parts sit inside factory robots, car batteries, medical equipment and AI data centers. It trades on Nasdaq as ADI and is in the S&P 500.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: $4.02 billion in the quarter to 1 August 2026, +40% a year; communications sales jumped 84%.
> - Very profitable: adjusted gross margin 72.5%, free cash flow $4.94 billion over the 12 months to August 2026 (36% of revenue).
> - In November 2026 the shares fell about 20% in a few weeks during a sell-off in AI and chip stocks.
> - For the quarter to October 2026 ADI guided to $4.3 billion of revenue, plus or minus $100 million (results due 24 November 2026).

## How it makes money

ADI sells tens of thousands of different chips, so no single gadget makes or breaks it. **Industrial** is the largest part: $1.97 billion in the quarter to August 2026, 49% of sales, +53% a year — factory automation, test gear, medical devices and energy. **Automotive** ($998 million, 25%, +16%) means battery management, in-car audio and connectivity. **Communications** ($655 million, 16%, +84%) grew fastest thanks to optical and power chips for AI data centers. **Consumer** ($397 million, 10%, +6%) covers phones and wearables.

Analog chips are less glamorous than AI processors, but hard to replace: once an engineer designs one into a car or a machine, it often stays in production for years. That is why margins are so high.

In its third fiscal quarter of 2026 (ended 1 August) ADI had revenue of $4.02 billion, up from $2.88 billion a year earlier. Net income was $1.34 billion, or $2.74 per share. Over those 12 months it returned $5.17 billion to shareholders in dividends and buybacks. The quarterly dividend was $1.10 a share, up from $0.99 a year earlier.

At the end of that quarter debt was about $9.1 billion against $2.3 billion of cash and short-term investments. For the fourth quarter ADI guided to $4.3 billion of revenue and adjusted earnings of $3.86 per share, plus or minus 15 cents.

## The stock's story

In August 2021 ADI completed its all-stock purchase of Maxim Integrated. Then came the post-pandemic hangover: customers had stockpiled chips, and fiscal 2024 revenue fell to $9.43 billion in what ADI called a historic decline. The rebound came next: $11.0 billion in fiscal 2025 (+17%).

The stock returned about 55% in the year to 5 November 2026, beating most chip makers, after a closing high of $433.28 on 29 October 2026. November 2026 turned rough: on 5 November it fell 11% as the Nasdaq dropped 5.5%, and on 20 November it slid about 8% in a sector-wide sell-off of AI and chip stocks, when ADI was valued at more than 40 times earnings.

## Competitors

Texas Instruments is the biggest analog chip maker and ADI's closest rival. In cars and industry ADI also competes with Infineon, NXP, STMicroelectronics, Microchip and Renesas.

## Main risks

- **Cycles:** chip demand swings hard, as fiscal 2024 showed.
- **Valuation:** in November 2026 the shares traded at more than 40 times earnings, so any disappointment can hit the price fast.
- **Cars:** a quarter of sales is automotive, which grew just 16% in the quarter to August 2026 while the whole company grew 40%.
- **Debt and dilution:** about $9.1 billion of debt; the diluted share count rose about 31% in the five years to 2026, largely because of the Maxim deal.

## What it means if you buy regularly

ADI is a profitable, dividend-paying company in a very cyclical industry: it lost about 20% in just a few weeks in autumn 2026. Regular buying gets you more shares in bad months, but does not protect you if the whole sector stays down for years. Many people hold one chip stock only as a small part of a broader portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
