**OKB** is the coin of OKX, one of the world's largest crypto exchanges. Since August 2025 it has had a fixed supply of 21 million coins and works as the "gas" — the fee money — of X Layer, a blockchain launched by OKX. OKB is worth {{mcap:okb}} today ({{rank:okb}} among cryptocurrencies).

## How it works

OKB is a token that OKX created in 2018, when the exchange was still called OKEx. For years the exchange used part of its income to buy OKB back and destroy ("burn") it, which slowly reduced the supply. In August 2025 OKX made one large final burn of 65,256,712 OKB, including coins it had bought back and held in reserve, and upgraded the token contract so that no new OKB can be created. That left a fixed total of 21 million coins — the same number as bitcoin's cap.

At the same time OKX merged its older OKTChain into X Layer. X Layer is a layer 2 network: it processes transactions cheaply and then settles them on Ethereum. It was built with Polygon's CDK toolkit, and anyone can launch apps on it.

## What the token is used for

OKB is the gas token of X Layer: every transaction on the network is paid for in OKB. OKX Pay, the exchange's payment service, uses X Layer as its default chain, which links OKB to payments and to the apps built there. In the past OKB also gave holders lower trading fees on the exchange and access to token sales; OKX's own help page now says holding OKB does not change trading fee tiers. So most of the demand comes from use on X Layer, from the fixed supply and from people who bet on the success of OKX.

## Its story so far

OKB launched in 2018 with a supply of 1 billion coins. In 2020 OKEx burned 700 million of them, cutting the supply to 300 million, and kept buying back and burning coins after that. In 2022 the exchange renamed itself OKX. In February 2025 OKX's operator pleaded guilty in the United States to violating anti-money-laundering rules and agreed to pay more than $504 million. On 13 August 2025 OKX announced the 21 million cap and the X Layer upgrade; OKB jumped about 160% that day, briefly trading around $135, before falling back.

## Alternatives

Most big exchanges have their own coin. The largest is Binance's [BNB]({lang}/bnb/), which also powers its own blockchain. Others include [Cronos]({lang}/cro/) from Crypto.com, [GateToken]({lang}/gt/) from Gate and [KuCoin Token]({lang}/kcs/). Among layer 2 networks, X Layer competes with chains such as Arbitrum, Base and Polygon.

## Main risks

- **One company:** OKB depends on OKX; trouble at the exchange, a hack or a loss of trust would hit the coin directly.
- **Regulation:** OKX has paid large US penalties and has been restricted or warned in several countries; new actions are possible.
- **Big swings:** OKB more than doubled in days in August 2025 and gave back much of that gain; sudden moves of this size can happen again.
- **Competition:** X Layer is one of many layer 2 networks; if apps and users choose other chains, demand for OKB as gas stays small.
- **Concentration:** a large share of the coins has been held by the exchange and early holders, so their decisions can move the price.

## What it means if you buy regularly

OKB's price has moved in sharp jumps around company news. Buying a small fixed amount regularly spreads your purchases over time, so you do not depend on buying at one moment — but it does not remove the risk that something goes wrong at OKX. Exchange coins are best treated as a bet on one company and kept to a small part of savings. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
