**Venice Token (VVV)** is the token of Venice, a private AI chat and image app with an API for developers, founded by crypto entrepreneur Erik Voorhees. Holders stake VVV to get a share of Venice's AI computing capacity for free, and part of the company's revenue buys back and burns tokens. > **Key takeaways**
> - VVV launched on 27 January 2025 on Base, an Ethereum layer 2, with 100 million tokens; 50 million were airdropped.
> - Yearly issuance was cut from 14 million VVV at launch to 2 million from October 2026.
> - The price fell to about $0.92 in December 2025, then rose more than 30-fold to a record in September 2026.
> - The largest wallets hold most of the supply, so the price can move very fast in both directions.

## How it works

Venice does not train its own large model. It runs open-source models such as Llama and DeepSeek on rented GPUs and promises not to store users' prompts or link them to an account. Anyone can use the app for free with limits, or pay a subscription in dollars. The token adds a second way in: if you stake VVV, you get a daily share of Venice's API capacity in proportion to your stake. Since 2025 staked VVV can also be locked to mint DIEM, a separate token worth $1 of Venice API credit every day for as long as you hold it.

## Why people think it has value

VVV is a claim on AI computing rather than a share in the company. Demand comes from developers and AI agents that want cheap, private access to models, and from holders who earn staking rewards. Venice also uses part of its revenue to buy VVV on the market and burn it, and in 2025 it burned the airdropped tokens nobody claimed. Business growth matters: Venice said in August 2026 that it had passed $100 million in annualised revenue, and in July 2026 it raised $65 million led by Dragonfly at a $1 billion valuation.

## Its story so far

Erik Voorhees, an early bitcoin entrepreneur and founder of the ShapeShift exchange, launched Venice in May 2024 as an AI chat without censorship or tracking. The VVV token followed in January 2025: half of the supply went to Venice users and to communities of AI projects on Base. The price spiked on launch day, then fell for most of 2025 as early holders sold, reaching about $0.92 in December. In 2026 a fall in new issuance, the buybacks, DIEM and fast revenue growth turned the trend: VVV rose about 3,000% to its record in September 2026 and stayed volatile afterwards, including a jump of about 24% on 22 November 2026.

Other crypto projects in AI include [Bittensor]({lang}/tao/), a network of AI models paid in TAO, the [Artificial Superintelligence Alliance]({lang}/fet/) (FET), [Render]({lang}/render/) for GPU power and [Virtuals Protocol]({lang}/virtual/) for AI agents. Outside crypto, ChatGPT, Claude and Gemini are the main competition for Venice's app.

## Main risks

- **Concentration:** the 100 largest wallets hold almost the entire supply, so a few holders can move the price.
- **One company:** the token's value depends on Venice's business, which relies on open-source models it does not control.
- **Big swings:** VVV lost more than 95% from its launch-day high before its 2026 rally; daily moves of 20–30% are common.
- **Regulation:** an AI service without content filters may draw scrutiny, and the rules for tokens like VVV can change.
- **Competition:** big AI labs keep cutting prices, which can reduce demand for VVV-based access.

## What it means if you buy regularly

VVV has less than two years of history and extreme volatility. Buying a fixed amount regularly averages the price you pay, but it cannot protect you if a small, concentrated token keeps falling. Many people keep tokens like this to a tiny share of savings they can afford to lose. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
