**American Tower** owns the tall steel towers that carry your phone signal — and rents space on them to mobile operators, who hang their antennas there instead of building their own masts. It owns more than 147,000 communications sites around the world plus a network of US data centres. It is organised as a REIT (a real estate company that must pay out most of its profit as dividends) and trades on the New York Stock Exchange.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: $2.79 billion in Q3 2026, +2.5% a year; data centres grew 17.4%.
> - Dividend: $1.79 a share per quarter, +5.3% a year — about $7.16 a year.
> - Debt is large: $35.7 billion net, 4.9 times yearly operating profit (Adjusted EBITDA).
> - The stock closed at a record in September 2021, then fell about 26% in 2022 as interest rates jumped.

## How it makes money

American Tower signs long, multi-year leases with carriers such as AT&T, Verizon and T-Mobile in the US and their peers abroad. The trick of the model: one tower costs roughly the same to run whether it carries one tenant or three, so every extra antenna is mostly profit. In Q3 2026 the US & Canada brought in $1.28 billion, Latin America $460 million, Africa & Asia-Pacific $428 million and Europe $258 million. The fastest-growing piece is data centres — CoreSite, bought for $10.1 billion in 2021 — with $313 million of revenue, up 17.4%, as cloud and AI customers need space close to users.

## The numbers right now

In the quarter to 30 September 2026 revenue was $2.79 billion and AFFO (the cash-profit measure REITs use) was $2.87 per share, up 3.2%. Net income fell 14.4% to $781 million, mainly because of losses on equity investments. Growth in the core tower business is slow: organic tenant billings rose just 1.1%, and US & Canada revenue fell 3.3%. Management raised its 2026 outlook for the third time this year, to AFFO of $11.04–11.16 per share. The company bought back about $315 million of its own shares in the quarter, the board added another $2 billion to the buyback, and it agreed to sell its Canadian business for about $202 million.

## The stock's story

American Tower was a long-term winner as smartphones spread, and it closed at an all-time high on 8 September 2021. Then rates rose: towers are financed with debt and valued like bonds, so the stock fell about 26% in 2022 and another 20% in 2024. The company has been simplifying — it sold its India business to Brookfield in 2024 and is now leaving Canada — while CEO Steve Vondran pushes data centres. Over the past year the share price moved between $160 and $202.50.

## Competitors

In towers its main rivals are Crown Castle and SBA Communications in the US and Cellnex in Europe; carriers can also build or sell their own towers. In data centres CoreSite competes with Equinix and Digital Realty. American Tower's edge is its global scale and long leases with the biggest operators.

## Main risks

- **Interest rates:** with $37.6 billion of total debt, higher rates raise borrowing costs and usually pull REIT prices down.
- **Few big customers:** a handful of carriers pay most of the rent; mergers or a weak operator can cut tenants.
- **Slow core growth:** organic tenant billings grew only 1.1% in Q3 2026, so the story now leans on data centres.
- **Emerging markets:** currency swings and customer troubles in Latin America, Africa and Asia hit results in dollars.

## What it means if you buy regularly

American Tower is a steady-income stock rather than a fast grower: the dividend has risen year after year, and reinvesting it adds up over a decade. But it is very sensitive to interest rates — it already lost more than a third of its value from the 2021 peak, and regular buyers spent years adding at lower prices. Buying monthly spreads that rate risk over time; it does not remove it. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
