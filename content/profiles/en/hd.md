**Home Depot** is the world's largest home-improvement retailer: the big orange warehouse stores where people buy paint, timber, tools, appliances and garden supplies. About half of its sales come from professionals — builders, roofers, plumbers and renovators — and the rest from homeowners doing projects themselves. It runs about 2,370 stores in the US, Canada and Mexico, plus a growing network of trade-supply branches.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Sales: $164.7 billion in fiscal 2025 (to 1 February 2026), +3.2%; net earnings $14.2 billion.
> - In the August–October 2026 quarter sales rose 4.9% to $43.4 billion; comparable sales grew 2.2%.
> - The quarterly dividend was raised to $2.33 per share in February 2026 — the 156th dividend in a row.
> - Richard McPhail, the long-time finance chief, became CEO on 23 November 2026; Ted Decker stayed as chair.

## How it makes money

Home Depot earns its money as a retailer: it buys products in huge volumes from suppliers and sells them at a markup in its stores and online. It serves two groups. Do-it-yourself customers come for weekend projects, paint and gardening; professional customers buy in bulk, often every week, and expect delivery to the building site. To win more of the pro market, Home Depot bought **SRS Distribution**, a supplier of roofing, pool and landscaping products, in 2024, and **GMS**, a distributor of wallboard and ceilings, in 2025. These businesses sell through more than 1,300 branches. In the third quarter of fiscal 2026 the average purchase in the stores was $93.09, up 3%, while the number of transactions was almost unchanged.

In the quarter to 1 November 2026 Home Depot's sales were $43.4 billion, up 4.9% from a year earlier, and net earnings were $3.7 billion, or $3.72 per share (adjusted $3.86, +3.2%). Operating margin was 12.6%. For the whole of fiscal 2026 the company expects sales to grow about 4.5%, comparable sales about 1.4% and adjusted earnings per share about 2% from $14.69 in fiscal 2025.

Fiscal 2025 was a year of slow growth: sales rose 3.2% to $164.7 billion, but net earnings fell 4.4% and diluted EPS was $14.23, partly because fiscal 2024 had an extra 53rd week. At the start of 2026 the dividend went up 1.3% to $2.33 a quarter, or $9.32 a year.

## The stock's story

Home Depot was founded in Atlanta in 1978 by Bernie Marcus and Arthur Blank and grew in the 1980s and 1990s into one of the biggest US retailers. The 2007–2009 housing crash hit it hard: home building collapsed and the shares lost a large part of their value. The long recovery that followed, plus big buybacks and rising dividends, made it one of the best-performing large stocks of the 2010s. During the pandemic in 2020–2021 people stuck at home renovated in record numbers, and sales jumped. When mortgage rates rose sharply in 2022, fewer homes were sold and fewer big projects started, so growth stalled and the share price moved mostly sideways for several years. In November 2026 the company named CFO Richard McPhail as CEO after Ted Decker returned from a medical leave.

## Competitors

The closest rival is **Lowe's**, the second-largest US home-improvement chain, which is more focused on DIY shoppers. Home Depot also competes with **Walmart**, **Amazon** and **Costco** for tools and household goods, with Menards and regional hardware chains, and in the pro market with building-supply distributors such as **Builders FirstSource** and **Ferguson**.

## Main risks

- **Housing and interest rates:** high mortgage rates mean fewer home sales and fewer large renovations, which slows sales.
- **The economy:** big projects are easy to postpone when people worry about their jobs or prices.
- **Debt from acquisitions:** the SRS and GMS deals added debt; net interest expense was expected at about $2.2 billion in fiscal 2026.
- **Thin growth:** comparable sales grew only 0.3% in fiscal 2025, so earnings depend on cost control.
- **Tariffs and costs:** many products are imported, so trade duties and wage rises can squeeze margins.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Home Depot is a large, profitable company with a long dividend record, but its results move with the housing market, and the shares can stay flat for years when rates are high. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more shares when the price is low and fewer when it is high, without trying to guess the next turn in housing. Many people hold one company only as part of a wider portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
