**HDFC Bank** is India's largest private-sector bank. It takes deposits from tens of millions of people and businesses and lends the money out as home loans, car and personal loans, credit cards and loans to companies, earning the difference in interest. Since it absorbed its former parent, the mortgage lender HDFC Ltd, in July 2023, it has been one of the biggest banks in Asia. The shares trade in Mumbai as HDFCBANK and in New York as HDB.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Net profit: ₹19,510 crore in July–September 2026 (standalone), +4.7% on the year; ₹20,700 crore for the whole group (+5.6%).
> - Net interest income — the bank's core earnings from lending — rose 9.7% to ₹34,619 crore.
> - Deposits grew 18.8% and loans about 16% on the year; bad loans fell to 1.11% of all loans (net 0.37%).
> - Anup Bagchi took over as managing director and CEO from Sashidhar Jagdishan on 27 October 2026.

## How it makes money

Most of HDFC Bank's income is interest: it pays savers less than it charges borrowers. The gap, measured as the net interest margin, was 3.16% of total assets in July–September 2026. On top of that it earns fees from cards, payments, loan processing and selling insurance and mutual funds, plus gains from trading bonds. Retail customers are the core: home loans (much bigger since the HDFC Ltd merger), personal loans and India's largest credit-card book. The group also owns stakes in HDB Financial Services (consumer loans), HDFC Life (insurance), HDFC ERGO (general insurance), HDFC Securities and HDFC Asset Management.

In the July–September 2026 quarter (the second quarter of India's financial year 2026–27) net interest income grew almost 10%, but profit grew only 4.7% because gains from bond trading fell to ₹780 crore from ₹2,390 crore a year earlier; without them, profit rose about 12.6%. Deposits reached about ₹33.2 lakh crore (₹33.2 trillion), up 18.8%. Loans to small and mid-sized firms grew fastest (+23.5%), retail loans +11.7%. The share of cheap current and savings deposits (CASA) slipped to about 31.6% from about 33.9% a year earlier, which squeezed the margin. Capital adequacy stood at about 18.9%.

## The stock's story

HDFC Bank was set up in 1994 by HDFC Ltd, the housing-finance company, as one of the first private banks allowed after India opened its economy. Under Aditya Puri, CEO from 1994 to 2020, it grew into India's most valuable bank with an unusually steady record of profit growth and few bad loans, and its shares became a core holding for Indian investors. Sashidhar Jagdishan led the bank from October 2020 to October 2026. The merger with HDFC Ltd took effect on 1 July 2023; it made the bank much bigger but left it with more loans than deposits to fund them, and the shares lagged other Indian banks while it rebuilt its deposit base. In 2026 the stock had a weak year: by 19 October 2026 it was down about 28% since the start of the year.

## Competitors

HDFC Bank's main rivals are ICICI Bank, Axis Bank and Kotak Mahindra Bank among private banks, and the state-owned State Bank of India, the country's biggest lender. In payments and consumer loans it also competes with fintechs such as Paytm and PhonePe and with non-bank lenders such as Bajaj Finance.

## Main risks

- **Deposit competition:** after the merger the bank needs to grow deposits fast; paying more for them lowers the margin, which fell to a record low in July–September 2026.
- **Interest rates:** when the Reserve Bank of India cuts rates, loan yields fall faster than deposit costs.
- **Bad loans:** unsecured personal and card loans and small-business loans can sour in a slowdown.
- **Leadership change:** a new CEO took over in October 2026 and has to prove the strategy.
- **The rupee and India's economy:** for foreign buyers, a weaker rupee cuts returns in dollars.

## What it means if you buy regularly

HDFC Bank is a large, profitable bank in a fast-growing economy, but its shares can go for years without beating the market, as they did after the 2023 merger. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more shares when the price is low and fewer when it is high, without trying to time the market. Many people hold one bank only as part of a wider portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
