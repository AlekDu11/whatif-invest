**Basic Attention Token (BAT)** is the token of the advertising system built into the Brave web browser. Brave blocks ordinary ads and trackers by default; people who choose to see Brave's own private ads earn BAT, and advertisers pay for those ads in BAT. ## How it works

Brave is a free browser based on Chromium, the same engine as Google Chrome. Instead of tracking people across the web, it matches ads to interests on the user's own device, so personal data does not leave the browser. Users who turn on Brave Rewards see occasional private ads and receive BAT for them; advertisers buy the campaigns. Users can keep the BAT in Brave's own wallet or, in many countries, through a linked custodial partner.

## What the token is used for

Advertisers use BAT to pay for campaigns, and users earn it for viewing ads. People can tip websites, YouTube channels and other creators who have signed up with Brave, or send BAT to friends. The Brave Rewards partner programme adds more uses: according to a June 2026 update, BAT can pay for .brave web domains, perks and items in some games, purchases through shopping partners and tips to livestreamers. Demand for BAT therefore depends on how much advertising runs through Brave and how many people actually use the token.

## Its story so far

Brave Software was founded in 2015 by Brendan Eich, who earlier co-founded Mozilla, and Brian Bondy. In May 2017 BAT raised about $35 million in a token sale that sold out in under a minute, one of the most talked-about sales of that boom. Brave Rewards with BAT payments for ads launched in 2019. BAT reached its highest price in November 2021, during the crypto bull market, and fell sharply in 2022. Brave kept growing: it reported 101 million monthly active users and about 42 million daily users as of 30 September 2025, and its search engine handled more than 1.6 billion queries a month.

## Alternatives

BAT has few direct twins: most crypto projects about advertising or "attention" are much smaller. Its real competition is the ad business of Google, Meta and other big networks, which pays creators in ordinary money. Among browsers, Chrome, Safari, Edge and Firefox compete with Brave for users, and they have no token at all.

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** BAT has fallen by more than 90% from its peak and can fall again.
- **Dependence on one company:** Brave decides how Rewards work, in which countries, and how much BAT users get.
- **No claim on profits:** browser growth does not automatically raise demand for the token.
- **Advertising market:** ad budgets shrink in downturns, and big ad networks can outspend Brave.
- **Regulation:** rules for crypto rewards, wallets and exchanges differ by country and can change quickly.

## What it means if you buy regularly

BAT has spent most of the last five years well below its 2021 peak, so regular buying would have spread purchases over a long sideways-to-falling period. Buying a fixed amount over time removes the stress of picking one moment, but it pays off only if demand for the token grows. Because the browser's success and the token's price are not the same thing, many people keep BAT to a small part of their crypto. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
