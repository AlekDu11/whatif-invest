**Marvell** makes the chips that move data inside AI data centers: the parts that turn electrical signals into light so servers can talk over fibre, network switches, and custom AI processors designed together with cloud giants such as Amazon and Google. In the quarter to August 2026 data centers brought 79% of its revenue, and in the year to November 2026 the stock swung from about $71 to $330 and back to $149.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: a record $2.739 billion in the quarter to 1 August 2026, +37% a year; data center sales +46%.
> - Guidance: about $3.15 billion for the quarter to 31 October 2026; management's outlook is roughly $12 billion for fiscal 2027 and $18 billion for fiscal 2028.
> - On 2 November 2026 Marvell agreed to buy optics maker Lumentum in an all-stock deal worth about $115 billion.

## How it makes money

Marvell designs chips and has them made by contract factories such as TSMC. In the quarter to 1 August 2026, data center products brought $2.17 billion. The biggest piece, by The Next Platform's estimate about $1.4 billion, is electro-optics: digital signal processors and drivers inside the optical modules that link AI servers. The second engine is custom silicon — chips Marvell co-designs for one big customer, such as Amazon Web Services' AI and server processors. The remaining $568 million came from older businesses: enterprise networking, telecom carriers, consumer and industrial chips.

In February 2026 Marvell completed the purchase of Celestial AI, a start-up that uses light to connect AI chips directly ("photonic fabric"), paying $1 billion in cash plus shares.

Quarterly revenue of $2.739 billion came with a GAAP gross margin of 53.1% (58.9% adjusted) and GAAP net income of $308 million, or $0.33 a share; on an adjusted basis earnings were $0.94 a share. Operating cash flow was $605.5 million. Marvell ended the quarter with $3.93 billion of cash and $4.96 billion of long-term debt, spent $200 million on buybacks.

In the Lumentum announcement Marvell said its October-quarter revenue is in line with the $3.15 billion guidance, narrowing the range to ±2%.

## The stock's story

In 2026 Marvell was an AI darling and a punching bag in the same year. In late 2025 the shares traded as low as $70.69; on 4 June 2026 they closed at $316.43, a record at that time, as AI optics orders surged. Then it fell 13.2% on 29 October 2026 on reports of a Lumentum deal, 10% the next day and 12.7% on 2 November when the deal was confirmed — 37% down in a month. Investors disliked the price — Marvell holders will own only about 67% of the combined company — and a mid-November chip sell-off added to the slide. Even so, on 2 November the shares were still up about 94% over twelve months.

## Competitors

In custom AI chips and data center networking the main rival is Broadcom, which is larger and works with Google, Meta and others; Taiwan's Alchip and MediaTek also design custom chips. Nvidia sells its own networking gear, Credo competes in connectivity chips, and in optics Coherent and Lumentum (which Marvell agreed to buy in November 2026) are the big names.

## Main risks

- **Customer concentration:** a few cloud giants buy most of the data center chips; losing one custom design to Broadcom or an in-house team would hurt.
- **The Lumentum deal:** it is huge, paid in shares that dilute current holders, and needs shareholder and regulatory approval, with closing expected only in the first half of 2028.
- **AI spending cycle:** if cloud companies slow their data center budgets, Marvell's fastest-growing products slow first.
- **Valuation:** in early November 2026 the stock traded at about 16 times trailing sales versus about 3 times for the S&P 500, so disappointments are punished hard.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Marvell can lose a third of its value in a month on one headline, as it did in autumn 2026. Regular buying averages entry prices across such moves, but a single chip stock tied to a few customers and one huge merger carries real company risk. Many people hold a stock like this only as a small part of a wider portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
