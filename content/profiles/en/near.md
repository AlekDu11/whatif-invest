**NEAR Protocol** is a blockchain designed to feel like a normal app: human-readable account names instead of long codes, blocks every 0.6 seconds, and swaps between dozens of other blockchains from one account. It was founded in 2018 by Illia Polosukhin — a co-author of the Google paper that gave birth to modern AI chatbots — and Alexander Skidanov.

## How it works

NEAR splits its network into parallel pieces called shards (the "Nightshade" design), so it can grow by adding shards instead of slowing down. It went from 6 to 9 shards in late 2025, and in 2026 it began splitting busy shards and merging quiet ones automatically. The NEAR token pays transaction fees and storage, and holders stake (lock up) NEAR with validators who secure the network and earn new tokens. Most of each fee is burned.

## Why it matters

NEAR's main product today is **NEAR Intents**: you say what you want ("swap my bitcoin for USDC"), and a network of solvers finds the route. Since late 2024 it has handled over $22 billion of cross-chain swaps across 35+ blockchains, according to Nansen. One surprising fact: in Q2 2026 a single app, HOT Wallet, made about 65% of all NEAR transactions. In October 2025 the network cut its maximum yearly inflation from 5% to about 2.5%, although the change did not reach a two-thirds majority in the validator poll.

## Main risks

NEAR competes with Ethereum, Solana and many fast newer chains, and much of its activity depends on a few apps. New tokens are still issued every year, so staking rewards dilute non-stakers. The token is far below its 2022 high and fell about 11% on November 20, 2026 alone. Rules for crypto differ by country. Regular buying smooths the swings but does not remove the risk.
