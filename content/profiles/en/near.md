**NEAR Protocol** is a blockchain built to feel like an ordinary app: readable account names such as alice.near instead of long codes, blocks every 0.6 seconds and swaps between dozens of other blockchains from one account. It was founded in 2018 by Illia Polosukhin — a co-author of the Google paper that gave birth to modern AI chatbots — and Alexander Skidanov. In November 2026 all NEAR tokens together were worth about $3.9 billion, around number 23 among cryptocurrencies.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Blocks arrive about every 0.6 seconds, and the network is split into parallel shards so it can grow without slowing down.
> - NEAR Intents has handled more than $22 billion of cross-chain swaps across 35+ blockchains since late 2024 (Nansen).
> - New-token issuance was cut from 5% to 2.5% a year in October 2025, and in November 2026 holders voted to cut it further to 1.6%.
> - The record price so far is $20.44 (January 2022); in November 2026 NEAR traded more than 80% below it.

## How it works

NEAR splits its network into parallel pieces called shards (the "Nightshade" design). Each shard handles part of the transactions, so capacity grows by adding shards: the network went from 6 to 9 shards in 2025, and in 2026 it began splitting busy shards and merging quiet ones automatically. Instead of miners, validators lock up (stake) NEAR to produce blocks and earn newly issued tokens. Accounts can have several keys with different permissions, which makes wallets easier and safer for beginners.

## What the token is used for

NEAR pays transaction fees and the storage that apps and accounts use on the blockchain, and most of each fee is burned — destroyed for good. Holders stake NEAR with validators to secure the network and earn rewards, and staked NEAR gives voting power in the "House of Stake" governance. NEAR's main product today is **NEAR Intents**: you say what you want ("swap my bitcoin for USDC"), and a network of solvers finds the best route across chains. The team also builds tools for AI agents that use crypto.

## Its story so far

Polosukhin and Skidanov started NEAR in 2018 as an AI company and turned to blockchain when they found paying people worldwide in crypto too hard. The mainnet launched in 2020, and in the 2021 boom NEAR raised hundreds of millions of dollars for its ecosystem; the price peaked at $20.44 in January 2022 and then fell sharply in the 2022 crypto crash. NEAR Intents launched in late 2024 and became its fastest-growing product. In October 2025 the network halved its maximum yearly inflation to about 2.5%, although that change did not reach a two-thirds majority in the validator poll. On 2 November 2026 holders approved proposal HSP-029 with 99.9% of the votes cast, cutting issuance to 1.6% over 24 months.

## Alternatives

Among fast smart-contract blockchains NEAR competes with [Ethereum]({lang}/eth/) and its layer-2 networks, [Solana]({lang}/sol/), [Avalanche]({lang}/avax/), [Sui]({lang}/sui/) and [Aptos]({lang}/apt/). For cross-chain swaps its intents compete with bridges and exchange aggregators.

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** NEAR has fallen more than 80% from its record and dropped about 11% on a single day in November 2026.
- **Competition:** many fast blockchains chase the same developers and users.
- **Concentration of activity:** in Q2 2026 one app, HOT Wallet, made about 65% of all NEAR transactions.
- **Dilution:** new tokens are still issued every year, so holders who do not stake lose share, even after the cuts.
- **Regulation and technology:** crypto rules differ by country, and bridges and smart contracts can be hacked.

## What it means if you buy regularly

NEAR's history shows a fast rise in 2021 and a long slide after it. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more when the price is low and less when it is high, without trying to guess the bottom — but it does not remove the risk of losses. Many people keep smaller coins like NEAR to a small part of their savings that they can afford to see fall sharply. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
