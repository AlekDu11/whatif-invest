**Nestlé** is the world's largest food and drinks company — the Swiss group behind Nescafé, Nespresso, KitKat, Maggi and Purina pet food, sold in almost every country on earth. It was founded in Vevey on Lake Geneva in 1866 and is one of the heaviest stocks in the Swiss market.

## How it makes money

Nestlé sells branded products through supermarkets, cafés and online: coffee is its largest category, followed by pet care, nutrition (baby food and medical nutrition), frozen and prepared food, confectionery and water. It earns through scale and brand power — big brands let it charge a bit more and win shelf space. In the first nine months of 2026 sales were CHF 65.9 billion, with organic growth of 3.7%, of which 1.9% came from selling more and 1.8% from higher prices.

## Why it matters

Nestlé is a classic "defensive" stock: people keep buying coffee and pet food in a recession, so its earnings usually swing less than the market. It has raised its dividend every year for three decades. The company is in a reset under CEO Philipp Navratil, who took over in September 2025: it plans to cut about 16,000 jobs over two years and is selling its mainstream vitamins business for $1 billion.

## Main risks

Nestlé's shares fell sharply in 2024–2025 as growth slowed and buyers switched to cheaper store brands. Coffee and cocoa prices can squeeze margins, and weight-loss drugs may change how much people snack. About 98% of sales are outside Switzerland, so a strong franc lowers reported results. Management turnover — two CEOs left within about a year — adds execution risk. Buying a fixed amount regularly smooths price swings but does not remove the risk.
