**Trust Wallet Token (TWT)** is the token of Trust Wallet, one of the most widely used self-custody crypto wallets — an app where you hold your own coins with your own keys instead of leaving them on an exchange. Trust Wallet has belonged to Binance since 2018, and TWT is worth {{mcap:twt}} in total today ({{rank:twt}} among cryptocurrencies). It gives holders discounts and rewards inside the app, not a share of the company.

## How it works

Trust Wallet is a free mobile app and browser extension that stores the private keys for your coins on your own device and works with dozens of blockchains. From it you can send crypto, swap tokens, stake and open decentralized apps. TWT itself is a BEP-20 token on BNB Smart Chain. It is not needed to use the wallet; it is an optional extra that unlocks better terms inside it.

## What the token is used for

According to the TWT litepaper (September 2025), holders can lock TWT to raise their level in the Trust Premium loyalty programme and earn rewards, get discounts on swap fees, pay network gas fees with TWT at a discount, get access to some services such as Trust Alpha token launches, and vote on community proposals (one address, one vote). The litepaper stresses that holding TWT gives no ownership, equity or debt claim on Trust Wallet. So demand for TWT rises and falls with the wallet's activity and with how attractive these perks are.

## Its story so far

Trust Wallet was created in 2017 by Viktor Radchenko and was bought by Binance in July 2018. TWT appeared in 2020 and was spread mainly through airdrops; in October 2020 almost 89 billion tokens were burned, leaving a fixed supply. In late 2022, after the collapse of the FTX exchange, many people moved coins to self-custody wallets and TWT rose to its highest levels, then fell back. In September 2025 a new litepaper and roadmap added utilities, and TWT jumped about 49% in a day. In December 2025 a compromised update (version 2.68) of the Trust Wallet browser extension let attackers drain about $7 million from users, and the company set up a process to compensate them.

## Alternatives

Other self-custody wallets compete for the same users, most of them without a token: MetaMask, Phantom, Coinbase Wallet and the Binance Wallet inside the Binance app. Among exchange-linked tokens, [BNB]({lang}/bnb/) is the main token of the wider Binance ecosystem and is used far more widely than TWT.

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** small utility tokens can fall by 70–90%, and TWT has done so before.
- **Dependence on one company:** Trust Wallet and Binance decide how useful TWT is and can change the perks at any time.
- **No claim on profits:** TWT does not give a share of Trust Wallet's revenue or business.
- **Security incidents:** hacks or fake versions of the app can hurt trust in the wallet and the token.
- **Regulation:** rules for Binance and for wallets differ by country and can change quickly.

## What it means if you buy regularly

TWT has fallen over the past five years, so regular buying would have spread purchases over a long decline rather than protected you from it. Buying a fixed amount over time removes the stress of picking a single moment, but it works only if the asset recovers. For a niche token tied to one app, many people keep only a very small part of their crypto in it. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
