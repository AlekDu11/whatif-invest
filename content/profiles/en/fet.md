**FET** is the token of the Artificial Superintelligence Alliance (ASI), a group of crypto projects built around Fetch.ai, a British company that develops autonomous "AI agents" — small programs that can search, negotiate and pay on someone's behalf. In 2024 the SingularityNET and Ocean Protocol tokens were merged into FET, which made it one of the best-known AI coins. Its total value is {{mcap:fet}} today ({{rank:fet}} among cryptocurrencies).

## How it works

Fetch.ai runs its own proof-of-stake blockchain built with the Cosmos SDK; FET also exists as a token on Ethereum and can be moved between the two. Developers publish AI agents in a directory called Agentverse, where agents can find each other, talk and settle small payments. ASI:One, a chat assistant launched in beta in 2025, is meant to route a user's request to the right agents. Validators who lock up FET keep the chain running and earn rewards.

## What the token is used for

FET pays fees on the Fetch.ai network and for services in the ASI ecosystem — registering agents, using AI models and computing power. Holders can stake it with validators for a yield and vote on proposals. The alliance plans its own chain, ASI:Chain, where FET would be the fuel for transactions; it was still in test stages in 2026. Much of the demand, however, comes from traders betting on the "AI crypto" theme rather than from real usage.

## Its story so far

Fetch.ai was founded in 2017 in Cambridge, UK, by a team led by Humayun Sheikh, an early investor in DeepMind. FET was sold on Binance Launchpad in February 2019. During the AI boom that followed ChatGPT, it rose sharply and peaked at about $3.45 in March 2024 — the same month Fetch.ai, SingularityNET and Ocean Protocol announced their alliance. Token conversions began on 1 July 2024, and CUDOS joined later that year. On 9 October 2025 the Ocean Protocol Foundation withdrew; Fetch.ai accused it of selling converted tokens without disclosure, Ocean denied this, and the case went to a US court. By late 2026 FET traded far below its 2024 peak.

## Alternatives

The best-known AI crypto rival is [Bittensor]({lang}/tao/), a network that rewards machine-learning models. [Virtuals Protocol]({lang}/virtual/) focuses on AI agents with their own tokens, while [Render]({lang}/render/) and [io.net]({lang}/io/) sell computing power for AI. [Ocean Protocol]({lang}/ocean/) went its own way after leaving the alliance.

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** AI tokens rise and fall with the hype around AI; FET has lost most of its value from the 2024 peak.
- **Usage is unproven:** few agents generate steady paid demand for FET so far.
- **Alliance disputes:** Ocean's exit and the lawsuit showed that the partners' interests can split.
- **Supply and governance:** the 2024 merger created new tokens, and a small group of foundations and insiders controls key decisions.
- **Regulation and security:** bridges between chains and changing crypto rules add technical and legal risk.

## What it means if you buy regularly

FET has had long rallies and deep falls tied to the AI theme. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your entry price over time, but it does not protect you if the project fails to attract real users. Many people keep a single theme token like FET to a small share of savings they can afford to see fall sharply. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
