**QBE Insurance Group** is one of Australia's largest companies and one of the world's big commercial insurers. Based in Sydney, it insures businesses, farms, ships, buildings and workers in North America, the UK and Europe (including through Lloyd's of London) and Australia and the Pacific, and it also sells reinsurance — insurance for other insurers. Its roots go back to 1886 in Townsville, Queensland. QBE reports in US dollars, but its shares trade on the Australian Securities Exchange in Australian dollars.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Gross written premium — the money customers agreed to pay for policies — was about US$23.9 billion in 2025, +7%.
> - Net profit after tax rose to US$2.16 billion in 2025 from US$1.78 billion; adjusted return on equity was 19.8%.
> - The combined operating ratio improved to 91.9%: for every US$100 of premium, about US$92 went on claims and costs.
> - In the first half of 2026 adjusted profit rose 4% to US$1.03 billion, while price increases on renewals slowed to 0.3%.

## How it makes money

An insurer earns money in two ways. First, from underwriting: it collects premiums and, if claims and expenses are lower than the premiums, keeps the difference. QBE's main measure of this is the combined operating ratio — anything below 100% is an underwriting profit. Second, from investing: premiums arrive before claims are paid, so QBE invests tens of billions of dollars in the meantime, mostly in bonds. In 2025 investment income was US$1.63 billion, a return of 4.9%.

QBE is mainly a commercial insurer: property, liability, marine, motor, workers' compensation, crop insurance for US farmers and specialist lines written at Lloyd's. It is organised in three divisions — North America, International and Australia Pacific. In 2025 most of the growth came from North America and International, while the Australian business was roughly flat.

In 2025 the net cost of catastrophes — including the January wildfires in California, Cyclone Alfred in Australia and Hurricane Melissa — was US$751 million, 4.1% of net insurance revenue and well below the US$1.16 billion QBE had set aside for them. The full-year dividend rose about 25% to A$1.09 per share, roughly half of profit. In the first half of 2026 gross written premium was US$15.1 billion (+6% at constant currency), the combined operating ratio was 92.8%, adjusted return on equity was 17.7% and the interim dividend rose 6% to A$0.33. For 2026 QBE aims for mid-single-digit premium growth and a combined operating ratio of about 92.5%. It finished a A$450 million share buyback in April 2026.

## The stock's story

QBE grew over decades through acquisitions around the world, becoming one of Australia's few truly global financial companies. In the 2010s that expansion backfired: losses in North America, profit warnings and a share price that spent years far below its 2007 peak. Andrew Horton, who previously ran the London insurer Beazley, became chief executive in 2021 and focused on cutting costs and exiting weak lines of business. With insurance prices rising across the industry, profits recovered strongly: shares jumped about 7% on 20 February 2026, the day of the 2025 results. In 2026 QBE announced plans to sell its trade credit business and agreed to move about US$1.6 billion of old long-tail claims reserves to a reinsurer.

## Competitors

In Australia QBE competes with [Suncorp](/en/sun/) and Insurance Australia Group (IAG). Globally its rivals in commercial insurance are giants such as [Chubb](/en/cb/), [AIG](/en/aig/), [Travelers](/en/trv/), [Allianz](/en/alv/) and [Zurich](/en/zurn/), plus many specialist syndicates at Lloyd's.

## Main risks

- **Catastrophes and climate:** wildfires, hurricanes, floods and storms can produce billions in claims in a single year.
- **The pricing cycle:** after several years of rising prices, renewal rate increases slowed to 0.3% in the first half of 2026; softer prices squeeze future margins.
- **Old claims:** liability claims can surface years later; if reserves prove too small, profits must be cut.
- **Interest rates and markets:** lower bond yields reduce investment income.
- **Currency:** QBE earns mostly in US dollars and pounds while its shares are priced in Australian dollars.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Insurance shares can look calm for years and then fall sharply after a disaster season or a reserve problem, as QBE did in the 2010s. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your purchases across good and bad periods instead of betting on one moment, and the dividend adds part of the return. Many people hold one company only as part of a wider portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
