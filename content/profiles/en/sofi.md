**SoFi** (short for Social Finance) is an American bank that lives in a phone app. People use it to take personal and student loans, refinance a mortgage, keep a checking and savings account, buy shares or crypto — all in one place. Started in 2011 by Stanford students who refinanced fellow students' loans, it listed on Nasdaq in June 2021 through a merger with a SPAC (a "blank-cheque" shell company) and became a nationally chartered bank in early 2022.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: a record $1.39 billion in Q3 2026, +44% a year; net income $241 million (+73%).
> - Members: 17.1 million (+35%), using 26.7 million products; deposits reached $51.3 billion.
> - The stock hit a record $32.21 in November 2025 and was about 54% below it in mid-November 2026.
> - Management raised its 2026 revenue goal to about $5.04 billion and expects about $6.4 billion in 2027.

## How it makes money

SoFi has three businesses. **Lending** is the biggest: $821 million of revenue in Q3 2026, mostly from personal loans, plus student and home loans. SoFi earns interest on loans it keeps and fees on loans it originates and sells to other investors — it originated $12.6 billion of personal loans in the quarter, $3.6 billion of them for third parties. **Financial Services** ($525 million, +25%) covers bank accounts, investing, credit cards and crypto; the bank deposits also give SoFi cheaper money to lend. **Technology Platform** ($87 million, −24%) is Galileo and Technisys, software that runs cards and accounts for other banks and fintechs.

## The numbers right now

In the quarter to 30 September 2026 SoFi earned $1.39 billion of net revenue, up 44% year on year, and $241 million of net profit ($0.18 per share). Total loan originations rose 71% to $16.9 billion. Members grew to 17.1 million and deposits to $51.3 billion. Tangible book value per share rose 25% to $7.48, and its main capital ratio (CET1) was about 16.9%. For 2026 the company now expects about $5.04 billion of adjusted revenue and about $840 million of adjusted net income; for 2027 it guides to about $6.4 billion of revenue and about $1.28 billion of net income.

## The stock's story

SoFi's ride has been extreme. Before the listing closed, the SPAC shares peaked at a close of $25.78 in February 2021; by December 2022 the price had fallen about 83% as rates rose and the US paused student-loan repayments. Steady growth and the first profits then pushed it to a record $32.21 in November 2025. In 2026 it slid back: a short-seller report in March questioned its accounting (management rejected it), several quarterly reports were followed by drops despite beating forecasts, and the prospect of higher-for-longer interest rates weighed on lenders. In mid-November 2026 the stock was around $15.

## Competitors

SoFi fights on many fronts: digital banks such as Chime, trading apps such as Robinhood, online lenders such as LendingClub and Upstart, and big banks with strong apps such as JPMorgan Chase and Ally. Its edge is the "one app for everything" model: the more products a member uses (about 1.56 each now), the cheaper each customer becomes to win and keep.

## Main risks

- **Credit:** personal loans are unsecured; the annualised charge-off rate was 2.26%, and a weaker US job market would push losses up.
- **Interest rates:** higher rates raise funding costs and cool demand for loans and refinancing.
- **Selling loans:** a big part of growth depends on investors buying SoFi's loans; that market can dry up quickly in a crisis.
- **Valuation and sentiment:** the stock has repeatedly fallen on good news; expectations are high and big one-day moves are common.
- **Shrinking tech unit:** Technology Platform revenue fell 24% in Q3 2026.

## What it means if you buy regularly

SoFi is one of the most volatile stocks on our site: it lost more than 80% in 2021–2022 and more than half again from its 2025 record. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you pick up more shares in the deep dips, but a single fast-growing lender can also stay down for years if credit turns. Many people treat a stock like this as a small slice of a broad portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
