**Microsoft** is the software and cloud giant behind Windows, Office, Xbox, LinkedIn and Azure — and one of the companies betting most heavily on artificial intelligence. Bill Gates and Paul Allen founded it in 1975.

## How it makes money

Microsoft earns in three big ways. Subscriptions to Microsoft 365 (Word, Excel, Outlook, Teams) and LinkedIn bring steady monthly income from businesses and households. The Azure cloud rents out computing power, storage and AI models to other companies. Windows licences, Xbox games and Bing search ads make up the rest.

In the quarter to September 2026 revenue rose 19% to $92.3 billion and net profit rose 32% to $36.6 billion. Microsoft Cloud brought in $63.9 billion, and Azure grew 48%.

## Why it matters

Azure is the world's second-largest cloud after Amazon's AWS, and Microsoft is the main cloud partner of OpenAI, the maker of ChatGPT, under a deal that runs to 2030. Its Copilot AI assistant is built into Office and Windows. One surprising fact: customers have already signed contracts worth $677 billion that Microsoft has not yet delivered — 73% more than a year earlier.

## Main risks

Microsoft is spending tens of billions of dollars a quarter on data centres and chips; if AI demand cools, those costs could weigh on profits. A large part of its order book is tied to OpenAI, so problems there would hurt. Amazon and Google compete hard in the cloud, and regulators in the US and Europe watch its size closely. A big, well-known company can still fall: the share lost more than a quarter of its value in 2022. Buying a fixed amount regularly smooths price swings but does not remove the risk.
