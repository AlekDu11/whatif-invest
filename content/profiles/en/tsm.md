**TSMC** (Taiwan Semiconductor Manufacturing Company) doesn't design chips — it manufactures them for others. When Nvidia, Apple, AMD or Broadcom draw up a new processor, the most advanced ones are almost all made in TSMC's factories ("fabs") in Taiwan. Founded in 1987 by Morris Chang, it trades in Taipei and, since 1997, as an ADR (one ADR = five Taiwan shares) on the New York Stock Exchange under the ticker TSM.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: US$46.7 billion in Q3 2026, +41% a year in dollars; net profit NT$763 billion, +69%.
> - About 51 cents of every dollar of revenue is net profit; gross margin 66.4%.
> - High-performance computing (AI and data-center chips) is 68% of sales; smartphones 21%.
> - The ADR's 52-week high was $487.47; it fell 10.8% in a single day on 5 November 2026.

## How it makes money

TSMC sells finished silicon wafers, priced by how advanced the manufacturing process is. The newer the "node", the smaller the transistors and the higher the price. In Q3 2026, chips made at 3 nanometres brought 33% of wafer revenue, 5 nm 28%, 7 nm 10% and the brand-new 2 nm already 8% — so 79% came from 7 nm and better, where it has few or no real rivals.

By end use, high-performance computing — AI accelerators such as Nvidia's GPUs, Google's TPUs built with Broadcom, and server processors — made up 68% of revenue, and smartphones (Apple's iPhone chips above all) 21%. TSMC also sells advanced packaging, which stacks AI chips next to fast memory, one of the tightest bottlenecks of the AI boom.

## The numbers right now

In the quarter to 30 September 2026 TSMC earned NT$1.49 trillion (US$46.7 billion) of revenue and NT$763 billion of net profit, or US$4.60 per ADR. Its operating margin was 58.7% — very high for a factory business. For Q4 2026 it guided to US$52.3–54.3 billion of revenue with a 65–67% gross margin, and it expects full-year 2026 revenue to grow about 44% in dollars.

It is spending heavily to keep up: the 2026 capital budget is a record US$60–64 billion. About 20 fabs are under construction worldwide, five to six of them abroad; the second Arizona fab was pulled forward to Q3 2027. Management says those overseas plants cut gross margin by about 2 percentage points this year, and 3–4 points later.

## The stock's story

For years TSMC rose and fell with smartphone and PC demand. In 2022, when the chip cycle turned and interest rates jumped, the ADR lost more than half its value. The AI boom then made TSMC the factory almost every AI company needs, and in 2026 the ADR reached a 52-week high of $487.47.

The ride has been bumpy. On 5 November 2026 the ADR fell 10.8% to $353.10 in a chip-sector sell-off with no company-specific news, then partly recovered to $405.49 by 12 November — about 17% below the high.

## Competitors

Samsung Foundry in South Korea and Intel Foundry in the US are the only other companies trying to make chips at the leading edge, and both have struggled to win big outside customers. China's SMIC, GlobalFoundries and Taiwan's UMC compete in older, cheaper processes. TSMC's advantage is that it never competes with its customers and has a long record of hitting yields (the share of usable chips per wafer) on schedule.

## Main risks

- **Geopolitics:** most of its capacity sits in Taiwan, across a narrow strait from mainland China; any blockade or conflict would hit the whole tech industry.
- **Customer concentration:** a handful of giants such as Nvidia and Apple account for a large part of revenue.
- **AI cycle:** if data-center spending slows, demand for its most profitable chips could drop quickly, as the November sell-off showed.
- **Margins abroad:** fabs in the US and elsewhere cost more to run and already dilute profitability.
- **Trade rules:** US tariffs and export controls can change what TSMC may sell and where.

## What it means if you buy regularly

TSMC is the company behind almost every AI chip, but it is still one stock in one country with a big geopolitical risk. Regular buying spreads purchases across the cycle's highs and lows; it does not remove the chance of a long decline. Many people hold it as part of a broader portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
