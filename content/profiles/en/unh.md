**UnitedHealth Group** is the biggest health insurer in the United States and one of the biggest companies in the world by revenue. It has two halves: **UnitedHealthcare**, which insures about 48 million people through employers, Medicare and Medicaid, and **Optum**, which runs doctors' clinics, a huge pharmacy-benefit business and health data and software. In 2025 the stock went through its worst crisis in decades.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: $447.6 billion in 2025, +12%; adjusted EPS $16.35 and GAAP EPS $13.23.
> - In the September 2026 quarter revenue was $112.7 billion, roughly flat, but adjusted EPS jumped to $4.69 from $2.92 a year earlier.
> - In October 2026 it raised its 2026 outlook to adjusted EPS of at least $20.50 (GAAP at least $19.50).
> - Stephen Hemsley, CEO from 2006 to 2017, returned as CEO in May 2025 after Andrew Witty stepped down and the company suspended its 2025 forecast.

## How it makes money

UnitedHealthcare collects premiums from employers, individuals and the US government (Medicare Advantage for older people, Medicaid for low-income people) and pays the medical bills of its members. Its profit is the small gap between premiums and costs: the "medical care ratio" shows how much of each premium dollar goes on care. Optum earns fees: Optum Rx manages prescription drug benefits for health plans, Optum Health runs clinics and care for millions of patients, and Optum Insight sells data, analytics and software to hospitals and insurers. In the September 2026 quarter UnitedHealthcare brought in $86.3 billion of revenue, Optum Rx $39.9 billion, Optum Health $22.9 billion and Optum Insight $5.5 billion (part of Optum's revenue comes from UnitedHealthcare itself).

## The stock's story

UnitedHealth grew from a small Minnesota health-plan company founded in the 1970s into the largest US insurer, and for many years its shares were one of the steadiest winners on Wall Street. In February 2024 a cyberattack on its Change Healthcare unit disrupted payments across the US health system. In December 2024 UnitedHealthcare's CEO Brian Thompson was shot and killed in New York, which put public anger at insurers in the spotlight. In April 2025 the company missed profit forecasts for the first time in more than a decade because medical costs rose faster than expected. On 13 May 2025 CEO Andrew Witty stepped down, Stephen Hemsley took over and the 2025 outlook was suspended; the shares fell sharply, and US authorities were also examining its Medicare business. The company then dropped weaker plans and businesses, and its 2026 results came in above its own early forecasts.

## Competitors

In insurance its rivals are Elevance Health, CVS Health (Aetna), Cigna, Humana and Centene, plus Blue Cross Blue Shield plans. In pharmacy benefits Optum Rx competes with CVS Caremark and Cigna's Express Scripts. Clinics and data businesses compete with hospital groups and health-tech firms.

## Main risks

- **Medical costs:** if people use more care than expected, the thin margin disappears quickly — as in 2025.
- **Government payments:** a large part of revenue comes from Medicare and Medicaid, whose rates Washington sets each year.
- **Regulation and lawsuits:** pharmacy-benefit rules, the DOJ's legal actions about its Medicare business and other probes could cost money or force changes.
- **Reputation:** public anger over denied claims and high prices can lead to political pressure.
- **Size and complexity:** UnitedHealth is both insurer and provider, which critics say creates conflicts of interest.

## What it means if you buy regularly

UnitedHealth showed that even a huge, profitable company can lose a large part of its value in a few months when its business model is hit. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more shares after a fall and fewer after a rise, and the dividend adds a little every quarter. Many people hold one company only as part of a wider portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
