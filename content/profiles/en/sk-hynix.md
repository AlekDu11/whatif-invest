**SK hynix** makes the high-speed memory that sits next to Nvidia's AI processors — and that single product turned it into one of the most profitable chipmakers on Earth. The South Korean company began in 1983 as Hyundai Electronics and joined the SK Group in 2012.

## How it makes money

SK hynix sells two kinds of memory chips: DRAM, the working memory of every phone, PC and server, and NAND flash for storage. The star is HBM (high-bandwidth memory) — stacks of DRAM chips that feed data to AI accelerators very fast. It is hard to make, so it sells at a premium. In 2025 revenue rose 47% to 97.1 trillion won and operating profit more than doubled to 47.2 trillion won, ahead of Samsung for the first time.

## Why it matters

SK hynix led the HBM market with 57% of global sales in the third quarter of 2025, according to Counterpoint Research. The AI boom then pushed results to a new level: in the third quarter of 2026 revenue topped 100 trillion won for the first time (104.6 trillion), and operating profit reached 83.3 trillion won. One surprising fact: that is about 80 won of operating profit on every 100 won of sales. The company says its HBM and DRAM are sold out through 2029 and announced a 100 trillion won shareholder return plan for 2026.

## Main risks

Memory is one of the most cyclical businesses in tech: when supply catches up with demand, prices and profits can collapse, as they did in 2023 when the company lost money. A large share of sales depends on Nvidia and AI data-centre spending. Samsung and Micron are racing to win back HBM orders. Export controls, US–China tension and the won exchange rate add uncertainty. Buying a fixed amount regularly smooths price swings but does not remove the risk.
