**SK hynix** makes the high-speed memory that sits next to Nvidia's AI processors — and that one product turned it into one of the most profitable companies on Earth. In July–September 2026 it kept about 80 won of operating profit from every 100 won of sales, and it says its memory supply is largely booked through 2029.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Q3 2026: revenue ₩104.6 trillion (the first quarter above ₩100 trillion) and operating profit ₩83.3 trillion.
> - In 2025 its operating profit of ₩47.2 trillion beat Samsung Electronics for the first time.
> - Long-term contracts with 14 major customers cover more than half of its capacity for 2027 and 2028.
> - Planned 2026 shareholder returns: about ₩100 trillion in dividends and share buybacks.

## How it makes money

SK hynix sells two kinds of memory chips. **DRAM** is the working memory of every phone, PC and server; **NAND flash** stores data in phones and SSDs, partly through its US unit Solidigm. The star is **HBM** (high-bandwidth memory): stacks of DRAM chips that feed data to AI accelerators very fast. HBM is hard to make, so it sells at a premium, and SK hynix has been the main HBM supplier for Nvidia's AI chips. In the third quarter of 2026 growth came from server DDR5 memory, the start of large HBM4 shipments and 321-layer SSDs for data centres.

In Q3 2026 revenue was ₩104.6 trillion, up 32% from the previous quarter and more than four times the ₩24.4 trillion of a year earlier. Operating profit was ₩83.3 trillion, an operating margin of 80%, and net profit ₩39.8 trillion. At the end of September the company held ₩114.5 trillion in cash against ₩15.8 trillion of borrowings. It expects HBM4 to overtake the older HBM3E in shipments in the fourth quarter and says demand for memory should exceed supply through 2030.

For 2026 SK hynix plans to return about ₩100 trillion to shareholders: at least ₩30 trillion in dividends and at least ₩60 trillion in share buybacks and cancellations. Its Q3 dividend was ₩20,000 per share, half of it a special payout from gains on its stake in the Japanese flash maker Kioxia.

## The stock's story

SK hynix began in 1983 as Hyundai Electronics. After the Asian financial crisis it merged with LG's chip business and became Hynix in 2001, survived years of losses and creditor control, and joined the SK Group in 2012. It was an early bet on HBM, and when the AI boom began in 2023 it was ready to supply Nvidia first. The memory slump of 2023 still brought losses, but in 2025 revenue rose 47% to ₩97.1 trillion and operating profit more than doubled to ₩47.2 trillion. In July 2026 it listed American depositary receipts on Nasdaq at $149 each, giving US investors a direct way to buy the shares ([SKHY page]({lang}/skhy-etf/)).

## Competitors

SK hynix's main rivals are **Samsung Electronics** ([page]({lang}/samsung-electronics/)) and **Micron** in the US — the only other companies that make HBM at scale. Chinese memory makers such as CXMT and YMTC are growing in ordinary DRAM and flash. Its biggest customer, **Nvidia** ([page]({lang}/nvda/)), can also shift orders between suppliers.

## Main risks

- **The memory cycle:** memory is one of the most cyclical businesses in tech. When supply catches up, prices can collapse, as in 2023.
- **Customer concentration:** much of the profit depends on Nvidia and a few cloud giants spending on AI.
- **Competition in HBM:** Samsung and Micron are racing to win back orders, which could squeeze today's high margins.
- **Huge investment:** new fabs in Yongin and Cheongju cost a lot and only pay off if demand stays strong.
- **Politics and currency:** export controls, US–China tension and the won exchange rate add uncertainty.

## What it means if you buy regularly

SK hynix has been one of the world's fastest-rising large stocks, but memory makers have also fallen by half or more in past downturns. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more shares when the price is low and fewer when it is high, without trying to time the cycle. The main shares trade in Seoul in won, so for foreign buyers the exchange rate also changes the result. Many people hold one company only as part of a wider portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
