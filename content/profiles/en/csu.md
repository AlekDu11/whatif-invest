**Constellation Software** is a Toronto company that buys small software businesses and keeps them forever. Each one makes specialised "vertical market" software for a single industry — programs that run a city's utility billing, a golf club, a dental practice, a public-transport timetable or a car dealership. Founded in 1995 by Mark Leonard, it has grown by buying well over a thousand such firms and reinvesting the cash they produce into buying more. Its shares trade on the Toronto Stock Exchange.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: $10.03 billion (US dollars) in the first nine months of 2026, +19% on the year; most of the growth came from acquisitions.
> - Organic growth — from businesses it already owned — was only 2% in the September 2026 quarter.
> - About three-quarters of revenue is recurring maintenance and subscription fees: $2.72 billion of $3.51 billion in that quarter.
> - Founder Mark Leonard resigned as president for health reasons in September 2025; Mark Miller took over.

## How it makes money

Customers pay Constellation mostly every year, not once. In the September 2026 quarter maintenance and other recurring fees brought in $2.72 billion, professional services (installation, training, custom work) $569 million, hardware $131 million and one-off licences $97 million. These programs are hard to replace — a hospital or a town hall rarely switches the software that runs its daily work — so customers tend to stay for many years.

The cash from those businesses goes into buying more of them. Constellation is run as a federation of operating groups — Volaris, Harris, Jonas, Perseus, Vela and others — whose managers look for small firms to buy at sensible prices. It has also spun off two groups as separately listed companies, Topicus.com in 2021 and Lumine Group in 2023, while keeping large stakes.

In the September 2026 quarter revenue rose 19% to $3.51 billion. Net income attributable to shareholders was $212 million, or $10.01 per share, almost flat from $210 million a year earlier. Cash flow from operations was $717 million, up 5%. "Free cash flow available to shareholders", the company's favourite measure, fell 16% to $443 million because of a $117 million revaluation charge linked to minority partners in Topicus; without it, the figure rose about 3%. For the first nine months net income was $853 million, up from $402 million.

## The stock's story

Constellation listed on the Toronto Stock Exchange in 2006 and for almost two decades was one of the best-performing large stocks in Canada, as its buying machine kept compounding. Mark Leonard became known for his annual letters to shareholders and for refusing to sell businesses it had bought. In September 2025 he stepped down as president for health reasons and Mark Miller, the long-time head of the Volaris group, replaced him; the shares fell about 17% that week. Then, in late 2025 and early 2026, investors began to worry that AI tools could let customers build or replace niche software themselves, and the stock fell roughly by half from its 2025 high. The company says it is experimenting with AI but that it has not yet seen customers drop its products because of it.

## Competitors

Constellation competes with many small rivals in each niche, and for acquisitions with private-equity firms that also buy software companies. Among listed companies, it is most often compared with other software groups that serve specific industries or governments, such as [Roper Technologies](/en/rop/) and [Tyler Technologies](/en/tyl/), and with large business-software makers like [SAP](/en/sap/) and [Microsoft](/en/msft/), whose broader products can replace niche programs.

## Main risks

- **AI disruption:** if AI makes it cheap to write or replace specialised software, customers may pay less or switch, and the value of each acquisition could fall.
- **Slow organic growth:** businesses it already owns grew only about 2% in the September 2026 quarter, so overall growth depends on buying more.
- **Competition for deals:** private-equity firms bid for the same companies, which can push prices up and returns down.
- **Life after the founder:** Mark Leonard set the culture and the rules; the company now has to prove they work without him.
- **Currency:** it reports in US dollars, but the shares trade in Canadian dollars, so exchange rates affect what investors see.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Constellation spent years as a steady compounder and then lost about half of its value in a matter of months, which shows that even a high-quality business can be very volatile as a stock. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your purchases across good and bad periods instead of betting on one moment. A single share has also been expensive, so a broker with fractional shares can help with small regular amounts. Many people hold one company only as part of a wider portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
