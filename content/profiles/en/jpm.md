**JPMorgan Chase** is the largest bank in the United States and the most valuable bank in the world: it earned a record $57 billion in 2025 and holds $4.4 trillion in assets. Its roots go back to 1799, and Jamie Dimon has run it since 2006.

## How it makes money

The bank has three big businesses. Consumer & Community Banking runs Chase: current accounts, credit cards, mortgages and car loans for millions of American households through about 5,000 branches. The Corporate & Investment Bank advises companies on deals, raises money for them and trades stocks, bonds and currencies. Asset & Wealth Management invests money for clients. Most profit still comes from net interest income — the gap between what the bank earns on loans and what it pays on deposits — plus fees.

## Why it matters

JPMorgan is often called a "fortress bank": its capital buffer (CET1 ratio of 14.5%) is well above what regulators require, and it has taken over weaker rivals in crises, including First Republic in 2023. It ranks number one in the world for investment-banking fees, with an 8.4% share. One surprising fact: its asset-management arm alone looks after $4.8 trillion, more than the yearly economic output of most countries.

## Main risks

Bank profits follow interest rates and the economy: lower rates shrink lending margins, and a recession means more unpaid loans. The bank plans to spend about $105 billion in 2026, up $9 billion, which squeezes margins if revenue stalls. Rules on capital can tighten, and Jamie Dimon's eventual succession is a question investors watch. In 2008 the share price fell by more than half. Buying a fixed amount regularly smooths price swings but does not remove the risk.
