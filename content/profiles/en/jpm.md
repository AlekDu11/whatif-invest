**JPMorgan Chase** is the largest bank in the United States. Ordinary Americans know it as Chase — current accounts, credit cards, mortgages and car loans — while companies and governments use J.P. Morgan to raise money, trade and move payments around the world. It earned $57 billion in 2025, and Jamie Dimon has run it since 2006.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Net income: $57 billion in 2025; total assets were about $4.4 trillion at the end of that year.
> - In the July–September 2026 quarter it earned $15.3 billion ($5.57 per share) on $50.9 billion of revenue, +8%.
> - Its capital buffer (CET1 ratio) was 13.9% at the end of September 2026, well above the legal minimum.
> - It pays a quarterly dividend of $1.65 per share.

## How it makes money

JPMorgan has three businesses. Consumer & Community Banking (Chase) earned $5.7 billion in the September 2026 quarter on $21.2 billion of revenue: it takes deposits, issues credit cards and lends to households and small firms. The Commercial & Investment Bank earned $8.2 billion on $22.7 billion — advising on mergers, selling shares and bonds for companies, trading in markets ($10.3 billion of revenue) and running payments ($5.6 billion). Asset & Wealth Management, which invests money for funds and rich families, earned $1.8 billion. Across the bank the biggest source is net interest income — the gap between what it earns on loans and bonds and what it pays on deposits: $27.1 billion in that quarter, up 13%. The rest is fees.

In the third quarter of 2026 (reported on 13 October 2026) managed revenue was $50.9 billion, up 8%, and net income $15.3 billion, up 6%. Without a $658 million loss on selling investment securities, profit would have been $15.8 billion, or $5.75 per share. Expenses rose 12% to $27.3 billion. The bank set aside $2.7 billion for bad loans, of which $2.2 billion covered loans actually written off. Return on tangible equity was 20%. Book value was $132.30 per share and tangible book value $112.64. The bank paid $4.4 billion in dividends and bought back only $664 million of its own shares in the quarter.

## The stock's story

The bank's roots go back to 1799. Today's company was formed in 2000, when Chase Manhattan bought J.P. Morgan & Co. In 2004 it merged with Bank One, which brought in Jamie Dimon; he became CEO at the start of 2006. In the 2008 crisis JPMorgan stayed profitable and took over Bear Stearns and Washington Mutual, but its share price still fell by more than half from its 2007 level. In 2012 the "London Whale" trading loss cost it more than $6 billion. In May 2023 it bought most of the failed First Republic Bank. Higher interest rates after 2022 lifted lending profits to records, and by 2025 JPMorgan was the world's most valuable bank. In early 2026 it agreed to become the new issuer of the Apple Card, taking the programme over from Goldman Sachs.

## Competitors

In the US its main rivals are Bank of America, Wells Fargo and Citigroup in consumer banking, and Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley in investment banking and trading. Card issuers such as American Express and Capital One compete for credit card customers, and fintechs and payment companies — PayPal, Block, Stripe — compete for payments. Abroad it faces HSBC, Barclays, BNP Paribas and big Asian banks.

## Main risks

- **Interest rates:** lower rates shrink the gap between what loans earn and deposits cost, and net interest income is about half of revenue.
- **Recession and bad loans:** in a downturn more credit card, car and business loans go unpaid, and provisions rise quickly.
- **Rising costs:** expenses grew 12% in the September 2026 quarter as the bank spent on technology, AI and branches.
- **Rules and capital:** regulators decide how much capital banks must hold, which affects how much can go to dividends and buybacks.
- **Succession:** Jamie Dimon has led the bank for two decades; who takes over, and when, is a question investors watch.

## What it means if you buy regularly

JPMorgan is a strong, profitable bank, but bank shares fall hard in crises — this one lost more than half its value in 2008 before recovering. Buying a fixed amount on a schedule means you buy more shares when fear pushes the price down and fewer when it is high, without trying to guess the moment. A single bank is usually held as one part of a wider portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
