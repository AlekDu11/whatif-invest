**Terra Luna Classic (LUNC)** is the token of the original Terra blockchain — the network that collapsed in May 2022 in one of the biggest crashes in crypto history. After the crash a new chain took the Terra name, and the old one lived on as "Terra Classic", run by its community. ## How it works

Terra was launched in 2019 by Terraform Labs, a company founded by Do Kwon and Daniel Shin. It was built with the Cosmos SDK and used Tendermint proof of stake: validators lock up tokens to confirm blocks and earn rewards. The network's big idea was UST, an "algorithmic" stablecoin. Instead of holding dollars in a bank, UST tried to stay at $1 through a swap: you could always turn $1 worth of LUNA into one UST, or one UST into $1 worth of LUNA, and traders were supposed to profit from fixing any gap. Today Terra Classic still runs on the same software, but it is maintained by volunteer developers and validators, without the company.

## What the token is used for

LUNC pays transaction fees on Terra Classic and is staked by validators and their delegators to secure the network. Holders vote on community proposals, such as changes to the burn tax. Most of the interest in LUNC, though, is speculative: people hope that burning enough tokens and new activity on the chain will push the price up from very low levels.

## Its story so far

Terra grew fast in 2021, helped by Anchor, a lending app that paid about 20% a year on UST deposits. LUNA reached about $119 in April 2022. In May 2022 large withdrawals broke UST's peg to the dollar; the swap mechanism then printed LUNA at a huge rate, its supply jumped to about 6.5 trillion tokens, and about $40 billion of value was wiped out within days. On 28 May 2022 a new blockchain, [Terra]({lang}/luna/), was launched with a new LUNA token, while the old chain was renamed Terra Classic, its token LUNC and its stablecoin USTC. In September 2022 the community introduced a 1.2% burn tax on on-chain transfers, and Binance started burning LUNC from trading fees. In 2023 the tax was lowered to 0.2% to bring back activity. Do Kwon was arrested in Montenegro in March 2023, extradited to the US in December 2024, pleaded guilty to fraud in August 2025 and was sentenced to 15 years in December 2025.

## Alternatives

The closest relative is [Terra]({lang}/luna/), the new chain created after the collapse. Terra Classic is built on the same technology as [Cosmos]({lang}/atom/) and many chains in its ecosystem. As a cheap token driven by its community, LUNC also competes for attention with meme-style coins. For a stable dollar in crypto, people today mostly use stablecoins backed by cash and government bonds rather than algorithmic designs like UST.

## Main risks

- **No company behind it:** the chain depends on volunteer developers and validators; if they leave, development can stall.
- **Huge supply:** trillions of tokens exist, so the amounts burned so far are small compared with the total.
- **Speculation:** the price moves mainly on hype and burn news, and it can rise or fall by tens of percent in days.
- **Legal legacy:** the Terra collapse led to court cases and stricter rules for stablecoins, and that history can still weigh on the token.
- **Exchange risk:** much trading happens on a few exchanges; if they delist LUNC or stop burning, buying and selling gets harder.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your entry price over time, but it does not make a risky coin safe. LUNC has already lost almost all of its value once, and a recovery depends on a small community. If you buy it at all, keep it to a very small share of money you can afford to lose, and follow the chain's governance news. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
