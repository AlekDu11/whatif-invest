**Bittensor** is a decentralized network where people compete to provide artificial-intelligence services — from text generation to price forecasts — and are paid in its token, TAO. Instead of one company owning the AI, many independent "subnets" run their own tasks and the network rewards the best work. ## How it works

Bittensor is split into subnets — over a hundred specialized marketplaces, each focused on one kind of AI work, such as language models, image generation, data scraping or computing power. In every subnet, "miners" do the work and "validators" test and score it. A shared rule set called Yuma Consensus turns those scores into rewards, so better results earn more TAO. Since February 2025 ("dynamic TAO") each subnet has its own token, and TAO holders choose which subnets get more rewards by staking into them. The network runs on its own blockchain, Subtensor, built with Polkadot's Substrate framework.

## What the token is used for

TAO is the reward paid to miners, validators and subnet owners, and the currency used to register on the network and to stake. Holders who stake TAO with validators share in the rewards. Because new issuance halves roughly every four years and the cap is fixed, the supply schedule mirrors bitcoin's. Demand depends on whether people believe the subnets produce useful AI — and on hype around AI in general.

## Its story so far

Bittensor was founded by Jacob Steeves and Ala Shaabana, two former AI researchers, and is supported by the non-profit Opentensor Foundation. The network launched in 2021 and was relaunched on its own chain in March 2023. During the AI boom TAO hit its record of $757.60 in March 2024. In July 2024 attackers stole about $8 million from users' wallets through a poisoned software package, and the network was briefly halted. Dynamic TAO arrived in February 2025, the first halving followed in December 2025, and in 2026 the price fell with the wider crypto market.

## Alternatives

Among crypto-AI projects Bittensor competes with the [Artificial Superintelligence Alliance]({lang}/fet/), decentralized computing networks such as [Render]({lang}/render/) and [Akash]({lang}/akt/), and AI-agent platforms such as [Virtuals Protocol]({lang}/virtual/). Its real competition, though, is the big centralized AI labs and cloud companies.

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** TAO has risen and fallen sharply; today it is {{fromath:tao}} from its record high.
- **Unproven usefulness:** it is hard to measure how much real demand there is for the AI that subnets produce.
- **Concentration:** large validators hold much of the staked TAO and therefore a lot of influence over rewards.
- **Security:** the 2024 wallet theft showed that software and key-handling bugs can cost users money.
- **Regulation:** rules for crypto and for AI services are still being written and differ by country.

## What it means if you buy regularly

TAO has less than three years of trading history, with a sharp rise in early 2024 and long declines since. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more when the price is low and less when it is high, without trying to guess the bottom — but it does not remove the risk of losses. Many people keep tokens like TAO to a small part of their savings that they can afford to see fall sharply. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
