**Bitcoin** is the first cryptocurrency: digital money that no bank or government controls. It was launched in January 2009 by an anonymous creator calling themselves Satoshi Nakamoto, and it is still the biggest crypto asset by far, worth {{mcap:btc}} today ({{rank:btc}} among cryptocurrencies). People buy it mainly as "digital gold" — something scarce that nobody can print more of.

> **Key takeaways**
> - There will never be more than 21 million bitcoins; about 95% of them have already been created.

> - Since January 2024 US spot bitcoin ETFs let people buy it through an ordinary brokerage account.
> - It has fallen by more than 70% several times — big drops are part of its history, not an exception.

## How it works

Every bitcoin transaction is written into a public ledger, the blockchain, which thousands of computers around the world keep in sync. About every 10 minutes "miners" add a new block of transactions and receive newly created bitcoins as a reward. Every 210,000 blocks — roughly every four years — that reward is cut in half. The last [halving]({lang}/bitcoin-halving/) in April 2024 lowered it to 3.125 BTC per block; the next is expected around 2028. Anyone can check the rules, and no single company can change them.

## Why people think it has value

The supply is fixed and known in advance, so nobody can inflate it away. You can hold it yourself with a private key, send it across borders in minutes and you do not need anyone's permission. That makes it attractive as a store of value, especially in countries with high inflation or capital controls. Demand also comes from large investors: spot bitcoin ETFs, funds and some public companies now hold bitcoin on their balance sheets.

## Its story so far

For its first years bitcoin was worth almost nothing; in 2010 someone famously paid 10,000 BTC for two pizzas. It reached about $20,000 in late 2017, then lost more than 80% in 2018. It climbed to about $69,000 in November 2021 and fell about 75% in 2022, when the crypto lender and exchange failures, including FTX, shook the market. In January 2024 the US SEC allowed spot bitcoin ETFs, the April 2024 halving followed, and the price set its record of $126,080 in October 2025 before falling back sharply in 2026.

## Alternatives

As "digital gold", bitcoin competes first of all with real gold. Among cryptocurrencies, [Ethereum]({lang}/eth/) is the main rival for investors' money, but it is a platform for apps and smart contracts rather than pure digital money. For everyday payments in crypto, people mostly use stablecoins pegged to the dollar, not bitcoin.

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** drops of 50–80% have happened several times and can happen again.
- **Storage:** if an exchange fails or you lose your private key, the coins can be gone for good.
- **Regulation:** rules differ by country and can change quickly — from taxes to outright bans.
- **Concentration:** ETFs, a few companies and early holders own a large share, so their decisions can move the price.
- **Technology:** in the long run, new threats such as quantum computers would require upgrades to the network.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Bitcoin's history shows long rises and deep falls. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more when the price is low and less when it is high, without trying to guess the bottom — but it does not remove the risk of losses. Many people keep crypto to a small part of their savings that they can afford to see fall sharply. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
