**Bitcoin** is the first cryptocurrency: digital money that works without a bank or a government. It was launched in January 2009 by an anonymous creator using the name Satoshi Nakamoto.

## How it works

Every transaction is recorded in a public ledger, the blockchain, kept by thousands of computers around the world. New blocks are added about every 10 minutes by "miners", who get newly created bitcoins as a reward. That reward is cut in half roughly every four years — the [halving]({lang}/bitcoin-halving/).

## Why it has value

There will never be more than 21 million bitcoins, and no one can print more. People use it as "digital gold": a store of value that is hard to seize or inflate away. Demand now also comes from big investors through US spot bitcoin ETFs.

## Main risks

Bitcoin is volatile: it has fallen by more than 70% several times and can do so again. Exchanges can fail, and a lost private key means lost coins. Rules differ by country. Buying a fixed amount regularly smooths out the swings but does not remove the risk.
