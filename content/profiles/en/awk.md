**American Water Works** is the largest listed water company in the US: it cleans water, pipes it to homes and businesses and takes the wastewater away, serving about 14 million people in 14 states and on 19 military bases. Water is a local monopoly, so state regulators decide how much it may charge. Founded in 1886, it listed on the New York Stock Exchange in April 2008, when Germany's RWE sold it.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: $1.51 billion in Q3 2026, up from $1.45 billion; adjusted EPS $2.00 vs $1.89 a year earlier.
> - It plans to invest $20+ billion in pipes and plants in 2026–2030 and $48+ billion over ten years.
> - Dividend: $0.895 a share per quarter, with a target of 7–9% growth a year.
> - A $63 billion all-stock merger with Essential Utilities is expected to close by the end of Q1 2027.

## How it makes money

Almost all profit comes from regulated utilities: a state commission sets the price of water so the company can cover costs and earn an allowed return on the money it has invested in its network (the "rate base"). That is the core of the model — the more it spends on replacing old pipes and building treatment plants, the bigger the base it can earn on, but each price rise needs a rate case (a formal request to the regulator). Since January 2026 it has won about $284 million of extra annual revenue this way, and requests worth another $360 million are pending. It also grows by buying small municipal water systems — about 81,000 customer connections so far this year — and runs water services on military bases.

## The numbers right now

In the quarter to 30 September 2026 revenue was $1.51 billion and GAAP earnings were $1.94 a share, flat on a year earlier; adjusted EPS rose to $2.00. Management narrowed its 2026 adjusted EPS guidance to $6.07–6.12 and expects $6.53–6.63 for 2027 on a standalone basis, about 8% growth. Over the last 12 months it earned about $1.14 billion of net profit on $5.34 billion of revenue.

## The stock's story

Water is a slow business, but for years it was a great stock: the price rose every calendar year from 2009 to 2021, often by 20–30%, and closed at a record on 31 December 2021. Then rising interest rates made safe dividend payers less attractive next to bonds, and the stock fell about 18% in 2022 and 12% in 2023. Over the past year it traded between $120.57 and $153.69. CEO John Griffith now leads the merger with Essential Utilities (owner of Aqua and Peoples Natural Gas): Essential holders get 0.305 American Water shares each and will own 31% of the combined company, which keeps the American Water name.

## Competitors

Customers can't switch water supplier, so competition is about buying systems and investor money. Other listed US water utilities include Essential Utilities (soon a partner), California Water Service, American States Water and SJW Group. Its edge is size: it is the biggest of them by market value and customers.

## Main risks

- **Regulators:** if a state allows smaller price rises than requested, profit growth slows.
- **Interest rates:** the investment plan is funded largely with debt; higher rates raise costs and make the dividend less attractive versus bonds.
- **Merger:** combining with Essential needs the remaining state approvals and adds a gas business with its own risks.
- **Weather and water quality:** cool, wet summers cut water use, and stricter rules on contaminants such as PFAS require costly treatment.

## What it means if you buy regularly

American Water is a defensive stock: people pay water bills in any economy, and the dividend has a stated growth target of 7–9% a year. That stability has a price — the shares often look expensive (about 26 times earnings) and can lag for years when rates rise, as in 2022–2023. Regular buying with reinvested dividends suits this kind of slow compounder, but there are no guarantees. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
