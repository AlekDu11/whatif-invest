**Celestia** is a blockchain with one narrow job: it stores and publishes the transaction data of other blockchains, mostly "rollups", so anyone can check it. It went live in October 2023 as the first "modular" network of this kind, and its token TIA is worth {{mcap:tia}} in total ({{rank:tia}} among cryptocurrencies). People follow it as a bet that many future blockchains will rent this service instead of building their own.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Celestia does not run apps or smart contracts — it only guarantees that data was published, and it sells this space for TIA.
> - 1 billion TIA existed at launch; there is no hard cap, but yearly issuance was cut from about 7.2% to about 5% in July 2025 and to about 2.5% in November 2025.
> - Its record high is {{ath:tia}} ({{athd:tia}}); today the price is {{fromath:tia}} from it.
> - Large token unlocks for early investors and the team, starting in October 2024, weighed heavily on the price.

## How it works

Rollups are networks that process transactions on their own and then post a compressed record of them to another chain. Celestia is a place to post that record. Rollups pay to put "blobs" of data into Celestia blocks, and the network makes sure the data is really there. Its key trick is data availability sampling: a light node on an ordinary phone or laptop downloads a few random small pieces of a block and, if they are all there, can be very confident the whole block was published. Celestia is built with the Cosmos SDK and secured by proof of stake. The Matcha upgrade in late 2025 raised the maximum block size from 8 MB to 128 MB.

## What the token is used for

TIA has three uses. Rollups and apps pay fees in TIA for the blob space they use. Holders stake TIA with validators, who produce blocks, and earn newly issued tokens as a reward. And TIA holders vote on network changes — for example the two issuance cuts. Demand for the token therefore depends on how many rollups actually choose Celestia and how much data they post.

## Its story so far

The idea started in 2019 as LazyLedger, a research paper by Mustafa Al-Bassam; the project was renamed Celestia in 2021. Mainnet Beta launched on 31 October 2023 together with a large TIA airdrop to developers and users. In the 2024 crypto rally the price climbed to about $20 in February 2024. From October 2024 big unlocks for early backers began, and by June 2025 TIA had fallen to about $1.32, a drop of about 93%, and it kept sliding in 2026. Co-founder Mustafa Al-Bassam said at the time that the project had a $100 million war chest and a six-year runway. The Lotus (July 2025) and Matcha (November 2025) upgrades cut issuance step by step toward a long-term rate of 1.5% a year.

## Alternatives

Celestia's biggest rival is [Ethereum]({lang}/eth/) itself: since March 2024 Ethereum has its own cheaper "blob" space for rollups. Other data availability networks include [Avail]({lang}/avail/), EigenDA from [EigenCloud]({lang}/eigen/) and NEAR's data layer ([NEAR]({lang}/near/)). Many rollups, such as [Arbitrum]({lang}/arb/) and [Optimism]({lang}/op/), still post their data to Ethereum.

## Main risks

- **Competition:** Ethereum blobs, Avail and EigenDA fight for the same customers, which keeps fees low.
- **Low fee income:** so far the fees rollups pay are small compared with the value of the network.
- **Supply growth:** there is no hard cap, and new TIA keeps being issued to stakers.
- **Unlocks and concentration:** early investors, the team and the foundation hold large shares, and their sales can move the price.
- **Big swings:** TIA lost more than 90% from its 2024 peak within about 16 months.

## What it means if you buy regularly

TIA's short history is mostly a long fall after an early peak, followed by sharp rebounds. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your entry price over time, but it does not protect you if adoption never comes — and with a young token, three years of history says little about the next three. Many people keep such coins to a small part of their savings. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
