**IOTA** is a crypto network run by the non-profit IOTA Foundation in Berlin. It started in 2015 with an unusual goal: to let machines and devices — the "Internet of Things" — send tiny payments and data to each other without fees. Since its big 2025 upgrade it works like a modern smart-contract blockchain, and its focus is on digital trade documents and identity. ## How it works

In the original design every new transaction had to confirm two earlier ones, so users did the work that miners do elsewhere and there were no fees. In practice the network relied on a central "Coordinator" run by the foundation for security, which critics called a weak point. The Rebased upgrade replaced that model: independent validators now confirm transactions, IOTA holders can stake their tokens with them for rewards, and developers write smart contracts in the Move language. Small fees exist now, and part of them is burned.

## Why people think it has value

IOTA's pitch is practical: moving trade paperwork, supply-chain records and digital identities onto an open, tamper-proof ledger. The foundation works with governments and trade bodies on such projects, for example TWIN, an initiative to digitise trade documents across borders. The IOTA token pays network fees, is staked to secure the network and is used in governance votes, so more real use means more demand for it. Its long history and non-profit structure also give it a recognisable brand in Europe.

## Its story so far

IOTA was founded in 2015 by David Sønstebø, Sergey Ivancheglo, Dominik Schiener and Serguei Popov, who sold its tokens in an early crowdsale. In the 2017 crypto boom it became one of the ten largest cryptocurrencies and set its record in December 2017, then lost most of its value in 2018. In February 2020 attackers stole funds through the official Trinity wallet, and the foundation stopped the Coordinator for about two weeks. After years of internal changes and a planned "IOTA 2.0", the team chose a new route and launched IOTA Rebased on mainnet in May 2025.

## Alternatives

Networks with a similar focus on business and real-world data include [Hedera]({lang}/hbar/), which also does not use a classic blockchain, and [VeChain]({lang}/vet/), known for supply-chain tracking. IOTA's new technology is closely related to [Sui]({lang}/sui/), which also uses Move. For general smart contracts, most developers and money are on [Ethereum]({lang}/eth/).

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** IOTA has fallen by more than 90% from its 2017 peak and can fall sharply again.
- **Adoption:** partnerships and pilots do not always turn into heavy everyday use of the network.
- **Change of course:** the 2025 switch to a new design shows the technology can still change in major ways.
- **New supply:** staking rewards create new tokens; if fees and demand stay low, that adds selling pressure.
- **Security and regulation:** past hacks show the risk; crypto rules differ by country and can change quickly.

## What it means if you buy regularly

IOTA's chart is dominated by the 2017 boom and long years well below that level. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your entry price and avoids putting everything in at a peak, but it does not protect you from a coin that never returns to its old highs. Many people keep smaller cryptocurrencies to a small share of their savings. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
