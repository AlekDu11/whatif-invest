## How it works

WLFI is a token on Ethereum. It does not run its own blockchain: it is a voting token for World Liberty Financial, a company based in Miami. Holders can vote on proposals — for example how many tokens to unlock or what to do with fees — but the company's team runs the products day to day. Those products are USD1, a stablecoin backed one to one by US Treasury bills, cash and cash equivalents, and World Liberty Markets, a lending and borrowing app launched in January 2026 where WLFI, USD1, ETH and other coins can be used as collateral.

## Why people think it has value

The case for WLFI rests on the project's business and its connections. USD1 grew quickly and in February 2026 was among the five largest dollar stablecoins; in August 2026 the US banking regulator OCC gave preliminary conditional approval for a World Liberty trust bank to issue and redeem it. In September 2025 holders were asked to back a plan to use fees from the project's own liquidity to buy back and burn WLFI. Many buyers are also drawn by the Trump name; Eric Trump is a co-founder and Donald Trump is listed as "co-founder emeritus".

## Its story so far

World Liberty Financial was announced in September 2024, and the public token sale began in mid-October 2024; at least $550 million was raised across two rounds. Tokens sold then could not be transferred. On 1 September 2025 WLFI started trading on major exchanges at a fully diluted value above $30 billion and fell sharply in its first days. Days later the project blacklisted an address linked to Tron founder Justin Sun, one of its biggest backers, freezing his tokens; he called himself innocent. In March 2026 holders approved a six-month lock-up for some transfers, and in May 2026 early supporters were offered a second unlock: a cliff until 6 May 2028, then daily release until 6 May 2030. Through 2026 the price kept falling and reached new lows in November.

## Main risks

- **Locked supply and unlocks:** most of the 100 billion tokens are locked; every unlock vote can add a lot of selling pressure.
- **Concentration:** the founders and a few large backers hold a big share, and the team can freeze addresses, as it did in September 2025.
- **Politics and conflicts of interest:** US senators have questioned the project's foreign investments, and a change in Washington could hurt it.
- **Weak holder rights:** WLFI gives votes, not a share of the company's profits, and the unlock terms include arbitration and a class-action waiver.
- **Big swings:** the price fell by more than 80% from its first trading day.

## What it means if you buy regularly

WLFI has a short history on the market, and almost all of it has been a decline. Buying a fixed amount regularly averages your price over time, but it does not protect you from large future unlocks or from risks tied to one company and one political family. Many people keep tokens like this to a small share of money they can afford to lose. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
