**Micron** makes the memory chips that every computer, phone and AI server needs to hold data while it works (DRAM) and to store it (NAND flash). The AI boom turned it from a cyclical chip company into one of the most profitable firms on the planet: revenue for the year to September 2026 was $133.2 billion, three and a half times the year before.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Fiscal 2026 revenue: $133.2 billion, +256% a year; GAAP net income $84.97 billion.
> - The quarter to 3 September 2026 alone brought $54.2 billion of sales (+379%); Micron guided to about $61.5 billion for the quarter to November 2026.

## How it makes money

Micron sells memory by the bit, and prices swing with supply and demand. In the quarter to 3 September 2026, DRAM brought 73% of revenue and NAND 26%. The prize is high-bandwidth memory (HBM): stacks of DRAM placed right next to AI processors such as Nvidia's GPUs. HBM grew faster than the rest of the company. In September 2026 Micron said contracts already covered most of its HBM supply for 2027.

The company reports four units. Core data center (server memory and SSDs) was the largest at $18.0 billion in the quarter to September 2026, followed by cloud memory at $16.3 billion, mobile and PCs at $13.1 billion, and automotive and embedded chips at $6.8 billion. Micron has signed 26 multi-year agreements with large customers to soften the cycle.

Fiscal Q4 2026 revenue was $54.2 billion, up 31% from the previous quarter, with an adjusted gross margin of 87%. GAAP net income was $37.7 billion for the quarter. Micron ended it with $73.5 billion of cash and investments against $5.2 billion of debt.

It is spending heavily to make more chips: $10.8 billion of capital spending in the quarter and about $25 billion planned for the first half of fiscal 2027, mostly for new fabs in the US and Asia. The quarterly dividend is $0.15 a share. In September 2026 management said it planned to return more cash from December 2026.

## The stock's story

For most of its history Micron was a boom-and-bust stock: memory prices rose, everyone built factories, prices crashed. It traded below $100 for years until mid-2025. Then the AI build-out created the tightest memory shortage in years. In 2025 the shares fell as low as $192.59; by late June 2026 Micron was valued at about $1.27 trillion, briefly more than Meta and Tesla, and the share price later reached about $1,255, a record at that time.

In autumn 2026 it fell hard. It lost 16.9% in October 2026 alone on fears that tech giants are overspending on AI, news that China's CXMT may ship server memory by the end of 2026, and higher-than-expected inflation. On 14 November 2026 a US court refused to dismiss a class action accusing Micron, Samsung and SK hynix of fixing DRAM prices.

## Competitors

In DRAM and HBM, Micron's rivals are South Korea's SK hynix — the HBM leader — and Samsung, plus fast-growing ChangXin Memory (CXMT) in China. In NAND it competes with Samsung, SK hynix, Kioxia and SanDisk. Its edge is a US base, valued by buyers who want supply outside Asia.

## Main risks

- **The memory cycle:** high prices bring new factories from every player; when supply catches up, prices and profits can collapse, as they have many times before.
- **AI spending:** a large share of demand depends on a few cloud companies and on Nvidia's sales; any pullback hits Micron fast.
- **China:** CXMT is adding capacity; trade rules can cut off markets.
- **Huge capital spending:** tens of billions a year in new fabs pay off only if demand holds.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Micron is one of the most volatile large stocks: in autumn 2026 it nearly halved from its peak within months, after rising more than sixfold from its 2025 low. Regular buying spreads purchases across those swings, but the price you pay at the top of a memory cycle can take years to recover. Many people keep such a stock as a small part of a broader portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
