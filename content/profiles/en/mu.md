**Micron** makes the memory chips that every computer, phone and AI server needs to hold data while it works (DRAM) and to store it (NAND flash). The AI boom turned it from a cyclical chip company into one of the most profitable firms on the planet: revenue for the year to September 2026 was $133.2 billion, three and a half times the year before.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Fiscal 2026 revenue: $133.2 billion, +256% a year; GAAP net income $84.97 billion.
> - Last quarter alone brought $54.2 billion of sales (+379%), and Micron guides to about $61.5 billion for the next one.
> - The stock spent years below $100, peaked at $1,255 within the past year and was near $697 on 17 November 2026 — about 44% below that high.
> - Micron holds roughly 21% of the DRAM market, behind SK hynix and Samsung.

## How it makes money

Micron sells memory by the bit, and prices swing with supply and demand. In the quarter to 3 September 2026, DRAM brought 73% of revenue and NAND 26%. The prize is high-bandwidth memory (HBM): stacks of DRAM placed right next to AI processors such as Nvidia's GPUs. HBM grew faster than the rest of the company, and Micron says contracts already cover most of its HBM supply for 2027.

The company reports four units. Core data center (server memory and SSDs) was the largest at $18.0 billion in the last quarter, followed by cloud memory at $16.3 billion, mobile and PCs at $13.1 billion, and automotive and embedded chips at $6.8 billion. Micron has signed 26 multi-year agreements with large customers to soften the cycle.

## The numbers right now

Fiscal Q4 2026 revenue was $54.2 billion, up 31% from the previous quarter, with an adjusted gross margin of 87%. GAAP net income was $37.7 billion for the quarter. Micron ended it with $73.5 billion of cash and investments against $5.2 billion of debt.

It is spending heavily to make more chips: $10.8 billion of capital spending in the quarter and about $25 billion planned for the first half of fiscal 2027, mostly for new fabs in the US and Asia. The quarterly dividend is $0.15 a share, and management says it plans to return more cash from December 2026.

## The stock's story

For most of its history Micron was a boom-and-bust stock: memory prices rose, everyone built factories, prices crashed. It traded below $100 for years until mid-2025. Then the AI build-out created the tightest memory shortage in years. The 52-week low was $192.59; by late June 2026 Micron was valued at about $1.27 trillion, briefly more than Meta and Tesla, and the share price later reached $1,255.

Since then it has fallen hard. It lost 16.9% in October alone on fears that tech giants are overspending on AI, news that China's CXMT may ship server memory by the end of 2026, and higher-than-expected inflation. On 14 November a US court refused to dismiss a class action accusing Micron, Samsung and SK hynix of fixing DRAM prices.

## Competitors

In DRAM and HBM, Micron's rivals are South Korea's SK hynix — the HBM leader — and Samsung, plus fast-growing ChangXin Memory (CXMT) in China. In NAND it competes with Samsung, SK hynix, Kioxia and SanDisk. Its edge is a US base, valued by buyers who want supply outside Asia.

## Main risks

- **The memory cycle:** high prices bring new factories from every player; when supply catches up, prices and profits can collapse, as they have many times before.
- **AI spending:** a large share of demand depends on a few cloud companies and on Nvidia's sales; any pullback hits Micron fast.
- **China:** CXMT is adding capacity; trade rules can cut off markets.
- **Huge capital spending:** tens of billions a year in new fabs pay off only if demand holds.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Micron is one of the most volatile large stocks: it has nearly halved from its high within months, after rising more than sixfold from its low. Regular buying spreads purchases across those swings, but the price you pay at the top of a memory cycle can take years to recover. Many people keep such a stock as a small part of a broader portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
