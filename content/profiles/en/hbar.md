**Hedera** is a public network for payments, tokens and apps that is run by a council of large companies instead of anonymous miners. It uses its own technology, hashgraph, rather than a classic blockchain, and promises fast transactions with tiny fees fixed in US dollars. HBAR is its coin; in November 2026 all HBAR in circulation was worth about $2.3 billion, around number 37 among cryptocurrencies.

> **Key takeaways**
> - All 50 billion HBAR were created at launch; about 44.3 billion circulate and the rest sits with the Hedera treasury.
> - The network is governed by a council of up to 39 organizations, including Google, IBM, Boeing and Deutsche Telekom.
> - A basic HBAR transfer costs about $0.0001, and fees are set in dollars, not in HBAR.
> - The record price so far is $0.569 (September 2021); in November 2026 HBAR traded about 90% below it.

## How it works

Hashgraph was invented by Leemon Baird, who co-founded Hedera with Mance Harmon. Instead of miners racing to add blocks, the computers in the network constantly "gossip" with each other about new transactions and then agree on their order by a kind of virtual vote. Finality comes in a few seconds, and the design tolerates some dishonest nodes. Hedera runs services for token creation, smart contracts compatible with Ethereum tools, and a "consensus service" that companies use to time-stamp and order their own records. In 2024 the core code was handed to the Linux Foundation as an open-source project called Hiero.

## What the coin is used for

HBAR pays the fees for every transaction, token and smart contract on the network. Holders can also stake HBAR to the network's nodes, which helps secure it and earns rewards. Because fees are fixed in dollars, a busy network does not automatically make each transaction more expensive — but heavy use also does not burn large amounts of HBAR. Demand for the coin therefore depends on how much real activity Hedera attracts and on investors' interest.

## Its story so far

Hedera raised money through token sales in 2018 and opened its mainnet to the public in September 2019. Big names joined the council over time, and the coin hit its record of about $0.57 in September 2021 during the crypto boom. It then fell more than 80% in 2022. In 2024 Hedera moved its code to the Linux Foundation, and in October 2025 the first US spot HBAR exchange-traded fund, from Canary Capital, began trading. In 2026 the price fell again with the wider crypto market.

## Alternatives

For fast, cheap payments and tokenized assets Hedera competes with [XRP]({lang}/xrp/), [Stellar]({lang}/xlm/) and [Algorand]({lang}/algo/). For smart contracts and apps the main rival is [Ethereum]({lang}/eth/), along with its layer-2 networks; [IOTA]({lang}/iota/) also uses a non-blockchain design.

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** HBAR has fallen about 90% from its 2021 record.
- **Treasury supply:** more than 5 billion HBAR are still held by the Hedera treasury and can be released over time.
- **Centralization:** the network nodes are run by council members, so a small group of companies controls it for now.
- **Usage vs. price:** famous council members and pilots do not automatically create demand for the coin.
- **Regulation:** rules for crypto and for tokenized assets differ by country and can change.

## What it means if you buy regularly

HBAR's history shows a sharp boom in 2021 and long periods far below it. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more when the price is low and less when it is high, without trying to guess the bottom — but it does not remove the risk of losses. Many people keep coins like HBAR to a small part of their savings that they can afford to see fall sharply. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
