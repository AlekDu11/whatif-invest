**SMC** is a Japanese company that makes the small "muscles" of automated factories: valves, air cylinders, filters, fittings and sensors that use compressed air to push, grip, lift and move things on production lines. You rarely see its parts, but they sit inside machines that make chips, cars, batteries, food packaging and medicines. SMC is widely described as the world's largest maker of pneumatic equipment.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Net sales: ¥842.5 billion in the year to 31 March 2026, +6.4%; operating profit ¥190.6 billion, almost flat.
> - Profit attributable to shareholders was ¥167.3 billion (+7.0%), or ¥2,640 per share; the dividend was ¥1,000 per share.
> - China (with Hong Kong) was the largest market with ¥234.9 billion of sales, ahead of Europe, Asia outside China and Japan.
> - In the first half of the next year (April–September 2026) sales jumped to about ¥554 billion as factory and chip-equipment orders recovered.

## How it makes money

SMC sells standard automation parts in huge variety — the company offers hundreds of thousands of product variations — to machine builders and factories. The main groups are directional control valves (which switch the air on and off), actuators such as air cylinders (which turn air pressure into motion), and "air line" equipment: filters, regulators, lubricators and fittings that prepare and carry the air. Newer lines include electric actuators, temperature-control units (chillers) for chip-making machines and sensors.

Its customers are spread across industries: semiconductor equipment, cars and electric-vehicle batteries, electronics, machine tools, food and drink, and medical devices. Sales by customer location in the year to March 2026: China ¥234.9 billion, Europe ¥162.3 billion, Asia outside China ¥159.0 billion, Japan ¥158.8 billion, the United States ¥83.7 billion and other regions ¥43.9 billion. SMC sells through about 500 sales offices in roughly 80 countries and regions and has factories in about 30.

In the fiscal year to 31 March 2026 net sales rose 6.4% to ¥842.5 billion. Operating profit was ¥190.6 billion, up only 0.2%, because costs, wages and depreciation rose faster than sales; the operating margin was 22.6%. Ordinary profit grew 12.2% to ¥235.6 billion, helped by foreign-exchange gains, and profit attributable to shareholders rose 7.0% to ¥167.3 billion. SMC is very conservative with money: its equity ratio was about 91% and it has almost no debt.

For the year to March 2027 SMC first guided in May 2026 to net sales of ¥1 trillion. In the first half of that year (April–September 2026) sales reached about ¥554 billion, up roughly 38% from a year earlier, as demand linked to AI and data-centre investment lifted orders.

## The stock's story

SMC was founded in Tokyo on 27 April 1959 as Shoketsu Kinzoku Kogyo ("sintered metal industry"), first making sintered metal filters. It began selling air filter-regulator units in 1961 and air cylinders in 1970, built a global sales network, and changed its name to SMC Corporation in 1986. Through the 2010s the shares climbed as factory automation spread and China became the world's workshop. They reached a high in late 2021, during the post-pandemic boom in chips and factory equipment, then fell through 2022–2025 as Chinese factory spending and semiconductor equipment orders slowed. In 2026 the stock recovered part of that ground as orders picked up again.

## Competitors

SMC's closest rival is Germany's privately owned Festo. Other makers of pneumatic and motion-control parts include [Parker Hannifin](/en/ph/), [Emerson Electric](/en/emr/) (Aventics), IMI (Norgren) and Japan's CKD. In factory automation more broadly it sits next to [Keyence](/en/keyence/), [Fanuc](/en/fanuc/), [Siemens](/en/sie/), [ABB](/en/abbn/) and [Rockwell Automation](/en/rok/).

## Main risks

- **Factory cycles:** orders swing with spending on chip-making equipment, cars and electronics; in a downturn sales and profit can fall quickly.
- **China:** it is SMC's biggest market, so a slowdown there or trade restrictions between China, the US and Japan hit it directly.
- **Electric competition:** some machines replace air cylinders with electric actuators; SMC makes both, but rivals are strong in electric motion.
- **The yen:** most sales are abroad, so a stronger yen lowers reported profit; currency moves also swing ordinary profit.
- **Cost pressure:** in the year to March 2026 rising costs kept operating profit flat despite higher sales.

## What it means if you buy regularly

SMC is a cyclical industrial company: its shares can fall for years when factories stop investing and rise fast when orders return, as in 2022–2026. Buying a fixed sum regularly spreads your entry points across those cycles instead of trying to time them. The shares trade in Tokyo in yen, so for a foreign buyer the exchange rate adds or subtracts on top of the share price. Many people hold one company only as part of a wider portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
