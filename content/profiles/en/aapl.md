**Apple** makes the iPhone, Mac, iPad and Apple Watch — and earns more profit than almost any company in the world by selling services to the people who own them. It was founded in 1976 by Steve Jobs and Steve Wozniak in California.

## How it makes money

In fiscal 2026 (to 26 September) Apple had $478.8 billion in sales and $132.2 billion in net profit. The iPhone alone brought $254.1 billion, or 53% of revenue. Second is Services — the App Store, iCloud, Apple Music, Apple Pay, AppleCare and the fee Google pays to be the default search engine in Safari — with $123.3 billion, about a quarter of sales. Services matter more than the share suggests: they are far more profitable than hardware and are paid again every month.

## Why it matters

More than two billion Apple devices are in active use, and each one is a customer who is likely to buy the next iPhone and keep paying for subscriptions. Apple returns most of its cash to shareholders through dividends ($0.27 a share per quarter) and some of the largest share buybacks in history. One surprising fact: Apple designs its own chips (the M and A series), but almost all of its devices are assembled by partners, mostly in China, India and Vietnam.

## Main risks

Apple depends heavily on one product: a weak iPhone cycle hits the whole company. China is both a key factory and a key market, so trade tensions and tariffs hurt twice. Regulators in the US and Europe are pushing against App Store fees and the Google search deal, which feed the Services business. Apple is also under pressure to show it can keep up in artificial intelligence. Buying a fixed amount regularly smooths price swings but does not remove the risk.
