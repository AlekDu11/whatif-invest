**BlackRock** is the world's largest asset manager: it invests other people's money — pension funds, governments, insurers and millions of ordinary savers — through funds, ETFs and separate accounts. It is best known for **iShares**, the biggest family of exchange-traded funds, and for **Aladdin**, the risk and portfolio software that many other banks and fund firms also rent. The company is based in New York and its shares trade on the New York Stock Exchange as BLK.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Assets under management: $15.49 trillion on 30 September 2026, up 15% in a year.
> - Clients added $205 billion of net new money in July–September 2026 and $869 billion over 12 months; iShares ETFs alone took in about $135 billion in the quarter.
> - Revenue rose 19% to $7.75 billion in the quarter; adjusted earnings per share rose 38% to $15.93.
> - It paid a dividend of $5.73 per share for the quarter and bought back $550 million of its own stock.

## How it makes money

BlackRock mostly earns a small yearly fee on every dollar it manages — often a few hundredths of a percent for an index ETF, more for active funds and private-market funds. In July–September 2026 these base fees and securities-lending income were about $6.0 billion of the $7.75 billion revenue. So when markets rise and clients add money, revenue grows almost automatically; when markets fall, it shrinks. On top of that come performance fees (about $630 million in the quarter, mainly from private markets and hedge-fund-style strategies) and technology revenue from Aladdin and related services ($588 million, +14%). Equity funds are 57% of assets, bonds 22%, multi-asset 9%, cash funds 7%, and alternatives such as infrastructure and private credit about 3%. ETFs make up 41% of everything it manages.

In the third quarter of 2026 GAAP net income was $1.66 billion (+25%) and diluted earnings per share $10.57. The gap to the adjusted $15.93 comes mostly from accounting charges for payments still owed on recent acquisitions. The adjusted operating margin reached 47.8%, up from 44.6% a year earlier. Of the $205 billion inflow, about $175 billion went into long-term funds and $31 billion into cash funds; private markets added about $15 billion, and BlackRock says it raised $68 billion for private markets over 12 months. Its digital-asset products, led by the iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT), held about $80 billion.

## The stock's story

Larry Fink and seven partners started the firm in 1988 inside Blackstone as a bond-investing unit; it took the name BlackRock in 1992, soon split from Blackstone and listed on the New York Stock Exchange in 1999. Two deals made it a giant: Merrill Lynch Investment Managers in 2006 and Barclays Global Investors — which owned iShares — in 2009, at the bottom of the financial crisis. Since 2024 it has moved hard into private markets, buying infrastructure investor Global Infrastructure Partners (GIP), the data firm Preqin and the private-credit manager HPS Investment Partners. Fink has led the company since it was founded.

## Competitors

In index funds and ETFs BlackRock's main rivals are Vanguard (a mutual company with no listed shares), State Street and Charles Schwab, with Invesco close behind. In active funds it competes with Fidelity, T. Rowe Price, Franklin Templeton and the asset-management arms of Morgan Stanley and Goldman Sachs. In private markets it faces Blackstone, Apollo, KKR, Brookfield and Ares; in investment software, firms such as MSCI, Bloomberg and SimCorp.

## Main risks

- **Market falls:** fees are a percentage of assets, so a stock or bond sell-off cuts revenue straight away.
- **Fee pressure:** index funds compete on price, and fees across the industry keep drifting down.
- **Private-markets bet:** the big acquisitions have to deliver; private assets are harder to value and to sell.
- **Politics and regulation:** BlackRock is criticised from the right over climate and ESG and from the left over fossil-fuel holdings, and its sheer size draws regulators' attention.
- **Succession:** Fink was born in 1952, and investors watch who will take over.

## What it means if you buy regularly

BlackRock is in effect a bet on the growth of saving and investing around the world: when markets and inflows rise, its earnings usually rise faster, but in a sell-off its shares tend to fall harder than the market. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more shares when the price is low and fewer when it is high, without trying to time the market. Many people hold one asset manager only as part of a wider portfolio — or simply own its index funds. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
