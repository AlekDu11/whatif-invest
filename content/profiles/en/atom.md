**Cosmos** is a family of tools and a network that let people build their own blockchains and connect them so they can send tokens and messages to each other — the project calls it "the internet of blockchains". ATOM is the token of the Cosmos Hub, the network's first chain, which went live in March 2019. It is worth {{mcap:atom}} today ({{rank:atom}} among cryptocurrencies).

## How it works

Cosmos has three building blocks. The **Cosmos SDK** is a free toolkit for building a blockchain with its own rules and token. **CometBFT** (formerly Tendermint) is the engine that lets a group of validators agree on new blocks within seconds. And **IBC**, the Inter-Blockchain Communication protocol switched on in 2021, lets separate chains pass tokens and data to each other without a central bridge operator; its second version, IBC Eureka, shipped in 2025 to reach networks outside Cosmos such as Ethereum. Each chain keeps its own validators and security — unlike [Polkadot]({lang}/dot/), where chains share one. The Cosmos Hub itself is run by 180 validators who stake ATOM.

## What the token is used for

ATOM secures the Cosmos Hub: holders stake it with validators and receive newly created ATOM plus fees as a reward. Unstaking takes 21 days, and validators who misbehave lose part of their stake. ATOM is also the vote — upgrades and the community pool are decided in on-chain proposals. Its weakness is that other Cosmos chains use their own tokens, so the success of the ecosystem does not automatically create demand for ATOM. Plans to give it a bigger role failed: holders rejected the "ATOM 2.0" plan in November 2022, and Interchain Security, which let the Hub protect smaller chains from 2023, was removed in 2026 after the last of those chains left.

## Its story so far

Jae Kwon, the creator of Tendermint, and Ethan Buchman published the Cosmos whitepaper in 2016, and a token sale in April 2017 raised about $17 million. The Cosmos Hub launched on 13 March 2019, and IBC went live in 2021. That year ATOM reached about $44 in September 2021. In May 2022 Terra, one of the biggest chains built with the Cosmos SDK, collapsed, hurting the whole ecosystem. In November 2023 holders voted to cut maximum inflation from 20% to 10%. In 2025 the developer company Interchain Labs was renamed Cosmos Labs, and the Interchain Foundation began work on a new token model with outside researchers. ATOM kept falling and set new lows in November 2026, more than 95% below its 2021 peak.

## Alternatives

Cosmos's closest rival in connecting many chains is [Polkadot]({lang}/dot/). For building apps, both compete with [Ethereum]({lang}/eth/) and its layer-2 networks and with fast single chains such as [Solana]({lang}/sol/), [Avalanche]({lang}/avax/) and [Sui]({lang}/sui/). Investors who like the Cosmos technology can also look at tokens of chains built with it, such as [Osmosis]({lang}/osmo/), [Injective]({lang}/inj/), [Celestia]({lang}/tia/) or [Cronos]({lang}/cro/).

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** ATOM has lost more than 95% from its 2021 peak; long falls can continue even while the technology is used.
- **Weak value capture:** Cosmos chains do not have to use or pay in ATOM, so the token may not benefit from the ecosystem's growth.
- **Inflation:** new ATOM is created every year, which dilutes holders who do not stake.
- **Governance and execution:** the token model is being redesigned, and past big plans were rejected or dropped; the result is uncertain.
- **Competition and regulation:** Ethereum's layer 2s and fast single chains attract most users, and rules for tokens and staking differ by country.

## What it means if you buy regularly

ATOM shows that widely used technology does not guarantee a rising token price. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more when the price is low and less when it is high, without trying to guess the bottom — but it does not remove the risk of losses. Many people keep crypto to a small part of their savings that they can afford to see fall sharply. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
