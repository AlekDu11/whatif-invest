**Oracle** is the old database company that turned itself into a landlord for AI. For decades it sold the software that banks, airlines and governments use to store their data. Today most of its growth comes from renting out huge data centres full of AI chips to companies such as OpenAI. By 31 August 2026 customers had signed contracts worth $664 billion that Oracle had not yet delivered. To build the capacity, it was spending more than it earned.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: $19.3 billion in the quarter to 31 August 2026, up 30%. Cloud infrastructure alone grew 121% to $7.4 billion.
> - Contract backlog (remaining performance obligations) reached $664 billion, $209 billion more than a year earlier.
> - Capital spending was $28.5 billion in one quarter, so free cash flow was −$5.4 billion. Debt was $125.3 billion at the end of August 2026.
> - The stock closed at $345.72 on 10 September 2025, then fell below $140 by July 2026, about 60% lower.

## How it makes money

Oracle has four businesses. **Cloud** became the largest: $11.6 billion in the June–August 2026 quarter, or 60% of revenue. It has two parts. Oracle Cloud Infrastructure (OCI) rents out computing power and storage, and grew 121%. Cloud applications (Fusion ERP for finance, HCM for HR, NetSuite for smaller firms, Oracle Health for hospitals) grew 10% to $4.2 billion. **Software licences and support**, the classic Oracle Database and Java business, brought in $5.55 billion and shrank 3%. **Hardware** ($774 million) and **services** ($1.41 billion) make up the rest.

The old software business brings in steady cash. The AI cloud needs chips and buildings paid for years before customers pay.

In the first quarter of fiscal 2027 (June–August 2026) Oracle earned $19.3 billion of revenue and $4.76 billion of net income, or $1.56 per share, up 55%. Operating cash flow was $23.1 billion. Capital spending, though, was $28.5 billion, against $8.5 billion a year earlier. It paid a quarterly dividend of $0.50 per share.

For the full fiscal year to May 2027, management expects at least $90 billion of revenue, up from about $72 billion in the 12 months to August 2026, and around $70 billion of capital spending. In fiscal 2026, free cash flow was already −$23.7 billion. To fund the build-out, Oracle said in June 2026 it plans to raise another $40 billion in debt and equity, including $20 billion of new shares. In September 2025 two co-CEOs, Clay Magouyrk and Mike Sicilia, took over from Safra Catz, who became vice chair.

## The stock's story

On 10 September 2025, after reports of a five-year, $300 billion cloud deal with OpenAI, Oracle shares jumped 36% in a single day to a record close of $345.72. Then investors began to worry about the cost. On 11 June 2026, after the annual results and the new funding plan, the stock fell 12% in one session. By July 2026 it was below $140; in the 52 weeks to mid-November 2026 it traded between $109.21 and $250.25.

## Competitors

In cloud infrastructure Oracle competes with Amazon Web Services, Microsoft Azure and Google Cloud, which are all bigger, and with specialist GPU clouds such as CoreWeave. In business applications its rivals are SAP, Workday and Salesforce. In databases it faces Microsoft SQL Server and free open-source options such as PostgreSQL.

## Main risks

- **Customer concentration:** about $300 billion of the backlog is reportedly tied to OpenAI, which still loses money. Oracle's own annual report warns that its largest AI customers might not meet their obligations.
- **Debt and dilution:** borrowing reached $125.3 billion by August 2026, and the new share sale makes each existing share a slightly smaller slice of the company.
- **Thinner margins:** renting out AI chips earns less per dollar than selling software, and the CFO has warned that gross margins will fall in fiscal 2027.
- **AI spending cycle:** if demand for AI computing cools, Oracle could be left with expensive data centres and fewer customers to fill them.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Oracle used to be a steady dividend payer; in 2025–2026 it turned into a big bet on AI demand. In the ten months after its September 2025 peak the stock lost about 60% of its value. Buying regularly spreads purchases across those swings, but the company's results still depend on a few very large contracts. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
