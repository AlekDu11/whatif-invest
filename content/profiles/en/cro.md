**Cronos (CRO)** is the token of the Crypto.com exchange and app and of the Cronos blockchains built around it. Crypto.com, based in Singapore and led by CEO Kris Marszalek, launched CRO in 2018; today it pays network fees, unlocks card and app perks and is the company's main way to reward loyal users. In November 2026 all CRO in circulation was worth about $2.1 billion, around number 40 among cryptocurrencies.

> **Key takeaways**
> - At most 100 billion CRO can exist; about 52 billion are in circulation.
> - In 2021 Crypto.com burned 70 billion CRO; in March 2025 a disputed vote brought them back into a locked "strategic reserve".
> - The record high came in November 2021, the month Crypto.com bought the naming rights to the Los Angeles arena; in November 2026 CRO traded about 95% below it.
> - In August 2025 Trump Media and Crypto.com announced a planned $6.4 billion company set up to hold CRO.

## How it works

CRO runs on two connected networks. Cronos POS (formerly Crypto.org Chain, 2021) is a proof-of-stake chain for payments and staking. Cronos EVM (November 2021) is compatible with [Ethereum]({lang}/eth/), so wallets such as MetaMask and Ethereum apps work on it with lower fees; a zero-knowledge layer, Cronos zkEVM, followed in 2024. Validators lock up CRO to secure the networks and earn rewards. In practice Crypto.com and companies close to it run much of the infrastructure, so Cronos is far less decentralized than Ethereum.

## What the token is used for

CRO pays transaction fees on the Cronos chains and is staked to secure them. For most holders, though, its value is tied to Crypto.com itself: staking CRO in the app raises cashback levels on the Crypto.com Visa card, earn rates and other perks, and it can lower trading fees. That makes CRO similar to other exchange tokens — demand rises and falls with the exchange's popularity and the benefits it decides to offer.

## Its story so far

Crypto.com started in 2016 under the name Monaco and switched to the CRO token in 2018 with 100 billion coins. In 2021 it burned 70 billion of them, and the November 2021 boom — with a $700 million, 20-year deal to rename the Staples Center the Crypto.com Arena — pushed CRO to its record. After the 2022 crash it lost most of its value. In March 2025 a governance vote approved reissuing the 70 billion burned CRO into a reserve wallet; critics said Crypto.com's own validators decided the outcome. In August 2025 Trump Media agreed to buy about $105 million of CRO as part of the treasury deal.

## Alternatives

CRO competes with other exchange tokens: [BNB]({lang}/bnb/) from Binance, [OKB]({lang}/okb/) from OKX, [Mantle]({lang}/mnt/) linked to Bybit, [Gate]({lang}/gt/) and [KuCoin]({lang}/kcs/). As a smart-contract network, Cronos EVM competes with Ethereum and its many cheaper layer-2 networks.

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** CRO has fallen about 95% from its record and can drop sharply again.
- **One company:** demand depends on Crypto.com's business, reputation and decisions about perks.
- **Supply changes:** the 2025 reissue showed that the supply rules can be changed; the reserve can be released into the market over time.
- **Concentration:** Crypto.com and related wallets control a large share of CRO and of the validators.
- **Regulation:** exchanges and their tokens are a focus for regulators in many countries.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Buying a fixed amount at regular intervals spreads your purchases over good and bad times, so you do not have to guess the right moment. With an exchange token, though, you are effectively betting on one company and its rules, which can change. Many people keep such coins to a small part of their savings. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
