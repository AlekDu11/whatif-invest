**AstraZeneca** is a British-Swedish pharmaceutical company based in Cambridge, England. It invents, makes and sells prescription medicines — above all for cancer, but also for heart, kidney and lung diseases and rare illnesses. It is one of the largest companies in the UK's FTSE 100 and, since February 2026, its shares also trade directly on the New York Stock Exchange under the same ticker, AZN.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Total revenue: $58.7 billion in 2025, +9% on the year; core earnings per share $9.16.
> - In January–September 2026 revenue was $46.9 billion (+7% at constant exchange rates) and core EPS $8.00 (+14%).
> - Cancer medicines brought in $21.9 billion in those nine months — 47% of all revenue.
> - The company aims for $80 billion of yearly revenue and 20 new medicines by 2030.

## How it makes money

AstraZeneca earns money from medicines that are protected by patents for many years after launch, so the price can cover the huge cost of research. In the first nine months of 2026 Oncology was the biggest area with $21.9 billion (+16%): lung-cancer pill Tagrisso, immunotherapy Imfinzi, blood-cancer drug Calquence and Enhertu, which it sells with Daiichi Sankyo. BioPharmaceuticals added $16.7 billion — cardiovascular, kidney and metabolism (CVRM, led by the diabetes and heart drug Farxiga) fell 14% to $8.7 billion as generics arrived, while respiratory and immunology rose 10% to $7.3 billion. Rare Disease, mostly from the 2021 purchase of Alexion (Ultomiris, Soliris), brought $7.5 billion (+11%). Partners such as Daiichi Sankyo also share profits with it ("alliance revenue", $2.8 billion).

## The stock's story

AstraZeneca was formed in 1999 when Sweden's Astra merged with Britain's Zeneca. When Pascal Soriot became chief executive in 2012, the company was losing patents on its old best-sellers and many investors doubted its future. In May 2014 it rejected a takeover offer from Pfizer worth about $118 billion, saying its own pipeline was worth more; the new cancer drugs that followed turned it into one of Europe's most valuable companies. In 2020 it developed a Covid-19 vaccine with the University of Oxford and supplied it at no profit during the pandemic. In 2021 it bought Alexion for about $39 billion. In late 2024 the head of its China business was detained by Chinese authorities, which weighed on the shares. In 2025 it pledged $50 billion of investment in the US, and on 2 February 2026 its ordinary shares began trading on the NYSE, replacing the old American depositary shares on Nasdaq.

## Competitors

AstraZeneca competes with the other big drugmakers: Roche, Merck & Co., Pfizer, Novartis, Bristol Myers Squibb, AbbVie and Johnson & Johnson in cancer; Eli Lilly, Novo Nordisk and Boehringer Ingelheim in diabetes and heart disease; GSK and Sanofi in respiratory medicine and vaccines. Once a patent ends, cheap generic copies become competitors too.

## Main risks

- **Patent cliffs:** Farxiga and Brilinta are already losing exclusivity, and every big drug eventually faces generics.
- **Drug prices:** US price negotiations under the Inflation Reduction Act, tariff threats and political pressure can cut what it earns per pill; in China, bulk-buying tenders push prices down.
- **Research can fail:** a late-stage trial that misses can wipe out years of work and a large part of the expected value.
- **China:** a sizeable market where it has faced a criminal investigation of staff; relations between China, the US and Europe add uncertainty.
- **Currencies:** results are in dollars, but costs and the London share price are in pounds, euros and other currencies.

## What it means if you buy regularly

AstraZeneca is a large, growing drugmaker, but a single drug company can fall sharply on one failed trial or a pricing decision. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your entry price over good and bad news, without trying to guess the next trial result. If you buy in London, your return also depends on the pound. Many people hold one company only as part of a wider portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
