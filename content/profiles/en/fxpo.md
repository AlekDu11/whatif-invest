**Ferrexpo** digs iron ore near Kremenchuk in Ukraine's Poltava region and turns it into pellets — small hard balls of iron ore that steel mills melt down. It is listed in London, but its mines and staff are in Ukraine, so the war decides its fate: in the first nine months of 2026 it produced less than half as much as a year earlier and had to sell $100 million of new shares to keep going.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Output in the first nine months of 2026: 2.33 million tonnes, down 54% a year; production stopped completely in August.
> - Revenue fell 57% to $196 million in the first half of 2026; 2025 ended with a $224 million net loss.
> - Trading in the shares was suspended from 1 May to 7 September 2026, until a $100 million share sale at 16.5 US cents a share closed.
> - Ukraine has withheld VAT refunds, citing sanctions on founder Kostyantyn Zhevago; the company says it is owed more than $88 million.

## How it makes money

Ferrexpo sells high-grade iron ore: premium pellets with 65% iron and direct-reduction (DR) pellets with 67% iron, used in cleaner, gas- or hydrogen-based steelmaking. Buyers are steel mills in Europe, Asia, the Middle East and North Africa. Its own fleet of more than 3,000 rail wagons and more than 200 barges carries ore to the Black Sea ports, to the Danube and by rail into Central Europe; in 2025 the Black Sea ports handled 58% of sales, and half of all sales went to Asia, up from 17% a year earlier.

DR pellets are the bright spot: in the first nine months of 2026 the company made 305,000 tonnes, 3.7 times more than a year earlier.

For 2025, revenue was $787 million, down 16%, and underlying EBITDA (profit before interest, tax and depreciation) was only $28 million. The net loss of $224 million included a $154 million write-down of its assets. In the first half of 2026 revenue was $196 million, the pre-tax loss narrowed to $11 million, and net cash at the end of June 2026 was $21 million.

The third quarter brought 780,000 tonnes of iron ore, down 19% a year. Russian attacks on Black Sea ports blocked sea exports in late July, the company suspended production in August and restarted one pellet line in September after the new money arrived.

## The stock's story

Ferrexpo has lived with war risk since 2022, but 2026 was its hardest year. On 22 April it warned that cash could run out by the end of August, and the shares fell 13%. On 1 May the London Stock Exchange suspended trading because audited 2025 results were not ready. In September the company raised $100 million: Andrii Verevskyi, owner of agribusiness Kernel, put in $50 million, and Fevamotinico, the company linked to Zhevago and the largest shareholder, committed almost $30 million. Trading resumed on 7 September, and on the half-year results the shares rose 12% to 32.02p.

## Competitors

In Ukraine the other big iron ore producer is Metinvest. Globally, high-grade pellets come from Vale (Brazil), LKAB (Sweden), Rio Tinto's Iron Ore Company of Canada, Champion Iron and Cleveland-Cliffs. Its strength is premium grades that suit low-emission steelmaking; its weakness is that it cannot ship freely.

## Main risks

- **War:** strikes on power plants and ports can stop production at any time; 751 employees were serving in Ukraine's armed forces at the end of 2025.
- **Cash:** the $100 million raise was meant to cover about 18 months; if exports stay blocked, the company may need more money again.
- **VAT and the founder:** refunds are withheld because of personal sanctions on Zhevago, who holds about 49.5% through Fevamotinico and is suspected in Ukraine of embezzlement.
- **Iron ore prices** swing with Chinese steel demand.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Ferrexpo is a special-situation stock: its value depends on the war, Ukrainian courts and access to ports more than on iron ore itself. Regular buying spreads purchases, but cannot protect against a company that runs out of cash or keeps issuing new shares. People who own a stock like this usually keep it as a very small part of a broader portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
