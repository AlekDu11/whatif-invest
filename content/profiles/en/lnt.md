**Alliant Energy** is the company that keeps the lights on and the gas flowing for about a million homes and businesses in Iowa and southern Wisconsin. It is a regulated utility: it owns power plants, wind farms, solar parks and the wires, and state regulators decide what it may charge. Lately it has become a data-centre story too, because tech companies are building server farms in its territory and need huge amounts of electricity.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Q3 2026 earnings per share: $1.52, up from $1.12 a year earlier (ongoing basis); net income $398 million.
> - Data centres have signed contracts for 3.4 gigawatts of power — a big addition for a utility of this size.
> - Investment plan for 2027–2030 raised by 19% to $15.9 billion.
> - Quarterly dividend $0.535 per share; the 2027 target is $2.25 a year.

## How it makes money

Alliant has two utilities. **Interstate Power and Light (IPL)** serves Iowa and **Wisconsin Power and Light (WPL)** serves Wisconsin. Together they have about 1,015,000 electricity customers and 435,000 natural gas customers. Electricity is the core: $1.20 billion of the $1.29 billion revenue in Q3 2026.

The utility model is simple. Alliant spends money on plants, batteries and power lines; regulators add that spending to its "rate base" (the asset value it is allowed to earn a return on), and customers pay bills that cover costs plus that return. So profit grows mainly when the company invests more — which is why the data-centre boom matters. Google and QTS chose sites in Alliant's Big Cedar Industrial Center in Cedar Rapids, Iowa, a park the utility owns, and both are supplied by Alliant.

## The numbers right now

In the quarter to 30 September 2026 Alliant earned $398 million for shareholders, or $1.52 per share, against $281 million a year earlier. Part of the jump is the timing of tax expenses, which the company says will even out by year-end; the rest came from new investments in generation and storage, higher electricity sales and weather. Total revenue was $1.29 billion, up 6%.

Management narrowed its 2026 forecast to $3.41–3.46 per share and expects $3.63–3.73 in 2027, about 8% growth. The new 2027–2030 plan totals $15.9 billion, peaking at about $4.7 billion in 2028; roughly $6.8 billion is earmarked for gas-fired and related generation and about $5.1 billion for renewables and storage. In Iowa, a rate stability plan keeps retail electricity base rates steady through the end of 2030.

## The stock's story

Utilities are usually slow movers, and Alliant is no exception. Rising interest rates hurt the stock in 2022 (−7.4%) and 2023 (−3.8%), because bonds started paying more than the dividend. As data-centre demand appeared, it rose 19.5% in 2024 and 13.5% in 2025, and set a record close of $74.06 on 1 May 2026.

## Competitors

Within its territory Alliant is a regulated monopoly, so it does not fight for every household. It competes for investor money and for big new customers with neighbouring utilities such as MidAmerican Energy (owned by Berkshire Hathaway) in Iowa, WEC Energy and Xcel Energy in Wisconsin and Minnesota. Data-centre builders compare power prices, speed of connection and clean-energy supply across states before choosing a site.

## Main risks

- **Regulation:** state regulators can approve lower returns or reject costs, and the Iowa rate freeze limits how fast bills can rise.
- **Heavy spending:** a $15.9 billion plan needs new borrowing and possibly new shares, which can dilute existing holders.
- **Data-centre dependence:** if tech companies slow or cancel projects, part of the planned growth would disappear.
- **Interest rates:** higher rates raise borrowing costs and make dividend stocks less attractive, as 2022–2023 showed.
- **Weather and fuel:** mild seasons cut sales, and gas price swings affect costs and customer bills.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Alliant is a dividend payer whose price usually moves less than the overall market, but it can lose money for years when interest rates rise. Regular buying lets dividends and steady purchases build up over time; a single utility still carries regulatory and project risks, so many people hold it as one part of a wider portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
