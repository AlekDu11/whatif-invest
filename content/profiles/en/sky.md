**Sky** is the new name of MakerDAO, one of the oldest projects in decentralized finance and the issuer of the DAI and USDS stablecoins — digital dollars that billions of dollars of crypto activity rely on. SKY is the token that governs the protocol and shares in its profits. In November 2026 all SKY in circulation was worth about $970 million, around number 70 among cryptocurrencies.

> **Key takeaways**
> - MakerDAO launched DAI in December 2017; in 2024 it became Sky, and every old MKR token can be swapped for 24,000 SKY.
> - About 23.4 billion SKY circulate, close to the maximum of roughly 23.5 billion.
> - Part of the protocol's surplus buys SKY on the market; more than 100 million USDS has been spent on buybacks so far.
> - The record price so far is $0.101 (5 October 2026); in November 2026 SKY traded about 60% below it.

## How it works

Sky creates dollar-pegged stablecoins backed by collateral: crypto loans, USDC held in reserve and tokenized US Treasuries. A tool called the Peg Stability Module lets anyone swap USDC for DAI or USDS one-to-one, which keeps the price close to $1. People who deposit USDS earn the Sky Savings Rate, paid from the interest the protocol collects on its loans and reserves. Parts of the business, such as the lender Spark, run as semi-independent "Stars" inside the Sky ecosystem.

## What the token is used for

SKY holders vote on interest rates, accepted collateral and risk settings — in effect they run the protocol. Staking SKY earns rewards, and the protocol uses part of its profit to buy SKY back on the open market. So the token's value depends on how many USDS and DAI are in use and how much interest the protocol earns on them. It is not a stablecoin itself: its price moves freely.

## Its story so far

Rune Christensen started MakerDAO in 2014, and DAI went live on Ethereum in December 2017, making it a pioneer of on-chain lending. It survived "Black Thursday" in March 2020, when a crash left the system short of collateral, and later added USDC and real-world assets such as Treasuries to its reserves. In 2024 MakerDAO rebranded as Sky, introduced USDS and split each MKR into 24,000 SKY — which is why SKY trades for a few cents. In November 2026 a report linked $518 million of past hack- and sanction-related flows to Sky's swap module, and SKY fell about 26% in a day; the Sky team disputes the conclusions.

## Alternatives

Among stablecoins, USDS and DAI compete with Tether's USDT and Circle's USDC, which are issued by companies, and with crypto-based designs such as [Ethena]({lang}/ena/). For on-chain lending the main rival is [Aave]({lang}/aave/); [Curve]({lang}/crv/) also runs its own stablecoin.

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** SKY lost about 60% between its October 2026 record and late November.
- **Dependence on USDC:** a large share of reserves is in USDC, which its issuer can freeze on a court or government request.
- **Compliance:** the November 2026 report on illicit flows through the swap module could attract regulators and freezes.
- **Concentration:** governance is decided by a few large holders, and the founder's influence remains strong.
- **Technology:** smart-contract bugs or a stablecoin losing its $1 peg could cause losses.

## What it means if you buy regularly

SKY has traded under its new name for just over a year, rising to a record in October 2026 and then falling sharply. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more when the price is low and less when it is high, without trying to guess the bottom — but it does not remove the risk of losses. Many people keep tokens like SKY to a small part of their savings that they can afford to see fall sharply. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
