**Sky** is the new name of MakerDAO, one of the oldest projects in decentralised finance and the issuer of the DAI and USDS stablecoins — digital dollars that billions of dollars of crypto rely on. SKY is the token that governs it and shares in its profits.

## How it works

Sky creates dollar-pegged stablecoins backed by collateral: crypto loans, USDC held in reserve and tokenised US Treasuries. The Peg Stability Module lets anyone swap USDC for DAI/USDS one-to-one, which keeps the price near $1. Holders who deposit USDS earn the Sky Savings Rate, paid from the interest the protocol collects. SKY holders vote on rates, collateral and risk settings; staking SKY earns rewards, and the protocol uses part of its surplus to **buy back SKY** on the market.

## Why it matters

MakerDAO was founded by Rune Christensen and launched DAI in December 2017, making it a pioneer of on-chain lending. In 2024 it rebranded as Sky, introduced USDS and turned each old MKR token into 24,000 SKY — which is why SKY trades for a few cents with about 23.4 billion tokens in circulation. A surprising fact: side projects such as the lender Spark now run as semi-independent "Stars" inside the Sky ecosystem.

## Main risks

Sky depends heavily on USDC: about $2.7 billion of the reserve sits at one Ethereum address, and Circle can technically freeze addresses on a court or government request. In November 2026 a report linked $518 million of past hack- and sanction-related flows to Sky's swap module, and SKY fell about 26% in a day; the foundation disputes the conclusions. Other risks: smart-contract bugs, a stablecoin losing its peg, governance controlled by a few large holders, and stablecoin regulation. The token is far below its October 2026 high. Regular buying smooths out the swings but does not remove the risk.
