**Arbitrum** is a "layer 2" network on top of Ethereum: it makes transactions faster and much cheaper while relying on Ethereum for security. It was built by the US company Offchain Labs, and its token **ARB** lets holders vote on how the network and its treasury are run. ## How it works

Arbitrum is an **optimistic rollup**: it executes transactions on its own chain and posts compressed batches of data back to Ethereum. A single operator, the **sequencer** (run by Offchain Labs), orders transactions and confirms them in a fraction of a second. Results are assumed correct ("optimistic") unless challenged: any validator can submit a **fraud proof**, and the dispute is settled on Ethereum. Since February 2025 the **BoLD** protocol lets anyone validate, and one honest validator is enough to defend the chain. The **Nitro** software works with Ethereum apps, and the **Orbit** programme lets other projects launch their own chains on Arbitrum technology.

## What the token is used for

ARB is mainly a voting token. Holders, or delegates they choose, vote in the **Arbitrum DAO** on network upgrades and on spending the treasury, which holds billions of ARB plus sequencer fee income. ARB is not needed to use Arbitrum: gas is paid in ETH. So the token's value rests on governance rights and on what the DAO does with its money, not directly on network usage.

## Its story so far

Offchain Labs grew out of research at Princeton University. Arbitrum One opened to the public in late August 2021, and the Nitro upgrade in August 2022 cut fees and raised capacity. On 23 March 2023 ARB launched with a large airdrop to users. Days later the first proposal, AIP-1, caused an uproar: the Foundation had started using a 750-million-ARB allocation before the vote. It then split the proposal and gave the DAO more control. ARB reached its peak of about $2.39 in January 2024. On 16 March 2024 about 1.1 billion team and investor tokens unlocked, followed by monthly unlocks. In 2025 BoLD went live, and the price fell sharply over 2025 and 2026.

## Alternatives

Arbitrum competes with other Ethereum layer 2 networks: [Optimism]({lang}/op/) and chains on its OP Stack, including Coinbase's Base, which has no token; zero-knowledge rollups such as [ZKsync]({lang}/zk/) and [Starknet]({lang}/strk/); plus [Mantle]({lang}/mnt/) and [POL (ex-MATIC)]({lang}/pol/). [Ethereum]({lang}/eth/) itself is an alternative too, as its upgrades keep making it cheaper.

## Main risks

- **New supply:** unlocks for the team and investors continue, and the DAO treasury holds a large share of all ARB.
- **Centralised sequencer:** one operator orders transactions; if it fails or misbehaves, users fall back on slower routes.
- **Competition:** Base and other layer 2 networks fight for the same users, apps and fees.
- **Weak link to usage:** fees are paid in ETH, so heavy use of Arbitrum does not by itself raise demand for ARB.
- **Regulation:** rules for tokens and DAOs differ by country and can change.

## What it means if you buy regularly

ARB has spent most of its life falling from its early peak, so a regular buyer kept getting more tokens at lower prices — which helps only if the price recovers. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads timing risk but does not remove the risk of losses. Many people keep tokens like this to a small part of their savings. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
