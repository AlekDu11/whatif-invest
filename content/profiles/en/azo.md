**AutoZone** is America's largest chain of car-parts shops. People who fix their own cars buy batteries, brake pads, oil and filters there, and a fast-growing share of sales goes to repair garages that order parts for same-day delivery. Founded in 1979 in Arkansas and based in Memphis, Tennessee, it had 8,031 stores in the United States, Mexico and Brazil at the end of August 2026.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Net sales: $20.3 billion in fiscal 2026 (52 weeks to 29 August 2026), +7.4%; net income $2.57 billion.
> - Sales to repair shops ("commercial") grew 10.6% to $5.76 billion, faster than the rest of the business.
> - AutoZone opened 374 stores in fiscal 2026, the most it has ever opened in one year.
> - It pays no dividend; it spent $2.0 billion buying back its own shares in fiscal 2026.

## How it makes money

AutoZone buys parts from manufacturers and sells them at a high markup: its gross margin was 52.3% in fiscal 2026. Two groups of customers matter. "Do-it-yourself" drivers come into the store, often for advice or a free battery test. "Commercial" customers are independent garages that need the right part fast; AutoZone serves them from 6,443 stores with commercial programmes and from large "Mega Hub" stores that stock many more parts. Business is steady because cars break down in good times and bad, and older cars need more repairs.

Outside the US, AutoZone had 1,001 stores in Mexico and 167 in Brazil at the end of fiscal 2026, and the international business is growing faster than the US one.

AutoZone ended the year with total debt of $9.08 billion and $1.6 billion left in its buyback authorisation.

## The stock's story

J. R. "Pitt" Hyde opened the first store, then called Auto Shack, in Forrest City, Arkansas, in 1979; it was renamed AutoZone in 1987 and listed on the New York Stock Exchange in 1991. In the 2008 financial crisis, when many stocks crashed, AutoZone's shares rose, as people kept old cars running instead of buying new ones. From the late 1990s the company used most of its cash to buy back shares, cutting the number of shares sharply and lifting earnings per share; this is a big reason the share price climbed into the thousands of dollars. The shares dipped in March 2020 with the COVID-19 crash, then rose strongly as Americans drove more and car prices jumped. Phil Daniele became CEO in January 2024.

## Competitors

AutoZone's biggest rival is O'Reilly Automotive, which is stronger with repair shops; others are Advance Auto Parts and NAPA (Genuine Parts). It also competes with Walmart and Amazon for do-it-yourself buyers, and with car dealers' own parts departments.

## Main risks

- **Electric cars:** EVs need fewer parts such as oil, filters and spark plugs, so a fast switch to EVs would shrink demand over time.
- **Online competition:** Amazon and other online sellers can undercut prices on parts that are not needed urgently.
- **The repair-shop race:** O'Reilly and others fight hard for garage customers, which can squeeze margins.
- **Tariffs and costs:** many parts are made abroad, so trade disputes and wage rises affect profits.
- **Debt-funded buybacks:** AutoZone has more liabilities than assets (a shareholders' deficit of $2.5 billion in August 2026); this works while profits are steady but leaves less room if they fall.

## What it means if you buy regularly

AutoZone has been a steady business, and its stock held up even in 2008. But a very high share price does not mean the shares are "expensive" on its own — the company simply has few shares because it has bought so many back. For small regular amounts it matters whether you can buy fractional shares, since one share costs thousands of dollars. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your purchases over good and bad periods. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
