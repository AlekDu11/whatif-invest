**Stable** is a blockchain built for one job: moving dollar stablecoins, above all Tether's USDT, quickly and cheaply. Its network, StableChain, launched in December 2025 with backing from Bitfinex, the exchange that shares a parent company with Tether. Fees on the network are paid in USDT itself, while the STABLE token is used for voting and securing the chain. ## How it works

StableChain is a separate blockchain that works with the same apps and wallets as Ethereum. Instead of buying a special coin to pay transaction fees, users pay them in USDT, so someone holding only dollars can send them straight away. Validators — the computers that confirm transactions — are chosen by STABLE holders, who delegate their tokens to them in a system called delegated proof of stake. Stakers earn a share of the network's fees, which are collected in USDT; the project says no new STABLE is created as rewards.

## Why people think it has value

USDT is the most used stablecoin in the world, and a lot of it moves on networks such as Tron and Ethereum, where fees must be paid in another coin. A chain designed around USDT, with ties to Tether's world — Tether CEO Paolo Ardoino is an adviser — could win part of that payment traffic. The project raised a $28 million seed round led by Bitfinex and Hack VC and announced partners including PayPal, Anchorage Digital and Standard Chartered's tokenization platform Libeara. If the network becomes busy, stakers' share of fees grows.

## Its story so far

Stable announced its seed round in July 2025 and ran two pre-deposit campaigns in the autumn, in which users locked stablecoins ahead of launch. On 8 December 2025 the main network, the STABLE token and the independent Stable Foundation, which hands out grants and supports the ecosystem, all went live. At launch 10% of the supply was distributed, 40% was set aside for developer grants and partnerships, and the team and investors' shares were placed under a one-year cliff and four-year vesting.

## Alternatives

[Plasma]({lang}/xpl/) is the closest rival: another blockchain built for USDT payments, also linked to Bitfinex. [Tron]({lang}/trx/) carries the biggest share of USDT transfers today, and [Ethereum]({lang}/eth/) and [Solana]({lang}/sol/) also move large amounts of stablecoins. Circle's Arc and Stripe's Tempo are payment blockchains backed by big companies.

## Main risks

- **Unlocks:** team and investor tokens, half of the supply, start unlocking after the one-year cliff, which can add selling pressure.
- **Tether dependency:** any problem with USDT's reserves or its legal status would hit the network directly.
- **Competition:** Plasma, Tron, Arc, Tempo and the big chains all fight for the same payments.
- **Young network:** StableChain has run for less than a year and has not been tested by a major crisis.
- **Concentration and regulation:** a small set of validators and big holders matter a lot, and stablecoin rules are still changing.

## What it means if you buy regularly

STABLE has traded only since December 2025, so its history is short. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your entry price over time, but it does not protect you from falls driven by unlocks or competition. Many people keep young tokens to a small part of their savings that they can afford to see drop sharply. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
