**Mitsubishi UFJ Financial Group (MUFG)** is Japan's largest banking group. It owns MUFG Bank, a trust bank, a securities business run with Morgan Stanley and a credit-card company, and it lends to big Japanese companies, households and businesses across Asia and the Americas. For years near-zero interest rates in Japan squeezed its profits; since Japan started raising rates in 2024, the money it makes on loans has been growing again.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Net profit: ¥1.63 trillion in April–September 2026, +26% on the year — a record for a first half.
> - In November 2026 MUFG raised its full-year profit target to ¥2.8 trillion, from ¥2.427 trillion in the year to March 2026.
> - Planned dividend ¥100 per share for the year to March 2027, plus a buyback of up to ¥200 billion.
> - It owns about a fifth of Morgan Stanley and, since April 2026, 20% of India's Shriram Finance.

## How it makes money

The biggest engine is interest: MUFG pays depositors a small rate and lends the money out at a higher one. In the first half of the 2026 fiscal year net interest income rose 29% to ¥1.85 trillion, and higher Japanese rates alone added about ¥140 billion to operating profit. The second engine is fees — from payments, trust and asset management, investment banking and cards — which rose 14% to ¥1.23 trillion. A third, growing part is its share of profits from companies it partly owns, above all Morgan Stanley: these equity-method earnings rose to ¥473.2 billion. Outside Japan MUFG also owns Krungsri in Thailand and Bank Danamon in Indonesia.

Results were released on 13 November 2026. Net operating profit rose 26% to ¥1.62 trillion in the half; MUFG kept its full-year target for it at ¥2.9 trillion and its forecast for loan losses at ¥350 billion. Bad loans fell to ¥1.25 trillion, or 0.81% of lending, from 0.96%. The weak spot was bonds: MUFG lost ¥196 billion on debt securities, and unrealized losses on bonds it plans to hold to maturity grew to ¥1.78 trillion as yields rose. The dividend plan of ¥100 is made of a ¥48 interim payment already made and ¥52 at year-end. The shares trade in Tokyo under code 8306 and in New York as MUFG.

## The stock's story

MUFG was formed in October 2005, when Mitsubishi Tokyo Financial Group merged with UFJ Holdings, creating the world's biggest bank by assets at the time. In the 2008 crisis it put $9 billion into Morgan Stanley, a stake that later became one of its best investments. Then came a long slump: Japan kept rates near zero and from 2016 below zero, and on the WhatIfStack chart (in dollars) the shares cost about $3 in January 2010 and still only about $3.60 in January 2021. Things changed when the Bank of Japan ended negative rates in March 2024 and kept raising them. MUFG also pushed to sell its cross-shareholdings in other Japanese companies and to pay out more to shareholders. The shares rose from about $3.60 in January 2021 to about $17.50 in January 2026 — almost fivefold. In 2024 Japan's financial regulator punished the group over improper sharing of client information between its bank and securities arms.

## Competitors

At home MUFG competes with the two other megabanks, Sumitomo Mitsui Financial Group and Mizuho, and with Japan Post Bank, regional banks and fast-growing online banks. In global corporate lending and investment banking it faces JPMorgan, Citigroup, HSBC and the big European banks; in Southeast Asia and India it competes with local banks and other Japanese groups that are also buying stakes there.

## Main risks

- **Interest rates:** profits now depend on the Bank of Japan continuing to raise rates; a pause or a cut would slow the growth of interest income.
- **Bond losses:** when yields rise, the bonds MUFG holds lose value — ¥1.78 trillion of unrealized losses at the end of September 2026.
- **Japan's economy:** a recession would raise loan losses and lower demand for credit.
- **Overseas bets:** stakes in Morgan Stanley, Thailand, Indonesia and India bring growth but also exposure to other economies and markets.
- **Compliance:** the 2024 penalties showed that misconduct can bring fines and damage trust.

## What it means if you buy regularly

A big bank like MUFG moves with interest rates and the economy, and its shares went sideways for more than a decade before rising sharply. The ¥100 dividend plan means part of the return comes as cash, but dividends are not guaranteed. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more shares when the price is low and fewer when it is high, without trying to time the market. Many people hold one company only as part of a wider portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
