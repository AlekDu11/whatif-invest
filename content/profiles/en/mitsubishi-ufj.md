**Mitsubishi UFJ Financial Group (MUFG)** is Japan's largest bank group and one of the biggest in the world — and after decades of near-zero interest rates in Japan, it is finally earning more on the money it lends. It was created in 2005 by the merger of Mitsubishi Tokyo Financial Group and UFJ Holdings.

## How it makes money

MUFG earns the gap between what it pays on deposits and what it charges on loans, plus fees from cards, trust banking, asset management, securities and corporate advice. In the six months to September 2026 net interest income rose 29% to ¥1.85 trillion as Japanese rates went up: its domestic loan-deposit spread widened to 1.17%. Fee income grew 14% to ¥1.23 trillion. Net profit hit a record ¥1.63 trillion, up 26%, and MUFG raised its full-year target to ¥2.8 trillion.

## Why it matters

MUFG is a global bank with a Japanese base. It is the largest shareholder in US investment bank Morgan Stanley and owns big lenders in Southeast Asia, including Krungsri in Thailand and Danamon in Indonesia; earnings from such stakes rose to ¥473 billion in the half-year. It returns much of its profit to shareholders: the dividend forecast is ¥100 per share (up from ¥96), plus a new buyback of up to ¥200 billion. One surprising fact: this was MUFG's fourth record first half in a row.

## Main risks

Higher rates help lending margins but hurt bond holdings: MUFG booked ¥196 billion of losses on debt securities, and unrealised losses on bonds held to maturity reached ¥1.78 trillion. If deposit costs catch up with loan rates, the spread gain shrinks. Part of the profit depends on Morgan Stanley and the global markets. Banks also suffer when borrowers default in a recession, and a stronger yen reduces overseas profits. Buying a fixed amount regularly smooths price swings but does not remove the risk.
