**Żabka** ("little frog") is the green corner shop on almost every Polish street: a small store open long hours where you grab coffee, a hot dog, a ready meal, groceries or a parcel. Most shops are run by franchisees — local entrepreneurs who operate the store while Żabka supplies the brand, goods, logistics and apps. The company listed on the Warsaw Stock Exchange in October 2024, and in 2026 Canada's Alimentation Couche-Tard, owner of Circle K, offered to buy it.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Network: 13,405 stores at the end of September 2026, +10.8% a year, including the first ones in Romania.
> - Revenue: 8.47 billion zł in Q3 2026, +13.8%; adjusted EBITDA +16.5% to 1.49 billion zł.
> - Takeover: Couche-Tard offers 32 zł a share in cash, about 49% above the 21.50 zł IPO price.
> - Same-store (like-for-like) sales growth slowed to 3.5% in Q3, from 4.5% a year earlier.

## How it makes money

Żabka earns mainly by selling goods to its franchisees, who sell them on to shoppers, so its revenue grows as the network grows. Every year it opens well over a thousand new stores: 1,394 in 2025 and 1,143 in the first nine months of 2026, 135 of them in Romania. On top of that it runs a digital business — the Żappka loyalty app, the online grocer delio and the meal-delivery brands Maczfit and Dietly — whose revenue grew 25% in 2025. The plan is 16,000 stores by the end of 2028.

## The numbers right now

In the first nine months of 2026 revenue rose 14.3% to 23.12 billion zł, and adjusted net profit jumped 36.6% to 886 million zł. Adjusted EBITDA (operating profit before depreciation) reached 3.39 billion zł, and the margin improved slightly. Debt is modest: net debt is about 0.7 times yearly adjusted EBITDA, down from 1.1 a year earlier. For all of 2025 revenue was 27.15 billion zł and net profit 1.06 billion zł.

## The stock's story

The IPO priced shares at 21.50 zł and valued the company at about 21.5 billion zł. The debut fell flat: shares closed day one exactly at the offer price and within a week slid below 19 zł on worries about the valuation and the high share of alcohol and tobacco in sales. Growth then won investors back. In July 2026 Couche-Tard agreed to buy Żabka, backed by the main owners CVC Capital Partners and Partners Group (together with executives, about 57% of the shares). The 32 zł offer values Żabka at about 32.6 billion zł; all approvals are in, acceptance ends on 2 December 2026, and in November 2026 the 52-week range was about 19.92–33.88 zł.

## Competitors

Żabka fights for everyday shopping with discounters Biedronka (Jerónimo Martins), Lidl and the fast-growing Dino, with small-format chains such as Lewiatan and Carrefour Express, and with petrol station shops like Orlen and Circle K — the chain that now wants to own it. Its edge is location: a shop within a few minutes' walk for millions of Poles.

## Main risks

- **Delisting:** if Couche-Tard reaches 95% of votes, it plans to buy out the remaining shareholders and take Żabka off the Warsaw exchange; if it gets less, the stock stays listed with a dominant owner and lower free float.
- **Slower same-store sales:** like-for-like growth fell to 3.6% for nine months, from 5.5% a year earlier.
- **Saturation:** with more than 13,000 shops, each new store can take some sales from existing ones.
- **Regulation:** alcohol and tobacco are a large share of sales, so stricter rules or higher excise taxes on them would hit Żabka harder than most retailers.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Żabka is a special case right now. With a 32 zł cash offer on the table, the share price mostly tracks that number, not the business, and buying regularly near it offers little upside if the deal closes. If the company is delisted, regular buying simply ends and holders receive cash. The small dividend (0.52 zł a share in our data) matters little next to that. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
