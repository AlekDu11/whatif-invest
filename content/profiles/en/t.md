**AT&T** is one of America's three big mobile carriers and its largest fiber home-internet provider. It earns money every month from about 110 million wireless customers and 16 million internet connections, and pays a big part of that cash out as a dividend. After years of buying and then selling media companies, it is now a pure connectivity business built around 5G and fiber.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Q3 2026 revenue: $32.3 billion, up 5.3% on the year; adjusted earnings $0.65 per share against $0.54.
> - 75.3 million postpaid phone customers and 13.3 million fiber connections at the end of September 2026.
> - The dividend is $1.11 per share a year — the level set in 2022, when AT&T spun off WarnerMedia.
> - Net debt was $149.8 billion at the end of September 2026, after a $23 billion spectrum purchase from EchoStar.

## How it makes money

Almost everything now sits in one segment the company calls Advanced Connectivity: mobile phone plans, 5G, fiber home internet and the wireless home internet product AT&T Internet Air. In Q3 2026 that segment brought in $29.5 billion, including $17.8 billion of wireless service revenue and $3.0 billion from advanced home internet (up 23%). The money is mostly monthly subscriptions, so it is steady; phones sold to customers add revenue but little profit. AT&T's strategy is "convergence": selling fiber or Internet Air to the same households that use its mobile service — 42.8% of its advanced home internet households also had AT&T wireless. A shrinking Legacy segment (old copper phone lines and copper internet) earned $1.5 billion in the quarter, down 24.5%; AT&T plans to switch off most of its copper network by the end of 2029. Latin America (AT&T Mexico) added $1.2 billion.

In the quarter to 30 September 2026, reported on 21 October, AT&T had revenue of $32.3 billion, up 5.3%. Adjusted EBITDA grew 5.4% to $12.5 billion and adjusted earnings were $0.65 per share. Reported earnings were only $0.13 per share because of a $2.6 billion non-cash write-down on the Mexico wireless business, which AT&T agreed to sell. Free cash flow was $5.0 billion. The company added 379,000 postpaid phone customers, 442,000 fiber and 327,000 fixed wireless customers — a record 769,000 home internet additions — and postpaid phone churn was 0.92% a month.

For 2026 AT&T guided to adjusted earnings of about $2.40 per share, free cash flow of $18 billion or more and $23–24 billion of capital investment. It plans about $10 billion of share buybacks in 2026 and more than $45 billion of dividends and buybacks over 2026–2028. The weak spot is debt: total debt was $159.6 billion, and net debt was 3.13 times adjusted EBITDA; the company aims to return to 2.5 times during 2029.

## The stock's story

AT&T was a classic "widow-and-orphan" dividend stock for decades, but the 2010s were costly. It bought DirecTV in 2015 and Time Warner for $85 billion in 2018, loading up on debt to become a media company. The plan was reversed: in April 2022 AT&T spun off WarnerMedia, which merged with Discovery to form Warner Bros. Discovery, and its shareholders received 0.24 WBD shares per AT&T share. At the same time the yearly dividend was cut from $2.08 to $1.11 per share. AT&T later sold its remaining DirecTV stake to TPG.

Since then AT&T has poured money into fiber and wireless. In 2026 it closed the purchase of Lumen's consumer fiber business (2 February) and of about 50 MHz of nationwide spectrum from EchoStar for about $23 billion (28 July). On 13 October 2026 it agreed to sell AT&T Mexico to Grupo Televisa and Fintech Holdings for $2 billion in cash, expected to close in early 2027. The shares had a hard autumn: the closing high of the past year was $29.10 on 27 March 2026, and in October 2026 the stock fell sharply on fears that SpaceX's Starlink would become a mobile competitor — closing at $20.93 on 23 October.

## Competitors

In US wireless AT&T's two direct rivals are [Verizon](/en/vz/) and [T-Mobile US](/en/tmus/), and cable companies [Comcast](/en/cmcsa/) and [Charter](/en/chtr/) sell cheap mobile plans to their internet customers. In home internet AT&T fiber competes with the same cable companies and with fixed wireless from T-Mobile and Verizon. A newer threat is satellite: SpaceX is buying spectrum to turn Starlink into a direct-to-phone carrier. AT&T's management argues that matching a large ground network with satellites would take many years and heavy investment.

## Main risks

- **Debt:** about $160 billion of total debt means a large part of the cash goes to interest, and higher rates make refinancing more expensive.
- **Price war:** the US phone market is mature; growth mostly means taking customers from Verizon, T-Mobile and cable, which costs promotions and discounts.
- **New competitors:** satellite-to-phone services such as Starlink could pressure prices, even if they cannot replace a full network soon.
- **Heavy spending:** $22–24 billion of capital investment a year for fiber, 5G and spectrum leaves less room for mistakes.
- **Legacy and one-offs:** retiring copper needs regulators' approval, and asset sales such as Mexico can bring write-downs.

## What it means if you buy regularly

AT&T is a slow-growing, high-dividend stock, so much of the long-term return comes from the dividend, especially if it is reinvested. Its history shows the other side: the share price can stay flat or fall for years, and in 2022 the dividend itself was cut. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more shares when the price is low and fewer when it is high, without trying to time the market. Many people hold one company only as part of a wider portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
