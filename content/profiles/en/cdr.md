**CD Projekt** is the Warsaw video-game studio behind The Witcher and Cyberpunk 2077 — big, story-driven role-playing games that take years to make and then sell for a decade. It makes money from game copies, expansions and, more and more, licensing its worlds to TV series, merchandise and card games. In August 2026 the listed company changed its legal name to CD Projekt Red S.A., after its game studio; the ticker on the Warsaw Stock Exchange is still CDR.

> **Key takeaways**
> - H1 2026: revenue PLN 435 million (+23%), net profit PLN 249 million (+37%) — a 57% net margin with no big new release.
> - The Witcher 3 has sold more than 65 million copies, Cyberpunk 2077 more than 40 million.
> - The company had more than PLN 1.3 billion in cash, deposits and bonds at the end of 2025.
> - The shares lost over 75% of their value after the broken Cyberpunk launch in December 2020; The Witcher 4 is planned for 2028.

## How it makes money

CD Projekt sells few products, but each one earns for years. The Witcher series has passed 90 million copies; Cyberpunk 2077 crossed 40 million nearly six years after launch, and its Phantom Liberty expansion sold 15 million in under three years. Most copies are sold digitally, so each extra sale costs almost nothing. Licensing is growing: Netflix's anime Cyberpunk: Edgerunners 2 premiered on 20 October 2026. At the end of 2025 the company sold its GOG online store to co-founder Michał Kiciński for PLN 90.7 million to focus only on making games.

## The numbers right now

In the first half of 2026 CD Projekt earned PLN 435 million of revenue, up 23% year on year, and PLN 249 million of net profit, up 37%. The second quarter alone beat analysts' forecasts by a wide margin. For the full year 2025 revenue was PLN 867 million (+9%) and net profit PLN 595 million, of which PLN 521 million came from the game business. In 2025 the group put almost PLN 513 million into new games and paid nearly PLN 100 million in dividends. Third-quarter results had not been published at the time of writing.

On 29 September 2026 the company released The Witcher 3: Wild Hunt – Remastered, free for owners of the original. A new Witcher 3 expansion, Songs of the Past, is set for 2027, and The Witcher 4, with Ciri as the hero, for 2028. Cyberpunk 2 and a new series codenamed Hadar are also in development.

## The stock's story

The Witcher 3 (2015) turned CD Projekt into Europe's most valuable game company: before Cyberpunk 2077 came out in December 2020 it was worth more than PLN 40 billion. The launch was a disaster — three delays, many technical problems, especially on consoles, refunds and removal from the PlayStation Store until June 2021. By July 2022 the company was worth less than PLN 10 billion, down more than 75%. Fixes and the Phantom Liberty expansion won players back, and the market value has since recovered to roughly PLN 27–28 billion — still well below the 2020 peak.

## Competitors

CD Projekt competes for players' time and money with big publishers such as Take-Two (Rockstar, GTA), Electronic Arts, Ubisoft, Sony's own studios and Microsoft's Xbox studios. Its strengths are two world-famous brands, a loyal fan base and more than PLN 1.3 billion of cash and bonds.

## Main risks

- **Release risk:** profit depends on a few huge games; a delay or another buggy launch like Cyberpunk's can hit the stock hard.
- **Long gaps:** The Witcher 4 is planned for 2028, so until then revenue relies on older games, the remaster and an expansion.
- **Rising costs:** building several big games at once cost almost PLN 513 million in 2025 alone, years before they earn anything.
- **High expectations:** at a P/E of around 45, the share price already assumes strong future hits.

## What it means if you buy regularly

CD Projekt is a hit-driven stock: it can rise fast before a release and fall fast after a disappointment, as the 75% drop after 2020 showed. Regular buying spreads purchases across those swings, but a single studio with a few big games stays a concentrated risk. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
