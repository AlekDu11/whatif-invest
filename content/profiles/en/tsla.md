**Tesla** builds electric cars — mostly the Model 3 sedan and the Model Y SUV — plus big home and grid batteries, and it runs one of the largest fast-charging networks. Led by Elon Musk, it is the most valuable carmaker in the world, but investors price it less on cars sold today and more on what it promises next: self-driving robotaxis and humanoid robots.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Q3 2026 revenue: $28.0 billion, flat on a year earlier; 486,532 cars delivered.
> - GAAP net income fell 43% to $787 million as research spending jumped 69%.
> - Cash and investments: $45.4 billion, while 2026 capital spending is expected at about $20 billion.
> - The share price fell more than 12% in one day in July 2026 and is still below its December 2025 record close of $489.88.

## How it makes money

Cars are still the core: automotive revenue was $20.6 billion in Q3 2026, about three quarters of the total. The Model 3 and Model Y made up 478,237 of the 486,532 deliveries; "other models" (Cybertruck, Model S/X, Semi) were the rest. Selling regulatory credits to other carmakers, once a nice extra, shrank to $113 million that quarter.

Energy generation and storage (the Megapack for power grids and the Powerwall for homes) brought in $2.9 billion, down 16% partly because of tariff refunds to customers, although deployments rose to 13.7 GWh. Services and other — charging, insurance, repairs, used cars and Full Self-Driving software — grew 33% to $4.6 billion. Tesla counts 1.74 million active FSD subscriptions (including upfront purchases).

## The numbers right now

In the quarter to 30 September 2026 Tesla had $28.05 billion of revenue, roughly unchanged year on year. The gross margin improved to 19.6% from 18.0%, but operating income fell 56% to $718 million — an operating margin of only 2.6% — because Tesla is spending heavily on AI training computers, robotaxis and the Optimus robot. Free cash flow was $1.53 billion after a negative quarter in Q2. Tesla gave no numeric delivery or profit guidance.

## The stock's story

Tesla listed in 2010 and became a pandemic-era phenomenon: in our split-adjusted data the price rose about 30 times from mid-2019 to late 2021. In 2022 it lost about two-thirds as interest rates rose and price cuts squeezed margins. It recovered, slumped again in early 2025, then hit a record close of $489.88 on 16 December 2025 on hopes for driverless rides in Austin. After weak Q2 2026 results — profit down and negative free cash flow — the shares fell more than 12% in one day in July 2026.

## Competitors

In cars, Tesla's toughest rival is China's BYD, followed by other Chinese brands such as Geely and Xiaomi, and by Volkswagen, Hyundai-Kia, GM and Toyota as they add electric models. In robotaxis, Alphabet's Waymo is already running paid driverless rides in several US cities. In batteries for the grid it competes with Fluence and Chinese makers such as CATL and BYD. Tesla's strengths are its brand, its software and its cost-efficient factories.

## Main risks

- **The valuation rests on promises:** robotaxis and Optimus earn little today; delays (unsupervised FSD was pushed back) can hit the price hard.
- **Shrinking car profits:** price cuts, cheap financing offers and Chinese competition keep margins thin, and regulatory credit income is fading.
- **Heavy spending:** around $20 billion of capital spending in 2026 can turn free cash flow negative again, as it did in Q2.
- **Key-person risk:** the stock moves on Elon Musk's statements, politics and attention spread across his other companies.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Tesla is one of the most volatile large stocks: it has lost two-thirds of its value in a year before and can swing 10% on a single earnings report. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more shares when it is down and fewer when it is up, which softens the timing problem but not the risk of the company itself. Many people keep a stock like this as a small slice next to a broad index fund. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
