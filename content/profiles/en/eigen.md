**EigenCloud**, called **EigenLayer** until June 2025, is a project on Ethereum that lets people "restake": put ether that already secures Ethereum to work a second time, securing other services in return for extra rewards. It is built by Eigen Labs, the Seattle company led by CEO Sreeram Kannan, a former University of Washington professor. ## How it works

Stakers deposit ETH or liquid staking tokens such as Lido's stETH into EigenLayer's smart contracts and delegate them to operators — companies that run software for outside services. These "actively validated services" (AVSs) include data storage for rollups (EigenDA), price oracles and bridges, and they pay operators and stakers for the security. If an operator breaks a service's rules, part of the restaked money can be taken away ("slashed"); slashing went live on mainnet in April 2025, and each service's penalties are kept separate. In June 2025 the project rebranded as EigenCloud, a platform for "verifiable" apps that combines EigenDA with EigenCompute for running code and EigenVerify for settling disputes.

## What the token is used for

Restakers stake ETH, not EIGEN, so EIGEN has a different job. It is staked to secure services against faults that code alone cannot prove, such as an oracle reporting a false price: in that case EIGEN holders can split (fork) the token so the cheaters lose out — the role EigenVerify relies on. EIGEN is also paid out as rewards to stakers and operators. Supply started at about 1.67 billion and keeps growing through yearly incentive issuance, while tokens for the team and investors unlock over several years.

## Its story so far

Eigen Labs raised $50 million in a Series A led by Blockchain Capital in March 2023, and restaking opened on Ethereum mainnet the same year. Hunting for points and a future airdrop, users poured in, and a16z crypto led a $100 million Series B in February 2024. In 2024 the team announced the EIGEN stakedrop; tokens could not be moved at first, and users in some countries, including the US, were left out, which drew criticism. EIGEN became transferable on 30 September 2024 and set its record that December. Deposits fell from their mid-2024 peak to about $12 billion by June 2025, when a16z bought another $70 million of EIGEN and the project became EigenCloud. In July 2025 Eigen Labs cut about 25% of its staff to focus on EigenCloud.

## Alternatives

The simplest alternative is ordinary staking on [Ethereum]({lang}/eth/) itself or through liquid staking with [Lido DAO]({lang}/ldo/). [ether.fi]({lang}/ethfi/) offers liquid restaking built on top of EigenLayer. Competing restaking protocols include Symbiotic and Karak, which accept a wider range of assets.

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** EIGEN has fallen far below its December 2024 record, and young tokens can keep falling.
- **Layered risk:** restaked ETH faces Ethereum's own staking risk plus smart-contract bugs and slashing from extra services.
- **Real demand:** rewards depend on services paying for security; if they pay little, restaking loses its appeal.
- **Supply:** yearly incentive issuance and unlocks for the team and early investors add new tokens to the market.
- **Competition and regulation:** rival restaking protocols compete for the same deposits, and rules for staking and tokens differ by country.

## What it means if you buy regularly

EIGEN has mostly fallen since it began trading in autumn 2024, so regular buyers so far have mostly bought into a falling price. Buying a fixed amount on a schedule spreads the risk of bad timing, but it does not make a falling token profitable; the result depends on whether EigenCloud's services win paying customers. Many people keep tokens like this to a small part of their savings that they can afford to see fall sharply. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
