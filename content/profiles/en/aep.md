**American Electric Power (AEP)** is one of the largest electricity companies in the United States: it delivers power to about 5.6 million customers in 11 states, from Ohio and Indiana to Texas and Oklahoma. It owns power plants and the biggest long-distance transmission network in the country, and state regulators set most of its prices. Based in Columbus, Ohio, it trades on Nasdaq.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Q3 2026 operating earnings: $2.23 per share, up from $1.80 a year earlier; revenue $6.83 billion.
> - New five-year capital plan: $100 billion for 2027–2031, with rate base expected to reach $162 billion.
> - Signed 15 GW of new load commitments in one quarter and expects 84 GW of extra contracted load by 2031.
> - Targets 9–11% yearly operating earnings growth from 2027 to 2031 and pays about $3.88 a share in dividends a year.

## How it makes money

AEP is mostly a regulated utility: it builds lines, substations and power plants, regulators add them to its "rate base" (the asset value it may earn a return on), and customer bills pay that back with an allowed profit. It has four segments. Vertically Integrated Utilities own plants and sell power directly and earned $697 million in Q3 2026. Transmission & Distribution Utilities (for example in Ohio and Texas) only deliver power, and AEP Transmission Holdco builds high-voltage lines — together about $500 million in the quarter. A smaller Generation & Marketing arm trades power in wholesale markets. The new plan splits spending into about $37 billion for transmission, $36 billion for generation and $22 billion for distribution.

## The numbers right now

In the quarter to 30 September 2026 AEP earned $1.19 billion on a GAAP basis, or $2.18 per share, against $1.82 a year earlier. Over nine months revenue was $18.3 billion, up from $16.6 billion. Over the last twelve months revenue was about $23.6 billion and net profit about $3.4 billion, and the stock trades at roughly 21 times earnings. Management expects 2026 operating earnings at or above the top of its $6.25–6.55 range, $6.75–7.05 in 2027 and $7.40–7.70 in 2028.

## The stock's story

For years AEP was a quiet dividend stock that moved with interest rates. That changed when big tech companies began building data centres and asking utilities for enormous amounts of power. AEP raised its spending plans several times, and the stock set new all-time highs — over the past 52 weeks it traded between $112.54 and $140.58. Since 2024 the company has been led by CEO Bill Fehrman, who has focused on signed contracts with large customers rather than forecasts alone.

## Competitors

In its regions AEP holds regulated monopolies, so it competes mainly for investors, for large new customers choosing where to build and, in wholesale markets, with independent power producers. Comparable listed utilities include Duke Energy, Southern Company, Dominion Energy, NextEra Energy and Exelon. AEP's edge is its huge transmission network, which data centres and new power plants need to connect to.

## Main risks

- **Data centre demand:** AEP itself warns that projected data centre demand is uncertain; if hyperscalers slow their building, part of the $100 billion plan may not pay off.
- **Regulators and politics:** rising bills are a political issue; this year Texas regulators disallowed part of AEP's costs and a West Virginia order also cut earnings.
- **Interest rates and debt:** a plan this size needs a lot of borrowing and new shares — the share count rose to 544 million from 535 million in a year.
- **Execution:** building plants and lines on time and on budget is hard when equipment and labour are in short supply.

## What it means if you buy regularly

AEP combines a regular dividend with a growth story that is unusual for a utility. Buying regularly and reinvesting dividends spreads purchases across rate scares and rallies, but utility stocks can drop sharply when interest rates rise or when growth hopes fade. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
