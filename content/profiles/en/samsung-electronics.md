**Samsung Electronics** is South Korea's largest company and the world's biggest maker of memory chips — the parts that store data in every phone, laptop and AI data centre. Most people know it for Galaxy phones and TVs, but in 2026 almost all of its profit comes from chips: in July–September it earned ₩107.4 trillion of operating profit, which Samsung says no tech company had ever done in a single quarter.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Q3 2026: revenue ₩195.3 trillion and operating profit ₩107.4 trillion, both all-time records.
> - The chip division (DS) made ₩106.5 trillion of that profit; phones, TVs and appliances together lost ₩0.8 trillion.
> - 2025 revenue was a record ₩333.6 trillion, but operating profit of ₩43.6 trillion fell behind SK hynix for the first time.
> - Samsung expects to return ₩90–110 trillion to shareholders for 2026 through dividends and share buybacks.

## How it makes money

Samsung has two big divisions. **Device Solutions (DS)** makes memory — DRAM, the working memory of computers, and NAND flash for storage — runs a contract chip factory (foundry) for other companies and designs processors. **Device eXperience (DX)** sells Galaxy phones, tablets, TVs and home appliances. On top of that, Samsung Display makes screens, including for other phone brands, and Harman makes car audio and electronics.

The most valuable product today is HBM (high-bandwidth memory): stacks of DRAM chips placed next to AI processors so they can be fed data very fast. Only three companies make it at scale — Samsung, SK hynix and Micron. In Q3 2026 the DS division had revenue of ₩147.1 trillion, about three quarters of the whole company.

In July–September 2026 revenue was ₩195.3 trillion, up 14% from the previous quarter. Chips made ₩106.5 trillion of operating profit and Samsung Display ₩1.1 trillion, while phones and networks lost ₩0.6 trillion and TVs and appliances lost ₩0.2 trillion. Samsung said it "significantly scaled up" sales of its newest HBM4 memory. It spent ₩29 trillion on factories and equipment in the quarter and expects about ₩84.4 trillion for the full year 2026.

For shareholders, Samsung paid a Q3 dividend of ₩4,601 per share (₩30 trillion in total) and estimates total 2026 returns of ₩90–110 trillion, including a large buyback and cancellation of shares. The final amount is due at a board meeting in late January 2027.

## The stock's story

Samsung Electronics was founded in 1969 as part of the Samsung group, which began in 1938 as a small trading shop in Daegu. It became the world's largest memory maker in the 1990s and later the biggest seller of smartphones. The 2020s were harder: when memory prices crashed in 2023, its chip division lost money, and it was late to supply HBM for Nvidia's AI processors, letting SK hynix take the lead. In 2025 the memory market turned up again — operating profit rose from ₩1.1 trillion in the chip division's first quarter to ₩16.4 trillion in its fourth — and Samsung started mass-producing first-generation 2nm chips. HBM4 shipments began in early 2026, and the AI memory shortage then lifted profits to records.

## Competitors

In memory Samsung competes with **SK hynix** ([page]({lang}/sk-hynix/)) and **Micron** in the US, plus Chinese makers such as CXMT and YMTC. In contract chipmaking it is far behind **TSMC**. Galaxy phones fight **Apple** ([page]({lang}/aapl/)), Xiaomi and other Android brands; its TVs and appliances compete with LG and Chinese brands like TCL and Hisense.

## Main risks

- **The memory cycle:** memory is a boom-and-bust business. When supply catches up with demand, prices and profits can collapse — as in 2023.
- **AI spending:** a big part of today's profit depends on data-centre spending by a handful of tech giants.
- **HBM competition:** SK hynix moved first, and Micron is growing; losing orders would hurt.
- **Weak consumer units:** phones, TVs and appliances barely break even, so the company leans almost entirely on chips.
- **Politics and control:** US–China export rules, tensions around Korea, the won exchange rate and the founding Lee family's control are long-running concerns.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Samsung's shares swing with the memory cycle, so long rises can be followed by deep falls even while the company stays profitable. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more shares when the price is low and fewer when it is high, without trying to time the cycle. The shares trade in Seoul in won, so for foreign buyers the exchange rate also changes the result. Many people hold one company only as part of a wider portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
