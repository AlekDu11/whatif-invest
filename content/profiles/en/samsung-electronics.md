**Samsung Electronics** is South Korea's largest company and the world's biggest maker of memory chips — the parts that store data in every phone, laptop and AI data centre. Most people know it for Galaxy phones and TVs, but today almost all of its profit comes from chips.

## How it makes money

Samsung has four main businesses. Device Solutions makes memory (DRAM and flash), runs a contract chip factory (foundry) and designs processors. Device eXperience sells Galaxy phones, tablets, TVs and home appliances. Samsung Display makes screens, including for other phone brands, and Harman makes car audio and electronics. In July–September 2026 the company had a record ₩195.3 trillion in revenue and ₩107.4 trillion in operating profit — and chips earned ₩106.5 trillion of that profit.

## Why it matters

AI models need huge amounts of fast memory, and Samsung is one of only three companies that can make high-bandwidth memory (HBM) at scale, alongside SK hynix and Micron. It sharply increased sales of its newest HBM4 chips in the third quarter. Samsung says this was the first time any tech company earned more than ₩100 trillion in operating profit in a single quarter. Samsung Electronics was founded in 1969 as part of the Samsung group, which began in 1938 as a small trading shop.

## Main risks

Memory is a boom-and-bust business: when supply catches up with demand, prices and profits can collapse — Samsung's chip unit lost money through most of 2023. Competition in HBM is fierce, and SK hynix moved first in AI memory. Its phone and TV units are under cost pressure and lost money last quarter. US–China export rules and tensions around Korea add political risk, and the founding Lee family's control is a long-running concern for minority shareholders. Buying a fixed amount regularly smooths price swings but does not remove the risk.
