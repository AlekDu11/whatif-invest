**Costco** runs huge no-frills warehouse stores where you can only shop if you pay a yearly membership fee. It sells a limited range of goods — groceries, fuel, electronics, tyres, its own Kirkland Signature brand — in bulk at very thin margins, and earns most of its profit from the fees. Its shares trade on Nasdaq under the ticker COST.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Net sales: $297.2 billion in fiscal 2026 (52 weeks to 30 August), +10.1% on the year; net income $9.2 billion ($20.76 a share).
> - Membership fees brought in $5.9 billion — only about 2% of revenue, but equal to about half of operating profit ($11.7 billion).
> - Comparable sales rose 8.4% in fiscal 2026 (6.6% without gasoline prices and currency); digital sales grew about 21%.
> - Costco ended the year with 939 warehouses in 14 countries and plans about 33 openings in fiscal 2027.

## How it makes money

Costco's model is simple: keep prices as low as possible, charge members a fee to get in, and let the fee carry the profit. A typical warehouse stocks around 4,000 products, far fewer than a supermarket, so Costco buys each one in enormous quantities and gets low prices from suppliers. It then marks goods up only a little. In fiscal 2026 the company made $297.2 billion of net sales and $5.9 billion of membership fees; operating income was $11.7 billion, so the fees alone covered about half of it. In September 2024 Costco raised its US and Canadian fees for the first time since 2017 — to $65 a year for Gold Star and $130 for Executive membership. Gasoline, pharmacy, optical, travel and a fast-growing online business add traffic and sales.

In the 16-week fourth quarter to 30 August 2026 net sales were $93.9 billion (+11.2%) and membership fees $1.85 billion (+7.3%). Net income rose 14.9% to $3.0 billion, or $6.75 a share, including a one-off benefit of $0.15 a share from refunded US tariffs, much of which Costco put back into lower prices. US comparable sales grew 10.7% in the quarter (7.2% without gasoline and currency), and digitally enabled sales rose 19.5%. For the full year net income was $9.23 billion, up from $8.10 billion. At the end of August Costco held $20.2 billion in cash; in the year it paid $2.46 billion in dividends and bought back $0.85 billion of shares.

## The stock's story

Costco opened its first warehouse in Seattle in 1983 and listed its shares in 1985. In 1993 it merged with Price Club, the company that invented the warehouse-club idea in 1976. Co-founder Jim Sinegal ran Costco until 2011 and became famous for refusing to raise the price of the $1.50 hot dog and soda combo. The shares were not always calm: in our monthly data they lost almost half their value between April 2000 and the end of 2002, and about a third during 2008. After that the stock climbed steadily for more than a decade, helped by store openings abroad, the fee rises of 2017 and 2024 and a jump in shopping during the 2020 pandemic. Ron Vachris took over as CEO in January 2024. In fiscal 2026 Costco also shut its Costco Next online marketplace and moved its best items into its main website.

## Competitors

Costco's closest rivals are Walmart's Sam's Club and BJ's Wholesale Club. It also competes with Walmart itself, Amazon, Target and supermarket chains such as Kroger, and abroad with local grocers and discounters like Aldi and Lidl. Its edge is volume: few rivals sell so much of each product at such low markups.

## Main risks

- **High expectations:** Costco shares have for years traded at a much higher price compared with profit than most retailers, so even a small slowdown can bring a sharp fall.
- **Membership renewals:** the model depends on members paying again every year; any loss of trust or value would hit the main source of profit.
- **Thin margins:** sales margins are tiny, so higher wages, freight costs or tariffs can quickly eat into earnings.
- **Gasoline and the economy:** fuel prices swing reported sales, and a weak economy can push shoppers to cheaper, smaller trips.
- **Growth abroad:** new countries take years to become profitable and face strong local competition.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Costco is a steady, well-run business, but a good company is not the same as a cheap stock — and the shares have halved before. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you get more shares when the price is low and fewer when it is high, without trying to time the market. Many people hold one company only as part of a wider portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
