**Aero** is a decentralized exchange — an app where people swap crypto tokens straight from their wallets, without a company holding their money. It was created in October 2026, when developer Dromos Labs merged its two exchanges, Aerodrome (the biggest on Coinbase's Base network) and Velodrome (the biggest on OP Mainnet), into one. ## How it works

Traders swap one token for another using pools of money that other users deposit, called liquidity pools. The people who fill the pools earn trading fees plus extra rewards paid in newly created AERO. Holders who stake their tokens as sAERO decide which pools get those rewards each period — the project calls it "predictive allocation" — and in return receive a share of the trading fees from the pools they back. Aero also connects trading across its seven networks, and its newest pools raise fees automatically when markets move fast.

## Why people think it has value

Before the merger, Aerodrome handled about 58% of all exchange trading on Base and Velodrome about 60% on OP Mainnet in the third quarter of 2026, according to the project. Base is the network run by Coinbase, one of the biggest crypto companies, so a lot of everyday on-chain trading passes through it. Unlike many exchange tokens, staked AERO is designed to collect the protocol's income, so its value is tied to how much trading the exchange attracts.

## Its story so far

Velodrome launched on Optimism's network in 2022 and introduced the "ve(3,3)" model: lock tokens, vote on where rewards go, earn the fees. In August 2023 the same team launched Aerodrome on Base, which became the main exchange there. In November 2025 Dromos Labs announced the plan to merge both into Aero, and both tokens fell about 20% on the news. The merger went live on 22 October 2026: AERO and VELO migrated into a new AERO, and locked veAERO and veVELO turned into sAERO. Self-custody holders have to start the upgrade themselves.

## Alternatives

[Uniswap]({lang}/uni/) is the best-known decentralized exchange and works on many of the same networks. [Curve]({lang}/crv/) focuses on swaps between similar assets such as stablecoins, [PancakeSwap]({lang}/cake/) leads on BNB Chain, and [Raydium]({lang}/ray/) and [Jupiter]({lang}/jup/) are the big names on Solana. Investors who want a bet on the networks themselves can look at [Optimism]({lang}/op/) or [Arbitrum]({lang}/arb/).

## Main risks

- **New issuance:** fresh AERO is paid out as rewards; the project expects about 12% inflation a year with a 20% ceiling, which can weigh on the price.
- **Migration:** wrong addresses, unsupported wallets or mistakes during the upgrade can delay or lose tokens.
- **Smart contracts:** a bug or hack in the exchange's code could hit users' funds.
- **Competition:** traders move quickly to whichever exchange offers the best prices and rewards.
- **Regulation:** rules for decentralized exchanges and their tokens are still unclear in many countries.

## What it means if you buy regularly

The new AERO has traded only since autumn 2026, so there is little history to judge it by. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your entry price and avoids putting everything in at a single high, but it cannot protect you from a token that keeps losing value. Many people keep exchange tokens to a small part of their savings. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
