**Nu Holdings** is the company behind **Nubank**, a bank with no branches that started in São Paulo in 2013 with a no-fee purple credit card run from a phone app. It now serves 142.6 million customers in Brazil, Mexico and Colombia — and it listed on the New York Stock Exchange in December 2021 at $9 a share.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Customers: 142.6 million, +3.7 million in Q3 2026 alone; about 120 million in Brazil.
> - Profit: a record $1.1 billion in the quarter, +39% a year (+31% at constant exchange rates), with a 32% return on equity.
> - Bad loans rose: loans more than 90 days late reached 7.3% of the portfolio, up from 6.9% a quarter earlier.
> - The stock set a record of $18.76 in January 2026 and was about 34% below it in mid-November.

## How it makes money

Nubank earns most of its money the way banks always have: interest on credit cards and personal loans, which in Brazil carry very high rates. Fees when shops accept its cards (interchange) add more, as do investments, insurance and its in-app shopping marketplace. Deposits — $47.5 billion at the end of Q3 2026 — fund the lending. Because it has no branches and runs on its own software, Nu spends very little to serve each customer: its efficiency ratio (costs as a share of income) was 19.3%, a level few banks reach. Each active customer brings in about $18 of revenue a month, and 83.6% of customers use the app actively.

## The numbers right now

In Q3 2026 (results published 12 November) Nu reported $6.3 billion of gross revenue, up 37% year on year at constant exchange rates, and $1.1 billion of net income. The credit portfolio grew 33% to $41.6 billion, but growth has slowed for two quarters in a row (from 40% in Q1). Mexico, where Nu now operates under a full banking licence, has 16.6 million customers (+27%) and Colombia 6.1 million (+62%). Profit also absorbed a one-off charge of about $53 million linked to payment companies in liquidation.

New markets are next: Nu US launched in September 2026 after Nu won conditional approval in January 2026 to set up a US national bank, and Nu Global, a multi-currency account built on stablecoins (crypto tokens pegged to the dollar), launched in October.

## The stock's story

Nu's debut came at the end of the 2021 tech boom. Within six months, from December 2021 to June 2022, the share price lost about 72% as interest rates jumped and investors fled loss-making fintechs. Rising customer numbers and fast-growing profits pulled it back; it regained its IPO-era highs by March 2024 and set a record of $18.76 in late January 2026. Since then worries about slower loan growth and rising late payments have pushed it down: it closed at $12.33 on 17 November 2026.

## Competitors

In Brazil Nu competes with the giant banks Itaú Unibanco, Bradesco and Banco do Brasil, digital rivals such as Inter&Co, PicPay and Mercado Pago (part of MercadoLibre), and in Mexico with BBVA México and other banks. Its advantages are scale and a much lower cost base.

## Main risks

- **Credit quality:** much of the lending is unsecured card and personal credit; the 90+ day late ratio is rising, and a downturn in Brazil would hit profit fast.
- **Currency:** results are reported in dollars, but income is in reais, pesos and Colombian pesos — a weaker real shrinks reported profit.
- **Brazilian politics and regulation:** caps on card interest, new rules on household debt or tax changes can cut margins.
- **Expansion costs:** Mexico, Colombia and the US need heavy spending before they earn as much as Brazil.
- **Slowing growth:** a cooling loan book may mean the stock no longer earns a "fast grower" price.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Nu is a growth bank in emerging markets, so it carries two kinds of swings: the stock's own and the currencies'. It already lost about 72% in its first six months, then took more than two years to recover. Regular buying spreads entry points across such periods, but one bank in one region is a concentrated bet — many people hold it only as a small part of a broader portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
