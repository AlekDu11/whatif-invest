**Zilliqa (ZIL)** is a blockchain from Singapore that became famous as one of the first networks to use **sharding** — splitting the work between groups of computers so that more transactions fit through at once. ## How it works

Zilliqa grew out of sharding research at the National University of Singapore. In its original design, nodes did a little proof of work to join and were split into shards that processed transactions side by side, then agreed on blocks with a fast voting method (pBFT). Smart contracts were written in Zilliqa's own language, Scilla. In April 2023 the network added support for the Ethereum Virtual Machine, so developers could deploy Solidity apps and users could connect with MetaMask. Zilliqa 2.0, launched in 2025 after a six-month test with external validators, moved the chain to a new proof-of-stake consensus.

## What the token is used for

ZIL pays the fees for transactions and smart contracts on the network, and holders can stake it to help secure the chain and earn rewards. The team says ZIL will stay the only token of the network after the move to a Layer 2. Its value depends on whether developers, apps and institutions actually use Zilliqa rather than one of the many faster-growing chains.

## Its story so far

Zilliqa published its whitepaper in 2017, sold tokens in a public sale that year and launched its mainnet in January 2019. Staking followed in 2020. In the 2021 bull market ZIL rose to about $0.26 in May 2021, and it spiked again in March 2022, but it then fell for years. Early 2025 brought trouble: an exploit forced its X-Bridge offline and the chief executive stepped down that spring. In 2026 the problems grew: a Ledger app bug paused legacy transactions in July, a compromised key was used to drain the X-Bridge in early October, and a flaw in some older tokens was exploited on 22–23 October. On 5 November 2026 the team announced it would replace the standalone chain with an Ethereum-settled Layer 2 for regulated institutions, without a launch date; on 20 November Binance put a monitoring tag on ZIL. The price fell to new lows that month.

## Alternatives

Zilliqa competes with other smart-contract platforms that promise speed, such as [Ethereum]({lang}/eth/) with its Layer 2 networks, [Solana]({lang}/sol/) and [NEAR]({lang}/near/), which also uses sharding. [MultiversX]({lang}/egld/) is another chain built around sharding.

## Main risks

- **Project survival:** the network is shrinking, and the move to a Layer 2 has no date yet; if it fails, ZIL could lose most of its use.
- **Security:** two bridge incidents and a token exploit in 2025–2026 show real technical risk.
- **Exchange risk:** a Binance monitoring tag and reports of other exchanges ending support can make ZIL harder to buy and sell.
- **Big swings:** ZIL has lost well over 99% from its 2021 high, and small coins can drop sharply in days.
- **Competition:** bigger chains with more apps and money attract the developers Zilliqa needs.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Regular buying spreads your entry price over time, but it only works out if the asset recovers. For a coin whose network is in the middle of a risky rebuild, steady buying can just as easily mean steadily adding to losses. If you hold ZIL, watch the official news about the Layer 2 move and exchange support, and keep it to a very small share of money you could afford to lose. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
