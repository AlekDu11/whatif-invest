**BNB** is the coin of Binance, the world's largest crypto exchange, and the fuel of BNB Chain — one of the busiest blockchains for cheap transfers and trading apps. It started in 2017 as a discount token for paying trading fees and grew into one of the five largest cryptocurrencies.

## How it works and what it is used for

BNB was sold in an initial coin offering in July 2017, days before Binance opened. In 2019 it moved to its own network, now called BNB Chain. Today the coin has several jobs: it pays the transaction fees ("gas") on BNB Smart Chain, it can be staked with the network's validators, and it cuts trading fees on Binance — by up to 25% on spot trades.

## Why it matters

BNB is the only top coin with a built-in buyback-style "burn". Every quarter a formula based on the price and network activity sends coins to an address no one can spend from; part of every transaction fee is burned too. The goal is to shrink supply from the original 200 million coins to 100 million. After the July 2026 burn about 133.2 million BNB were left. One surprising fact: that single quarterly burn destroyed coins worth about $930 million.

## Main risks

BNB's fate is tied to one company. In 2023 Binance agreed to pay $4.3 billion in US penalties over money-laundering and sanctions failures and now works under a compliance monitor; new legal trouble anywhere could hit the price. BNB Smart Chain runs on just 45 validators, far fewer than Bitcoin or Ethereum, so it is more centralised. Crypto prices can fall 50–80% in a bear market. Buying a fixed amount regularly smooths the swings but does not remove the risk.
