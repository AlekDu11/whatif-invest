**Ethena** (ENA) is the token of the Ethena protocol, which issues USDe — a "synthetic dollar" that is not backed by money in a bank but by crypto plus an opposite bet on the futures market. Launched in 2024 by founder Guy Young, USDe grew within a year and a half into one of the largest dollar tokens in crypto. > - There will never be more than 15 billion ENA; team and investor tokens have been released step by step since April 2025.

## How it works

When someone mints USDe, Ethena takes their crypto — mainly ether and bitcoin, plus stablecoins — and at the same time opens a short position of the same size in perpetual futures on big exchanges. If the price of ether rises, the collateral gains and the short loses the same amount, so the total stays near $1 ("delta-neutral"). The yield comes mostly from funding payments: in rising markets traders who bet on higher prices pay those who are short. Users who stake USDe get sUSDe, which collects that yield; when funding turns negative, a reserve fund is meant to cover the gap.

## What the ENA token is used for

ENA holders vote on the protocol's rules, risk settings and the use of its money. Staked ENA (sENA) has so far earned incentives rather than a share of fees. In August 2026 Ethena proposed, and holders approved, a "fee switch": once USDe supply exceeds $7.5 billion, part of the protocol's revenue — rising in steps with supply — goes to buying back ENA. Buyers of ENA are therefore betting that USDe grows again and that the token will capture more of its income.

## Its story so far

Ethena raised money from investors such as Dragonfly and Arthur Hayes' Maelstrom and opened USDe to the public in February 2024. ENA launched with an airdrop in April 2024 and peaked at about $1.52 that month. USDe supply passed $7.5 billion in July 2025 and reached almost $15 billion in October 2025. During the crash of 10 October 2025, USDe briefly traded far below $1 on Binance, which shook confidence, and as funding rates fell its supply shrank to about $4.2 billion by September 2026. In August 2026 the Ethena Foundation bought out several large investors and said the remaining investor tokens would be released at once from October 2026 instead of monthly.

## Alternatives

USDe competes with dollar stablecoins such as USDT and USDC, which are backed by cash and Treasuries, and with [Sky]({lang}/sky/), whose USDS and savings rate offer another on-chain dollar yield. Among DeFi tokens, [Aave]({lang}/aave/) and [Pendle]({lang}/pendle/) are closely linked: much USDe is lent on Aave and traded as yield on Pendle.

## Main risks

- **Funding rates:** when markets cool, futures yields shrink or turn negative, USDe becomes less attractive and protocol revenue falls.
- **Token unlocks:** large team, foundation and investor releases add supply; buybacks start only above $7.5 billion of USDe.
- **Concentration:** StablecoinX, a treasury company, held about 20% of all ENA in 2026, and a few exchanges hold the hedges.
- **Exchange and peg risk:** USDe relies on centralized exchanges and custodians; a failure or a crash like October 2025 can break its $1 price for a while.
- **Regulation:** synthetic dollars sit in a grey zone; German regulator BaFin acted against Ethena's local entity in 2025.

## What it means if you buy regularly

ENA's history is short and mostly downhill after its first month, and its value depends on how USDe grows. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your entry price over time, but it does not protect you from a long decline. Remember that ENA is not USDe: it does not stay at $1 and can swing sharply. Many people keep such tokens to a small part of their savings. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
