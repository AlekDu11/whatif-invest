**Flare** is a blockchain built for data: it gives apps reliable prices and facts from other blockchains and the internet, so that coins such as XRP, which have no smart contracts of their own, can be used in DeFi. Its token FLR reached the market on 9 January 2023 through an airdrop to XRP holders, and today the network is worth {{mcap:flr}} ({{rank:flr}} among cryptocurrencies). People buy FLR mainly as a bet that Flare becomes the main place where XRP and other coins without smart contracts earn yield.

## How it works

Flare is an EVM layer 1 network, so apps written for [Ethereum]({lang}/eth/) run on it with little change. What sets it apart are two data systems built into the protocol itself. The Flare Time Series Oracle (FTSO) publishes prices for crypto, stocks and other assets: around 100 independent data providers support each feed, and the fastest feeds update with every block, roughly every 1.1 seconds, free for apps to read. The Flare Data Connector (FDC) confirms events on other blockchains and on the web — for example, that a payment really happened on the XRP Ledger. On top of both sits FAssets: independent agents lock collateral, users deposit XRP, and the network mints FXRP one-to-one, which can then be lent, swapped or used to earn yield.

## Why people think it has value

FLR is used to pay fees on the network, to stake with validators and to delegate to the data providers who run the oracle, earning rewards for both. It also gives a vote in Flare's governance. The main idea behind the token is demand from XRP: XRP is one of the largest cryptocurrencies, but on its own it can do little in DeFi. If more XRP moves onto Flare as FXRP, more fees, staking and oracle usage follow. In 2026 Flare started collecting protocol fees from FAssets and the Data Connector and raised the base transaction fee, part of which is burned.

## Its story so far

Flare was announced in August 2020 by Hugo Philion (CEO) and Sean Rowan (CTO). In December 2020 it took a snapshot of XRP wallets and promised holders a token, then called Spark, one for one; many exchanges supported the claim for their users. The token was renamed FLR in October 2022, and after a delay of about two years the first 15% was finally airdropped on 9 January 2023. The price set its record on launch day and then fell by more than 75% within days, as many recipients sold. The other 85% of the airdrop was paid out in monthly FlareDrops over 36 months, which ended on 30 January 2026. FTSOv2 brought faster price feeds, FXRP went live on the main network in September 2025, and in spring 2026 the community voted (FIP.16, 98% in favour) to cut yearly issuance from up to 5 billion to about 3 billion FLR.

## Alternatives

In oracles, Flare competes with [Chainlink]({lang}/link/), the market leader, and with [Pyth]({lang}/pyth/), [Band Protocol]({lang}/band/) and [API3]({lang}/api3/). For using XRP in DeFi, the alternatives are the XRP Ledger's own features and wrapped XRP on other chains — or simply holding [XRP]({lang}/xrp/) itself. [Songbird]({lang}/sgb/) is Flare's "canary" test network, where new features such as FAssets run first.

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** FLR has spent most of its life far below its launch-day record, and smaller coins can fall much faster than bitcoin.
- **Supply:** new FLR is still issued every year; if usage does not grow, rewards paid in new tokens can weigh on the price.
- **Dependence on XRP:** much of the plan rests on XRP holders moving coins to Flare; if they do not, demand for FLR stays small.
- **Competition:** Chainlink and other oracles are far bigger and already used by most large DeFi apps.
- **Technology:** FAssets rely on agents, collateral and bridges between chains — a bug or a collateral crash could cause losses even after audits.

## What it means if you buy regularly

FLR's short history is mostly a long slide after a hyped start, with sharp rebounds along the way. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your entry price over time instead of betting on one moment — but with a young, small coin it can still mean losing a large part of the money. Many people keep coins like this to a very small part of their crypto, which itself is a small part of their savings. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
