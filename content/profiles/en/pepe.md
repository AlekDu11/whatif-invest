**Pepe** is a memecoin: a token with no product behind it, built around the Pepe the Frog internet meme. It launched on Ethereum in April 2023, became one of the fastest-growing coins in crypto history within weeks and is now one of the largest memecoins, worth {{mcap:pepe}} in total today ({{rank:pepe}} among cryptocurrencies). People buy it for the community, the jokes and the hope of big price swings, not for any use.

> **Key takeaways**
> - There are about 420.69 trillion PEPE, all created at launch; there is no mining, no new issuance and no transaction tax.
> - Its record high is {{ath:pepe}} ({{athd:pepe}}); today the price is {{fromath:pepe}} from it.
> - The team is anonymous, and in August 2023 three former members moved about 16 trillion tokens (around $15 million) to exchanges.
> - PEPE has no official link to Matt Furie, the artist who created Pepe the Frog.

## How it works

PEPE is a standard ERC-20 token on Ethereum, so it lives in ordinary crypto wallets and trades on decentralized exchanges such as Uniswap as well as on big exchanges like Binance and Coinbase. At launch 93.1% of the supply went into a Uniswap pool, and the pool's tokens were burned, so that liquidity cannot be pulled. The other 6.9% went to a team wallet with several signers, reserved for exchange listings and bridges. Ownership of the contract was renounced: nobody can mint more tokens, freeze wallets or change the rules.

## What the token is used for

PEPE has no built-in use: it does not pay fees, give votes or earn income. Its value depends only on how many people want to hold and trade it. The project describes itself as a meme with no utility. Demand comes from its large online community, social media hype, exchange listings and traders looking for fast moves — which is why the price can jump or fall by double digits in a day.

## Its story so far

PEPE launched in mid-April 2023 with no presale. Within weeks its market value passed $1 billion and peaked around $1.5 billion in May 2023, and Binance listed it. On 24 August 2023 three former team members cut the team wallet's required signers from five of eight to two of eight and sent about 16 trillion PEPE to exchanges; the price fell about 18%. In October 2023 6.9 trillion PEPE were burned. In November 2024 Robinhood and Coinbase added PEPE, and in December 2024 the price set its record before falling sharply in 2025 and 2026.

## Alternatives

PEPE competes with other big memecoins: [Dogecoin]({lang}/doge/), the original joke coin from 2013, [Shiba Inu]({lang}/shib/), [BONK]({lang}/bonk/) and [dogwifhat]({lang}/wif/) on Solana, and [FLOKI]({lang}/floki/). New memecoins appear every day on platforms such as pump.fun, so attention can move quickly from one to another.

## Main risks

- **Extreme swings:** memecoins can lose 80–90% of their value, and PEPE has had several drops of more than half.
- **No fundamentals:** there is no income, product or asset behind the token; if interest fades, little supports the price.
- **Concentration:** a small number of large wallets, including exchanges, hold a big share of the supply, so their sales can move the price.
- **Anonymous team:** the 2023 multisig incident showed that insiders can sell large amounts with little warning.
- **Regulation and listings:** an exchange delisting or new rules for memecoins could cut access and liquidity.

## What it means if you buy regularly

PEPE's history shows huge rallies and deep falls in short periods. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more when the price is low and less when it is high, without trying to guess the bottom — but it does not remove the risk of losses, and with a memecoin those losses can be close to total. Many people keep memecoins to a very small part of their savings that they can afford to lose. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
