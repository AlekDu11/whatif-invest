**Meta Platforms** owns the apps much of the world opens every day: Facebook, Instagram, WhatsApp, Messenger and Threads. They are free to use; Meta earns money by showing ads to the people scrolling them. Started as Facebook by Mark Zuckerberg in 2004, the company listed on the Nasdaq in May 2012 and renamed itself Meta in 2021. Zuckerberg is still CEO and controls the votes.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: $64.6 billion in Q3 2026, +26% a year; 3.64 billion people used at least one Meta app each day in September.
> - A $9.89 billion charge for a teen mental-health settlement with US states cut the quarter's operating margin to 20% (from 40%).
> - Meta plans $135–145 billion of capital spending in 2026, mostly on AI; free cash flow in Q3 was minus $2.12 billion.
> - From January 2027 every shareholder gets one new non-voting Class C share per share held, so the price should look roughly halved, like a 2-for-1 split.

## How it makes money

Almost everything — $64.16 billion of the $64.6 billion in Q3 2026 — came from the "Family of Apps", and that means ads. Businesses pay to reach exactly the people likely to buy, and Meta's AI decides who sees which ad. In Q3 the number of ads shown rose 10% and the average price per ad rose 14%. The second segment, Reality Labs, makes VR headsets (Quest) and smart glasses; it brought in only $443 million and lost $4.13 billion in the quarter. Meta is also building its own AI models and assistants: Meta AI inside its apps and Muse, a personal AI agent launched in August that more than 10 million people in the US and Canada have set up.

## The numbers right now

In the quarter to 30 September 2026 operating income was $12.99 billion, down 37%; without the legal charge it would have been $22.88 billion. Net income was $9.86 billion and diluted earnings per share $3.83 ($7.40 without the charge). Capital spending was $34.91 billion in one quarter, above the $32.79 billion of cash the business generated. Meta held $82.45 billion of cash and securities against $80.92 billion of long-term debt, up from $58.74 billion at the end of 2025. It paid $1.36 billion in dividends and bought back no shares. Headcount fell 15% to 66,775. For Q4 2026 it guides to $73–76 billion of revenue.

## The stock's story

Meta had a rough 2022: Apple's privacy changes hurt ad targeting, TikTok pulled users away, and Zuckerberg poured billions into the "metaverse". From its 2021 high the stock fell about 75% by October 2022, by our monthly price data. Then came the "year of efficiency": job cuts, tighter spending and better AI ad tools. The stock multiplied and reached a month-end record of about $770 in July 2025. Since then the huge AI spending and legal costs have kept it below that peak.

## Competitors

For ad money Meta competes with Google and YouTube, TikTok, Amazon's ads business, Snap, Pinterest and X. In AI it races OpenAI, Google and Anthropic; in headsets and glasses, Apple and Google. Its edge is scale: no other company reaches 3.6 billion people a day.

## Main risks

- **AI spending:** $135–145 billion of capital spending this year pushed free cash flow below zero in Q3; Meta says it will keep investing aggressively in 2027.
- **Teens and the law:** the settlement with state attorneys general could cost up to about $18.59 billion, and more youth-safety trials are scheduled in the US.
- **Reality Labs losses:** Meta expects $18–19 billion of losses there in 2026 and around $15 billion in 2027.
- **Dependence on ads:** when the economy slows, advertisers cut budgets first, and privacy rules in the EU and elsewhere can limit targeting.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Meta shows how deep a big, profitable company can fall: about −75% in under two years, then a full recovery. Regular buyers who kept going through 2022 bought cheap shares; those who stopped missed the rebound. Remember the Class C dividend in 2027: the price per share will look lower, but the dividend by itself doesn't change what your holding is worth. Past results don't guarantee future ones.
