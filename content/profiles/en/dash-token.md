**Dash** — short for "digital cash" — is one of the oldest cryptocurrencies, built for fast, cheap everyday payments. It started in 2014 as a fork of Bitcoin's code and added a second layer of servers, called masternodes, that confirm payments in seconds and vote on how the project spends money. ## How it works

Like Bitcoin, Dash uses miners and proof of work (with its own X11 algorithm) to add a new block about every 2.5 minutes. On top of that sit thousands of masternodes. They lock payments almost instantly with InstantSend, so a shop does not have to wait for several blocks, and they lock each new block with ChainLocks, which makes the kind of attack where someone rewrites recent history much harder. Masternodes also run CoinJoin, an optional feature that mixes coins between users for more privacy. The block reward is split between miners, masternodes and the treasury.

## Why people think it has value

The supply is capped and known in advance, payments settle in seconds for a fraction of a cent, and the network funds its own development through the treasury instead of relying on a company. Masternode owners earn a share of new coins, which creates steady demand from people who want to run one. Dash has been used for payments in countries with high inflation, such as Venezuela, and in July 2024 it launched Dash Platform (Evolution), which adds usernames, data storage and simpler apps.

## Its story so far

Evan Duffield launched the coin on 18 January 2014 as XCoin; it was renamed Darkcoin weeks later and Dash in March 2015. A bug in the first days let about 1.9 million coins be mined very quickly, an "instamine" that critics still raise. Dash grew into one of the ten largest cryptocurrencies and set its record in December 2017, close to $1,500. It then lost more than 95% by late 2018 and traded mostly between $20 and $200 in the years after. In 2019 it added ChainLocks; in 2024 it launched Evolution and was delisted by some exchanges that dropped privacy-related coins. In October 2025 its price more than doubled in a rally in privacy coins led by Zcash, then fell back in 2026.

## Alternatives

For quick, cheap payments with a Bitcoin-style design, [Litecoin]({lang}/ltc/) and [Bitcoin Cash]({lang}/bch/) are the closest rivals. For people who want privacy, Monero and [Zcash]({lang}/zec/) offer much stronger built-in protection. For everyday digital payments, many people now use dollar stablecoins instead of any of these coins.

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** DASH has lost more than 95% from its 2017 record, and deep falls can happen again.
- **Losing ground:** it has dropped far down the market rankings as newer networks and stablecoins took over payments.
- **Regulation:** because of its privacy feature some exchanges have delisted DASH, and more could follow.
- **Concentration:** large masternode owners hold a lot of coins and voting power over the treasury.
- **Storage:** if an exchange fails or you lose your private key, the coins can be gone for good.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Dash's history since 2017 has been a long decline broken by short, sharp rallies. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more when the price is low and less when it is high, without trying to guess the bottom — but it cannot turn around an asset that keeps losing its place in the market. Many people keep smaller, older coins like DASH to a small part of their savings that they can afford to see fall sharply. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
