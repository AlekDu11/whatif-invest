**Uber** is the app that sends you a car, brings you dinner and, for businesses, books a truck. It owns almost none of the cars, bikes or trucks: it matches customers with independent drivers, couriers and carriers and keeps a cut of every trip. In Q3 2026 people and companies spent $59.8 billion through the app — 20% more than a year earlier — yet the stock fell about 28% in the year to September on fears that robotaxis could cut Uber out.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Gross bookings (everything spent on the app): $59.8 billion in Q3 2026, +20%; revenue $14.8 billion, +10%.
> - 215 million people used Uber at least once a month (+14%), taking 4.1 billion trips.
> - Free cash flow $2.5 billion in the quarter; $750 million spent buying back its own shares.
> - It bid about $14.8 billion for Delivery Hero, with that company's board's support; closing is expected in the second half of 2027.

## How it makes money

Uber has three segments. **Mobility** (rides, taxis, rentals) handled $29.5 billion of bookings in Q3 2026 and brought in $7.5 billion of revenue. **Delivery** (Uber Eats, groceries, shop orders) handled $28.5 billion of bookings and $5.5 billion of revenue, up 23%. **Freight** connects shippers with truckers and earned $1.8 billion. Uber also sells ads in its apps and runs the Uber One membership, which passed 50 million subscribers in 2026.

Revenue grew more slowly than bookings (10% against 20%) mainly because of business model changes in some markets, which cut revenue growth by 8 percentage points — so bookings are the better gauge of how busy the platform is.

In the quarter to 30 September 2026 Uber made $1.4 billion of operating income (+30%) and $3.0 billion of adjusted EBITDA (+31%), about 5% of bookings. Net income was only $512 million, down 92%, because a year earlier it had booked a one-off $4.9 billion tax gain and this time it lost $476 million on the value of stakes it holds in other companies. It ended the quarter with $10.1 billion in cash and short-term investments. For Q4 2026 it expects bookings of $63.25–65.25 billion, up 18–22%.

## The stock's story

Uber listed on the New York Stock Exchange in May 2019 at $45 a share. In the 2020 crash it fell under $15. Cost cuts brought its first full year of GAAP profit in 2023, it joined the S&P 500, and in 2025 the shares passed $100 for the first time. Then the mood turned: as Tesla, Waymo and others pushed self-driving taxis, Uber fell to $69.42 by 23 September 2026, about 28% lower in a year. That month CEO Dara Khosrowshahi bought about $10 million of shares himself, his first open-market purchase since 2022.

## Competitors

In rides Uber competes with Lyft in the US, Bolt in Europe and Africa, and DiDi and Grab in Asia and Latin America. In food delivery its main rival is DoorDash. The new threat is robotaxi fleets: Waymo is a partner in some cities but plans its own app in Austin and Atlanta, and Tesla launched its Cybercab. Uber's answer is to be the network for many self-driving companies at once; it expects about 700 driverless vehicles on its platform by the end of 2026 and more than 2,000 in 2027.

## Main risks

- **Robotaxis:** if car makers and tech companies run their own driverless fleets and apps, Uber could lose its role as the middleman.
- **A big, slow deal:** the Delivery Hero takeover (€41.50 a share) faces a long competition review and adds integration risk.
- **Driver rules:** courts and governments keep debating whether drivers are employees, which could raise costs.
- **Noisy profits:** gains and losses on stakes in other companies swing net income by billions.
- **Competition** in delivery and rides keeps prices and incentives under pressure.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Uber has lived through a fall of more than two-thirds from its IPO price to the 2020 low, then a recovery to over $100, then a new slide in 2026. The business kept growing through most of it, but the share price moved on fears about the future. Regular buying spreads purchases across such moods; it does not remove the risk that the robotaxi shift hurts Uber. Many people keep a single stock like this as a small part of a broader portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
