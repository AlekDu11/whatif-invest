**Nebius** rents out AI computing power. It builds data centres full of Nvidia chips and sells time on them to companies training and running AI models, including Microsoft and Meta. Until 2024 the same listed company owned Yandex, the Russian search giant. After selling the Russian business it rebuilt itself as an AI cloud based in the Netherlands.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: $980.8 million in Q3 2026, up 571% from $146.1 million a year earlier (about $325 million of it was one-off).
> - Its annualised AI cloud revenue run rate reached $6.4 billion at the end of September.
> - Management expects $12–13 billion of revenue in 2027 but plans to spend $40–45 billion on data centres that year.
> - Back on Nasdaq since October 2024, the stock reached a 52-week high near $300 in 2026, then fell 16.8% in one day after the Q3 report.

## How it makes money

Most of the money comes from the AI cloud: customers rent GPUs (graphics chips) by the hour or reserve capacity for years, and large deals now drive growth. In September 2025 Microsoft signed a contract worth about $17.4 billion through 2031, which could rise to $19.4 billion. Meta followed with a $3 billion, five-year deal in November 2025. In March 2026 Meta added $12 billion of dedicated capacity starting in early 2027, with up to $15 billion more possible. Nvidia is both a supplier and a backer, with a $2 billion investment in March 2026.

Nebius also owns smaller businesses: TripleTen (online courses that retrain people for tech jobs), Avride (self-driving cars and delivery robots) and stakes in the database company ClickHouse and the data-labelling firm Toloka.

## The numbers right now

In the quarter to 30 September 2026 revenue was $980.8 million. Adjusted EBITDA, a profit measure before interest, taxes and equipment wear, was $465.8 million. The net loss from continuing operations narrowed to $37.5 million, from $119.6 million a year earlier.

The 2026 outlook was trimmed. Revenue should land at the low end of $3.0–3.4 billion, and the $7 billion run-rate goal moved from December 2026 to February 2027. Capital spending for 2026 rose to about $27 billion. For 2027 Nebius expects $12–13 billion of revenue, $40–45 billion of spending and a pre-tax break-even in Q4 2027. It ended September with $7.43 billion of cash and about $14.2 billion of debt, and the share count grew about 8% in the quarter.

## The stock's story

Nasdaq halted trading in the shares in 2022, after Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine. Trading resumed on 21 October 2024, once the Russian business was gone and the company had about $2 billion of cash. The AI boom and big contracts brought new records in mid-2026, after Nvidia's investment and praise from Jensen Huang in June. After the Q3 report the stock fell 16.8% in one day to $128.64 on 12 November 2026, less than half the peak.

## Competitors

Nebius competes with CoreWeave, Lambda and other specialist "neoclouds", and with the big clouds, Amazon Web Services, Microsoft Azure, Google Cloud and Oracle. Nebius pitches itself as a full-stack provider, from GPU clusters to developer tools.

## Main risks

- **Huge spending, borrowed money:** $40–45 billion of planned 2027 spending, mostly funded by customer prepayments and debt.
- **Slipping targets:** the run-rate goal and 2026 revenue have already been pushed back or lowered.
- **Few big customers:** Microsoft and Meta contracts make up a large part of future revenue.
- **Dilution:** new shares and convertible notes keep raising the share count.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Nebius is a fast-changing company with a short trading history since 2024, and its price has already risen and fallen sharply. Our "$10 a day for 2 years" figure covers almost exactly that ride, so it reflects one unusual period. Regular buying spreads your entry points but cannot remove the risk of one company missing its plans. Many people keep a stock like this as a small part of a broader portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
