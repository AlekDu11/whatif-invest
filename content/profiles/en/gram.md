**Gram (GRAM)**, called Toncoin (TON) until June 2026, is the coin of The Open Network (TON), a blockchain closely tied to Telegram and its roughly one billion users. It pays network fees, is staked to secure the chain and powers the wallet inside Telegram. All Gram in circulation was worth about $2.8 billion in November 2026.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Telegram raised about $1.7 billion for TON and its Gram token in 2018, then dropped the project in 2020 after a US SEC case; independent developers relaunched it as Toncoin.
> - On 15 June 2026 Toncoin became Gram again after 81% of votes backed the change; balances converted 1:1 with no swap.
> - About 2.9 billion coins circulate (November 2026); in 2023 about 1.08 billion coins of inactive early miners were frozen for four years.
> - The $8.25 record (June 2024) dates from the Toncoin era; our price history under the Gram name starts in July 2026.

## How it works

TON is a proof-of-stake blockchain: validators lock up coins to confirm blocks, and holders can delegate coins to them and share the rewards. An April 2026 upgrade made it about ten times faster, with finality under a second, and fees were then cut about sixfold to a fraction of a cent.

## Why people think it has value

Gram pays fees, is staked and is used to vote — the rename itself was decided by a coin vote. The main draw is Telegram: a wallet sits inside the app, Mini Apps must use TON, channel owners get their ad revenue share in the coin, and Telegram usernames are sold for it on Fragment. In 2024 TON games and coins such as [Notcoin]({lang}/not/) and [DOGS]({lang}/dogs/) drew millions of users. In May 2026 Telegram replaced the TON Foundation as the network's main steward and largest validator.

## Its story so far

In 2018 Pavel and Nikolai Durov sold future Gram tokens to private investors for about $1.7 billion. In October 2019 the SEC sued over an unregistered securities offering and won an injunction. In 2020 Telegram quit, returned more than $1.2 billion and paid an $18.5 million penalty. Independent developers relaunched the network, later steered by the Swiss TON Foundation, as Toncoin. A 2024 boom in tap-to-earn games lifted it to $8.25 that June. That August Durov was detained in France and placed under formal investigation over crimes on Telegram; his travel ban was lifted in November 2025. In April 2026 he launched a seven-step "Make TON Great Again" plan; step four, announced on 1 June 2026 when the coin traded near $2, restored the original name.

## Alternatives

For payments and apps TON competes with [Ethereum]({lang}/eth/), [Solana]({lang}/sol/) and [BNB Chain]({lang}/bnb/), and for cheap stablecoin transfers with [Tron]({lang}/trx/).

## Main risks

- **Concentration:** CoinMarketCap data cited in June 2025 showed whale wallets holding over 68% of the supply.
- **Frozen coins:** the 1.08 billion coins frozen in February 2023 for four years could add supply once the freeze ends in 2027.
- **Telegram and Durov:** the rename, fee cuts and stewardship come from Telegram, so trouble for it or its founder hits the coin.
- **Regulation:** the project already lost to the SEC once, and rules for crypto in messaging apps vary by country.
- **Swings and scams:** the 2024 game boom faded when rewards ended; any message asking you to "swap" or "claim" Gram is a scam.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Under the Gram name the coin has only a few months of history on our site; as Toncoin it soared in 2024, then lost most of the gain. Regular buying averages your price but does not protect you from a long decline or from risks tied to one company. Many people keep such coins to a small share of money they can afford to lose. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
