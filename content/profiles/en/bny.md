**BNY** (The Bank of New York Mellon) is not a bank for ordinary customers: it is the "plumbing" of global finance. It holds and administers securities for pension funds, insurers, asset managers and governments, processes payments, settles trades and runs funds. At the end of September 2026 it had $63.4 trillion of assets under custody and administration — the largest figure of any bank in the world.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Q3 2026 revenue: $5.68 billion, up about 12% on the year; net income $1.6 billion (+20%).
> - Earnings per share rose 24% to $2.34; return on tangible common equity was 29%.
> - Assets under custody and administration reached $63.4 trillion (+10%); assets under management $2.2 trillion.
> - In one quarter BNY returned $1.4 billion to shareholders: $937 million in buybacks and $436 million in dividends.

## How it makes money

Most of BNY's revenue is fees, not loan interest. Securities Services — custody, fund accounting, issuer services and securities lending — brought in about $2.8 billion in Q3 2026, up 15%. Market and Wealth Services runs Pershing (back office for brokers and advisers), payments and treasury services, and collateral management, where BNY handles more than $8 trillion of collateral on an average day. Investment and Wealth Management (asset manager BNY Investments and wealth management for rich families) earned about $562 million. The second source is net interest income — about $1.4 billion a quarter — on the roughly $311 billion of client deposits it holds.

In the quarter to 30 September 2026 fee revenue grew 12% and expenses 8%, so profits rose faster than sales: the pre-tax margin was 38.5%. Issuer services fees jumped 43% and asset servicing fees 12%. The core capital ratio (CET1) was 10.9%, down from 11.7% a year earlier as the bank returned more money to shareholders. For 2026 management had guided to revenue growth of about 10–11% excluding one-offs and net interest income growth of 12–13%.

## The stock's story

The Bank of New York was founded in 1784 by Alexander Hamilton, later the first US Treasury Secretary, and its shares were among the first traded on the New York Stock Exchange in 1792. It merged with Pittsburgh's Mellon Financial in 2007 to become The Bank of New York Mellon, and in 2024 it shortened its brand to BNY. Robin Vince became CEO in 2022 and reorganised the bank into "platforms", cut costs and pushed AI tools across the company. The stock spent years going nowhere after the 2008 crisis and then rose strongly in 2024–2026 as profits grew.

## Competitors

The main custody rivals are State Street and Northern Trust in the US, and the securities services arms of JPMorgan Chase, Citi and BNP Paribas. In asset management BNY competes with BlackRock, Vanguard and many others; in wealth and broker services Pershing competes with Charles Schwab and Fidelity.

## Main risks

- **Interest rates:** lower rates shrink the income BNY earns on client deposits.
- **Market levels:** many fees depend on the value of assets it holds, so a stock or bond crash lowers revenue.
- **Technology and cyber risk:** an outage or hack at a firm that settles trillions a day would hurt clients and trust.
- **Regulation and capital:** as a systemically important bank, BNY must keep large capital buffers, which limits buybacks.
- **Tough competition:** custody fees are under constant pressure from big rivals and new technology.

## What it means if you buy regularly

BNY is a steady, fee-driven business, but bank shares can still fall sharply in a crisis — in 2008–2009 BNY's stock lost more than half its value. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more shares when the price is low and fewer when it is high, without trying to time the market. Many people hold one company only as part of a wider portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
