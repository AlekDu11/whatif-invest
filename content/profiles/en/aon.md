**Aon** is one of the world's biggest insurance brokers and risk advisers: it helps companies buy insurance, helps insurers buy reinsurance, and designs health and pension plans for employers. It does not carry the risk itself — it earns commissions and fees for advice and deal-making. Aon is based in Dublin, has about 60,000 colleagues in more than 120 countries and is listed on the New York Stock Exchange.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: $4.10 billion in Q3 2026, +3% reported and +6% organic (same businesses, no currency effects).
> - Adjusted earnings per share rose 10% to $3.36; the adjusted operating margin reached 27.0%.
> - Aon agreed on 31 August 2026 to buy USI for $17 billion in cash; total debt jumped from $15.2 billion to about $28.0 billion.
> - The stock fell from $382 in July 2026 to $266 in October (−30%) and is still below its March 2025 record of $412.97.

## How it makes money

Aon reports two segments. **Risk Capital** covers Commercial Risk Solutions — placing property, liability, cyber and other insurance for businesses — and Reinsurance Solutions, where Aon helps insurers pass part of their own risk to reinsurers. **Human Capital** covers Health Solutions (employee health benefits) and Wealth Solutions (pensions and retirement advice). In 2025 Risk Capital brought in $11.29 billion and Human Capital $5.91 billion, out of $17.18 billion in total. The client base is spread out: the largest single client was about 1% of revenue, and 51.8% of revenue came from outside the US.

## The numbers right now

In the quarter to 30 September 2026 Commercial Risk earned $2.11 billion (+7% organic), Reinsurance $572 million (+7%), Health $992 million (+6%) and Wealth $429 million (+4% organic). Wealth looks 21% smaller only because Aon sold most of NFP's wealth businesses for $2.3 billion in October 2025. GAAP earnings per share were $2.23. Over nine months Aon generated $1.94 billion of free cash flow, bought back $1.1 billion of its shares and paid $511 million in dividends; the quarterly dividend is $0.82 per share.

## The stock's story

Aon has grown by buying rivals. In 2021 a planned merger with Willis Towers Watson collapsed after the US Department of Justice sued to block it, and Aon had to pay its partner a break fee. In 2024 it bought NFP, a broker for mid-sized US companies. The share price set a record of $412.97 on 3 March 2025, then ended 2025 roughly flat at $352.88. In 2026 it climbed back to $382.34 on 28 July — until the USI deal. USI is the tenth-largest US broker, with about $3 billion of revenue and more than 10,500 staff, and Aon is paying for it with debt. After the announcement the shares slid to $266.33 by 5 October. On 20 November the stock closed at $333.16.

## Competitors

Aon's main global rivals are Marsh McLennan and WTW (Willis Towers Watson), with Arthur J. Gallagher and Brown & Brown competing hard in the US middle market that USI targets. Big brokers win on data, global reach and long client relationships, but price competition for advice and placement is constant.

## Main risks

- **Debt and integration:** the USI deal is paid with new debt — $13.5 billion of bonds issued in September 2026. Aon expects to stop buybacks for now, reach its leverage target in about 24 months, and sees the deal adding to adjusted EPS only from 2028.
- **Insurance prices:** brokers earn a share of the premium, so a softer insurance market slows revenue growth.
- **Legal and regulatory matters:** Aon names proceedings and investigations in Ireland, the UK, the US and elsewhere linked to deals where capital was arranged by Vesttoo.
- **Currency and the economy:** about half of revenue is earned outside the US, and a downturn means fewer insured assets and fewer hires to cover.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Aon sells something businesses keep buying in good and bad years, yet its stock still dropped 30% in three months over one acquisition. Regular buying spreads purchases across such dips and peaks, but a single company can lag for years if a big deal disappoints — many people hold a stock like this only as part of a broader portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
