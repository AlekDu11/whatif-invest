**Bank of America** is the second-largest bank in the United States. It holds more than $2 trillion of customer deposits, serves about 69 million consumer and small-business clients, owns the Merrill wealth business and runs one of Wall Street's biggest trading desks. In 2025 it earned $30.5 billion, and most of that money came from one simple thing: the gap between what it earns on loans and what it pays on deposits.

> **Key takeaways**
> - 2025: revenue $113.1 billion, net income $30.5 billion ($3.81 per share); net interest income $60.1 billion.
> - Q3 2026: net interest income rose 8% to $16.4 billion, but profit fell to $6.9 billion from $8.3 billion after a $1.2 billion anti-money-laundering penalty.
> - At 30 September 2026 the bank had $2.04 trillion in deposits, $1.25 trillion in loans and $5.06 trillion in client balances.

## How it makes money

Bank of America has four big businesses. **Consumer Banking** runs current accounts, savings, credit cards, mortgages and car loans through 3,628 US financial centers (end of 2025) and an app with about 59 million verified digital users. **Global Wealth & Investment Management** is Merrill and the Private Bank: advisers who look after the savings of wealthy families, earning fees on the money they manage. **Global Banking** lends to companies, handles their payments and earns investment banking fees for deals and share or bond sales. **Global Markets** trades stocks, bonds, currencies and commodities for big investors.

Across all four, the main engine is **net interest income** — about $60.1 billion of the $113.1 billion of revenue in 2025. The rest is fees: card fees, advisory fees, asset management fees and trading. In the September 2026 quarter, Consumer Banking earned $3.5 billion, Global Banking $2.0 billion, wealth management $1.5 billion and Global Markets $1.2 billion.

In the third quarter of 2026 revenue rose 3% to $30.0 billion and net interest income rose 8% to $16.4 billion, helped by more loans and deposits. But net income fell 17% to $6.9 billion, or $0.91 per share (from $1.04 a year earlier). The reason was a $1.2 billion charge to cover fines from US regulators over failures to detect and report potential money laundering; it cut earnings by about $0.17 per share. Investment banking fees fell 11% to $1.8 billion and sales and trading revenue fell 5% to $5.1 billion. Wealth management did well: Merrill client balances reached $4.24 trillion.

The bank returned $8.3 billion to shareholders in that quarter through dividends and buybacks; the number of shares fell to 6.92 billion from 7.33 billion a year earlier. The quarterly dividend was $0.32 per share. Its core capital ratio (CET1) was 10.9%, above the regulatory minimum.

## The stock's story

Today's Bank of America was formed in 1998, when NationsBank of Charlotte bought BankAmerica of San Francisco and kept the more famous name. During the 2008 financial crisis it bought the troubled mortgage lender Countrywide and, on 1 January 2009, completed the takeover of Merrill Lynch for shares then worth about $24 billion. Bad Countrywide mortgages cost the bank tens of billions of dollars in losses and settlements in the years that followed. The government put $45 billion into the bank through the TARP bailout ($25 billion in autumn 2008, $20 billion in January 2009); Bank of America repaid it all in December 2009. Brian Moynihan became CEO in January 2010.

In August 2011, when the shares were trading far below the bank's book value, Warren Buffett's Berkshire Hathaway invested $5 billion in preferred shares and received the right to buy 700 million common shares at about $7.14. Berkshire used that right in August 2017, when its stake was worth about $16.5 billion. From July 2024 to September 2025 Berkshire sold about 465 million shares, or 45% of its holding, though Bank of America remained one of its largest investments. In October 2026 the bank reported the $1.2 billion money-laundering penalty and was placed under a regulatory order to review past transactions.

## Competitors

Bank of America's closest rival is [JPMorgan Chase](/en/jpm/), the largest US bank. In everyday banking it also competes with [Wells Fargo](/en/wfc/) and [Citigroup](/en/c/), plus regional banks and online banks. In wealth management Merrill fights [Morgan Stanley](/en/ms/) and [Charles Schwab](/en/schw/); in trading and investment banking the rivals are JPMorgan, [Goldman Sachs](/en/gs/) and Morgan Stanley. In 2026 Bank of America slipped to fourth place in global investment banking fees, from third a year earlier, according to Dealogic.

## Main risks

- **Interest rates:** profits depend on net interest income; if rates fall quickly or deposits become more expensive, the bank earns less on its money.
- **The economy:** in a recession more people and companies fail to repay loans and credit cards, and the bank has to set aside more money for losses.
- **Regulation and fines:** banks this size are closely supervised; the 2026 penalty and the new order bring extra costs and limits.
- **Costs:** expenses rose 14% in Q3 2026 to $19.7 billion, so profit can lag even when revenue grows.
- **Crisis memory:** in 2008–2009 bank shares lost most of their value; big banks can fall far faster than the wider market.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Bank of America is a huge, profitable bank that pays a dividend and buys back shares, but bank stocks move with interest rates and the economy and can drop sharply in a crisis. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more shares when the price is low and fewer when it is high, without trying to time the market. Many people hold one bank only as part of a wider portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
