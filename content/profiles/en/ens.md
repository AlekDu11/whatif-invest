**Ethereum Name Service (ENS)** turns long crypto addresses like 0x4b2f… into readable names such as alice.eth — a bit like the internet's domain-name system does for websites. It started in 2017 as a project at the Ethereum Foundation, and the ENS token, created in 2021, gives holders a vote on how the service and its treasury are run. ## How it works

A .eth name is registered for a yearly fee paid in ether and is held in your wallet as an NFT, so you can transfer or sell it. Behind the name sit records: wallet addresses on many blockchains, a profile picture, a website or social accounts. Wallets and apps that support ENS — more than 600 of them in 2026 — look up these records, so people can send money to a name instead of a 42-character address. Owners of ordinary web domains such as .com can link them to ENS as well. ENSv2 added finer control over subnames like pay.alice.eth.

## Why people think it has value

Readable names solve a real problem: copying long addresses is easy to get wrong, and a single typo can send money to the wrong place. ENS is the most widely used naming system on Ethereum, with more than 36 million names created by 2026, and many people use their .eth name as an online identity. The fees from registrations and renewals go to the treasury of the ENS DAO, and ENS token holders decide how that money and the protocol's rules are used. The token's value therefore rests on control of a busy piece of Ethereum infrastructure rather than on a share of the income.

## Its story so far

Developer Nick Johnson launched ENS in May 2017; the project later moved out of the Ethereum Foundation into a separate non-profit team, today ENS Labs. In November 2021 it airdropped 25% of the new ENS token to people who had registered names, handed control to the ENS DAO and set its price record in the same month, before falling by more than 90% over the following bear market. In November 2024 the team announced its own layer-2 network, Namechain, but in February 2026 it dropped the plan, saying Ethereum fees had fallen so much that staying on the main chain was better. ENSv2 launched on Ethereum mainnet on 27 October 2026.

## Alternatives

Other naming services include [SPACE ID]({lang}/id-crypto/), which offers names on several chains, and [Bonfida]({lang}/fida/), the naming service on Solana. Unstoppable Domains sells names without a tradable token. Investors who want exposure to Ethereum's growth itself usually choose [Ethereum]({lang}/eth/) instead.

## Main risks

- **Big swings:** ENS has lost more than 90% from its 2021 peak before and can fall sharply again.
- **Governance only:** the token gives votes, not a share of fees, so its price depends on how much people value that control.
- **Concentration:** the DAO treasury and early contributors hold a large part of the supply; selling by them can weigh on the price.
- **Competition and adoption:** wallets could favour other naming systems, and many users never register a name.
- **Regulation and technology:** rules for DAOs and tokens are still unclear, and smart-contract bugs are always possible.

## What it means if you buy regularly

ENS's chart starts with the 2021 airdrop spike and a long fall after it. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your entry price and avoids putting everything in at a peak, but it does not protect you from a token that never returns to its old highs. Many people keep smaller cryptocurrencies to a small share of their savings. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
