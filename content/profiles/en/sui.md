**Sui** is a fast, low-fee blockchain for apps, games, payments and DeFi, built by Mysten Labs, a company founded by former engineers of Meta's abandoned Diem cryptocurrency project. Its coin, SUI, pays for transactions and secures the network through staking. SUI was worth about $2.6 billion in November 2026, far below its peak.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Sui's mainnet launched in May 2023; there will never be more than 10 billion SUI, and about 4.1 billion circulate.
> - The record price so far is $5.35 (January 2025); in November 2026 SUI traded more than 85% below it.
> - New coins are unlocked every month under a schedule that runs to about 2030.
> - In May 2025 about $220 million was stolen from Cetus, Sui's largest exchange app; validators froze most of it.

## How it works

Most blockchains process transactions one after another. Sui treats every coin, NFT or game item as a separate "object" with an owner, so transactions that touch different objects can run in parallel — which is why simple transfers confirm in under a second. Apps are written in Move, a programming language that grew out of the Diem project and is designed to make common smart-contract bugs harder. Around a hundred validators run the network; anyone can stake SUI with them (delegate) and share the rewards. Part of the supply, the "stake subsidies", tops up those rewards in the early years.

## Why people think it has value

You need SUI to pay gas fees on the network, to stake and to vote on some changes to the protocol. If more people use apps on Sui — exchanges, games, stablecoin payments — demand for block space and staking grows. Supporters point to the speed, a growing DeFi and payments ecosystem, and institutional interest: Nasdaq-listed SUI Group Holdings staked its whole treasury of more than 108 million SUI in 2026, and in May 2026 Mysten Labs announced plans for zero-fee stablecoin transfers.

## Its story so far

Mysten Labs was founded in 2021 by Evan Cheng, Sam Blackshear, Adeniyi Abiodun, George Danezis and Kostas Chalkias, all of whom had worked on Diem. In 2022 it raised $300 million at a valuation above $2 billion. Mainnet and the SUI token launched on 3 May 2023, partly through public sales and a Binance Launchpool. The price fell below $0.40 in late 2023, then rose sharply through 2024 and peaked at $5.35 in January 2025. The network halted for about two hours in November 2024. In May 2025 a hacker drained about $220 million from the Cetus exchange; Sui validators froze most of the stolen funds, which helped users but drew criticism about how decentralised Sui really is. Like most altcoins, SUI fell heavily in 2026.

## Alternatives

Sui's closest rival is [Aptos]({lang}/apt/), built by another former Diem team on the same Move language. It also competes with other fast chains — [Solana]({lang}/sol/), [Sei]({lang}/sei/) and [Avalanche]({lang}/avax/) — and with [Ethereum]({lang}/eth/) and its layer 2 networks.

## Main risks

- **Token unlocks:** monthly unlocks for investors, the team and reserves add new coins until about 2030; more than half of the maximum supply is not yet in circulation.
- **Concentration:** the Sui Foundation, Mysten Labs and early investors control a large share of SUI.
- **Hacks and control:** the Cetus hack showed that apps can fail — and that validators can freeze funds, which some see as a risk in itself.
- **Competition:** many fast blockchains chase the same developers and users.
- **Big swings:** SUI has lost more than 85% from its record; young coins can fall further.

## What it means if you buy regularly

SUI has only a few years of history, most of it volatile. Buying a fixed amount regularly averages the price you pay, but it does not protect you from a long decline or from dilution by unlocks. Many people keep small, young coins to a very small share of savings they can afford to lose. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
