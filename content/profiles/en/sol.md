**Solana** is a blockchain built for speed: it confirms transactions in under a second for a fraction of a cent, which made it the home of trading apps, memecoins and crypto payments. Its mainnet went live in March 2020, created by former Qualcomm engineer Anatoly Yakovenko.

## How it works

Like Ethereum, Solana runs smart contracts — programs that power exchanges, lending apps and tokens. Validators lock up ("stake") SOL to earn the right to confirm blocks. A clock-like system called Proof of History puts transactions in order before they are confirmed, which lets the network process thousands of them per second.

## What the SOL token is for

SOL pays transaction fees and is staked to secure the network. Stakers earn rewards of about 7% a year, paid partly in newly created coins, so there is no hard cap on supply. Demand also comes from funds: in October 2025 Bitwise launched the first US spot Solana ETF, which stakes its coins.

## Why it matters

Solana is one of the ten largest cryptocurrencies by market value. One surprising fact: its worst outage, in February 2023, stopped the whole network for almost 19 hours — and the last full halt was in February 2024. It has run without a network-wide stop since then.

## Main risks

SOL is far more volatile than bitcoin: it lost more than 90% in 2022 and is now more than 70% below its 2025 peak. Speed relies on powerful hardware run by fewer validators, and the network has stopped several times before. Much activity is speculative memecoin trading that can vanish quickly. Ethereum and newer chains compete for the same apps, and new coins keep diluting holders. Buying a fixed amount regularly smooths the swings but does not remove the risk.
