**Solana** is a blockchain built for speed: it confirms transactions in about a second for a fraction of a cent, which made it the home of trading apps, memecoins and stablecoin payments. Its coin, SOL, pays the fees and secures the network through staking. SOL's market value was about $41.5 billion in November 2026, making it one of the largest cryptocurrencies.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Solana's mainnet launched in March 2020; SOL has no maximum supply, and about 592 million coins circulated in November 2026.
> - The record price so far is $293.31 (19 January 2025); in November 2026 SOL traded more than 70% below it.
> - The first US spot Solana ETFs started trading in late October 2025; by August 2026 they had drawn about $1.22 billion of net inflows.
> - The network has stopped completely several times, the longest for about 19 hours in February 2023; the last full halt was in February 2024.

## How it works

Solana runs smart contracts — programs behind exchanges, lending apps, games and tokens — like Ethereum, but on one fast chain instead of many add-on networks. A clock-like system called Proof of History orders transactions, and many are processed in parallel. Validators, who need powerful computers, stake SOL to produce blocks; anyone can delegate SOL to them and share the rewards. Two big upgrades changed the engine: Firedancer, a new validator program from Jump Crypto, went live in December 2025, and Alpenglow, a voting system that replaced TowerBFT, was switched on in October 2026 to cut final confirmation from about 12.8 seconds to a fraction of a second.

## Why people think it has value

Every transaction on Solana is paid in SOL, and part of each fee is burned. Stakers earn new coins: inflation started at 8% a year and falls by 15% each year towards 1.5%; it was about 3.8% in June 2026, when roughly 68% of all SOL was staked. Supporters point to cheap, fast trading, a large app and stablecoin ecosystem, and Wall Street interest — staking ETFs from Bitwise, Grayscale, Fidelity, VanEck and 21Shares, and listed "treasury" companies such as Forward Industries, which bought nearly 7 million SOL in 2025.

## Its story so far

Former Qualcomm engineer Anatoly Yakovenko described Proof of History in 2017 and co-founded Solana Labs; the mainnet beta opened in March 2020. SOL climbed to about $260 in November 2021, then collapsed when FTX, whose founder Sam Bankman-Fried was one of Solana's best-known backers, went bankrupt; by late December 2022 SOL was below $10. The network also had long outages — about 17 hours in September 2021 and 19 hours in February 2023. Memecoin mania brought it back: sites such as pump.fun made Solana the main place to launch tokens, and the TRUMP memecoin launched there on 17 January 2025, two days before SOL's record. In 2025 validators approved Alpenglow with 98% of votes, and US ETFs arrived in October. Like most altcoins, SOL fell heavily in 2026.

## Alternatives

Solana's biggest rival for apps and money is [Ethereum]({lang}/eth/) and its layer 2 networks. It also competes with other fast chains — [BNB Chain]({lang}/bnb/), [Sui]({lang}/sui/), [Aptos]({lang}/apt/) and [Avalanche]({lang}/avax/).

## Main risks

- **Dilution:** SOL has no supply cap; new coins are issued every epoch, so holders who don't stake slowly lose their share.
- **Concentration:** running a validator takes costly hardware, and early investors, the Solana Foundation and large staking providers hold a big share of the stake.
- **Outages and new code:** the network has halted before, and big changes like Alpenglow and Firedancer can bring new bugs.
- **Speculation and regulation:** much activity is memecoin trading that can vanish fast; in 2023 the US SEC called SOL a security in lawsuits that were later dropped, and rules can change again.
- **Big swings:** SOL lost more than 95% from its 2021 peak to its 2022 low; it can fall that hard again.

## What it means if you buy regularly

SOL has a little more than six years of price history, with two huge crashes and two big rallies. Buying a fixed amount regularly averages the price you pay, but it does not protect you from a long decline. Staking can offset inflation, but it adds its own risks. Many people keep a single altcoin to a small share of money they can afford to lose. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
