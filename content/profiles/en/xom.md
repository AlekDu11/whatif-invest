**ExxonMobil** finds and pumps oil and natural gas around the world, turns it into petrol, diesel and jet fuel in its refineries, and makes plastics, chemicals and Mobil 1 motor oil. It is the largest US oil company, based in Spring, Texas, and its profits rise and fall with oil, gas and fuel prices. In 2025 it earned $28.8 billion.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Earnings: $28.8 billion in 2025 ($6.70 per share), down from $33.7 billion in 2024; cash from operations was $52.0 billion.
> - Production reached 4.7 million barrels of oil equivalent a day in 2025, the most in more than 40 years, led by the Permian Basin and Guyana.
> - It paid shareholders $37.2 billion in 2025: $17.2 billion of dividends and $20.0 billion of buybacks.
> - Earnings in the first nine months of 2026 were $34.1 billion, against $22.3 billion a year earlier.

## How it makes money

ExxonMobil has four businesses. **Upstream** finds and produces oil and gas — its biggest growth areas are the Permian Basin in Texas and New Mexico (a record 1.6 million barrels a day in 2025) and the offshore fields of Guyana (more than 700,000 barrels a day, gross, in 2025). **Energy Products** refines crude into fuels and sells them, including under the Exxon, Esso and Mobil brands. **Chemical Products** makes the basic plastics used in packaging, cars and household goods. **Specialty Products** sells lubricants such as Mobil 1 and other high-value materials. In the September 2026 quarter Upstream earned $7.1 billion, Energy Products $7.2 billion, Specialty Products $1.3 billion and Chemical Products $0.7 billion.

In the September 2026 quarter ExxonMobil earned $15.4 billion, or $3.72 per share, up from $14.5 billion in the June quarter; without one-off items and timing effects, earnings were $17.5 billion. Cash flow from operations was $25.7 billion and free cash flow $19.1 billion. It returned $9.4 billion to shareholders in the quarter — $4.3 billion of dividends and $5.1 billion of buybacks. Production was 4.5 million barrels of oil equivalent a day.

At the end of September 2026 the company had $34.3 billion of debt and $12.2 billion of cash, so net debt was $22.1 billion. It declared a dividend of $1.07 per share for the fourth quarter of 2026 and has raised its yearly dividend for 44 years in a row. It said it planned $20 billion of share buybacks in 2027 if market conditions stay reasonable, and that it had cut structural costs by $16.9 billion compared with 2019.

## The stock's story

ExxonMobil traces its roots to John D. Rockefeller's Standard Oil, founded in 1870 and broken up by the US Supreme Court in 1911; two of the pieces, Exxon and Mobil, merged again in 1999. In the 2000s it was often the world's most valuable listed company. The oil crash of 2014–2016 and the pandemic hit it hard: in 2020 it posted its first annual loss in decades and in August 2020 it was removed from the Dow Jones Industrial Average, where it had been since 1928. When energy prices jumped after Russia's full-scale invasion of Ukraine, ExxonMobil earned a record $55.7 billion in 2022. In May 2024 it completed the purchase of Pioneer Natural Resources, which made it the largest producer in the Permian. Guyana, where it found oil in 2015 and started producing in late 2019, passed 1 billion barrels of total output in 2026.

## Competitors

Its closest rivals are the other oil majors: Chevron, Shell, TotalEnergies and BP, plus Saudi Aramco and other state oil companies. In US shale it competes with ConocoPhillips, EOG and Occidental; in chemicals with Dow, LyondellBasell and Sinopec.

## Main risks

- **Oil and gas prices:** they move with the economy, OPEC+ decisions and wars, and profits swing with them — 2020 brought a loss, 2022 a record.
- **Refining margins:** fuel profits can change a lot from one quarter to the next.
- **Energy transition:** electric cars, climate rules and carbon prices could reduce long-term demand for oil.
- **Big projects and politics:** Guyana, LNG in Papua New Guinea and Mozambique cost billions and depend on governments; Venezuela claims part of the area around Guyana's fields.
- **Lawsuits:** US states and cities have sued oil companies over climate change.

## What it means if you buy regularly

ExxonMobil pays a large, steadily rising dividend, but its share price follows the oil cycle and can fall sharply in a bad year, as in 2020. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more shares when the price is low and fewer when it is high, which suits a cyclical stock better than trying to guess the top or bottom of oil prices. Many people hold one company only as part of a wider portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
