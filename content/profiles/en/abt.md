**Abbott Laboratories** makes healthcare products you can find in a hospital, a lab, a pharmacy and a supermarket aisle. Its range runs from the FreeStyle Libre glucose sensor worn on the arm and heart devices to Similac baby formula, Ensure drinks, blood-test machines and branded generic medicines sold outside the US. Since March 2026 it also owns Exact Sciences, the maker of the Cologuard colon cancer test.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Sales: $13.0 billion in Q3 2026, +14.5% reported and +7.0% on a comparable basis.
> - Adjusted EPS rose 9.2% to $1.42; full-year 2026 guidance is $5.50–5.55.
> - The share price fell about 42% from its December 2021 record of $141.46 to a low in May 2026.
> - Abbott has paid a dividend for 411 quarters in a row, currently $0.63 a quarter.

## How it makes money

Abbott reports four segments. Medical Devices is the largest, $5.83 billion in Q3 2026, or about 45% of sales. Inside it, Diabetes Care (mainly Libre sensors) brought in $2.19 billion, electrophysiology (tools that treat heart rhythm problems) grew 15%, and there are pacemakers, stents and heart valves too. Diagnostics, $3.31 billion, now includes the new Cancer Diagnostics business ($968 million, driven by Cologuard) next to lab and hospital testing machines. Nutrition (Similac, Ensure, Glucerna) added $2.26 billion and Established Pharmaceuticals, branded generics sold in emerging markets, $1.61 billion. Most sales, about 58%, come from outside the United States.

## The numbers right now

In the quarter to 30 September 2026, reported on 21 October, Abbott sold $13.01 billion of products. The 14.5% reported jump is inflated by Exact Sciences; counting that business in both years, growth was 7.0%. GAAP earnings per share fell to $0.53 from $0.94 because of about $670 million of legal settlements, mostly for lawsuits over necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC), a serious bowel disease in premature babies. Adjusted EPS was $1.42. For 2026 Abbott expects about 6% comparable sales growth and adjusted EPS of $5.50–5.55. In August the FDA approved Libre Duo, a sensor that tracks glucose and ketones together.

## The stock's story

Abbott's price peaked at $141.46 on 27 December 2021, when pandemic test sales were still strong. As that demand faded the stock drifted for years. 2026 was harsh: weak Q4 2025 sales ($11.46 billion against higher expectations) and a soft outlook sent shares down about 14% in six trading days in January. In April Abbott cut its 2026 profit forecast to $5.38–5.58 per share, partly because of new shares tied to the roughly $21 billion Exact Sciences deal, and the stock lost about 6% in a day. The slide bottomed on 11 May 2026, about 42% below the record.

## Competitors

Each business has its own rivals. In glucose sensors it is Dexcom and Medtronic; in heart devices, Medtronic, Boston Scientific and Edwards Lifesciences; in lab testing, Roche, Siemens Healthineers and Danaher; in baby and adult nutrition, Nestlé, Danone and Reckitt (Mead Johnson). Abbott's strength is that no single product decides its fate.

## Main risks

- **Lawsuits over premature-infant formula:** the Q3 2026 charge alone covered NEC claims for about 2,000 more infants, and legal costs can return.
- **Integration of Exact Sciences:** a large purchase adds debt, new shares and the task of keeping Cologuard growing.
- **Competition in diabetes:** Libre is Abbott's biggest growth engine, and Dexcom and others keep launching new sensors.
- **Slower growth than investors hoped:** the guidance cut in April showed how quickly the share price reacts to small misses.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Abbott is a broad, steady business, but its stock still lost more than 40% over four years after the 2021 peak. Regular buying meant purchases at lower prices during that slide, and the dividend adds a small income, yet a recovery is not guaranteed and can take years. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
