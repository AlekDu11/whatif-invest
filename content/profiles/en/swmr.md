**Swarmer** writes software that lets one soldier steer a whole group of drones at once instead of flying each one by hand. It was founded in 2023 by Serhii Kupriienko and Alex Fink, its code first went into combat in Ukraine in April 2024, and today the company is registered in Austin, Texas, with teams in Ukraine, Poland and Estonia. It listed on Nasdaq in March 2026.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue is still tiny: $25,554 in Q3 2026, against a net loss of about $9.3 million.
> - The software has supported more than 100,000 combat missions in Ukraine, the company says.
> - IPO at $5 a share in March 2026; the stock hit an intraday $65.04 on its second day, then swung hard.
> - A pending deal for ground-robot maker Ratel Robotics, worth up to $224 million in cash and shares, would change the company's size.

## How it makes money

So far, barely at all. Swarmer sells software, not drones: a command-and-control system called STYX, an autonomy layer called MINAS and an operating system called Trident OS. The code works with drones from different makers, so cheap platforms act as one team and fewer operators are needed. In Q3 2026 its revenue came from support services under existing customer agreements.

In September 2026 Swarmer agreed to acquire **Ratel Robotics**, a Ukrainian maker of unmanned ground vehicles used for front-line logistics, evacuating the wounded, reconnaissance and demining. Ratel's main customer is the Ukrainian government; by its own management's estimate it took about 37% of the state's UGV purchases in the spring 2026 procurement round.

## The numbers right now

In the quarter to 30 September 2026 Swarmer reported revenue of $25,554, operating expenses of about $9.4 million and a net loss of about $9.3 million. Cash rose to about $37.3 million, mostly from selling new shares through an equity line of credit.

Ratel's figures are preliminary, unaudited and not prepared under US accounting rules: $42.1 million of revenue in the first nine months of 2026 and a government-contract backlog of about $65.1 million. For Q4 2026 Ratel management expects $35–40 million of revenue at a 13–17% operating margin. At closing Swarmer pays $7.2 million in cash plus about 1.06 million shares, with more payments tied to 2026–2028 targets. Shareholders approved the share issue on 2 November; the deal had not closed by mid-November.

## The stock's story

Swarmer sold 3 million shares at $5 on 17 March 2026, raising about $15 million. The stock closed its first session at $31 and touched $65.04 intraday the next day — more than ten times the IPO price. In the week of 15 September 2026 it fell more than 41% as the post-IPO lock-up ended (about 9.35 million shares, roughly 59% of the total, became free to sell) and investors worried about new shares for the Ratel deal.

Erik Prince, founder of Blackwater, has chaired the board since December 2025; co-founder Serhii Kupriienko stepped down as global CEO in July 2026, leaving Alex Fink in charge.

## Competitors

In the US, Anduril (Lattice software), Shield AI and Auterion work on drone autonomy and swarm control. In Ukraine many Brave1-backed start-ups build similar tools, and Ratel competes with other Ukrainian UGV makers for the same state budget. Swarmer's pitch: software proven in combat under heavy jamming.

## Main risks

- **Almost no revenue of its own:** without Ratel, sales are measured in thousands of dollars and losses are funded by selling shares.
- **Dilution:** the equity line, the Ratel shares and earnouts all add new stock, which can push the price per share down.
- **Unaudited numbers:** Ratel's revenue and backlog are management figures, not audited GAAP results.
- **War and peace:** a ceasefire could cut orders, and Ukrainian defence exports are tightly controlled.
- **Extreme volatility:** from $5 to $65 and back down within months.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Swarmer is an early-stage, loss-making company whose stock has had huge swings in less than a year of trading. Regular buying spreads your entry over many prices but cannot help if the plan fails; many people keep only a very small share of their money in a stock like this. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
