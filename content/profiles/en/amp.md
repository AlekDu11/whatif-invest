**Ameriprise Financial** is the company behind the financial adviser many American families sit down with to plan retirement, college savings or insurance. It also runs a large fund house, Columbia Threadneedle, and sells annuities and life insurance. Based in Minneapolis, it was spun off from American Express in September 2005 and trades on the New York Stock Exchange.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Assets under management, administration and advisement reached about $1.8 trillion in Q3 2026, up 8% in a year.
> - Q3 2026 adjusted operating net revenue was $5.1 billion (+10%), and GAAP net income rose 23% to $1.13 billion.
> - The company returned $1.03 billion to shareholders in the quarter — 93% of operating earnings — and has a new $5.5 billion buyback plan.
> - The quarterly dividend is $1.70 per share since May 2026, up 6%.

## How it makes money

Most of the money comes from fees charged as a share of the assets clients keep with Ameriprise, so revenue rises and falls with the markets. The biggest segment, Advice & Wealth Management, earned $3.37 billion of adjusted operating net revenue in Q3 2026 (+13%): advisers charge for financial plans and managed "wrap" accounts, and the firm also earns on brokerage, banking and client cash. Asset Management (Columbia Threadneedle) brought in $990 million (+9%) from managing funds for individuals and institutions. Retirement & Protection Solutions — annuities and life insurance — added $997 million, 10% less than a year earlier. Each adviser now brings in $1.24 million of revenue a year on average, a record.

## The numbers right now

In the quarter to 30 September 2026, adjusted operating earnings per share were $11.26 (+14%), or $12.07 excluding a yearly update of insurance assumptions. Return on equity was about 55% — very high, partly because the company keeps buying back its own shares. Over the last 12 months revenue totalled about $20.1 billion with a profit margin near 21%, and the stock trades at roughly 11 times earnings, with a market value around $43.5 billion. Wrap accounts attracted $4.8 billion of new money, but overall client flows were negative ($8.5 billion out) because of a planned exit of Comerica-related assets. In October 2026 more than 250 advisers from Huntington Bank joined, bringing about $28 billion of client assets through early 2027.

## The stock's story

Ameriprise is a market-sensitive stock: in 2008 it lost about 57% as markets crashed, then rebounded by about 70% in 2009. Since then, rising markets, steady buybacks and growing advice fees pushed it up for most years — it gained 42% in 2024 alone. Over the past 52 weeks the share price has moved between about $422 and $573. Jim Cracchiolo has led the company as chairman and CEO since the 2005 spin-off.

## Competitors

In advice, Ameriprise competes with Edward Jones, LPL Financial, Raymond James, Morgan Stanley and Merrill, plus low-cost platforms such as Charles Schwab and Fidelity. In fund management it faces giants like BlackRock and Vanguard, whose cheap index funds keep pulling money away from active managers.

## Main risks

- **Markets:** fees are a share of client assets, so a stock-market fall cuts revenue quickly, as 2008 showed.
- **Fund outflows:** Asset Management saw $9.3 billion of net outflows in Q3 2026; the shift to index funds is a long-term headwind.
- **Interest rates:** lower rates reduce what the firm earns on client cash and can change the economics of annuities.
- **Insurance assumptions:** the yearly review cut Q3 earnings by about $0.81 a share; such updates can swing profit either way.
- **Advisers:** growth depends on recruiting and keeping advisers, who can leave with their clients.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Ameriprise pays a growing dividend ($6.80 a year at the current rate) and spends heavily on buybacks, but its fortunes are tied to the stock market. Regular buying spreads purchases across good and bad months; in a downturn like 2008 you would have kept buying at much lower prices, but you would also have seen your balance fall by half. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
