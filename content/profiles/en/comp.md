**Compound** is one of the oldest lending apps in decentralized finance (DeFi): a set of smart contracts where people lend out crypto to earn interest or borrow against the crypto they deposit — with no bank in the middle. ## How it works

Compound was started in 2018 by Robert Leshner and Geoffrey Hayes on Ethereum. Lenders deposit assets such as USDC or ETH into a market and earn interest paid by borrowers. Borrowers must first put up more value as collateral than they take out; if the value of their collateral falls too far, the protocol automatically sells part of it (liquidation) to repay the loan. Interest rates move by formula: the more of a market is borrowed, the higher the rate. Since August 2022 the main version, Compound III, has one borrowable asset per market — for example USDC — which makes the risk easier to follow. Compound now runs on Ethereum and several other networks.

## What the token is used for

COMP is a governance token. Holders, or people they delegate their votes to, propose and approve changes: which assets can be used as collateral, interest rate settings, rewards and how the treasury is spent. COMP is also handed out as a reward to some lenders and borrowers. For a long time the token gave holders no share of the protocol's income, which is why its price has depended more on attention to DeFi than on revenue; after the 2024 dispute the community discussed ways to share part of the reserves with COMP stakers.

## Its story so far

Compound went live in 2018, and in June 2020 it began giving away COMP to users, which drew billions of dollars into DeFi within weeks. In May 2021 COMP traded above $800. In September 2021 a bug in an upgrade sent out tens of millions of dollars' worth of COMP by mistake, and Leshner asked users to return it. Compound III arrived in August 2022. In July 2024 a whale known as "Humpy" and allies passed a proposal over the objections of others to move about 499,000 COMP into a vault they influenced; the proposal was later withdrawn in a truce that promised a staking product for COMP holders.

## Alternatives

[Aave]({lang}/aave/) is the largest DeFi lending protocol and Compound's main rival. [Morpho]({lang}/morpho/) has grown fast with its own lending markets, and [Sky]({lang}/sky/) (formerly Maker) lets people borrow its own stablecoin against crypto. Outside DeFi, centralized exchanges and crypto lenders offer similar products, but you then trust a company with your coins.

## Main risks

- **Smart contract bugs:** a mistake in the code can lose or misdirect funds, as the 2021 incident showed.
- **Governance capture:** anyone who gathers enough COMP can push through proposals, including ones that harm other holders.
- **Competition:** Aave and newer protocols have taken much of the lending market.
- **Liquidations and market crashes:** sharp price falls trigger forced sales and can strain the protocol.
- **Regulation:** rules for DeFi lending and governance tokens are unsettled and could change.

## What it means if you buy regularly

COMP's history shows a huge spike in 2020–2021 and a long decline after it, so a regular plan has been very bumpy. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your entry price over time, but it does not protect you if the token keeps losing ground to rivals. Many people keep tokens like this to a small part of a crypto allocation they can afford to lose. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
