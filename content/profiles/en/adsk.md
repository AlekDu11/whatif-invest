**Autodesk** makes the software that architects, engineers, builders, product designers and film studios use to draw, model and plan things before they are built. Its best-known program, AutoCAD, came out in 1982, the year the company was founded in California; today its biggest products are Revit and other tools for buildings and construction, Fusion and Inventor for manufacturing, and Maya and 3ds Max for films and games. Customers pay for it mostly as a yearly or monthly subscription.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: $7.21 billion in fiscal 2026 (year to 31 January 2026), +18%; GAAP net income $1.12 billion.
> - Architecture, engineering and construction software brought in $3.58 billion, about half of sales.
> - Subscriptions made up about 94% of fiscal 2026 revenue, so most income repeats every year.
> - In the October 2026 quarter revenue rose 17% to $2.16 billion, and Autodesk raised its full-year forecast.

## How it makes money

Autodesk sells access to its programs, not boxes of software. Since 2016 new customers can only subscribe, which turned one-off licence sales into steady income. In fiscal 2026 the architecture, engineering, construction and operations family (Revit, Civil 3D, Autodesk Construction Cloud and others) earned $3.58 billion, AutoCAD and AutoCAD LT $1.79 billion, manufacturing software $1.38 billion, and media and entertainment $332 million. By region the Americas brought $3.18 billion, Europe, the Middle East and Africa $2.79 billion and Asia-Pacific $1.23 billion.

Autodesk is also moving to a "new transaction model", in which it bills more customers directly instead of through resellers, and it is building AI features into its tools. In 2026 it bought MaintainX, a maintenance-management software company, to reach the people who run factories and buildings after they are built.

In the quarter to 31 October 2026 revenue was $2,160 million, up 17%; net income was $563 million, or $2.68 per share (helped by a tax benefit of about $105 million), and the GAAP operating margin was 26%. Autodesk then raised its fiscal 2027 forecast to revenue of $8.36–8.375 billion and free cash flow of $2.725–2.75 billion. It pays no dividend; instead it bought back $1.25 billion of its shares in the first nine months of fiscal 2027.

## The stock's story

Autodesk grew up with the personal computer: AutoCAD let engineers draw on a PC instead of a drawing board, and it became the standard in many offices. The shares more than halved in the 2008 financial crisis, then recovered in the 2010s. Andrew Anagnost became CEO in 2017 and finished the switch to subscriptions, which made revenue steadier and lifted the stock strongly until 2021. In 2022, as interest rates rose and investors sold software stocks, the shares fell sharply, then recovered part of the loss in 2023–2025. Revenue kept growing through this period, from $6.13 billion in fiscal 2025 to $7.21 billion in fiscal 2026.

## Competitors

In building design Autodesk competes with Bentley Systems, Nemetschek (Graphisoft, Vectorworks) and Trimble; in manufacturing with Dassault Systèmes (SolidWorks, CATIA), Siemens and PTC; in 3D for films and games with Adobe, Blender (free) and game engines from Epic and Unity. Cheaper or free tools, and AI start-ups, also try to win smaller customers.

## Main risks

- **Building and factory cycles:** customers spend less on software seats when construction or manufacturing slows.
- **AI disruption:** if AI tools make design work much easier, customers may need fewer licences, or new rivals may appear.
- **Sales changes:** the move to direct billing and less discounting on multi-year deals can make growth figures jumpy.
- **Acquisitions:** large purchases such as MaintainX have to pay off; the goodwill on Autodesk's balance sheet rose to $7.4 billion after it.
- **A strong dollar:** about 55% of sales come from outside the Americas, so a rising dollar reduces reported revenue.

## What it means if you buy regularly

Autodesk earns most of its money from subscriptions, but its shares have still swung widely — they fell by more than half in 2008 and dropped sharply again in 2022. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your purchases over such swings instead of betting on one moment. Many people hold a single company only as part of a wider portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
