**REA Group** is the Melbourne company behind **realestate.com.au**, Australia's most visited property website. Estate agents and developers pay it to advertise homes for sale and rent, and home sellers pay extra to make their listing stand out. It also runs realcommercial.com.au, property.com.au and the mortgage broker Mortgage Choice, and owns a 20% stake in Move, the operator of realtor.com in the US. Rupert Murdoch's News Corp is its majority owner, and the shares trade on the Australian Securities Exchange.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Revenue: A$1.79 billion in fiscal 2026 (to 30 June), +7%; without the Indian business it was A$1.73 billion, +12%.
> - Core net profit rose 15% to A$650 million, while the number of homes listed for sale across Australia was flat.
> - The growth came from price: "Buy yield", the average money earned per sale listing, rose 13%.
> - realestate.com.au averaged a record 12.7 million visitors a month in fiscal 2026.

## How it makes money

Most revenue comes from residential listings — A$1.29 billion in fiscal 2026, up 12%. When someone sells a house, their agent usually lists it on realestate.com.au, and sellers can pay for bigger, higher placements such as the top tier "Premiere+". REA raises those prices every year: about 7% on average for Premiere+ in fiscal 2026 and a planned 8% for fiscal 2027. Because almost every agent in Australia feels it has to be on the site where buyers are, REA can grow even in years when fewer homes are sold.

Smaller sources of money are commercial property and new-development listings, advertising and data products for agents, banks and developers, and financial services through Mortgage Choice, which earns commissions on home loans. In July 2026 REA agreed to sell its Indian property site Housing.com to Aurum PropTech, keeping a 24.9% stake in the buyer.

In fiscal 2026 operating EBITDA rose 12% to A$1.09 billion, a margin of about 61% of revenue. Reported net profit fell 19% to A$552 million because of a A$111 million write-down of the Indian business. Core earnings per share were A$4.93, and the full-year dividend rose 20% to 297 Australian cents, fully franked. REA finished a A$200 million share buyback in 2026. In the September 2026 quarter revenue rose about 11% to A$450 million, even though listings slipped 2%. For fiscal 2027 the company expects national sale listings to be flat to slightly lower.

## The stock's story

realestate.com.au started in Melbourne in 1995. Over the following two decades REA became one of the best-known growth stocks on the ASX, because the website turned into the default place Australians look for a home. In 2025 its main rival, Domain, was bought by the US property-data group CoStar, a company known for spending heavily to win audience. In July 2026 Domain sued REA in the Federal Court over its "9 in 10 properties" advertising claim, and REA filed a counterclaim. On the day of the fiscal 2026 results, 6 August 2026, the shares swung between flat and about 5% higher. The chief executive is Cameron McIntyre.

## Competitors

In Australia REA's main competitor is Domain, now owned by [CoStar Group](/en/csgp/), which also runs Homes.com in the US. Agents can also advertise on social media and on their own websites. Its US stake, Move, competes with [Zillow](/en/z/). REA is controlled by [News Corp](/en/nwsa/), which owns realtor.com's operator jointly with it.

## Main risks

- **Housing cycle:** if fewer Australians sell homes, listing volumes fall; fiscal 2027 started with listings in Sydney and Melbourne down sharply.
- **Pricing pushback:** growth relies on yearly price rises; agents and sellers could rebel or switch to cheaper rivals.
- **A rich competitor:** CoStar has the money to spend on Domain for years to win audience and agents.
- **AI search:** if people start looking for homes through AI assistants instead of portals, REA's audience advantage could weaken.
- **Control by News Corp:** the majority owner decides key matters, and minority shareholders have limited influence.
- **Currency:** the shares are priced in Australian dollars, so for foreign investors exchange rates change the result.

## What it means if you buy regularly

REA has been a strong long-term performer, but its shares are sensitive to interest rates and the housing market and have had deep drops along the way. Buying a fixed amount regularly spreads your purchases across good and bad periods instead of betting on one moment. Many people hold one company only as part of a wider portfolio. Past results do not guarantee future returns.
