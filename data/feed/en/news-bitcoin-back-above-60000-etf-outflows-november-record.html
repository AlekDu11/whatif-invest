Update November 28, 06:30 UTC: Bitcoin slipped back below $60,000 early on Saturday and traded near $59,700 at 06:07 UTC, down about 3% in 24 hours, after swinging from a Friday high near $62,100 to a low near $58,700 (Investing.com). About $7 billion of bitcoin options expired on Deribit on Friday and roughly $478 million of leveraged crypto positions were liquidated. Ether fell about 4.5% to near $1,657, and the Trump-linked WLFI token crashed about 40% after a reserve wallet sold ETH at a loss (our brief). November outflows from US spot bitcoin ETFs passed $5 billion. On the other side, CryptoQuant data show wallets holding 1,000–10,000 BTC bought about 121,000 BTC between September 30 and November 21 while the price fell almost 30%.

Update November 27, 13:05 UTC: Bitcoin kept rising and traded near $61,700 on Friday, about 8% above Wednesday's two-year low near $57,300 and its highest level since Monday; it is on course for its first weekly gain in eight weeks (Analytics Insight, Coinspeaker). It is still down about 20% for November, from roughly $77,600 on November 1. Ether added about 2.3% to near $1,734. The bounce came with a wider relief rally in AI and chip stocks and thin holiday trading; Binance also said on Thursday it had fully lifted withdrawal limits imposed after last month's $6.3 billion hack. Persistent US inflation and a likely Fed rate hike at the December 8–9 meeting remain the main headwinds, and traders watch resistance near $64,600.

Bitcoin climbed back above $60,000 early on Thursday, November 26, rising about 3% in 24 hours to near $60,850 at 05:40 UTC, a day after it briefly dropped below $58,000. The rebound came as Wall Street recovered most of Wednesday's early tech losses, but money kept leaving US spot bitcoin ETFs.

What happened

  • The bounce. Bitcoin's 24-hour range ran from about $57,240 to $61,530 (crypto.news); ether traded between $1,600 and $1,700.
  • Liquidations. Around $692 million of leveraged positions were wiped out in 24 hours, $264 million of them in bitcoin, split almost evenly between shorts and longs (CoinGlass).
  • ETF outflows. US spot bitcoin ETFs lost about $232 million on November 24 and $453 million on November 25 (SoSoValue). KuCoin's daily report puts November outflows near $4.9 billion, the largest month since the funds launched in 2024.
  • Mood. The Crypto Fear & Greed Index sat at 11, deep in "extreme fear", as hot US inflation kept a December Fed hike almost fully priced.
