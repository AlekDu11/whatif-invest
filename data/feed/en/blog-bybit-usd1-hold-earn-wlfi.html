Bybit pays a daily reward in WLFI to anyone who simply keeps the USD1 stablecoin in their account — and in month 7 of the program, from November 16 to December 15, 2026, the pool is 23.5 million WLFI with "up to 10% APR". There is no lock-up and no subscription. The catch: the rate is not fixed, it is paid in a volatile token rather than in dollars, and the stablecoin itself is a young one tied to a politically exposed company.

It is a marketing budget split among holders, not a savings rate

Bybit has a fixed monthly pile of WLFI, the governance token of World Liberty Financial, and divides it among everyone holding USD1, the dollar stablecoin the same company issues. The more USD1 people park, the lower the rate. Bybit publishes the updated APR every day around 06:00 UTC and says it depends on "the remaining monthly WLFI reward budget and total effective USD1 holdings". "Up to 10%" is the ceiling of the headline, not a promise for your balance.

The program has already outlived its own ending. Bybit launched it in May 2026, called month 5 (until October 16) "the fifth and final month", then added month 6 and month 7 with smaller pools. No month 8 has been announced, so plan as if it stops on December 15.

How the daily reward is counted

Bybit takes a USD1 snapshot every hour, 24 times a day, and uses the lowest one as your effective daily holding. That means moving USD1 out for one hour cuts the whole day. The reward is that holding × the daily APR ÷ 365, converted into WLFI at the average Bybit spot price between 23:30 and 23:59 UTC. WLFI lands in the Funding wallet of your main account around 06:00 UTC the next day.

For scale: at a full 10% a year, 1,000 USD1 would earn about $0.27 of WLFI a day, or roughly $8 over the 30 days of month 7 — before any change in the WLFI price.

How people take part

  1. Check that Bybit serves you. Residents of Bybit's Service Restricted Countries are excluded, and Bybit says the campaign may not be available in some jurisdictions. If that is you, skip it — never use a VPN; it breaks the terms and can freeze the account.
  2. Complete identity verification Lv.1 and make sure your account is eligible for Bybit Savings. Islamic accounts are not eligible.
  3. Hold at least 1 USD1 in the Main account, a subaccount, a trading-bot account or the Unified Trading Account. You can buy it on Bybit spot with USDT or deposit it.
  4. Do not move it into Flexible or Fixed Savings — USD1 there, USD1 borrowed via loans or margin, and USD1 in Copy Trading Pro subaccounts do not count.
  5. Keep the balance steady through the day and check the Funding wallet the next morning (UTC).

Use only bybit.com or the official app. "USD1 bonus" links in messages and "claim WLFI" sites that ask you to connect a wallet are fake — see our 9 airdrop safety rules.

The risks sit in three places

The reward token. WLFI's price moves like any small-cap token, so a 10% yield can be worth much less by the time you sell — or you may receive amounts too small to trade without fees eating them. The stablecoin. Bybit says USD1 is backed by short-term US Treasuries and cash, but it is newer and smaller than USDT or USDC, and World Liberty Financial's ties to the Trump family make it a regulatory and reputational lightning rod. The exchange. Your dollars sit on Bybit the whole time; Bybit lost about $1.5 billion of ETH to hackers in February 2025 and covered it, but exchange risk never goes to zero. Bybit can also change the terms or disqualify users without notice.

What it means if you buy regularly

If you keep cash on an exchange between purchases, this pays something for money that would otherwise earn nothing — as long as you are comfortable holding that cash in USD1 and on Bybit. It does not change the result of the buying itself. For scale: $10 a day in Bitcoin over the last year turned $3,650 into $3,076 (−15.7%); a month of the WLFI bonus on a small cash buffer is a few dollars next to that.
