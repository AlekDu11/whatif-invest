What is a seed phrase, and how do you keep it safe? A seed phrase (also called a recovery phrase or secret recovery phrase) is a list of 12 or 24 ordinary words that your wallet app shows you once, when you create the wallet. Whoever has these words controls every coin in that wallet — from any phone, anywhere — so you write them on paper, keep them offline and never tell them to anyone.

Key takeaways

  • A seed phrase is usually 12 or 24 words taken from a fixed list of 2,048 English words. It is the master key to your wallet, not just a password.
  • Wallet makers such as Phantom say their support will never ask for your phrase. Anyone who asks for it is trying to steal from you.
  • Americans reported $9.3 billion of crypto-related fraud losses to the FBI in 2024, 66% more than in 2023 (FBI IC3 report, April 2025).

Why these words matter so much

When you create your own wallet (an app like Binance Wallet, Phantom, MetaMask or Trust Wallet), the app makes a secret key for you. The seed phrase is that key written as words, so a human can copy it. If your phone breaks or is stolen, you install the app on a new phone, type the words, and your coins are back.

The same works for a thief. There is no bank to call and no "forgot password" button: the blockchain only checks the key. If someone types your words into their own phone, they can send your coins away in a minute, and nobody can reverse it.

On an exchange account (like your Binance or Kraken login) there is usually no seed phrase — the exchange holds the keys and you protect the account with a password and 2FA. The seed phrase is about wallets you control.

Step 1 — Write it down on paper when the app shows it

Create the wallet only in the official app (open the app store from the wallet's own website, check the developer name). When the app shows the 12 or 24 words, write them by hand on paper, in order, with numbers 1, 2, 3… Check each word twice. Many apps then ask you to tap the words back in the right order — that is the test that you wrote them correctly.

Step 2 — Never make a digital copy

No screenshot, no photo, no notes app, no email to yourself, no cloud drive, no chat to a friend. Phantom's help center lists exactly these places as forbidden. Phones and cloud accounts get hacked, and some malware searches photos for seed words.

Step 3 — Store it where you would store cash

Keep the paper in a safe place at home, away from water and fire. Many people keep two copies in two different places (for example home and a trusted relative), each as protected as the first. For larger amounts, a metal backup plate survives fire and floods. Do not write "CRYPTO SEED" on the envelope.

Step 4 — Never type it into a website or give it to "support"

Real wallets ask for the phrase in only one place: inside the app, when you restore the wallet yourself. A website, a pop-up, a form, a "wallet validation" bot or a support agent asking for it is a scam — every time. Real support never writes to you first in Telegram, Discord or X.

Step 5 — Never use a phrase someone else gives you

A trick that sounds generous: a stranger shares "his" seed phrase with coins inside and asks you to help move them. The wallet is set up so you must send your own coins first to pay fees — and they are gone. Phantom warns that a wallet created from someone else's words is controlled by that person. Always create your own wallet and your own phrase.

⚠️ Watch out

  • Fake support. "Hi, I'm from Binance/MetaMask support, please verify your wallet by entering your 12 words." Real support never asks.
  • Fake apps and fake sites. A copy of a wallet app in the store or an ad at the top of Google asks you to "restore" your wallet. Install only from the official website's link.
  • The screenshot "just for now". A photo in your gallery syncs to the cloud; one hacked account empties your wallet.

What it means if you buy regularly

If you buy a little every month and later move coins to your own wallet (see how to withdraw crypto to your own wallet), the seed phrase protects years of savings — for example a wallet with bitcoin, which is at $59,589.20 −3.40% right now (−34.1% over 12 months). Small regular buys can stay on a well-protected exchange account; once the amount is large, your own wallet plus a seed phrase on paper is the usual next step.
