The German stock exchange: DAX, Xetra and Frankfurt

The main German stock exchange is run by Deutsche Börse in Frankfurt. Most trading happens on its electronic venue Xetra, open 9:00–17:30 Central European Time on weekdays; Börse Frankfurt also quotes shares outside those hours. The headline index is the DAX, which holds the 40 largest German companies (it was expanded from 30 in September 2021). Mid-sized firms sit in the MDAX, smaller ones in the SDAX.

Deutsche Börse itself is a listed stock and one of the steadier names on the list: +68.1% over five years and +17.8% over the last year.

How to buy German stocks from abroad

  • A broker with Xetra access. Most European brokers offer it; German shares trade one by one, there are no 100-share lots.
  • A DAX ETF. One fund buys all 40 DAX companies. Investors in Europe usually choose a UCITS ETF listed on Xetra.
  • US listings. A few names (SAP, Deutsche Bank) also trade in New York; EWG covers Germany broadly in dollars.

Germany withholds tax on dividends; depending on where you live, part of it can be reclaimed — check your broker's tax information.

What it means if you buy regularly

Regular buying worked in Germany over five years: 30 of the 39 stocks ended above the money put in. But the spread is huge — from +376% to Porsche SE's −41.6% — and someone who started a year ago is slightly under water. A plan on a broad DAX fund or many stocks depends far less on picking the right sector. Past results, not a forecast.
