Trading memecoins means buying joke-themed tokens on a decentralized exchange (DEX), usually hours or days after they launch, and hoping to sell higher. It can multiply money fast, but it is closer to a casino than to investing: a 2025 Solidus Labs study found that 98.6% of tokens launched on Pump.fun were rug pulls or pump-and-dump schemes.

This guide explains how it works, which numbers to check before you click «buy», and what most people get wrong.

Key takeaways

  • Only about 97,000 of 7 million Pump.fun tokens kept at least $1,000 of liquidity (Solidus Labs, Jan 2024 – Mar 2025).
  • Of 2+ million tokens launched in 2024, only 1.7% were still actively traded 30 days later (Chainalysis).
  • Liquidity under $50K, a pair younger than 3 days and one wallet holding a big share are the three loudest warning signs.

A memecoin trade happens in three steps

  1. You need a self-custody wallet that works on the token's network — for example OKX Wallet, Phantom (Solana) or MetaMask (Base, BNB Chain, Ethereum). You fund it with the network's coin: SOL, ETH or BNB.
  2. You find the token on a tracker. Our DEX memecoins page shows what is trending right now on Solana, Base, BNB Chain, Ethereum and TON, with price, 1-hour and 24-hour change, volume, liquidity and age.
  3. You swap your SOL, ETH or BNB for the token on a DEX or a trading terminal such as GMGN or the OKX Wallet swap. To exit, you swap back.

Nobody holds your money in between — that is the appeal and the danger. There is no support desk to reverse a mistake.

Liquidity decides whether you can sell at all

Liquidity is the money sitting in the trading pool. If a pool holds $20,000 and you try to sell $5,000 of a token, your own sale moves the price a lot (this is called slippage). If the creator removes the liquidity — a rug pull — the token can become unsellable in seconds.

Rough rule of thumb used by many traders:

  • Under $50K liquidity: extremely fragile, a few sellers can crash it.
  • $50K–$500K: still risky, but you can usually get out with small amounts.
  • Over $1M and weeks old: the survivors. Still volatile, but no longer a lottery ticket.

Age and holders matter as much as the chart

A token that is 3 hours old and up 800% has no history — the chart only shows the first buyers. Before buying, check:

  • Pair age. On our page tokens younger than 3 days get a «New» flag.
  • Top holders. If one or two wallets hold 20–30% of the supply, they can dump on everyone else. Terminals like GMGN show holder concentration.
  • Volume vs liquidity. Daily volume 20× bigger than liquidity often means bots trading with themselves to look popular; we flag it as «Unusual volume».
  • The contract address. Scammers launch copies with the same name and ticker. Copy the address from the project's official account, never from a reply or a DM.

Most of the money is made by people who sell early

The uncomfortable math: when a token goes up 1,000%, someone bought at the bottom and someone else bought near the top. Creators and early bots usually sell into the first wave of buyers. Solidus Labs found the median rug pull on Raydium took about $2,832 — small, fast, repeated thousands of times.

That is why experienced traders decide their exit before they buy: for example, «sell half if it doubles, sell everything if it falls 30%». Without a plan, a winning trade often turns into a losing one within the hour.

What it means if you buy regularly

If you invest a fixed amount every week or month, new memecoins are not a good fit: they rarely live long enough for regular buying to work.
