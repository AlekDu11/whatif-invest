Polygon wants to look more like a payments company than a blockchain. Co-founder Sandeep Nailwal says the project is considering applying for a US banking license and plans to spend about $50–100 million on each acquisition of licensed payment businesses abroad.

What happened

  • A bank charter is on the table. Nailwal told India's Moneycontrol that Polygon is weighing a US banking license, separately from its buying spree.
  • Licenses by acquisition. Over one to two years Polygon wants agent-banking and payment-license businesses in North America, the EU, Brazil, Mexico and Southeast Asia. It has already made an offer for a European company and picked a North American target.
  • It already has a US foothold. Its Coinme purchase, which closed in October, brought 37 state money-transmitter licenses and 11 exemptions — enough, Polygon says, to cover 48 states — plus FinCEN registration.
  • More funding later. Polygon has raised about $450 million so far and expects another round in late 2027 or early 2028.

The numbers right now

POL, the network's token, trades at $0.0752 — about 94% below its record of $1.29 from March 2024. A license strategy can bring stablecoin payment volume onto the network, but it does not automatically lift the token's price. See how other coins compare on the from-ATH page.
