Nvidia's board unanimously reinstated Jensen Huang as president and CEO on Monday, November 23, eleven days after he stepped down. The stock jumped about 32% and pulled the whole market up with it: by 17:30 UTC the Nasdaq was up about 7% and the S&P 500 about 5%.

What happened

  • The decision. The board said it had received input from senior levels of the US government and was confident Huang's return poses no national security risk. The independent investigation that followed his resignation on November 12 will continue to its end.
  • Nvidia. Shares traded near $166 at 17:30 UTC, up from Friday's close of $125.53 — after a 14% crash on Friday.
  • The market. The Nasdaq rose about 6.9%, the S&P 500 about 5% and the Dow about 3.2%. The Nasdaq-100 ETF QQQ had its biggest opening jump since its 1999 launch, and the VIX fear index fell about 24%.
  • AI and crypto stocks. Micron rose about 19%, CoreWeave and Nebius up to 30%, IREN about 29%. Bitcoin gained only about 2%, to roughly $61,700.

Our angle: a relief rally, not a reset

Nvidia now trades at $155.38 −5.86% (−12.0% over 12 months), still below its record of $228.38 (September 2026). Strategists warned that a mechanical bounce after a deep sell-off guarantees nothing, and this week still brings the PCE inflation report on Wednesday and Nvidia's delayed quarterly filing, due by December 4.
