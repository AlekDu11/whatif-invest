Micron shares jumped about 19% on Monday, November 23, one of the biggest gains among large chip stocks, after Nvidia's board reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO. The memory maker had lost almost a quarter of its value in the previous five sessions.

What happened

  • Last week: five losing days wiped out 23.6% of Micron's market value, including a drop of almost 14% on Friday to $594.51, the lowest close since May.
  • Monday, 14:30 UTC open: the stock gapped higher with the whole tech sector; by about 15:26 UTC it traded near $717 (+20.7%), with a high of $718.72.
  • 16:31 UTC: about $710, up roughly 19%, while the Nasdaq was up about 7%.

Why it moved

The trigger was Nvidia: Micron makes the high-bandwidth memory (HBM) that sits next to Nvidia's AI processors, so worries about Nvidia's leadership had hit memory stocks too. Citi kept its Buy rating and called the memory sell-off overdone. Micron's CEO said on November 18 that about 75% of the company's 2027 memory supply is already sold out. On the other side, TrendForce warned on Thursday that NAND contract prices could slip up to 5% in early 2027 — one reason for last week's slide.
