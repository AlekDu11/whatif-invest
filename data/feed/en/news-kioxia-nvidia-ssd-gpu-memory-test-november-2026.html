Kioxia, the Japanese flash-memory maker, said it is testing with Nvidia whether fast SSDs can act as an extra, petabyte-scale layer of memory for AI chips. The news sent Kioxia shares up as much as 14.5% to 7,895 yen in Tokyo on Thursday, November 26, as AI chip stocks rebounded after a wild week.

What happened

  • The test. Kioxia put 144 of its NVMe SSDs, about 1.1 petabytes of raw storage, into an Nvidia GB200 NVL72 rack, using Nvidia's SCADA software and its "Storage-Next" design. The setup could handle a database of about 2 trillion vectors, the kind of data AI search and chatbots look up.
  • Still a proof of concept. Both companies say feasibility and performance are still being evaluated; there is no product or order yet.
  • Chip stocks bounced. Advantest rose 7.8% and SoftBank 5.3%; the Nikkei 225 closed 1.3% higher at 57,745.27.
  • A volatile week. Kioxia gained about 10% on Tuesday after Nvidia brought back Jensen Huang as CEO, then fell 14.2% on Wednesday on doubts about AI spending and new US tariffs, before Thursday's jump.

Why it matters for AI stocks

AI chips are limited by how much fast memory (HBM) sits next to them, and HBM is scarce and expensive. If cheaper flash storage can stretch that memory, demand for NAND chips from Kioxia, SanDisk and others could grow with every new AI data center. That is the bet behind the jump. It is early: the test says nothing yet about speed in real workloads or costs.
