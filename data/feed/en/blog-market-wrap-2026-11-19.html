Thursday, November 19 was a sell-everything day. The S&P 500 fell 2.0% for its sixth losing session in a row, the Dow dropped about 1,100 points, gold sank to its lowest since September 2025 and Bitcoin slid to $61,828 (live: $58,660.70). Even Anthropic's record stock-market debut could not lift the mood: worries about higher US interest rates won.

Key takeaways

  • S&P 500 −2.0%, Nasdaq −2.3%, Dow −2.2% to 49,097; only 11 of the 100 largest US companies closed higher.
  • Spot gold fell about 2.6% to near $3,625 and silver about 4.7%, as the dollar hit its strongest level since April 2025.
  • Bitcoin −1.3% to $61,828 and Ethereum −1.7% to $1,740 in 24 hours; both are down 13–14% in a week.
  • The Crypto Fear & Greed Index reads 12/100 ("extreme fear"), after 10 a day ago and 20 a week ago.

The big picture: rates beat the AI party

The day started well. Nasdaq-100 futures were up about 2% in the morning ahead of Anthropic's listing. By the close the mood had flipped: the S&P 500 ended at about 7,177, the Nasdaq Composite at 23,886, and the Nasdaq-100 fund QQQ lost 2.2%. The VIX "fear gauge" neared 29, its highest since March. Selling spread far beyond tech — industrials were the worst sector, and only energy and consumer staples ended roughly flat.

The main pressure was interest rates. Fed minutes released the day before showed almost all officials ready to raise rates again, and futures markets now price roughly a 94% chance of a December hike. The 10-year Treasury yield stayed above 5%. Higher yields and a strong dollar hurt everything that pays no interest — growth stocks, crypto and gold alike.

Crypto fell less than stocks on the day but is weaker over the week. Total crypto market value is about $2.14 trillion (−3.7% in 24 hours, CoinGecko), with Bitcoin's share at 58.1%. Only 3 of 46 coins worth $1 billion or more rose. Oil eased too: Brent −2.2% to $89.92.

Biggest winners

  • Palo Alto Networks +9.9% — raised its yearly sales forecast and announced a $4.5 billion all-stock purchase of AI startup Applied Compute.
  • CrowdStrike +7.9% — pulled up by Palo Alto; the whole cybersecurity group rallied on expectations of more security spending as AI helps attackers.
  • Arista Networks +4.0% and Western Digital +3.0% — we found no company-specific news.
  • In crypto, Mantle +1.2% and Bitcoin Cash +0.2% simply held up; no clear reason.

Biggest losers

  • AMD −7.7% — short seller Hunterbrook said it bets against the company over alleged links to blacklisted Chinese firms (our brief).
  • UnitedHealth −5.6% — health insurers are still under pressure from a proposed tougher audit rule for Medicare Advantage (brief).
  • Royal Caribbean −5.5% and Marvell −4.9% — fell with the market; no single company reason found.
  • Williams-Sonoma −15.2% — results that disappointed high expectations (brief).
  • NEAR −7.8%, the biggest drop among the 20 largest assets — AI-linked tokens are having a bad week (brief).
  • Ethena −7.8% (−28% in a week) and Uniswap −5.6% — no specific news; selling across DeFi tokens.

The news that moved markets

  1. Anthropic's debut. The Claude maker priced its IPO at $83, raising a record ~$81 billion. Shares opened at $111.50 and closed at $97.17 — still 17% above the offer price (brief).
  2. Fed hike bets. The October minutes and talk of a possible half-point move kept a December hike almost fully priced (brief).
  3. Weak factory data. The Philadelphia Fed index fell to 6.3 from 20.9 (18 expected), while weekly jobless claims stayed low at 203,000.
  4. Nvidia slipped 3.3% even after strong quarterly results — a sign that good AI news is not enough right now.
  5. Binance hack money moves. Wallets tied to the October theft sent about $248 million in ether to new addresses.

After the close, Intuit agreed to sell Mailchimp and its shares fell sharply in late trading.
