Etherscan, the most widely used Ethereum block explorer, warned users on November 18 after detecting attempts to get unauthorized access to its customer support and ticketing system, which runs on Freshworks. The company says it is investigating and has taken precautions.

What happened

  • The target was support, not the blockchain. The attempts hit Etherscan's help-desk tools. There is no report that the explorer itself, transaction records or anyone's wallet was affected.
  • Open questions. Etherscan has not said who was behind it, how it was done, whether the attackers got in, or whether any customer data was exposed.
  • Phishing risk. Attackers who reach support tools can send convincing fake "support" emails. Etherscan says its staff will never ask for private keys or recovery phrases, and it cannot cancel, speed up or reverse transactions.
  • Part of a pattern. This week's Optimizely script attack on 186 websites and earlier leaks at outside vendors of Coinone and Trezor show that crypto users are now often reached through third-party providers.

What it means for you

Your coins on Ethereum are not at risk from a help-desk incident by itself; the risk is a message that tricks you into signing or sharing something. Ignore unexpected "support" contact, never type a seed phrase into a website, and check addresses on the device screen.
