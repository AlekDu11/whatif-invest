For a few minutes on Saturday, bitcoin was "worth" about $17,000 on one of Kraken's markets. The BTC/CAD pair fell to C$24,250 while bitcoin traded near $57,650 against the US dollar on the same exchange — a gap of roughly 71%. Kraken says it found no breach and that the trades will not be reversed.

What happened

  • The print. In the 15 minutes from 08:00 UTC on November 21, BTC/CAD on Kraken hit C$24,250. At the Bank of Canada's Friday rate of C$1.4325 per dollar, that is about $16,900. Around 122 trades for about 10.7 BTC went through below $20,000, per Yahoo Finance data cited by Coin-Turk.
  • Kraken's explanation. The exchange said heavy selling on its Canadian-dollar pairs began around 20:00 UTC on Friday, its security team found no compromised accounts, and executed trades stand.
  • A possible cause. Blockstream CEO Adam Back suggested a USD/CAD market maker ran out of money, so liquidity vanished and the selling hit stop-losses and liquidations. Kraken has not confirmed this.
  • Back to normal. BTC/CAD was back above C$80,000 by Saturday afternoon and traded near C$86,200 on Sunday.

What it means for you

This was a liquidity accident in one thin market, not a real crash: on the main markets bitcoin bottomed near $55,130 on Friday and has since bounced. Bitcoin now trades at $60,720.00 +2.75% (−32.8% over 12 months), well below its record of $126,080.00 (October 2025) — see the from-ATH page. The lesson for ordinary buyers: stop-loss and market orders on a small trading pair can fill at absurd prices when liquidity disappears. Limit orders and the deepest pairs (usually against USD or USDT) are safer — compare platforms on our exchanges page. Someone who put $10 a day into bitcoin for five years invested $18,260 and holds $25,697 (+40.7%) — history, not a forecast (calculator).
