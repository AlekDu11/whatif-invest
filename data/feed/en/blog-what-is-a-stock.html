What is a stock? A stock (also called a share) is a small piece of ownership in a real company. If you own one share, you own a tiny slice of the business: its profits, its future and its risks.

Key takeaways

  • A share is a piece of a company. Its price goes up and down every day as people buy and sell.
  • You can make money in two ways: the price rises, or the company pays you part of its profit (a dividend).
  • You can also lose money — a share can fall a lot, and if a company goes bankrupt, shareholders are paid last and often get nothing.

Why companies sell shares

A company that wants money to grow can sell pieces of itself to the public instead of borrowing from a bank. The first sale is called an IPO (initial public offering). After that, the shares trade on a stock exchange (a market such as the New York Stock Exchange or Nasdaq), where people buy them from and sell them to each other.

When you buy a share later, your money usually goes to another investor, not to the company. The company only got money at the IPO.

What you actually get as a shareholder

  • A share of the profit. Not in your pocket directly: the company decides whether to keep the profit to grow, buy back its own shares or pay a dividend.
  • A vote. Ordinary shares usually let you vote on big decisions, such as electing the board of directors — one vote per share.
  • A claim, but the last one. If the company fails, it pays workers, banks and bondholders first. Shareholders get what is left, often nothing.

A worked example: one share of Apple

In the September 2026 quarter Apple made a profit (net income) of $30.7 billion, which it reported as $2.10 per share. That means the company has roughly 14.6 billion shares ($30.7 billion ÷ $2.10). One share is about one 14.6-billionth of Apple.

It also pays a dividend of $0.27 per share each quarter. So if you held 10 shares, you would get about $2.70 every three months (before tax), plus or minus whatever the share price does.

The share price itself changes all the time: right now Apple is $358.54 +0.83% (+29.0% over 12 months), and the whole company is valued at $5.10 T. That value (market capitalisation) is simply the share price × the number of shares.

⚠️ Watch out

  • One stock is not "safe" because the company is famous. Big, profitable companies have also lost half of their value in bad years. Do not put all your money in one name — see the lesson on diversification.
  • "Hot tips" and guaranteed returns. The SEC warns about "pump and dump" schemes: someone hypes a small stock on social media, sells to the buyers and the price collapses. No honest person can promise a stock will go up.
  • Confusing a cheap price with a cheap company. A $5 share is not cheaper than a $300 share — what matters is what you get for the money (profit, growth), not the number on the screen.

What it means if you buy regularly

Many beginners do not pick single stocks at all: they buy a fund that holds hundreds of companies, such as one that follows the S&P 500 (SPY). For example, $10 a day into SPY over the last 5 years would now be worth $26,643 (you would have put in $18,260) — past results, not a forecast. Buying a fixed amount regularly means you buy more shares when prices are low and fewer when they are high.
