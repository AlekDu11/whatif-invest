Hewlett Packard Enterprise had two very different sessions on Monday, November 23. In regular trading the stock jumped about 17% with the rest of the AI trade. After the close it reported a record quarter, raised its outlook, and still fell about 7%.

What happened

  • Regular session (14:30–21:00 UTC). HPE closed up 17.4% at $48.02 as Nvidia's 31.5% jump lifted AI hardware names. Dell rose about 20% the same day.
  • Results after the close. Fiscal Q4 revenue rose 50% to a record $14.5 billion, in line with forecasts of about $14.47 billion. Adjusted earnings were $1.43 a share against roughly $1.28 expected.
  • AI orders. AI orders hit a record $5.7 billion in the quarter, and the AI backlog reached $10.5 billion, up more than 80% from a year earlier.
  • After hours. Shares fell 6.7% to $44.81, even as HPE guided fiscal 2027 earnings of $5.05–5.35 a share (analysts expected about $4.77), raised its dividend 19% to $0.17 and planned about $3 billion of buybacks.

Why it moved

The company did not explain the share move. The most likely reason, based on the reports, is that a 17% run-up in one day had already priced in a strong report. Revenue only matched forecasts, the networking margin slipped to 22.1% from 23.4%, and management warned that memory and other component shortages could last until at least late 2028.
