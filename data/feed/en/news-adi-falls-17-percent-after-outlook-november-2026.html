Analog Devices, one of the biggest makers of analog and power chips, reported a record quarter before the open on Tuesday, November 24 — and the stock still fell 17%, its worst day since 1998 according to TheStreet. Investors focused on the next quarter, not the last one.

What happened

  • Results (before 14:30 UTC). Fiscal Q4 revenue rose 42% to $4.37 billion against about $4.32 billion expected; adjusted earnings were $4.06 a share versus roughly $3.90.
  • Outlook. For fiscal Q1 ADI guided revenue of about $4.4 billion (± $150 million), in line with forecasts, but adjusted earnings of about $3.82 a share, slightly below the roughly $3.88 analysts wanted.
  • Close (21:00 UTC). Shares ended down 17.4% at $312.95 after falling as much as 19.7% in the morning.
  • Peers. Texas Instruments fell about 8% with no news of its own; the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index lost 7.9% and Nvidia 5.9%.

Why it moved

The miss on profit guidance was small, but the stock traded near 37 times trailing earnings, so there was little room for disappointment. Management said margins will dip in the next quarter because of higher component costs and holiday fab shutdowns, the factories are already running at full capacity, and capital spending will jump to $1.6–1.9 billion in fiscal 2027 from about $0.7 billion — new capacity only starts producing in the second half of fiscal 2028. Inventory also rose to 141 days from 134. The sell-off came a day after Monday's sharp rally in chip stocks.
