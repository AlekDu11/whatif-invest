Micron, the biggest US maker of memory chips, was one of the worst performers on Wall Street on Friday. Its shares are now worth about half of what they were at the June peak, even though the company has not reported any new bad news of its own.

What happened

  • Friday, November 20. Micron closed Thursday at about $690. By 17:30 UTC it was down roughly 13% at about $603, after losing more than 11% in the first hour and a half of trading.
  • The whole memory group fell. Western Digital and Seagate lost about 12%, SanDisk about 15%. In Seoul, Samsung dropped about 7% and SK Hynix about 6%.
  • The wider chip sell-off. AMD and Broadcom were down 9–10% and Nvidia about 7%. The Nasdaq 100 lost about 3% by late morning, its second heavy day in a row.

Why it moved

We found no Micron-specific announcement. The stock fell with the AI trade: investors are questioning whether the huge spending on AI data centers will pay off, and the Anthropic IPO did not lift sentiment. On top of that, research firm TrendForce expects NAND flash contract prices to start falling in the first quarter of 2027, a direct worry for memory makers after a year of rising prices. Not everyone is selling: a Barron's columnist called memory stocks very cheap, at about 3.8 times expected earnings.
