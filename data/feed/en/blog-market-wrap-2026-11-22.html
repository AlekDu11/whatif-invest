Sunday, November 22 was the quiet day after the storm. Bitcoin held above $60,000 all day and ended near $60,860, up 1.2% in 24 hours (live: $59,208.00 −2.15%), and Ethereum gained 2.0% to about $1,715. In the evening US stock futures opened sharply higher — S&P 500 futures +2.1% — a first sign of relief before Monday's open.

Key takeaways

  • Bitcoin +1.2% to about $60,858 and Ethereum +2.0% to $1,715 in 24 hours; Bitcoin is still −6.5% for the week.
  • 31 of 46 coins worth $1 billion or more rose, but most moves were small (under 3%).
  • Sunday-evening futures: S&P 500 +2.1%, Nasdaq +2.75%, Dow about +800 points (Fortune).
  • The Crypto Fear & Greed Index is at 11/100 — "extreme fear" — after 12 a day ago and 10 a week ago.

The big picture: crypto steadies, stocks wait for Monday

Stock, gold and oil markets were closed on Sunday, so Friday's closes still stand: the S&P 500 fund SPY ended the session −3.6%, the Nasdaq-100 fund QQQ −5.7%, gold −4.6% to about $3,515 and silver −7.3%. Brent oil was near $90.

Crypto had a calm day. Total crypto market value was about $2.08 trillion late on Sunday (CoinGecko), and Bitcoin's share rose to 58.8% from 57.9% a day earlier — money stayed in the biggest coin. About $1.8 billion of leveraged bets had been wiped out during the weekend's swings, which removed much of the forced selling.

The mood is still very dark. Fear & Greed slipped to 11, one of its lowest readings of the year. The biggest mover among the 20 largest assets was Chainlink, and even that was only +2.2%.

Biggest winners

Among coins worth $1 billion or more, in 24 hours:

  • Bitway +10.0% — a rebound after it lost about 55% in a week earlier this month (our brief); no new announcement found.
  • Pieverse +8.2% to about $3.67 — a new record and roughly double in a week, on its AI-agent products (brief).
  • Venice Token +6.1% to about $26 — a second strong day after Saturday's jump (brief).
  • Mantle +4.0% and OKB +3.7% — exchange-linked tokens led the bounce; no single cause found.
  • Ethereum +2.0% — the best of the very large coins, still −5.9% for the week.

Biggest losers

  • Rain −12.4% — the weakest $1B+ coin; it doubled in a day on November 16 with no news (brief) and keeps giving back. No specific cause found.
  • Canton −5.3% — no clear reason; −13.5% for the week.
  • MemeCore −1.7%, Stellar −1.6% (the weakest of the top 20) and Pepe −1.5% — small moves against the trend.

The news that moved markets

  1. Stock futures bounce. On Sunday evening Dow futures rose about 800 points (+1.7%), S&P 500 futures 2.1% and Nasdaq futures 2.75%, after a week in which all three major indexes fell into a correction on fears about the AI boom (Fortune).
  2. Saylor hints at another buy. Michael Saylor posted his purchase chart, which usually comes a day before Strategy announces a buy. Its 850,849 BTC cost about $75,452 each, roughly 20% above today's price (brief).
  3. Chainflip hack grows to $12 million. The swap protocol now says the attacker also took bitcoin; it offers a 10% bounty if the rest is returned by Monday evening (brief).
  4. Cyber bridge exploit. 5.2 million CYBER tokens were minted that should not have existed; Upbit and Bithumb froze transfers (brief).
  5. TRUMP memecoin dinner. Donald Trump hosted the coin's biggest holders on Sunday; the token is still about 99% below its January 2025 peak (brief).

What to watch this week

  • Monday, Nov 23: US stocks reopen (Japan is closed for a holiday); November flash PMIs; Chainflip's bounty deadline at 18:38 UTC. Strategy usually publishes its weekly bitcoin filing on Mondays.
  • Tuesday, Nov 24: US consumer confidence, Germany's Ifo survey; Workday and Autodesk report after the close.
  • Wednesday, Nov 25: US PCE inflation for October, the second estimate of Q3 GDP, durable goods and jobless claims (preview).
  • Thursday, Nov 26: US markets closed for Thanksgiving. Friday, Nov 27: US stocks close early.

What it means if you buy regularly

A calm Sunday after a crash week feels like relief, but one quiet day says nothing about the next one — futures can turn before the open.
