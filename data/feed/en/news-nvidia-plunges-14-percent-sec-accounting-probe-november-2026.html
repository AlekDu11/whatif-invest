Nvidia had its worst day in years on Friday. The world's best-known AI stock lost 14% after a report that US regulators are examining its accounting, and it is now roughly half the price it was in October.

What happened

  • Friday, November 20 — the drop. NVDA closed at $125.53, down $20.45 or 14.0%. It traded between $124.50 and $143.73, and about 633 million shares changed hands, roughly four times a normal day.
  • Seventh weekly loss in a row. That is Nvidia's longest losing streak since 2008, per CNBC.
  • The whole market went with it. The Nasdaq fell 5.3% and the S&P 500 3.7%; the Dow and the S&P 500 entered correction territory, per Fox Business.

Why it moved

Reuters reported that the SEC's investigation of Nvidia is looking at potential accounting issues; earlier in the week the Wall Street Journal said the probe covers whether its disclosures misled investors. The SEC declined to comment, and an investigation is not proof of wrongdoing. Investors were already nervous: Nvidia said its quarterly 10-Q filing would come later than usual, an independent board committee is running its own review, Jensen Huang stepped down as CEO on November 12, and California's attorney general served a subpoena on Thursday. This came despite strong results on November 17, with revenue up 103% to $115.8 billion.
