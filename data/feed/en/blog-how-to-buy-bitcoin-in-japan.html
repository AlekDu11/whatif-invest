In Japan you buy Bitcoin on an exchange registered with the Financial Services Agency (FSA), such as bitFlyer, Coincheck, bitbank or GMO Coin, and pay with a yen bank transfer, often from a few hundred yen. Foreign exchanges without Japanese registration are not allowed to serve residents, and the big ones have left. Key takeaways

  • The FSA register listed 27 crypto-asset exchange service providers on October 31, 2026, including bitFlyer, Coincheck, bitbank, GMO Coin, SBI VC Trade, Rakuten Wallet, Binance Japan and OKJ.
  • Today crypto gains are usually "miscellaneous income" (雑所得), taxed together with your salary at up to about 55% including resident tax. Swapping one crypto for another is taxable.
  • On July 15, 2026 the Diet moved crypto under the Financial Instruments and Exchange Act. A separate flat rate of about 20% is expected from January 1, 2028, but the start date is not final.

Who regulates crypto in Japan

Japan was one of the first countries with clear crypto rules. Exchanges must register with the FSA, keep customer money and most coins separate from their own, and follow anti-money-laundering checks. The FSA publishes a list of registered firms and a separate list of unregistered foreign platforms it has warned, including Bybit, MEXC, KuCoin, Bitget and LBank. In February 2025 Apple removed several of those apps from the Japanese App Store at the FSA's request.

So don't use a foreign platform just because it lists more coins. Binance's global site stopped serving Japanese residents on December 1, 2023 and moved them to Binance Japan. Bybit paused new Japanese sign-ups in October 2025 and announced in December 2025 that it would phase out Japanese accounts. OKX's Japanese business is the separate company OKJ, and Kraken left Japan in 2023. For that reason this guide has no partner links: none of our partner exchanges' global sites serves Japan.

The 2026 law (promulgated July 23, 2026, in force within a year) treats crypto more like securities: stricter insider-trading rules, disclosure by issuers, up to 10 years in prison for running an unregistered exchange, and a path to domestic crypto ETFs.

How people in Japan pay

  1. Bank transfer (銀行振込). You send yen from your own bank to your personal deposit account at the exchange. bitFlyer, for example, uses accounts at SMBC, docomo SMBC Net Bank and GMO Aozora Net Bank; the fee is your bank's transfer fee.
  2. Quick deposit (クイック入金). Pay instantly through internet banking or Pay-easy. On bitFlyer it costs ¥330, and it's free from docomo SMBC Net Bank.
  3. Convenience store. Some exchanges accept payment at a konbini, usually with a fee (¥330 on bitFlyer).

The account name must match the name on your exchange account.

Step by step: your first Bitcoin

  1. Pick an FSA-registered exchange. Popular choices are bitFlyer, Coincheck, bitbank, GMO Coin, SBI VC Trade and Rakuten Wallet. Check the FSA register before you sign up, and compare fees on our exchanges page.
  2. Verify your identity online. You'll need your My Number card or a driver's licence and a smartphone for the eKYC check. You must live in Japan.
  3. Deposit yen. Start small, for example ¥1,000.
  4. Choose "exchange" or "sales office". The simple sales screen (販売所) is easy, but its spread can be several percent. The order book (取引所) is usually much cheaper.
  5. Keep your trade history. You'll need it for the tax return (確定申告).

Scams to avoid in Japan

The National Police Agency and the FSA keep warning about "investment" scams that start on social media or dating apps, where a friendly contact pushes you onto a trading site that is not registered. Rules of thumb: check every platform against the FSA register, never send coins to a "teacher" or a LINE group, and treat any promise of a fixed monthly return as a scam. Japan also learned from exchange failures: Mt. Gox in 2014, Coincheck's hack in 2018 and DMM Bitcoin's 2024 loss of BTC worth about ¥48 billion, after which DMM wound up its exchange. Don't keep more on one exchange than you need.

Taxes in 2026

  • Type of income: gains are normally miscellaneous income (雑所得) and are added to your other income, so the rate depends on your total income.
  • Rate: national income tax of 5–45% plus the 2.1% reconstruction surtax, and 10% resident tax, so up to about 55%.
  • What triggers tax: selling for yen, swapping one crypto for another, and paying for goods or services with crypto. Simply holding is not taxed.
  • Losses: crypto losses can't reduce your salary or other income, and they can't be carried forward.
  • The ¥200,000 rule: an employee paid by one employer usually doesn't have to file a national tax return if other income is ¥200,000 or less, but that income still has to be declared for resident tax.

What is changing: the tax reform passed in March 2026 depends on the new law coming into force. It is expected to move crypto to separate taxation at about 20%, with a three-year loss carry-forward, from January 1, 2028. Until it starts, the current rules apply.

Where to keep your Bitcoin

Japanese exchanges must keep most customer coins in offline (cold) wallets, so for small regular buys an exchange is a reasonable place. As the amount grows, many people move coins to their own wallet, where only they hold the keys. Write the recovery phrase (12–24 words) on paper, never photograph it and never type it into a website.

What it means if you buy regularly

You don't need to pick the perfect moment. Many people buy a fixed amount every week or month (積立), an approach called DCA, and several Japanese exchanges offer automatic monthly purchases. Someone who put $10 a day into Bitcoin for 5 years invested $18,260 in total, and it's worth $25,154 today (+37.8%). But Bitcoin has fallen more than 70% from its peak in the past. Only invest money you can leave alone for years. Try your own numbers in the Bitcoin calculator, compare with Japanese stocks or see current sign-up bonuses, such as bitFlyer's invite programme.

FAQ

Can I use Binance in Japan?

Not the global site. Residents use Binance Japan, which is a separate FSA-registered exchange with its own coin list.

Which exchange is best for beginners in Japan?

All registered exchanges follow the same FSA rules. Compare the spread on the sales screen, the fees for deposits and withdrawals, and whether the exchange has an order book for BTC.

Do I need to file a tax return for crypto?

If your crypto and other side income is over ¥200,000 and you are a salaried worker, yes. Below that you still declare it for resident tax.

When will Japan's 20% crypto tax start?

It is expected from January 1, 2028, once the amended law is in force. The exact date can still change.
