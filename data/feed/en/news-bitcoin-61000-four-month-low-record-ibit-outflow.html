Bitcoin slid to about $61,270 early on Friday, November 20, its weakest level since early July. The drop came after US spot bitcoin ETFs reported $582 million of net outflows for Thursday, including the biggest one-day withdrawal ever from BlackRock's iShares Bitcoin Trust (IBIT).

What happened

  • Down about 11% in a week. Bitcoin traded near $61,500 in the Asian morning, roughly 2.9% lower over 24 hours. Ether fell to around $1,730.
  • Record IBIT outflow. Farside data show IBIT lost $541 million on November 19. Spot bitcoin ETFs have now seen about $3.5 billion leave in November, with outflows on 12 of 14 trading days.
  • Leverage flushed again. Around $700 million of crypto positions were liquidated in 24 hours, per CoinGlass, most of them bets on higher prices.
  • A risk-off backdrop. The S&P 500 fell 2% on Thursday for a sixth straight loss, Anthropic's first trading day did not lift tech, and markets still expect the Fed to raise rates in December. The Fear & Greed Index sat at 12 out of 100.

Analysts at Bitfinex flagged the July low near $57,800 as the next level traders are watching if selling continues.

What it means if you buy regularly

For someone buying a fixed amount every week or month, a falling price means each purchase buys more bitcoin, but it does not mean the bottom is in.
