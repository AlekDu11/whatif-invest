WLFI, the token of the Trump family–linked crypto project World Liberty Financial, crashed about 40% in a few hours early on Saturday, November 28. The trigger was an on-chain sale: a reserve wallet linked to the project sold its ether at a heavy loss, and leveraged bets on WLFI were wiped out.

What happened

  • About 01:00 UTC — the sale. A wallet labelled as World Liberty's strategic reserve sold 12,121 ETH for about $20.06 million in USDC and USD1, at roughly $1,655 per ether. It had bought that ETH at an average of about $3,244, so the sale locked in a loss of about $19.3 million (KuCoin, TokenPost).
  • About 04:00–05:00 UTC — the crash. WLFI lost about 25% in a single hour on Coinbase and OKX; on OKX it briefly touched about $0.021. By 05:00 UTC it was down about 37–39% in 24 hours, near $0.025, and its market value had shrunk to about $1.1 billion (The Defiant, ChainCatcher).
  • Forced selling. More than $10 million of WLFI positions were liquidated within an hour, mostly bets on a rise.

Why is WLFI falling?

Traders read the reserve sale as a sign that insiders are raising cash, and in thin weekend trading the order book could not absorb the selling. We found no official statement from World Liberty Financial about the sale at the time of writing. The wider market did not help: bitcoin slipped below $60,000 and ether lost about 5% after a big options expiry, while November outflows from US bitcoin ETFs passed $5 billion.
