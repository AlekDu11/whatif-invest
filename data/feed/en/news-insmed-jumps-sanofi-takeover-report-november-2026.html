Insmed, the US biotech behind the lung-disease drug Brinsupri, had its best day in a long time on Thursday — not because of a trial result, but because of a takeover rumour. A report that French drugmaker Sanofi is studying a deal sent the stock up more than a fifth.

What happened

  • Thursday, November 19, US session. Bloomberg, citing people familiar with the matter, reported that Sanofi is working with advisers on a possible acquisition of Insmed.
  • The stock spiked. INSM jumped more than 18% within minutes and was briefly halted by a volatility circuit breaker. It closed at about $144.46, up roughly 20.6% from $119.80 the day before.
  • Size of a possible deal. Before the jump Insmed was valued at about $26.6 billion — close to a quarter of Sanofi's own market value of roughly €101 billion. A deal of that size would be one of the largest biotech takeovers in years.
  • Nothing confirmed. Both companies declined to comment. No price or premium has been reported, talks could still take weeks, and there is no guarantee of an agreement; other bidders could also appear.

Why it moved

This is a classic takeover-report jump: traders bid the shares up toward a price a buyer might pay. Insmed was an easy target for the story. Its shares had fallen about 40% over the previous 12 months, while Brinsupri — the first approved treatment for non-cystic fibrosis bronchiectasis, a chronic lung condition — gives a big pharma company a ready-made growth product. Big drugmakers are hunting for exactly that as patents on their older blockbusters run out.

The flip side: if the talks fall apart, a rumour-driven gain can disappear as quickly as it came.
