How do you check a memecoin before buying? Find the token by its contract address, then check six things: the security audit, liquidity, number of holders, how much the top 10 wallets own, how old it is and whether the creator can still change it. If one check is red, skip the coin — there are thousands of others.

Key takeaways

  • These checks do not make a memecoin safe. They only help you avoid the most obvious traps: tokens you cannot sell, fake copies and coins that a few wallets can crash.
  • Binance Wallet's Audit tab checks contract, trading and scam risks (for example a "honeypot" — a token you can buy but never sell). Binance itself says the audit is not foolproof.
  • On our memecoins page the quality filter is on by default: only tokens with $100K+ liquidity that are at least 1 day old.

This lesson continues How to connect Binance Wallet to a DEX. Memecoins are extremely risky — only use money you can afford to lose.

Step 1 — Get the right contract address

Every token has a contract address (its unique ID on the blockchain, a long string of letters and numbers). Anyone can create a token with the same name and logo, so the name means nothing.

  1. Copy the address from the project's official account or from a trusted data site.
  2. Paste it into the search in your wallet. Compare the first and last 4 characters.
  3. If you see several tokens with the same name, the others are copies.

Step 2 — Open the security audit

In the Binance app open the token in Wallet and tap the Audit tab (on the website: click the token, then Audit). On our memecoin token pages you see the same check as Binance security check: Low / Medium / High risk.

Look at three groups:

  • Contract risk — can the creator block selling, blacklist wallets, change the tax or change balances?
  • Trading risk — honeypot risk, unusual buy or sell tax (a fee taken on every trade), low liquidity, rug-pull risk.
  • Scam risk — fake token, known scam, spam.

Any red item = do not buy. "Low risk" still does not mean safe: the creator can still do things after you buy.

Step 3 — Check liquidity

Liquidity is the money in the trading pool that lets you sell. If it is tiny, even a small sale pushes the price down hard, and the creator can pull it out (a rug pull). On WhatIfStack, our default filter hides tokens with less than $100K in liquidity. Also check whether the pool's liquidity is locked or burned (removed for good), if the data site shows it.

Step 4 — Count holders and the top-10 share

On the token page look at Holders (how many wallets own it) and Top-10 own (what share of all coins the 10 largest wallets hold). A few hundred holders and a big top-10 share mean a handful of people can sell and crash the price. Be careful: the pool and burn addresses also appear in the top 10 — that part is normal.

Step 5 — Check the age

Most scam tokens live for hours or days. A token that is less than a day old has no history at all. Our filter on /memes/ shows only tokens at least 1 day old. Older is not safe — just less unknown.

Step 6 — On Solana: can the creator still change the token?

On Solana two settings matter:

  • Mint authority — the right to create new tokens. If it is still on, the creator can print more and your share shrinks.
  • Freeze authority — the right to freeze a wallet. If it is still on, the creator could stop you from moving or selling.

The safer state is "revoked" or "disabled" (nobody holds it). You can see both on the token page of a block explorer such as Solscan.

⚠️ Watch out

  • A fake copy with the same name. Always buy by contract address, never by name or logo.
  • A honeypot. You can buy, the chart goes up, but nobody can sell. A history with buys but no sells is a red flag.
  • Trusting one green light. An audit, a big holder count or a famous name do not protect you if the price simply falls — most memecoins end near zero.

What it means if you buy regularly

Regular small buys work best with assets that are likely to still exist in five years. Most memecoins are not. If you still want to try one, keep it to a tiny, fixed amount, and track it next to the rest of your money in your portfolio.
