Wall Street ended a brutal week on Friday with one of its worst days in years. The S&P 500 and the Dow Jones are now in a correction — at least 10% below their recent record highs — as the sell-off in AI stocks spread across the market.

What happened

  • Friday's close. The S&P 500 fell 3.7% to 6,910, the Dow lost about 950 points (1.9%) to 48,147, and the Nasdaq dropped 5.3% to 22,618 — its second-largest one-day point loss on record, which wiped out its gain for the year.
  • Long losing streak. It was the S&P 500's seventh losing session in a row, the longest run since February 2020. The Nasdaq had its biggest weekly percentage loss since March 2020.
  • Fear gauge up. The VIX volatility index posted its highest close since April 2025.
  • AI at the centre. Nvidia fell 14% on a reported SEC accounting probe, and newly listed Anthropic lost about 8.5% to $88.91.

What it means if you buy regularly

The S&P 500 ETF now trades at $689.81 −3.64%. Corrections of 10% or more have happened many times and are part of owning stocks; nobody knows whether this one deepens. Someone who put $10 a day into the S&P 500 for 5 years has invested $18,260, now worth $25,286 (+38.5%); the same plan in the Nasdaq-100 is worth $26,493 (+45.1%). History, not a forecast.
