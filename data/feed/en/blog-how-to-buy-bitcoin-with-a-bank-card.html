How do you buy Bitcoin with a bank card? In your verified exchange account you press Buy, choose BTC, enter an amount in your own currency, add your debit card, check the fee and the amount of Bitcoin you will get, and confirm. It takes about five minutes — but card purchases usually cost more than a bank transfer, so always look at the final number before you press the button.

Key takeaways

  • You do not need to buy a whole Bitcoin. You can buy a small piece, often from about $10 or the same in your currency. Bitcoin is at $60,059.40 −2.17% right now.
  • A card is the fastest way to pay, but often the most expensive: card fees of roughly 1–4% are common. Coinbase, for example, says its debit card purchases cost up to 3.99%.
  • Use a debit card, not a credit card. Many banks treat a crypto purchase on a credit card as a "cash advance", with an extra fee and interest from day one.

Before you start

You need an exchange account with 2FA (two-factor authentication — a second code from your phone) switched on and the ID check (KYC) finished. If you don't have it yet, do the previous lesson first: How to register on a crypto exchange. Which exchanges work in your country: see our exchanges page.

You also need a Visa or Mastercard debit card in your name. The name on the card must match the name on your exchange account — cards of other people are refused.

Step 1 — Open the "Buy" screen

  1. Log in to the exchange app (from the official App Store / Google Play app or your bookmark, never from an ad).
  2. Press Buy or Buy crypto. On some apps it is under Trade → Buy or a big "+" button.
  3. Choose Bitcoin (BTC). Check the ticker: it must be BTC, not a coin with a similar name.

Step 2 — Enter the amount in your currency

  1. Pick your currency (USD, EUR, UAH, INR…). Paying in your card's own currency avoids a second conversion fee from your bank.
  2. Type the amount, for example 20. The app shows how much BTC you will get, like 0.0002 BTC. Bitcoin has up to 8 digits after the dot, so small numbers are normal.
  3. Start small. Your first purchase is mostly a test that everything works.

Step 3 — Add your card

  1. Choose Credit/Debit card as the payment method and press Add card.
  2. Enter the card number, expiry date, CVV (the 3 digits on the back) and your billing address.
  3. Your bank may send a code by SMS or ask you to confirm in your bank app (this is called 3-D Secure). Confirm only if you started the payment just now.
  4. Some exchanges block a small amount on the card for a minute to check it, then return it.

Step 4 — Check the fee and the final amount

This is the most important step. Before confirming, look at the preview screen:

  • Price — the price per Bitcoin you are paying. It is often a bit higher than the price on the chart. This gap is called the spread.
  • Fee — the card fee, in money or in %.
  • You will receive — the exact BTC amount after all costs.

A quick check: divide what you pay by what you receive. If the result is much higher than the BTC price on our Bitcoin page, you are paying a lot in fees and spread. If the fee is high, the cheaper way is a bank transfer to your exchange balance first (it can take hours or a day), then buy BTC from that balance.

The preview price is usually fixed for a few seconds only. If the timer runs out, the app shows a new price.

Step 5 — Confirm and find your Bitcoin

  1. Press Buy / Confirm.
  2. Wait a few seconds. The app shows "Order completed".
  3. Open Wallet or Assets — your BTC is there. You can see your trade history in Orders or History.
  4. Add your purchase to your portfolio on WhatIfStack to follow its value in one place.

Some exchanges hold coins bought with a card for a few days before you can withdraw them to another wallet. This is a normal anti-fraud rule.

Step 6 — Decide what's next

Leave it on the exchange for now if the amount is small, and learn how to keep it safe. Bigger amounts are often moved to your own wallet — that is a later lesson. Do not rush to sell if the price drops in the first week: Bitcoin often moves 10% or more in days.

⚠️ Watch out

  • "Instant Bitcoin" sites and people. Never buy through a stranger, a Telegram "manager" or a site from an ad. Real exchanges never ask you to send card details in a chat.
  • Credit cards and loans. Buying crypto with borrowed money adds interest to a risky asset. Use only money you can afford to lose.
  • Hidden costs. A "0% fee" offer can hide a wide spread. Always compare the "You will receive" amount, not the advertised fee.

What it means if you buy regularly

You don't have to find the "right day". Many beginners set a small amount every week or month. For example, $10 a day in Bitcoin for 5 years ($18,260 in total) became $25,377 (+39.0%). Bitcoin's change over the last 12 months: −33.6%. Past results, not a forecast — the road included drops of more than 50%. If you buy regularly, card fees add up, so a bank-transfer balance usually works out cheaper.
