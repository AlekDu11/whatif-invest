Ether dropped below $1,800 on Thursday, about 64% under its August 2025 record. The selling is coming from several directions at once: US spot ether ETFs keep losing money, and companies that once bought ETH for their balance sheets are now selling some of it.

What happened

  • Seven weeks of ETF outflows. US spot ether funds have lost about $2.4 billion in seven straight weeks. Wednesday, November 18, was the third red day in a row ($39.8 million), and the last five sessions added up to about $145.5 million.
  • CoinShares sold all of its ether. The asset manager confirmed it sold its entire ETH treasury for $16.6 million and half of its SOL for $2.7 million to help pay for a share buyback of up to $40 million.
  • Leverage near the line. BTCS, which borrows against its ether on Aave, has sold ETH below cost to manage that debt; an analyst estimate puts its liquidation level near $1,717.
  • Old wallets wake up. A 1inch team fund moved about 8,978 ETH ($16.2 million) after a year of silence. It bought in early 2025 at an average of about $2,590, so it sits roughly 30% under water.

The numbers right now

Ether trades at $1,756.69, against an all-time high of $4,946.05 from August 24, 2025. The daily RSI dropped to 26, its lowest since June. Compare every top coin's drawdown on our from-ATH page.

What it means if you buy regularly

Forced and planned selling by big holders can push prices lower than fundamentals alone would. A regular buyer does not need to time it: $10 of ETH a day for 5 years is $18,260 invested, now worth $14,642 (−19.8%), versus $26,166 (+43.3%) for the same plan in bitcoin.
