Update November 27, 18:30 UTC: Stocks closed higher in the short Black Friday session: the Nasdaq Composite rose 2.3% (+543 points), the S&P 500 1.6% and the Dow 1.2% (+594 points), per TipRanks. That caps a week of roughly +5% for the S&P 500 and the Dow and about +6% for the Nasdaq — the Dow's best week in about 18 months. Memory-chip makers led: SanDisk was up about 13% and Micron about 6–7% during the session, with Broadcom and AMD up 2–3%. Nvidia missed the rally: after a premarket gain of more than 3% it turned lower and traded 1–2% down in the morning, still about 11% below Monday's close, as a Financial Times report on halted chip exports to Southeast Asia and S&P's negative credit watch weighed (Yahoo Finance, StockTitan). Anthropic rose about 10%, back above its $83 IPO price.

The US stock market is set to open higher on Black Friday, November 27, because chip and AI stocks are bouncing back after weeks of selling: Nasdaq-100 futures were up about 2% and S&P 500 futures about 1.5–1.7% before the open, with Nvidia up nearly 4% and Micron about 6% in premarket trading (Yahoo Finance).

What happened

  • Futures (around 10:40–12:00 UTC). Nasdaq-100 futures gained 2.1–2.4%, S&P 500 futures 1.5–1.7% and Dow futures 1.2–1.3%, according to TipRanks and Yahoo Finance. The VIX "fear index" eased to about 25.6.
  • Short day. Markets were closed on Thursday for Thanksgiving. Stocks trade from 14:30 to 18:00 UTC (9:30 a.m.–1 p.m. ET), bonds until 19:00 UTC, and volume is expected to be thin.
  • Data and yields. US third-quarter GDP growth was revised up to 4.3% a year (from 3.4%), and the 10-year Treasury yield slipped to about 4.85%.
  • Asia led the way. China's CSI 300 jumped 4.2% after a state pledge to support the market, and the Nikkei added about 2.1%.

Why are stocks up today?

There was no single new catalyst in the US. Reports describe a relief bounce: worries about whether AI spending will pay off had sent chip stocks sharply lower, and investors are buying some of the biggest losers back. China's support pledge lifted Asian chipmakers overnight, and early Black Friday numbers looked solid (the NRF expects holiday sales above $1 trillion). Thin holiday trading can exaggerate moves in both directions.
