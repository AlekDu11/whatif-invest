Prediction market Kalshi lost a key appeal on November 17: the US Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit ruled 2–1 that Maryland may apply its sports betting laws to Kalshi's sports event contracts. The decision clashes with an earlier Third Circuit ruling in Kalshi's favour, making a Supreme Court showdown more likely.

What happened

  • A split panel. The court upheld an August district court decision that had refused to block Maryland. The two judges in the majority gave different reasons, and the third judge dissented, arguing the contracts are swaps that only federal regulators oversee.
  • Maryland can act now. The state had agreed not to enforce its gambling rules until the appeal was decided; its attorney general said Kalshi must meet the same consumer standards as any sportsbook.
  • Courts disagree. The Third Circuit sided with Kalshi in a New Jersey case, and New Jersey has asked the Supreme Court to review it; Kalshi's reply is due December 7.
  • Kalshi's response. The company called the ruling splintered and may seek a rehearing or appeal.

What it means for users

Prediction markets are bets on events, not investments that grow over time, and in several US states access to sports contracts is already being cut off by location.
