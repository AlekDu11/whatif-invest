How do you register on a crypto exchange? You choose an exchange that is allowed in your country, sign up with your email or phone, protect the account with a strong password and two-factor authentication (2FA), and then pass an ID check (KYC). It usually takes 10–30 minutes, and you should never pay anyone to "help" you.

Key takeaways

  • A crypto exchange is a website and app where you swap normal money (dollars, euros, hryvnias…) for crypto and back. First check that it is licensed or registered in your country — our exchanges page is a good starting point.
  • Turn on 2FA (a second code from an app on your phone) before you deposit any money. A password alone is not enough.
  • The ID check (KYC, "know your customer") is normal: licensed exchanges must check who you are by law. Only do it inside the official app or website.

Before you start

You need: a smartphone, an email address that only you can open, an ID document (passport, ID card or driving licence) and 10–30 minutes in a well-lit place for a selfie.

Step 1 — Choose an exchange that works in your country

Not every exchange is allowed everywhere. Open our exchanges page and look for one that:

  • accepts customers from your country and your currency;
  • is licensed or registered with your country's financial regulator (search "<exchange name> licence <your country>");
  • has been running for years and is large, not a brand-new site from an ad.

In April 2024 the US FBI warned people to avoid crypto services that are not registered and skip ID checks: if such a service is shut down by the authorities, customers can lose access to their money.

Step 2 — Download the app or open the website (the real one)

  1. Install the app only from the official App Store or Google Play, or type the website address yourself.
  2. Do not click on exchange links from ads, Telegram groups or messages — fake copies of exchange sites are a common trick.
  3. Check the developer name in the app store and the address in the browser bar letter by letter.

Step 3 — Sign up with email and a strong password

  1. Press Register (or Sign up).
  2. Enter your email (or phone number). The exchange sends you a code — type it in to confirm.
  3. Create a unique password: at least 12 characters, letters, numbers and a symbol, and not the same as your email password. A password manager helps.
  4. Skip the "referral code" field if a stranger asks you to enter theirs for a "bonus" — it is optional.

Step 4 — Turn on 2FA (two-factor authentication)

2FA means that to log in or withdraw you need your password and a 6-digit code that changes every 30 seconds.

  1. Install an authenticator app (Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator or similar).
  2. In the exchange go to Account → Security → Authenticator app and scan the QR code.
  3. Write down the backup key on paper and keep it safe. If you lose your phone, this key restores the codes.
  4. If possible, choose the authenticator app or a passkey instead of SMS — SMS codes can be stolen by swapping your SIM card.

Step 5 — Pass the ID check (KYC)

  1. Open Verification (or Identity verification) in the app.
  2. Pick your country, enter your name and date of birth exactly as in your document.
  3. Take a photo of the document (both sides if it is a card) — no glare, all corners visible.
  4. Take a selfie or a short face video when the app asks.
  5. Wait for approval. It is often a few minutes, sometimes up to a few days. Some exchanges also ask for proof of address (a bank statement or utility bill).

After approval you can deposit money with a card or bank transfer — that is the next lesson.

⚠️ Watch out

  • Fake "support" and helpers. Nobody from a real exchange will message you first, ask for your password, 2FA code or a screen share, or ask you to "verify" on another site.
  • Phishing sites. A copy of the exchange site steals your login. Use a bookmark or the app.
  • Selling or lending your account. Never let someone else pass KYC in your name or "rent" your verified account — it can be used for crime, and you are responsible.

What it means if you buy regularly

Once your account is verified, you can buy a small fixed amount every week or month instead of guessing the right day. For example, $10 a day in Bitcoin for 5 years ($18,260 in total) became $25,684 (+40.7%). Past results, not a forecast.
