The AI sell-off is no longer an American story. On Friday, 20 November, Taiwan's main index, the Taiex, was down about 6.6%, South Korea's Kospi about 5.2% and Japan's Nikkei 225 about 4.9%, after the S&P 500 fell 2% on Thursday for a sixth losing day in a row. Taiwan and Korea are home to TSMC, Samsung and SK Hynix, the companies that actually make the chips behind the AI boom, so when AI stocks fall, these two markets fall hardest.

Key takeaways

  • Asia on Friday: Taiex about −6.6%, Kospi about −5.2% (Korea's exchange paused program trading), Nikkei about −4.9%, with SoftBank the worst performer in Tokyo.
  • New US export rules made TSMC and Samsung pause some chip shipments to Chinese customers while they wait for licences.
  • On our data TSMC (TSM) is 26% below its best monthly close, the chip ETF SOXX 35% and Micron 40%. Yet $10 a day in TSMC for 5 years is worth $44,395 (+143%) on $18,260 invested.

What happened in Asia

On Thursday in New York the S&P 500 lost about 2%, the Dow closed below 50,000 for the first time since June and the VIX "fear gauge" jumped about 16% to above 28. Overnight, the selling moved east. In Seoul, the Korea Exchange activated a "sidecar", a short automatic pause in program trading that kicks in on sharp falls. In Tokyo, SoftBank, one of the biggest investors in OpenAI and AI chip projects, led the losses.

Europe had already slipped on Thursday: the DAX and FTSE 100 lost about 1%. US stock futures pointed lower again on Friday.

Why the sell-off spread

1. New US chip export rules. On 16 November the US Commerce Department released a rule on remote access to advanced chips. One effect, reported by the Financial Times and Reuters, is that TSMC must stop taking orders for chips made on 16-nanometre or finer processes from covered Chinese designers unless they hold a US export licence. Samsung and TSMC also paused some shipments to Chinese customers, including smartphone makers and AI chip units, while licences are processed. An industry source said consumer-chip licences will likely be approved, but delays are possible.

2. A hawkish Fed. Minutes of the Federal Reserve's last meeting showed nearly all officials expect a 0.25-point rate hike in December, and markets price it at about 96%. The 10-year Treasury yield sits around 5%. When safe bonds pay that much, investors pay less for growth stocks whose profits lie far in the future.

3. AI stocks were already wobbling. This week Nvidia beat its own forecasts and still fell, and Anthropic's record IPO on Thursday drew money towards one new name while the rest of the sector sold off. In Korea, Samsung also fell on the China shipment news.

Not everything was red: Alibaba rose after its quarterly profit more than tripled, and its CEO said its next AI models are being trained on its own chips. That is a reminder that export rules can also push Chinese companies to build their own hardware, which is a long-term risk for US and Taiwanese chipmakers.

The two sides of the debate

Why some think the fall is overdone. Demand for AI chips has not disappeared: Nvidia just beat its forecasts, Alibaba's profit jumped, and many of the paused shipments are consumer chips such as smartphone processors, where an industry source expects licences to be approved. Big one-day falls in Asia often come from forced selling and automatic trading, not from new information about profits.

Why others think it can go further. Chip stocks were priced for years of fast growth. Higher interest rates, tighter trade rules and China building its own chips all chip away at that story at once. If the Fed hikes in December and yields stay above 5%, high-valuation stocks may stay under pressure.

Both can be true: AI can keep growing as a technology while AI stocks go through a long, painful correction.

FAQ

Why did Taiwan and Korea fall more than the US? Their markets are dominated by chipmakers. TSMC alone is a large part of the Taiex, and Samsung and SK Hynix are the biggest weights in the Kospi.

What is the new US chip rule? A Commerce Department rule announced on 16 November that tightens how advanced chips reach China, including through remote access. It made TSMC and Samsung pause some shipments until customers get licences.

Is a chip ETF safer than one stock? It spreads risk across many companies, but SMH and SOXX are still about a third below their best months. A broad index fund like SPY has fallen far less.
