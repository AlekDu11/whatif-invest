AI stocks enter December after their wildest month in years, and four scheduled dates will decide much of what happens next: Jensen Huang's closed-door interviews in Congress on 1 December, the Fed's rate decision and Broadcom's results on the same day, 9 December, Oracle's results in mid-December and Micron's on 17 December. None of them tells you where prices go, but each one answers a question the market is asking right now.

Key takeaways

  • On 25 November the Nasdaq fell as much as 4.6% during the day before closing only 0.6% lower; Nvidia lost 3.9% after S&P Global Ratings warned it could cut Nvidia's credit rating over governance concerns.
  • 9 December is the busiest day: the Fed decides at 2:00 p.m. New York time (markets price a quarter-point hike to 4.00–4.25%), and Broadcom reports after the close.
  • Micron reports on 17 December after guiding for record quarterly revenue of about $61.5 billion. Regular buyers do not need to time any of this: $10 a day in Nvidia for five years is worth $60,837 (+233%).

Why December matters more than usual

November was a stress test for the AI trade. Nvidia's chief executive resigned, the board brought him back on 23 November and the stock had its biggest one-day gain on record, then gave part of it back. On Wednesday, 25 November, the S&P 500 dropped as much as 3.2% intraday and recovered to close 0.3% lower, AP reported. The reasons were familiar: doubts that AI will earn back its data-centre bills, calls to slow AI down over safety, new tariff plans and a 10-year Treasury yield near 4.86%.

On Thursday, with US markets closed for Thanksgiving, Asian AI names bounced: SoftBank rose about 7–9% and memory maker Kioxia about 12%. That back-and-forth is why the next scheduled events matter. Each one is a chance for real numbers to replace fear or hope.

1 December: Huang in Congress

Huang is due for transcribed, closed-door interviews with three House committees (Foreign Affairs, Intelligence and the China select committee) on 1 December, with Nvidia's general counsel Tim Teter the next day, Axios reported. Lawmakers want to know whether Nvidia chips ended up with China's military or intelligence services and how well the company guarded its technology, including chips that were smuggled.

Why it matters: the hearings are not about sales, but they touch two things investors price in: export rules for China and how Nvidia is run. S&P Global's warning shows governance is now part of the story. The interviews are closed, so news will come through leaks and statements, not live video.

9 December: the Fed and Broadcom on the same day

The Federal Reserve meets on 8–9 December. Its range is 3.75–4.00% after a hike in September, and futures treat another quarter-point increase as almost certain. The surprise would be the message about 2027: more hikes would make future AI profits worth less today, a pause signal would help.

A few hours later Broadcom reports its fiscal fourth quarter and gives its outlook. Broadcom designs custom AI chips for Google, Meta and others and sells the networking gear that links data-centre GPUs together, so its forecast is one of the cleanest readings of whether Big Tech keeps spending. Its chart: −24.8% over 12 months, record $445.25 (May 2026).

Mid-December: Oracle

Oracle's fiscal second-quarter results are expected in mid-December, but the company has not confirmed the date yet. Oracle has become a test for debt-funded AI building: its cloud-infrastructure revenue grew at triple-digit rates in the last quarter, while it borrows heavily to build data centres for OpenAI and others. Watch two numbers: cloud growth and how much it spends (capex).

17 December: Micron and the memory boom

Micron reports after the US close on 17 December. In October it guided for revenue of about $61.5 billion, give or take $1.5 billion, well above analysts' estimates at the time, on demand for high-bandwidth memory used in AI servers. Memory is the most cyclical part of the chip business: when prices peak, profits can fall fast, so the outlook for 2027 will matter more than the quarter itself.

Both sides of the debate

The optimistic case: demand is still visible in the numbers. Micron's guidance and Broadcom's custom-chip orders are hard data, not stories, and the big buyers have not cut their capex plans.

The cautious case: high rates, possible regulation after the midterms, China export rules and a governance cloud over the biggest AI company arrive in the same month. Good results did not stop Nvidia from falling in November.

What it means if you buy regularly

DCA (dollar-cost averaging) means buying a fixed amount on a schedule, whatever the price. Event days like these are where it helps most: you do not have to guess whether Broadcom beats or what the Fed says, because the plan buys either way. If prices drop after a bad report, the same $10 buys more shares.

Over five years, $10 a day would have grown to $52,974 in Broadcom (+190%), $114,503 in Micron (+527%) and $26,229 in an S&P 500 fund (+43.6%), on $18,260 invested. Single chip stocks swing much harder than a broad fund, in both directions. Past results are not a forecast.

FAQ

Are the Huang interviews public? No. They are transcribed closed-door sessions; what is said becomes known through statements and reports.

Will the Fed raise rates on 9 December? Futures price a quarter-point hike as almost certain, but nothing is final until 2:00 p.m. New York time that day.

When exactly does Oracle report? Oracle usually reports its second quarter in mid-December and announces the date a few weeks ahead; check our earnings calendar.
