The thief behind the October 29 Binance hack — the largest crypto theft on record, at about $6.3 billion — is moving money again, and in much bigger size. On November 19, wallets linked to the attack sent about 137,602 ETH, worth roughly $248 million, to separate addresses, according to on-chain tracker Lookonchain. That is about four times the $63 million transfer we reported two days ago.

What happened

  • Large round chunks. The ether left in several transactions, a number of them for 12,000 ETH each.
  • Laundering has been going on for weeks. Since early November, parts of the loot have passed through decentralized exchanges, cross-chain swaps such as THORChain, Tornado Cash (about 11,000 ETH since November 8) and bitcoin coinjoins (about 700 BTC since November 1).
  • Most of it is still out there. Blockchain analytics firm Elliptic counts about $2.85 billion frozen or intercepted and estimated about $2.56 billion still sitting in theft-linked wallets as of November 17, spread across roughly 27,000 wallets on at least 16 blockchains.
  • Who is behind it. The FBI attributed the attack to North Korea on October 31. Binance has said it covered user losses from its own funds.

What it means for ordinary holders

If the stolen ether is eventually sold on the market, it adds supply at a time when ether is already weak. Ether trades at $1,619.51, far below its all-time high of $4,946 from August 2025 — see every coin's distance from its peak on our from-ATH page.
