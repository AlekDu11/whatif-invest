Why buy a little every month? Because you do not have to guess the "right moment". You invest the same amount on the same day every week or month, whatever the price — this is called DCA (dollar-cost averaging). It keeps emotions out and lets you start with small money.

Key takeaways

  • DCA = the same amount, at regular intervals, no matter if the price is up or down.
  • When the price is low, your money buys more; when it is high, it buys less. Your average price evens out.
  • DCA lowers the risk of buying everything at a bad moment, but it does not guarantee a profit. Over long periods, investing a lump sum at once has often earned more.

What DCA means, in plain words

The US regulator's investor site (Investor.gov) describes DCA as investing equal amounts at regular intervals, regardless of the market's ups and downs.

A simple example. You buy 50 of a fund every month:

  • Month 1: price 10 → you get 5 units.
  • Month 2: price 5 (it fell!) → you get 10 units.
  • Month 3: price 10 again → you get 5 units.

You spent 150 and own 20 units. Your average price is 7.50, even though the price was 10 for two of the three months. The month when the price fell helped you, because the same money bought more.

Why it works well for beginners

  1. No timing. Nobody knows tomorrow's price. With DCA you do not need to.
  2. Less emotion. FINRA (the US broker regulator) notes that DCA can reduce emotional buying and selling. A fixed plan stops you from panic-selling after a fall or buying everything after a big rise.
  3. Small amounts. You can start with 10 a week. It becomes a habit, like a subscription.
  4. A falling price is less scary. If the price drops, your next buy simply gets more units.

Its limits — be honest with yourself

  • No guarantee. If an asset keeps falling for years, DCA keeps buying a falling asset. You can still lose money.
  • A lump sum often wins. FINRA says DCA often gives lower returns than investing everything at once, especially over long periods, because markets have risen more often than they have fallen. DCA trades some return for a smoother ride.
  • Fees add up. If your app charges a fixed fee per purchase, many small buys can be expensive. Check the fee before you choose daily or weekly buys.

How to start: step by step

Step 1 — Pick an amount you will not miss. Look at your monthly budget after bills and your emergency fund (money kept for surprises). Choose an amount you could keep investing even in a bad month, for example 20 a week or 50 a month.

Step 2 — Pick the day and how often. Weekly or monthly is enough. Many people choose the day after payday, so the money goes out before they spend it.

Step 3 — Choose what to buy, simply. Beginners often pick a broad index fund (one fund holding hundreds of companies), gold, or a small share in bitcoin. Avoid "hot" coins and single stocks for your main plan. Compare assets side by side on our compare page.

Step 4 — Turn on automatic buying. Most brokers and many crypto exchanges have a "recurring buy" or "savings plan" button. You choose the asset, the amount and the day; the app buys for you. Find a provider that works in your country on our exchanges page.

Step 5 — Track it, but do not touch it. Add each purchase to your free portfolio. Check it once a month, not every hour. Stick to the plan for years, not weeks.

⚠️ Watch out

  • Stopping after the first fall. The months when prices are low are the ones where DCA buys the most units. Quitting then breaks the whole idea.
  • "Auto-invest" schemes that promise a fixed return. A real recurring buy just buys an asset at the market price. A promised 2% a week is a scam.
  • Using money you need. DCA is for money you will not need for 5+ years. Keep your emergency fund separate.
