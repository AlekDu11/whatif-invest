Do memecoins launched by celebrities and politicians make their buyers rich? For most buyers, no. From the wave of celebrity tokens in 2024 to TRUMP, LIBRA and Kanye West's YZY in 2025, the pattern repeated: a famous name, a price spike in the first hours — and then a crash in which a small group of early wallets took most of the profit.

Key takeaways

  • About a month after more than 30 celebrity tokens launched on Solana in June 2024, half of them were down more than 99% (analyst count reported by Cointelegraph, July 2024).
  • In February 2025 the LIBRA token, promoted by Argentina's president, rose to over $4.5 billion in market value and crashed about 95% within hours; 8 wallets linked to the team sold about $107 million.
  • A famous name does not protect you. In the YZY launch in August 2025, about 74% of traders lost money while 11 wallets took nearly 30% of all profits (Bubblemaps).

What is a celebrity memecoin?

It is a memecoin (a joke-style token with no business behind it — see what memecoins are) that a famous person launches or promotes on social media. Fans buy because they know the name. But the token gives you no share of the person's money, music or company. Its price depends only on whether someone will buy it after you.

The story in dates and numbers

May–June 2024 — the celebrity wave. After Caitlyn Jenner launched a token in May 2024, rapper Iggy Azalea launched MOTHER, and singer Jason Derulo, 50 Cent's name and others appeared on new Solana tokens. More than 30 celebrity tokens launched in June 2024 alone. By late July 2024 every one of them had lost at least 73%, half were down more than 99%, and only a handful kept more than a quarter of their value (Cointelegraph). Derulo said he did not sell his tokens; the analytics firm Bubblemaps said its data showed he did.

4 December 2024 — HAWK. Haliey Welch, known online as the "Hawk Tuah girl", launched the HAWK token. Within hours of the launch its price fell more than 90%. Buyers accused insiders of selling early, and a group of investors later filed a lawsuit against the token's promoters. She said her team had not sold.

17–19 January 2025 — TRUMP and MELANIA. Three days before his inauguration, Donald Trump announced the TRUMP memecoin; MELANIA followed two days later. TRUMP's market value reached nearly $15 billion at its January 2025 high. By the end of June 2026, the analytics firm Nansen counted about 989,000 buyer wallets in a loss, together about $3.81 billion, while about 492,000 wallets were in profit (The Block, July 2026). Today TRUMP trades at $0.8310 −7.87% (−86.2% over 12 months), MELANIA at $0.0481 −8.57%. A separate lesson tells this story in detail.

14–15 February 2025 — LIBRA. Argentina's president Javier Milei posted a link to LIBRA, a token said to fund small businesses. Its market value shot above $4.5 billion, then fell about 95% within hours. On-chain trackers (people who follow wallet transactions in public blockchain data) found that 8 wallets tied to the team had sold about $107 million. Milei deleted the post and withdrew his support. Nansen later counted about $251 million in combined losses: more than 86% of wallets that sold with over $1,000 at stake lost money. Investigators also linked the LIBRA launch team to the people behind MELANIA (Decrypt).

August 2025 — YZY. Kanye West launched the YZY token. It fell almost 70% within hours of the launch. Bubblemaps counted 70,201 traders: 51,862 of them (73.8%) lost money, about $74.8 million in total. Of the 18,333 wallets in profit, 86% made less than $1,000 — while just 11 wallets took nearly 30% of all profits. Bubblemaps also said that Hayden Davis, the businessman behind the LIBRA launch, made about $12 million by sniping YZY.

Why the same thing happens again and again

  1. Insiders get the cheap tokens first. The team and "snipers" (bots that buy in the first seconds after launch) hold a big share before fans can even click "buy". Words like this are in our memecoin glossary.
  2. The famous post is the top. The moment millions of fans see the announcement is when the most buyers arrive — and when insiders can sell to them.
  3. No business, no floor. When the hype fades, nothing supports the price: no profits, no product, no dividends.
  4. The fame protects the creator, not you. Lawsuits were filed after several launches, but they take years, and buyers rarely get money back.

What beginners can learn from it

  • Check who holds the coins. Before any memecoin, look at the top-10 holders' share and the token's age — the lesson How to check a memecoin before buying shows how, and the safety light on our memecoins page helps.
  • Never buy in the first hours of a famous launch. That is when bots and insiders sell.
  • Only money you can lose completely. If you still want to try, set a small limit in advance and keep track of it in your portfolio.

⚠️ Watch out

  • Fake celebrity tokens. Scammers create tokens with a star's name and photo within minutes. Only trust a contract address published on the person's verified account — and even then, the risks above stay.
  • Hacked accounts. Several celebrity X accounts were hacked to promote fake tokens. A surprising post about a new coin is a red flag.
  • "It's backed by a president / a star." A famous name is marketing, not a guarantee. Nobody has to buy your tokens back.
