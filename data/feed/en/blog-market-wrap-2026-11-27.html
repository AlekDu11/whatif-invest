Friday, November 27 split the markets in two. In a short Black Friday session the S&P 500 rose 1.6% to 7,297.49 and the Nasdaq 2.3%, as chip stocks bounced back, while Bitcoin lost about 2.2% in 24 hours and dipped below $59,000 during the day (live now: $59,589.20 −3.40%). Gold and silver kept rebounding from their 14-month lows.

Key takeaways

  • S&P 500 +1.60%, Nasdaq +2.31%, Dow +1.18% on Friday; for the week +5.6%, +6.7% and +5.5% (AP).
  • 247 of the 300 largest companies in our data rose; Micron +7.6%, Anthropic +11.0%, SMIC in Shanghai +15.4%.
  • Only 8 of the 47 coins worth $1 billion or more rose; Bitcoin −2.2%, Ethereum −3.6%, World Liberty Financial −17.9%.
  • Crypto Fear & Greed is 10/100 today, 12 a day ago and 12 a week ago — "extreme fear".

The big picture: stocks rallied, crypto did not follow

Bitcoin failed to climb back above $62,000 on Friday morning and slipped under $59,000 in the afternoon, Coin-Turk reported. About $6.8 billion of Bitcoin options expired on Deribit at 08:00 UTC; the report notes there is no evidence the expiry alone caused the drop. In our data Bitcoin was near $60,090 early on Saturday and Ethereum near $1,668 (−3.6%). Total crypto market value was about $2.08 trillion (CoinGecko) and Bitcoin's share 57.8%.

Wall Street had a very different day. The S&P 500 closed at 7,297.49 (+114.71 points), the Dow at 50,774.94 (+591.99) and the Nasdaq Composite at 24,129.50 (+543.70); the small-cap Russell 2000 gained 1.7% (AP). The VIX "fear index" fell about 11% to 23.8. The S&P 500 ETF rose 1.6% and the Nasdaq-100 ETF 2.7% in our data (our brief).

Metals rose for a second day: gold +3.8% to about $3,669 an ounce and silver +9.1% to about $43.70 in our data (why gold is up). Brent crude was near $91 a barrel and US crude near $83 ahead of Sunday's OPEC+ meeting.

Biggest winners

Among the 300 largest companies:

  • Anthropic +11.0% to about $91.90, above its $83 IPO price — no company-specific news found; it rose with the rest of AI.
  • Micron +7.6%, Seagate +7.9%, Western Digital +7.2% and SK hynix +7.5% — memory and storage makers led the chip rebound as worries about AI spending eased.
  • SMIC +15.4% in Shanghai (+16.4% in Hong Kong) and Alibaba +7.0% — after Beijing pledged state buying to steady its stock market (brief).

In crypto, Bitway jumped +19.2% and Sky +9.0%, with no news found for either; JUST added 3.5%. Away from the big coins, FET rose about 12% on Friday (brief).

Biggest losers

  • World Liberty Financial −17.9% to about $0.034. Early on Saturday it fell another 28% within one hour, wiping out about $10.9 million of leveraged long bets (TokenPost). No cause has been reported.
  • Mantle −5.2%, MemeCore −3.9%, Venice Token −3.8% and Polkadot −3.6% — no coin-specific news found; they fell with the market.
  • Ethereum −3.6% was the biggest mover among the 20 largest coins.
  • In stocks, Vertex Pharmaceuticals −5.2% led the decliners, and Chinese energy and bank giants such as China Shenhua (−4.0%) and Sinopec (−3.4%) lagged; we found no specific news.

The news that moved markets

  1. Chips came back. The Philadelphia semiconductor index rose nearly 5% as fears about AI profits eased, Yahoo Finance reported; Nvidia, which jumped after Jensen Huang's return as CEO earlier in the week, ended only +0.8%.
  2. Strong US growth, higher yields. Third-quarter GDP growth was revised up to 4.3% a year from 3.4%; the 10-year Treasury yield rose to about 4.9%, keeping a December Fed rate hike in play.
  3. Beijing steps in. A State Council pledge to use the state-backed "national team" to steady prices sent China's chip index up a record 17.6%.
  4. Crypto funds keep bleeding. US spot Bitcoin ETFs lost $453 million on Wednesday, part of a six-session outflow streak of about $2.6 billion (Coin-Turk).
  5. Holiday shopping. The NRF expects US holiday sales above $1 trillion, up 4.2%.

What to watch today

  • Saturday, Nov 28: about 2.4 million FET tokens unlock for the SingularityNET migration. Crypto trades all weekend; stocks are closed.
  • Sunday, Nov 29: OPEC+ meets on oil output for 2027.
  • Monday, Nov 30: China's lower minimum margin ratio (80% instead of 100%) takes effect.
  • December 9: the Fed's rate decision.
