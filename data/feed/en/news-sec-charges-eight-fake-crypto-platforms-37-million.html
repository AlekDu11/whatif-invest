The US Securities and Exchange Commission said on Thursday, November 19, that it has charged eight Colorado-registered entities over a scheme that allegedly took more than $37 million from American investors. Victims believed they were buying real crypto; the SEC says the trading platforms were fake.

What happened

  • Ads, then chat groups. According to the SEC, social-media ads pulled people into group and private chats, likely run from overseas, that first handed out stock tips and later pushed members to send money to crypto "exchanges".
  • Four pairs, rotated. The scheme used four pairs of a supposed investment adviser plus a trading platform between August 2024 and May 2026, dropping each pair after a few months and starting a new one.
  • The names. Among the entities: Advisor WeChat Capital, IdeaCrypto Exchange, RealSimple Crypto Exchange and Essential Crypto Exchange.
  • What the SEC wants. Permanent injunctions, return of the money with interest, and civil penalties.

How to protect yourself

The red flags are classic: a stranger's "investment club" in a messenger, guaranteed returns, and a platform you have never heard of that shows big paper gains but makes withdrawals hard.
