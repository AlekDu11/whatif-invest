Crypto exchanges in the UK will no longer be allowed to act as market makers on their own platforms. Under final rules from the Financial Conduct Authority, an operator of a regulated crypto trading platform may not trade its own capital on its own order book from October 25, 2027, when the UK's new crypto regime takes effect.

What happened

  • The ban. The FCA rule (CRYPTO 6.6.1R) stops platform operators from quoting buy and sell prices with their own money on their venue. The reason is a conflict of interest: the exchange sees every client order first.
  • Outside firms welcome. A Treasury instrument signed on November 18 (SI 2026/1219) lets independent firms make markets on these platforms, including in spot bitcoin, without a separate dealing licence.
  • Narrow exceptions. An operator may act as a matched principal with extra permission and no discretion over matching, and a separate affiliate may trade under the same access rules as everyone else. Exchanges must also identify and monitor their market makers and disclose any incentives they pay them.
  • Timeline. Existing firms need to apply for authorisation by February 28, 2027, to keep operating during the transition.

What it means for you

For UK users the change aims at fairer prices: the venue running the order book can no longer trade against them. It may also push big exchanges to spin off trading desks.
