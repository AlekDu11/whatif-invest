Wednesday, November 25 was a round trip. Hotter US inflation and new tariff plans pushed the Nasdaq down as much as 4.6% and the Dow more than 1,100 points, but buyers came back in the afternoon: the Dow closed +0.33%, the S&P 500 −0.25% and the Nasdaq −0.62%. Bitcoin dipped to about $57,300, then recovered to near $60,900 in our data, up 2.6% in 24 hours (live: $59,589.20 −3.40%).

Key takeaways

  • Dow +0.33% to 50,182.95; S&P 500 −0.25% to 7,182.78; Nasdaq −0.62% to 23,585.80 — after intraday drops of 3.2% (S&P) and 4.6% (Nasdaq).
  • October PCE inflation rose to 3.8% a year; core PCE 3.3% vs 3.2% expected. Markets now price a December Fed rate hike at about 98%.
  • 42 of the 47 coins worth $1 billion or more rose; Pieverse +20.6%, Litecoin +7.9%, Bitcoin +2.6%, Ethereum +2.2%.
  • The Crypto Fear & Greed Index is at 11/100, "extreme fear" — 13 a day ago and 10 a week ago.

The big picture: a scare, then a comeback

The morning was rough. After the PCE report at 13:30 UTC, chip stocks slid again and the Nasdaq-100 touched a level 20% below its October record — the usual definition of a bear market. Losses shrank late in the day after reports that US officials found no evidence of Chinese spying in the Jensen Huang affair, and money kept moving from tech into "old-economy" stocks: about three in four S&P 500 companies rose, and energy led. The 10-year Treasury yield eased to about 4.85%.

Gold rose about 1.9% to near $3,605 an ounce in our data, silver about 2.8%, and US crude about 0.7% to near $84.

Crypto followed stocks down and then up. Total crypto market value was about $2.11 trillion (CoinGecko) and Bitcoin's share was 57.9%. Among the 20 largest coins the biggest mover was Litecoin, +7.9%.

Biggest winners

  • HP Inc +11.5% — quarterly revenue of $16.0 billion and profit of $0.83 a share beat forecasts (our brief).
  • Charter Communications +8.4% and Axon +7.4% — part of the move into steadier names; we found no company-specific trigger.
  • Analog Devices +6.4% and Texas Instruments +4.6% — a partial rebound after Tuesday's 17% and 9% falls.
  • Deere +4.4% — earnings and sales fell less than feared.
  • In crypto: Pieverse +20.6%, Bitway +14.8%, MemeCore +11.4% and Venice Token +10.2% — we found no coin-specific news; smaller coins bounced harder with the market.

Biggest losers

  • Lam Research −5.7% and Micron −4.5% — chip-equipment and memory stocks led the sell-off again.
  • Oracle −5.1% to $103.88, close to its 52-week low of $99.34.
  • Nvidia −3.9% to $149.30 — it was down more than 9% at one point before the afternoon bounce.
  • In crypto only five $1B+ coins fell: Rain −3.1% and TRON −1.4% were the weakest.

The news that moved markets

  1. Inflation is still rising. PCE prices rose 3.8% in the year to October, the highest since 2023; core PCE hit 3.3%, a tenth above forecasts (brief).
  2. The economy is strong. Q3 GDP growth was revised up to a 4.3% annual rate, the fastest in three years, and weekly jobless claims were about 189,000 — another reason the Fed is expected to raise rates, not cut them.
  3. New tariffs. The US announced extra tariffs of up to 20% on China, the EU and 12 other partners, to take effect in January at the earliest after a comment period (brief).
  4. Bitcoin's dip and bounce. Bitcoin fell below $58,000 after the PCE data, with about $515 million of leveraged bets liquidated, then climbed back above $60,000 as stocks recovered (brief). US spot Bitcoin ETFs had six straight days of outflows through Tuesday.
  5. AI stocks under pressure. Anthropic briefly fell further below its $83 IPO price before closing at $82.79 (brief).

What to watch today

  • Thursday, Nov 26: US stock markets are closed for Thanksgiving; crypto trades as usual.
  • Friday, Nov 27: US stocks trade a half day, closing at 1:00 p.m. New York time.
  • Later: Salesforce reports on December 2; the Fed decides on rates on December 9.

What it means if you buy regularly

Wednesday showed how much can happen inside one day: a 4.6% drop in the Nasdaq shrank to 0.6% by the close. Someone who sold in the morning locked in the worst prices of the day. Someone who put $10 a day into Bitcoin over the last year ($3,650 in total) now has $3,026 (−17.1%). The same habit in Ethereum stands at −22.4%, in the S&P 500 at +1.23%, in gold at −15.1% and in Nvidia at −22.1%.
