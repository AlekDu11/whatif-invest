S&P Global Ratings placed Nvidia's AA credit rating on CreditWatch with negative implications on Wednesday, November 25, saying the chipmaker's board has not yet answered its questions about oversight of the chief executive. S&P expects to decide within 90 days and says a cut would most likely be a single notch, to AA−.

What happened

  • The warning. S&P flagged long-serving directors whose independence may be weakened, thin executive oversight and the lack of a succession plan ("key-man" risk), and lowered its management and governance assessment to neutral from positive.
  • The backdrop. Jensen Huang resigned on November 12 over a code-of-conduct breach; the board reinstated him on November 23. S&P said the return alone does not settle board-oversight concerns and also pointed to Nvidia's China exposure.
  • Moody's differs. Moody's kept Nvidia at Aa1 with a stable outlook, saying Huang's return reduces near-term uncertainty.
  • The stock. Nvidia closed Wednesday at $149.30, down 3.9%, in a broad AI sell-off, and edged up about 1.5% after hours. Huang is due to testify before the Senate Banking Committee on December 3.

What it means for regular buyers

A credit watch is about Nvidia's bonds, not a verdict on its chips: the company had about $53.5 billion in cash and securities against $32.4 billion of long-term debt at the end of October. But governance questions can keep the stock volatile.
