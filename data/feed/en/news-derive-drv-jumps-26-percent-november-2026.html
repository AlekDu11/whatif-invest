Derive, an on-chain options and perpetuals exchange, was one of the few green spots in a red crypto market on Saturday. Its DRV token climbed about 26% in 24 hours, while bitcoin sank to its lowest level since September 2024.

What happened

  • Saturday, November 21. By 10:37 UTC DRV was up about 20% near $0.30, with a 24-hour range of roughly $0.237 to $0.306, per CoinGecko data cited by CoinGabbar. Our data later showed about $0.31, up roughly 26%.
  • Volume. 24-hour trading volume reached about $40 million, more than double the figure seen in the morning.
  • Week. DRV is up about 17% over seven days, while bitcoin and ether each lost around 13%.

Why it moved

We found no Derive announcement or exchange listing behind Saturday's jump, and a CoinStats summary also reported no confirmed catalyst. Chart watchers noted that DRV broke out of a falling wedge it had traded in since mid-November. In the background: Derive launched options and perpetuals on Nvidia stock on November 16, it spends 50% of protocol fees on DRV buybacks since mid-October, and Citrini Research gave DRV a 10% weight in its October crypto report. None of these is new this week, so treat the move as flow-driven.

The numbers right now

DRV trades at $0.3133. Its all-time high was about $0.569 on October 9, 2026, so it is still roughly 45% below the top — see the from-ATH page. About 1.06 billion of the 1.5 billion maximum supply is in circulation, so roughly 29% is still to come.

What it means if you buy regularly

Someone who put $10 a day into DRV over the last year has invested $3,650 and now holds $14,414 (+295%). The same plan in bitcoin is worth $2,993. Small tokens can swing 20% in either direction in a day, which regular buying does not remove. History, not a forecast. Compare in the DCA calculator.
