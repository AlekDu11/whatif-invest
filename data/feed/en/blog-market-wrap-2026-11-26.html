Thursday, November 26 was a holiday for Wall Street but not for crypto. With US stock and bond markets closed for Thanksgiving, Bitcoin rose about 2.8% in 24 hours (CoinCodex, CryptoTicker), touched about $61,400–61,600 and was near $61,500 in our data early on Friday (live: $61,695.00 +1.26%). The bounce came mostly from traders closing bets against Bitcoin, not from new money: US spot Bitcoin ETFs lost another $453 million on Wednesday.

Key takeaways

  • Bitcoin +2.8% on Thursday after Wednesday's dip to about $57,300; Ethereum about +2% to near $1,690–1,730.
  • 36 of the 47 coins worth $1 billion or more rose in our data; Sky +10.0% and Hyperliquid +6.9% led, Rain −8.4% was the weakest.
  • US spot Bitcoin ETFs had a seventh straight day of outflows on Wednesday (−$453 million); November outflows are about $5.4 billion.
  • Crypto Fear & Greed is 12/100 today, 11 a day ago and 12 a week ago — still "extreme fear".

The big picture: crypto moved while Wall Street rested

In thin holiday trading, about $144 million of short positions on Bitcoin (bets on a fall) were forced closed over 24 hours, more than long positions, according to CoinGlass data cited by analyst Ted Pillows. Open interest rose to about $24.8 billion. Total crypto market value was about $2.13 trillion (CoinGecko) and Bitcoin's share 58.0%.

Stocks outside the US were calmer but positive: Europe's STOXX 600 rose about 1.6% and Japan's Nikkei 0.9%, while US stock futures were up 0.3–0.5%. Spot gold was little changed near $3,580–3,600 an ounce after Wednesday's dip to $3,446, its lowest since August 2025; silver gained about 1% to near $42. Brent crude was near $91–92.

The US S&P 500 did not trade, so its last close is still Wednesday's 7,182.78 (−0.25%); the Nov 25 wrap covers that session.

Biggest winners

Among the 20 largest coins the biggest mover was Hyperliquid, +6.9% to about $66.

  • Sky +10.0% — a partial rebound after its 26% crash on November 19 (our brief); no new news found.
  • Hyperliquid +6.9% — we found no confirmed trigger; derivatives tokens tend to gain when trading picks up.
  • Pieverse +6.3% — extends its run after doubling in a week (brief).
  • Bitway +5.3% and WhiteBIT Coin +5.1% — no coin-specific news found.
  • Chainlink +3.3% and Bitcoin Cash +3.8% among the older large coins.

Biggest losers

  • Rain −8.4% — a thin, volatile token that doubled in a day earlier this month (brief); no news behind Thursday's drop.
  • JUST −4.4% and Canton −3.2% — no clear reason found.
  • World Liberty Financial −1.4%, Gram −1.2% and Zcash −1.2% — small dips in a rising market.

The news that moved markets

  1. Short squeeze, not new buyers. Analysts linked the rise to forced closing of bets against Bitcoin in thin holiday trading, while ETF selling continued (brief).
  2. Rate fears linger. Wednesday's core PCE inflation of 3.3% left markets pricing an almost certain Fed rate hike on December 9; gold stayed under pressure for the same reason.
  3. Korea raised rates. The Bank of Korea lifted its key rate to 3.25%, its third hike of 2026 (brief).
  4. A big options expiry. About $6.5–7 billion of Bitcoin options expire on Deribit at 08:00 UTC on Friday (brief).

What to watch today

  • Friday, Nov 27: Bitcoin options expiry at 08:00 UTC; US stocks trade a half day and close at 1:00 p.m. New York time; spot ETF flows resume.
  • Sunday, Nov 29: OPEC+ meets on 2027 oil output.
  • December 9: the Fed's rate decision.

A holiday rally of 2.8% feels good, but it came after a 52% fall from Bitcoin's record, and one day says little. Someone who put $10 a day into Bitcoin over the last year ($3,650 in total) now has $3,128 (−14.3%). The same habit in Ethereum stands at −19.2%, in the S&P 500 at −0.25%, in gold at −14.4% and in Hyperliquid at −18.3%.
