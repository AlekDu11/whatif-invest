Friday, November 20 was the worst day of a very bad week. The Nasdaq Composite fell 5.3% and wiped out its gain for 2026, the S&P 500 lost 3.7% for a seventh straight loss and closed in a correction, and Nvidia sank 14%. Bitcoin fell below $60,000 for the first time since July and ended near $58,660 (live: $60,857.60 +1.18%), while gold dropped to a 14-month low.

Key takeaways

  • Nasdaq −5.28% to 22,618, S&P 500 −3.70% to 6,910, Dow −1.93% (about −950 points) to 48,147. Only consumer staples rose among S&P sectors.
  • Nvidia −14.0% after a Reuters report of an SEC accounting examination; Micron, AMD and Broadcom lost 12–14%.
  • Bitcoin −5.2% to $58,661 and Ethereum −6.8% to $1,620 in 24 hours; only 1 of 45 coins worth $1 billion or more rose.
  • Gold −4.0% to $3,539 and silver −7.3%. The Crypto Fear & Greed Index reads 12/100, the same as a day ago, after 13 a week ago.

The big picture: the AI trade unwinds

The selling was centred on AI. Technology was the worst S&P sector at about −7%, while the equal-weight S&P 500 fell only about 2% — a sign that the giants did most of the damage. The Nasdaq-100 fund QQQ lost 5.7% and is down 10.5% in seven days; SPY fell 3.6% (−8.6% in a week). For the S&P 500 it was the worst week since April 2025, for the Nasdaq the worst since March 2020. The VIX "fear gauge" jumped about 26% to near 36.

Bonds were the one place money ran to: the 10-year Treasury yield fell about 12 basis points to 4.92%. Of the 200 largest US companies on our list, 48 closed higher — mostly health care, telecoms and food and household brands.

Crypto followed stocks down. Total crypto market value slipped under $2.0 trillion (CoinGecko), with Bitcoin's share at 57.6%. Bitcoin is −13.7% in a week, Ethereum −16%. Gold and silver did not work as a hiding place either. Brent oil was little changed, near $90.

Biggest winners

  • Global Payments +9.9% — reports of buyout interest in the payments company.
  • HCA Healthcare +3.3% and UnitedHealth +2.8% — health care drew money moving out of tech; no single company news found.
  • AT&T +2.1% and Walmart +1.5% — classic "safe" names in a falling market.
  • Gap +11.4% (a smaller company) — profit beat expectations (our brief).
  • In crypto, only Venice Token +1.4% rose among $1B+ coins; Monero was flat. No clear reason.

Biggest losers

  • Nvidia −14.0% to $125.53, its lowest close since May 2025 — the SEC report on top of AI spending doubts (brief).
  • Arm −14.1%, Micron −13.9% and AMD −13.2% — the chip and memory sell-off deepened (brief).
  • Intuit −12.8% — a soft outlook alongside the Mailchimp sale.
  • SoftBank Group −14.5% in Tokyo — the AI sell-off spread across Asia.
  • Zcash −17.5%, the biggest drop among the 20 largest assets; it is about 55% below its $1,680 peak of two months ago. We found no specific cause beyond the market-wide selling.
  • Hyperliquid −9.5% and NEAR −9.8% — fell with the market, no single reason.

The news that moved markets

  1. Nvidia and the SEC. Reuters reported an SEC examination of Nvidia's accounting. It is a report, not a charge, but it hit a stock already down for weeks.
  2. AI spending doubts. Investors keep asking whether huge data-centre budgets will pay off; AI giants have lost hundreds of billions of dollars in value this week (brief).
  3. Anthropic gave back most of its debut gain. In its second session it fell 8.5% to $88.91, still above the $83 IPO price.
  4. Bitcoin broke $60,000. About $1.6 billion of crypto bets were liquidated in 24 hours, most of them bets on a rise (brief).
  5. Rates. Markets still price a December Fed hike at roughly 90%, and Fed governor Lisa Cook warned against waiting too long.

What to watch today and next week

  • Saturday: US stock markets are closed; crypto trades all weekend.
  • Monday, Nov 23: US flash PMI surveys for November, a 2-year Treasury auction and a speech by New York Fed President John Williams.
  • Thursday, Nov 26: US stock and bond markets closed for Thanksgiving; Friday, Nov 27, stocks close early.
