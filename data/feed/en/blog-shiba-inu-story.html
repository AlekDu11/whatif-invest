How did Shiba Inu start? An anonymous creator called "Ryoshi" launched SHIB in August 2020 as a cheap, playful rival to Dogecoin. A year later it was one of the most valuable memecoins in the world — and its huge supply, its 2021 rally and the fall after it are a perfect beginner's lesson.

Key takeaways

  • SHIB is a memecoin (a coin built around a joke or a picture, see the memecoin glossary) on Ethereum, created with 1 quadrillion tokens (1 followed by 15 zeros). That is why one coin costs a tiny fraction of a cent.
  • In October 2021 it rose hundreds of percent in weeks and set a record of about $0.000086 on 28 October 2021. Today it trades at $0.00000363 +4.61%, −95.8% from its record.
  • A low price per coin does not mean "cheap". What matters is the market value (price × number of coins): now $2.1 B.

This lesson continues the story of Dogecoin.

Chapter 1 — August 2020: a dog coin with a giant supply

Dogecoin already had a Shiba Inu dog as its logo. In August 2020 a person or group using the name Ryoshi made a new token with the same dog and called it the "Dogecoin killer". It was an ERC-20 token (a token that lives on the Ethereum network instead of its own blockchain).

Two choices made it famous:

  • A gigantic supply: 1,000,000,000,000,000 SHIB. With so many coins, the price per coin is tiny, and a few dollars buy millions of tokens. That feels exciting, but it changes nothing about the real value.
  • Half of all tokens were sent to Vitalik Buterin, the co-founder of Ethereum, without asking him. The idea was to show the creators were not keeping the supply for themselves.

Chapter 2 — May 2021: Vitalik burns most of it

In May 2021 memecoins were booming, and Buterin's "gift" was suddenly worth billions on paper. He did not sell it on the market:

  • On 13 May 2021 he donated more than 50 trillion SHIB — worth over $1 billion at the time — to the India COVID-Crypto Relief Fund.
  • Days later he sent about 410 trillion SHIB to a "dead" address that nobody can open. This is called a burn (tokens removed forever). Reports at the time valued them at about $7 billion, and he said he would burn 90% of what was left in his wallet too.

The price jumped about 40% on the news. Lesson: a big holder's decision can move a memecoin more than anything else.

Chapter 3 — October 2021: the mania

In October 2021 SHIB went up by around 900% in one month. Exchanges added it, people shared screenshots of tiny bets turning into fortunes, and petitions asked the app Robinhood to list it. On 28 October 2021 it reached its record of about $0.000086 and, for a short time, its total value passed Dogecoin's on CoinGecko.

Stories went viral: according to tweets reported by CryptoSlate, someone who had put $8,000 into SHIB in 2020 held coins worth billions at the peak. Stories like that are real but extremely rare — and they bring in many late buyers. In November 2021 the price lost roughly half of its value, and in the following years it stayed far below the record.

Chapter 4 — 2023–2025: building a "real" project, with bumps

The team tried to turn the meme into an ecosystem: the exchange ShibaSwap, the tokens BONE and LEASH, and Shibarium, its own network built on top of Ethereum.

  • 16 August 2023: Shibarium launched, but on day one more than 1,003 ETH (about $1.8 million) got stuck while being moved onto it. SHIB fell 9% in a day.
  • 12 September 2025: an attacker exploited Shibarium's bridge (the tool that moves coins between two networks) and drained about $4.1 million in ETH, SHIB and other tokens. The team rotated keys and planned refunds.

Even big, famous memecoins carry technical risks on top of price risk.

What beginners can learn from it

  1. Price per coin is a trap. "It only costs $0.00001, it can easily reach $1" sounds logical, but with 1 quadrillion coins (minus burns) that would make SHIB worth more than all the money in the world. Always look at the market value, not the price tag.
  2. Who holds the supply matters. One wallet holding half of all coins could have crashed the price at any moment. Check the top holders before buying any memecoin (see How to check a memecoin).
  3. Manias end. The record came after a month of 900% gains and endless headlines. When everyone is posting about a coin, latecomers usually buy from early holders.
  4. "Ecosystem" plans do not remove the risk. Bridges and new networks can be hacked. Its 12-month change today is −56.6%.

⚠️ Watch out

  • Copycat tokens. Thousands of "Shiba", "Baby Shiba" and "Shib 2.0" tokens exist. Real SHIB on Ethereum is listed under the ticker SHIB on big exchanges — always check the contract address.
  • Fake burn and airdrop sites. "Connect your wallet to claim free SHIB" pages steal everything in the wallet.
  • Buying because of a "millionaire" screenshot. For every viral winner there are many people who bought near the top.

What it means if you buy regularly

Buying a small fixed amount on a schedule means you never put everything in on the most exciting day. $10 a day in Shiba Inu for 5 years ($18,260 in total) became $6,794 (−62.8%); the same in Dogecoin became $10,033. Past results, not a forecast — and with memecoins, keep it to a tiny share of your money. Follow the price on the Shiba Inu page and today's memecoins on our memes page.
