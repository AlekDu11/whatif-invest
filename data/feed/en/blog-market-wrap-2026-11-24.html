Tuesday, November 24 undid part of Monday's party. Chip stocks fell hard — Nvidia about −5.9%, Analog Devices −17% — and the Nasdaq lost 1.48%, yet the Dow rose 0.85% and closed back above 50,000 as money moved into healthcare, retail and other "value" stocks. Crypto slipped again: Bitcoin was near $60,060 in our data, down 2.2% in 24 hours (live: $61,485.10 +0.85%).

Key takeaways

  • Dow +0.85% to 50,018.70; S&P 500 −0.50% to 7,201.09; Nasdaq −1.48% to 23,733.54; the chip index fell about 7.9%.
  • US consumer confidence jumped to 103.3 from 91.9 — far above the 92.2 economists expected.
  • Not one of the 45 coins worth $1 billion or more rose; Zcash fell 12.5%, Bitcoin 2.2%, Ethereum 2.9%.
  • The Crypto Fear & Greed Index sits at 11/100, "extreme fear" — the same as a day ago and 12 a week ago.

The big picture: a rotation, not a crash

Monday's rally was led by chips; on Tuesday those same stocks led the way down. The Philadelphia Semiconductor Index lost about 7.9% (Bloomberg, via Startup Fortune), while the Dow — full of banks, healthcare and consumer names — gained 423 points. The S&P 500 slipped 0.5%, its eighth fall in nine sessions. The 10-year US Treasury yield stayed near 4.90%.

Gold fell about 2.5%, to near $3,555 an ounce, and silver lost about 5.6%. US crude edged down about 1.6%, to near $81.

Crypto drifted lower all day. Total crypto market value was about $2.09 trillion (CoinGecko) and Bitcoin's share fell to 57.9% from 58.9% a day earlier — so smaller coins fell more than Bitcoin. Among the 20 largest coins the biggest mover was Zcash, −12.5%.

Biggest winners

Large stocks (closing moves) and the coins that held up best:

  • Dick's Sporting Goods about +17% — quarterly profit of $1.94 a share beat the $1.42 estimate, and Foot Locker's sales grew again (our brief).
  • Dollar Tree +11.2% — quarterly results beat expectations (Kohl's, a smaller retailer, jumped 19.5% for the same reason).
  • Sanofi +10.9%, Moderna +7.4% and Amgen +5.5% — drug makers led the move into defensive stocks; we found no single company-specific trigger.
  • Take-Two +5.2% — no specific news found.
  • In crypto, nothing rose among $1B+ coins: Monero was flat and TRON lost only 1.2%.

Biggest losers

  • Analog Devices −17.3% to $313.16 — it reported record quarterly results that morning, but the stock still fell with the chip sector and dragged Texas Instruments about 9% lower.
  • Hewlett Packard Enterprise −17.1% and Dell −12.0% — AI server makers were hit hardest (HPE brief).
  • Nvidia about −5.9% — profit-taking a day after its record 31% jump; AMD and Micron lost about 9–10% (our brief).
  • Zcash −12.5%, Rain −9.9%, Quant −8.8% and Hyperliquid −6.9% — the weakest large coins; we found no coin-specific news, they fell with the market.

The news that moved markets

  1. Chip stocks gave back gains. After Monday's 31% jump on Jensen Huang's return, Nvidia lost roughly $240 billion of market value as investors took profits and doubts about AI spending returned. Anthropic shares fell below their $83 IPO price, CNBC reported.
  2. Americans feel better. The Conference Board's consumer confidence index rose 11.4 points to 103.3, well above forecasts — one reason shop and consumer stocks rose.
  3. New tariffs are being prepared. The US is getting ready to raise tariffs on China, the EU, India and 14 other economies over industrial subsidies, the WSJ reported; nothing is official yet (brief).
  4. Bitcoin ETFs keep bleeding. US spot funds lost another $569.7 million on Monday, per SoSoValue, and Bitcoin slipped back under $60,000 in Asian trading (brief). Strategy still bought 457 BTC last week (brief).
  5. Washington on crypto and chips. Prosecutors moved to seize $1.17 billion in USDC from the Binance hack (brief), and the US blacklisted an Alibaba unit over suspected Nvidia chip diversion (brief).

What to watch today

  • Wednesday, Nov 25: US PCE inflation for October and the second estimate of Q3 GDP, plus durable goods and weekly jobless claims. The new US export curbs on the blacklisted firms take effect.
  • Thursday, Nov 26: US markets closed for Thanksgiving. Friday, Nov 27: US stocks close early.
  • Later: Salesforce reports on December 2; Nvidia's delayed quarterly filing is due by December 4; the Fed meets December 8–9.

What it means if you buy regularly

Monday and Tuesday together show why guessing single days rarely works: the chip rally that looked unstoppable on Monday gave back part of its gains a day later. Someone who put $10 a day into Bitcoin over the last year ($3,650 in total) now has $3,113 (−14.7%). The same habit in Ethereum stands at −19.1%, in the S&P 500 at −0.25%, in gold at −15.5% and in Nvidia at −22.7%.
