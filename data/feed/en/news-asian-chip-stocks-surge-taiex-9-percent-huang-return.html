The AI rebound that started on Wall Street moved to Asia on Tuesday, November 24. A day after Nvidia's board brought Jensen Huang back as CEO and its shares jumped 31.5%, chipmakers in Taiwan, South Korea and Japan posted some of their biggest gains in years.

What happened

  • Taiwan. The Taiex rose more than 9%. TSMC, which makes most of Nvidia's chips, and MediaTek each gained about 10%.
  • South Korea. The Kospi climbed 6.5% to 5,014.41. Samsung Electronics added 7.5% and SK hynix, a key supplier of AI memory, 8.6%.
  • Japan. The Nikkei 225 rose 3.9% to 58,654.40, led by chip-equipment makers Tokyo Electron (+10.5%) and Advantest (+14.1%). Hong Kong (+0.2%) and Shanghai (+0.7%) lagged.
  • The trigger. On Monday the Nasdaq rose 6.5% and the Philadelphia semiconductor index about 14%, after Nvidia had lost nearly 23% the week before.

Our angle: one good day after a hard month

These markets are heavy with AI suppliers, so they swing with Nvidia in both directions. Barclays analysts still expect the AI buildout to become slower and more selective, and Nvidia's delayed quarterly report is due by December 4. Right now TSMC trades at $347.12 −4.53% (+20.3% over 12 months, record $476.29 in June 2026), SK hynix at $807.42 −2.52%, Samsung Electronics at $132.09 −3.06% and Tokyo Electron at $52.17 −7.06%.
