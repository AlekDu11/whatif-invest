On Friday, 20 November, the Nasdaq Composite fell 5.28% to 22,617.73, and the AI sell-off finally reached the whole US market: the S&P 500 lost 3.70% for a seventh losing day in a row and closed in a correction, more than 10% below its record. On our data the Nasdaq-100 fund QQQ is now about 0.6% below its 2025 closing price — the whole year's gain is gone — and about 18% below its best monthly close in September.

Key takeaways

  • 20 November: Nasdaq −5.28%, S&P 500 −3.70%, Dow −1.93%. Nvidia fell 14.0% in a day and about 23% in a week, its worst week since October 2008, according to CoinGabbar.
  • QQQ is −10.5% in seven days and −18% from its September monthly peak; the S&P 500 (SPY) is about −10% from its August peak.
  • $10 a day in QQQ for 5 years is still worth $26,493 (+45.1%) on $18,260 invested. In 2000–02 the Nasdaq-100 fell 81%, and a monthly buyer who started at the very top was back in profit by January 2004.

What happened on Friday

The selling was concentrated in the AI trade. Nvidia dropped 14.0% to $125.53, its lowest close since May 2025. Broadcom lost about 12%, AMD 13.2% and Micron 13.9%, while TSMC's US shares fell 9.5%. Anthropic, which listed on Wednesday at $83, fell 8.5% to $88.91, about 20% below its first trade of $111.50.

The rest of the market fell less, and some defensive names rose: Walmart gained 1.5% and UnitedHealth 2.8%, as money moved toward retail and consumer staples. Gap jumped 11% on earnings. Bond prices rose and the 10-year Treasury yield slipped to 4.91%.

Crypto and gold did not offer a hiding place. Bitcoin was near $59,000, down about 5%, Ethereum fell about 6% to roughly $1,620 and gold lost about 3%. The Crypto Fear & Greed Index sat at 12, "extreme fear".

Why the sell-off accelerated

1. Doubts about AI spending. The question hanging over the market for weeks is whether hundreds of billions spent on AI data centres will earn enough back. Each weak headline now hits the whole chain, from chip makers to cloud companies.

2. Nvidia-specific headlines. The Motley Fool and CoinGabbar reported news of possible regulatory probes into Nvidia, including a potential SEC accounting inquiry. These are reports, not charges, and nothing has been proven. Broadcom fell on unconfirmed reports that Google may cut next year's chip orders.

3. Rates. Markets already price a Fed rate hike in December. On Friday Fed governor Lisa Cook said waiting too long to raise rates risks falling behind, which kept pressure on expensive growth stocks. (Our earlier piece looked at what past hikes did to Bitcoin, stocks and gold.)

4. Forced selling and fear. After a 10% weekly drop, some funds have to cut risk. Strategists were split: BNP Paribas called it panic selling, Deutsche Bank said the market looks very oversold. Neither view tells you what happens on Monday.

What it means if you buy regularly

A regular buyer does not need the top to come back to be in profit, because the cheaper months buy more shares. Our data, $10 a day (about $300 a month) in QQQ:

  • Started at the 2000 peak: after 31 months, at the 2002 low, $9,300 invested was worth about $4,560 (−51%). By January 2004 the portfolio was back above what had been put in — more than ten years before the index itself regained its high.
  • Started at the 2021 peak: at the end of 2022 it was −14%. By March 2023 it was even, and by December 2023 it was +26%.
  • Started at the September 2026 peak: $900 invested is now about $810 (−10%).

FAQ

Did the Nasdaq really erase all of 2026's gains? Yes, on Friday's close. QQQ ended at $608.45 against about $612 at the end of 2025, roughly −0.6% for the year.

Is this a bear market? A bear market usually means −20% from the peak. The Nasdaq-100 is about −18% on month-end data, the S&P 500 about −10%, which is a correction.

What should I watch next? The December Fed meeting, any official news on the Nvidia reports, and whether the selling spreads from AI to the rest of the market. Live headlines are on our news page.
