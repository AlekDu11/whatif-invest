How do you turn crypto back into money in your bank? It takes two steps on the exchange: first sell the coin for your own currency (euros, dollars, hryvnias…), then withdraw that money to a bank account or card in your own name. Before you confirm, look at the fee lines so you know exactly how much will arrive.

Key takeaways

  • Selling and withdrawing are two separate actions. After step one the money sits on the exchange; only step two sends it to your bank.
  • The exchange needs your ID check (KYC) done before you can sell for regular money — Binance says identity verification is mandatory for selling crypto to fiat (government money such as euros or dollars).
  • Every route has a cost: the spread (the gap between the buy and sell price), a trading fee and a withdrawal fee. Compare the "you will receive" line, not only the price.

This lesson continues How to withdraw crypto to your own wallet. If your coins are in your own wallet, first send them back to the exchange (open Deposit on the exchange, copy its address, and send a small test first — the same rules about the network apply).

Before you start

  • Your exchange account has 2FA turned on and the ID check finished (see How to register on a crypto exchange).
  • You know which of your currencies the exchange supports. Not every exchange pays out in every currency or to every country — check our exchanges page for the ones that work where you live.
  • The bank account or card is in your own name. Exchanges usually refuse payouts to other people's accounts.

Step 1 — Sell the coin for your currency

On Binance on the website: press Buy Crypto at the top, open the Sell tab, choose the coin (for example Bitcoin), enter the amount and press Sell BTC. In the app (Lite mode) it is the Trade button in the middle → Sell. Other exchanges have a similar Sell or Buy & Sell screen.

  1. Choose the coin and how much to sell. You can usually type the amount in your own currency instead of in coins.
  2. Choose where the money goes: to your exchange balance (then you withdraw it in Step 3) or, where offered, straight to your card or bank.
  3. Press Preview and read the screen: price, fee and "you will receive". The price can be recalculated after a few seconds — that is normal.
  4. Press Confirm and enter your 2FA code.

Tip: if the exchange does not pay out your currency directly, people often sell the coin for a stablecoin such as USDT first and then use the exchange's official way to cash that out — see what a stablecoin is.

Step 2 — Check the sale in the history

Open the order history (on Binance: Trade → Fiat History → Sell, or the orders list). The status should be Completed, and your currency should now show in the wallet balance.

Step 3 — Withdraw the money to your bank

  1. Open your wallet on the exchange, find your currency (for example EUR) and press Withdraw.
  2. Choose the method: bank transfer (in Europe often SEPA), card, or a local payment method the exchange shows for your country.
  3. The first time, you add your bank details. Some exchanges check the account with a tiny test payment — Binance, for example, asks EU users to verify the bank account with a 2 EUR transfer.
  4. Enter the amount, read the fee and arrival time, and confirm with 2FA.

A bank transfer can arrive in minutes or take a few working days, depending on the country and method.

Step 4 — Keep a record

Save the sale and the withdrawal (screenshot or a CSV export). In many countries profit from selling crypto is taxed, and you may need these numbers later. Check the rules where you live. You can keep your buys and sells in your portfolio to see what you really earned or lost.

What about P2P?

P2P (peer-to-peer) means you sell your coins to another person, who pays you directly by bank transfer; the exchange holds the coins until you confirm the payment. It is useful where the exchange has no direct payout, but it is where most beginner mistakes happen (see the box below).

⚠️ Watch out

  • Releasing coins before the money is really in your bank. In P2P, check your banking app yourself — not a screenshot or an e-mail "from the bank". Make sure the payment came from an account in the buyer's name.
  • Fake support. Nobody from an exchange will message you first to "help with a withdrawal" or ask for your password, 2FA code or seed phrase.
  • Selling everything because of a scary day. Decide in advance why and how much you sell. Selling in a panic locks in a loss; prices can move fast either way.

What it means if you buy regularly

If you buy a little every month, you do not have to sell everything at once either. Some people sell in parts — for example a fixed share each month when they need the money — which spreads the price just like buying regularly does. Example from our calculator: $10 a day in Bitcoin for 3 years ($10,960 in total) became $9,003 (−17.9%). Past results, not a forecast.
