On Monday 23 November 2026 Nvidia closed 31.5% higher at $165.05, after its board reinstated Jensen Huang as CEO. It was the largest one-day gain of any US company on record, adding roughly $950 billion in market value. For anyone who buys a little every day or week, the lesson is simple and uncomfortable: a single session can be worth more than a year of patient buying, and nobody gets a warning before it comes.

Key takeaways

  • Nvidia closed at $125.53 on Friday 20 November, its seventh weekly loss in a row and 47.5% below its record close of $239.24 on 6 October. On Monday it closed at $165.05 (+31.5%), on Tuesday it fell about 5.9%.
  • Every Nvidia share bought before Monday gained 31.5% in one day. Without that session, the same holding would be worth about 24% less.
  • J.P. Morgan's 2024 Guide to Retirement found that 7 of the S&P 500's 10 best days over two decades came within two weeks of the 10 worst.

How Nvidia went from its worst week to its best day

Nvidia's fall started weeks earlier. The stock dropped 9.3% on 5 November on fears that the US midterm results could bring AI regulation. On 8 November the board opened an independent investigation, and on 12 November it announced that Huang had resigned, with finance chief Colette Kress as interim CEO. In the week to 20 November Nvidia lost about 23% and the Philadelphia chip index about 17%, its worst week since September 2001, according to Admirals' timeline.

On Monday the board unanimously brought Huang back. Nvidia rose 31.5%, the VanEck Semiconductor ETF (SMH) 15%, the Nasdaq Composite 6.5% and the S&P 500 4.7%. Nomura's Charlie McElligott called it largely a mechanical bounce: traders who had bet on further falls rushed to buy back shares.

The relief lasted one day. On Tuesday Nvidia fell about 5.9%, CoreWeave 11.8%, and Anthropic slipped below its $83 IPO price, AP reported. The worries had not gone away: doubts that AI will earn enough to pay for data-centre spending, a 10-year Treasury yield near 4.9%, possible AI regulation after the midterms, and a hearing in Congress where Huang is due to answer questions in early December.

Today Nvidia trades at $155.38 −5.86%; its 12-month change is −12.0%.

The best days hide next to the worst

The pattern is not unique to Nvidia. In J.P. Morgan Asset Management's 2024 Guide to Retirement, seven of the S&P 500's ten best days over the previous two decades came within two weeks of its ten worst, and missing those ten best days cut a portfolio's value roughly in half.

Nvidia's November is a textbook case. The worst week for chip stocks in 25 years was followed, on the very next trading day, by the best single day in US stock market history. Someone who sold on Friday to "wait until things calm down" was out for the one session that mattered.

What it means if you buy regularly

DCA (dollar-cost averaging) means putting in a fixed amount on a schedule, for example $10 a day, whatever the price. Its main strength is not that it buys low. It is that it keeps you in the market on days like 23 November without any decision on your part.

Put in numbers: every share a regular buyer bought before Monday, at any price, rose 31.5% in one session. If that day had not happened, the same holding would be worth about 24% less. A person who paused their plan during the scary week also missed the cheapest prices in months: Friday's $125.53 close was the lowest of that week.

What $10 a day in Nvidia built so far: $2,923 over one year (−19.9%) and $63,379 over five years (+247%), against $18,260 put in.

The other side matters just as much. Huge up days happen mostly in falling, nervous markets, and they are often followed by more falls: Tuesday took back about a fifth of Monday's gain. Nvidia is still well below its record of $228.38 (September 2026), now −32.0% from it. Regular buying spreads your entry price; it does not stop a single stock, or a whole sector, from falling further. A broad fund such as the S&P 500 ($718.78 −0.49%) moved 4.7% that Monday, not 31.5%, and fell much less on the way down.

Two views on the rally

Why some see a turning point. The main uncertainty, who leads the company, was removed. Bernstein upgraded the stock to Outperform with a $200 target the same day, and Nvidia had beaten its forecasts in its quarterly report the week before.

Why others see a relief rally. The questions behind the sell-off are about the whole AI trade, not one CEO: debt-funded capex, high interest rates, possible regulation and the congressional hearings. Monday's jump was driven partly by short sellers covering bets, which can fade quickly, as Tuesday showed.

FAQ

Was 23 November really a record? Yes. Nvidia's 31.5% gain added about $950 billion in value, the largest one-day gain in market value for any US company, according to Admirals and Fox News.

Should I wait for a calm market before buying? We do not give advice. History shows that calm often returns only after the biggest rebounds have already happened.
