Nutanix stock is down because the cloud-software company said its CEO is leaving and kept its full-year revenue outlook flat, even though its latest quarter beat forecasts. The shares fell 15.2% to $46.30 on Wednesday, November 25, their worst day since May 2024, after trading as much as about 17% lower during the session.

What happened

  • Monday, Nov 24 (after the close, about 21:00 UTC). Nutanix reported fiscal Q1 2027 (quarter to October 31): revenue $772.4 million, up 15% from a year earlier and above the roughly $763 million analysts expected; adjusted earnings per share $0.73 against about $0.61 expected.
  • CEO change. In a filing the same day, Nutanix said Rajiv Ramaswami, CEO for about six years, will step down once a successor starts, and no later than April 30, 2027. The board has begun a search.
  • Outlook. Full-year revenue guidance stayed at about $3.18–3.23 billion, roughly in line with forecasts, and bookings slipped about 2% year on year on delayed large deals.
  • Wednesday, Nov 25. The stock closed at $46.30, down $8.29. BofA cut its rating to Neutral; Piper Sandler, Rosenblatt and Needham lowered price targets but kept buy-type ratings.

Why is Nutanix stock falling?

The quarter itself was strong, so the drop is about the road ahead. Investors dislike uncertainty at the top, and a leadership search that may take months landed together with a flat sales outlook, weaker bookings and plans to spend more on AI products. A shaky day for tech stocks added pressure. Some analysts called the sell-off an overreaction; that is an opinion, not a prediction.
