The US Justice Department has gone to court to take ownership of about $1.17 billion in USDC stolen in the October 29 Binance hack, the largest crypto theft on record. The civil forfeiture complaint, filed on Monday, November 23, in federal court in Washington, D.C., covers USDC held at 36 addresses on 18 blockchains.

What happened

  • Frozen, not moved. Circle, which issues USDC, blocked the addresses around October 29. It cannot move the original tokens, so it has agreed to issue the same amount of new USDC and hand it to the US if the court orders forfeiture.
  • One big wallet. The largest piece, about 823 million USDC, sits at a single Ethereum address the hackers used on the day of the theft.
  • A civil case. The action is against the coins, not people, and brings no criminal charges. US agencies blame North Korea's Lazarus Group (APT38). Prosecutors also asked the court to keep the funds restrained while the case runs.
  • The bigger picture. The DOJ says more than $2.9 billion in crypto has been frozen, seized or returned since the hack. Binance put its loss at $6.34 billion and says it covered users from its own funds.

What it means for ordinary holders

The case shows a trade-off of stablecoins: a central issuer can freeze stolen money, which helps victims, but it also means your coins can be blocked.
