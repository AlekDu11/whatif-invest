Gap, the owner of Old Navy, Gap, Banana Republic and Athleta, was one of the few big gainers on a red day for US stocks. Investors focused on better profits and brushed off a weaker forecast.

What happened

  • Thursday, November 19, after the US close. Gap reported third-quarter earnings of $0.82 per share, above the roughly $0.78 analysts expected and more than 30% higher than a year earlier.
  • Sales were flat-ish. Net sales rose 2% to about $4.0 billion, a touch below the $4.02 billion estimate. Comparable sales were flat; online grew 3% and made up 40% of sales.
  • The outlook went down. Gap now expects full-year earnings of $2.29–2.34 per share, slightly below the $2.35 consensus, after softer demand in October and early November.
  • Friday, November 20. The stock was up only about 4% before the open, but climbed to roughly +15% by late morning in New York.

Why it moved

The profit beat mattered more than the guidance cut. Gap's gross margin held at about 42.4% excluding a tariff refund, and the company has returned $788 million to shareholders this year. CEO Richard Dickson blamed warm weather for the slow start to autumn and said demand improved once it got colder, and he pointed to a clear improvement at Old Navy. Jefferies called the quarter mixed and trimmed its price target to $22 from $24.
