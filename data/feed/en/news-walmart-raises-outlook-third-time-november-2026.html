Walmart, the world's largest retailer, reported a better-than-expected quarter on Thursday, November 19, and raised its full-year forecast for the third time this year. Revenue for the three months to October 31 rose 5.2% to $188.8 billion, ahead of the roughly $187.3–187.4 billion analysts expected. Yet the stock swung before the opening bell, at one point trading about 2% lower, as the new outlook still fell short of some forecasts.

What happened

  • Small earnings beat. Adjusted earnings were $0.65 a share versus $0.64 expected. Comparable US sales rose 2.9%, held back partly by a federal rule capping prices of some high-cost Medicare drugs.
  • Online keeps growing fast. US e-commerce sales rose 23% (17% globally), US advertising 37% and the marketplace and fulfilment business 49%. More than 80% of US online orders were filled from stores.
  • New full-year range. Walmart now expects earnings of $2.84–2.89 a share (was $2.80–2.87) and sales growth of 4.9–5.2% (was 4–5%). The top of the EPS range only matches analysts' $2.89 estimate.
  • Shoppers are slowing. US retail sales rose just 0.2% in October, down from 0.8% in September.

What it means if you buy regularly

Walmart shares closed at $127.95 before the report. A single quarter rarely changes a long-term picture: someone who put $10 a day into Walmart over the last 5 years would now have $36,467 (+99.7%), versus $25,292 (+38.5%) for the S&P 500.
