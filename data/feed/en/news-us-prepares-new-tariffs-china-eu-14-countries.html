The United States is getting ready to raise tariffs on China, the European Union, India and 14 other economies, the Wall Street Journal reported on Tuesday, November 24. Nothing has been announced officially yet, but the report added to a nervous day: stocks, bitcoin and gold were all lower.

What happened

  • The plan. The tariffs would target industrial subsidies and excess manufacturing capacity. Per Bloomberg, the legal basis is a Section 301 trade investigation, and the new duties would replace tariffs the Supreme Court struck down.
  • Who pays what. The EU, Japan and South Korea, which have trade deals with Washington, are likely to stay at overall rates of 15% or lower. China is expected to face steeper rates; India and Vietnam could also pay more. Steel, aluminium and cars are likely excluded because they already carry separate tariffs.
  • Timing. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer could announce the findings as early as this week. Final rates, products and start dates are not decided.
  • Markets. Around 17:00 UTC the Nasdaq was down about 2%, bitcoin was near $59,000–60,000 and gold was down about 2%. CoinDesk linked the morning sell-off partly to the tariff report.

Our angle: tariffs, inflation and rates

Tariffs make imported goods more expensive, which can keep inflation up — and markets already price about 95% odds of a Fed rate hike in December.
