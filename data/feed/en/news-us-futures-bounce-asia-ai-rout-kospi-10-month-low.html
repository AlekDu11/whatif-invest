US stock futures rebounded sharply at the start of the week: Nasdaq 100 futures rose nearly 3% and S&P 500 futures about 2% in early Asian trading on Monday, November 23. Asia itself had a rougher open, as governments in South Korea, Taiwan and China moved to calm markets after last week's AI-driven crash.

What happened

  • Korea hit hardest. The KOSPI fell as much as about 5% to a 10-month low near 4,700 before steadying; the finance minister said a stabilisation plan is ready if needed.
  • Support measures. Taiwan's regulator tightened short-selling rules and Chinese exchanges eased margin-financing requirements. Taiwan's chip-heavy index, after its worst week in more than three decades, rose about 2%.
  • Last week's damage. Nvidia fell about 14% on Friday, the VIX fear gauge hit its highest since April 2025 and Anthropic shares slid about 8.5% on their second trading day.
  • The worry. Investors doubt that the borrowing behind new data centres will pay off fast enough, even as Morgan Stanley expects big-tech AI spending to jump over 70% next year to more than $1.6 trillion.
